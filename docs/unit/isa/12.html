Īśopaniṣad with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 12

andhaṃ tamaḥ praviśanti ye 'sambhūtim upāsate | tato bhūya iva te tamo ya u sambhūtyāṃ ratāḥ

Translation. Into blind darkness enter those who worship the Unmanifest (asambhūti), causal prakṛti; into darkness greater than that, as it were, enter those indeed who are devoted to Manifestation (sambhūti), the produced Brahman called Hiraṇyagarbha.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The object of upāsate is interpreted through Śaṅkara’s explicit identification of asambhūti as prakṛti, kāraṇa/avidyā, called avyākṛta, and as the seed of desire and action, of the nature of non-seeing.
→ asambhūti = the unmanifest causal prakṛti, the cause/avidyā, not simply “non-existence” or “destruction.”
isup [1]tasyā anyā asambhūtiḥ prakṛtiḥ kāraṇamavidyā avyākṛtākhyā tāmasambhūtimavyākṛtākhyāṃ prakṛtiṃ kāraṇamavidyāṃ kāmakarmabījabhūtāmadarśanātmikāmupāsate ye
Different from that is asambhūti: prakṛti, the cause, ignorance, called the unmanifest; those who worship that asambhūti—called unmanifest, prakṛti, the cause, ignorance, become the seed of desire and action, of the nature of non-seeing.
The locative sambhūtyām is not left as generic “birth” or “production”; Śaṅkara specifies the referent as kāryabrahman, Hiraṇyagarbha.
→ sambhūti = the effected/produced Brahman, called Hiraṇyagarbha.
isup [1]sambhavanaṃ sambhūtiḥ sā yasya kāryasya sā sambhūtiḥ
Coming-into-being is sambhūti; that product which has it is sambhūti.
isup [2]ya u sambhūtyāṃ kāryabrahmaṇi hiraṇyagarbhākhye ratāḥ
Those who are devoted to sambhūti, the effected Brahman called Hiraṇyagarbha.
Śaṅkara makes the darkness not merely physical gloom but adarśanātmaka, of the nature of non-seeing, and says it corresponds to the worshipped asambhūti.
→ andhaṃ tamaḥ is “blind/sightless darkness,” a state of non-seeing corresponding to the object worshipped.
isup [1]te tadanurūpamevāndhaṃ tamo 'darśanātmakaṃ praviśanti
They enter just a blind darkness corresponding to that, of the nature of non-seeing.
The second pāda is comparative, not merely temporal “then”; Śaṅkara glosses tataḥ as tasmād api and bhūyaḥ as bahutaram iva, and explicitly repeats tamaḥ praviśanti.
→ tataḥ bhūya iva means “greater than that, as it were,” and the verb praviśanti is supplied in the second half.
isup [2]tatastasmādapi bhūyo bahutaramiva tamaḥ praviśanti
tataḥ means even than that; they enter darkness more, much greater as it were.
Śaṅkara’s paraphrase preserves the accusative tām asambhūtim with upāsate and the locative sambhūtyām with ratāḥ, fixing the syntactic construal.
→ asambhūtim is the accusative object of upāsate, while sambhūtyām is a locative with ratāḥ.
isup [1]tāmasambhūtimavyākṛtākhyāṃ prakṛtiṃ kāraṇamavidyāṃ kāmakarmabījabhūtāmadarśanātmikāmupāsate ye
Those who worship that asambhūti, called unmanifest, prakṛti, the cause, ignorance, of the nature of non-seeing.
isup [2]ya u sambhūtyāṃ kāryabrahmaṇi hiraṇyagarbhākhye ratāḥ
Those who are devoted in/to sambhūti, the effected Brahman called Hiraṇyagarbha.
