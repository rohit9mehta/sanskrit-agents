Īśopaniṣad with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 1

oṃ īśā vāsyam idaṃ sarvaṃ yat kiñca jagatyāṃ jagat | tena tyaktena bhuñjīthā mā gṛdhaḥ kasya sviddhanam

Translation. Om. By the Lord (Īśa), as the inward Self, all this is to be covered—whatever moving thing there is in the world. By that renunciation you should maintain yourself; do not covet anyone’s wealth (or: do not covet—for whose wealth is it?).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The opening word is not the feminine noun ‘dominion’ and not part of a split īśā vā + āsyam. Śaṅkara defines the governing īś as the Lord/supreme Self and glosses the instrumental sense with īśena and ātmanā.
→ īśā = ‘by the Lord’, namely by the supreme Self as the inner Self
isup [2]īśitā parameśvaraḥ paramātmā sarvasya /
The ruler is the Supreme Lord, the supreme Self of all.
isup [3]sa hi sarvamīṣṭe sarvajantūnāmātmā sanpratyagātmatayā tena svena rūpeṇātmaneśā vāsyamācchādanīyam /
For he rules all, being the Self of all creatures as the inward Self; by that own form, by the Self, it is to be covered by the Lord.
isup [4]tatsarvaṃ svenātmanā īśena pratyagātmatayāhamevedaṃ sarvamiti paramārthasatyarūpeṇa
All that is to be covered by one’s own Self, by the Lord, as the inward Self, in the ultimately real form ‘I alone am all this.’
The gerundive does not mean ‘to be inhabited’ or ‘to be used’; Śaṅkara repeatedly glosses it as ācchādanīyam and explains the covering as a contemplative superimposition of the real Self over the unreal world, like natural sandal fragrance covering adventitious bad odor.
→ vāsyam = ‘to be covered/enveloped’ by the Lord as one’s own Self
isup [3]īśā vāsyamācchādanīyam /
‘vāsyam by the Lord’ means ‘to be covered’.
isup [4]anṛtamidaṃ sarvaṃ carācaramācchādanīyaṃ svena paramātmanā /
All this unreal moving-and-unmoving world is to be covered by one’s own supreme Self.
isup [5]yathā candanāgarvāderudakādisambandhajakledādijamaupādhikaṃ daurgandhyaṃ tatsvarūpanigharṣaṇena ācchāghate svena pāramārthikena gandhena /
As the adventitious bad odor of sandalwood and the like, born of contact with water and dampness, is covered by its own true fragrance when its surface is rubbed.
Although jagatyām is glossed as ‘on the earth’, Śaṅkara says it is an upalakṣaṇa, an indicative mention, so the scope is all name-form-action transformations, not merely terrestrial moving beings.
→ idaṃ sarvaṃ yat kiñca jagatyāṃ jagat has universal scope
isup [4]idaṃ sarva yatkiñca yatkiñcijjagatyāṃ pṛthivyāṃ jagattatsarvaṃ
This all—whatever moving thing there is in jagatī, on the earth—all that.
isup [6]jagatyāṃ pṛthivyām, jagatyāmiti upalakṣaṇārthatvātsarvameva nāmarūpakarmākhyaṃ vikārajātaṃ
‘In jagatī’ means ‘on the earth’; since ‘in jagatī’ has an indicative function, it means the whole mass of transformations called name, form, and action.
Śaṅkara explicitly rejects the idea that ‘by the renounced’ means being sustained by an abandoned object such as a dead son or servant. The Vedic meaning, he says, is tyāgena, ‘by renunciation’, in the context of renouncing the three desires.
→ tena tyaktena = ‘by that renunciation’
isup [7]evamīśvarātmabhāvanayā yuktasya putrādyeṣaṇātrayasaṃnyāsa evādhikāro na karmasu /
For one endowed with this contemplation of the Lord as the Self, the entitlement is solely to renunciation of the three desires, beginning with desire for sons, not to rites.
isup [8]tena tyaktena tyāgenetyarthaḥ /
‘By that tyaktena’ means ‘by renunciation’.
isup [9]na hi tyakto mṛtaḥ putro vā bhṛtyo vā ātmasambandhitāyā abhāvāt ātmānaṃ pālayati atastyāgena ityayameva vedārthaḥ
For a renounced or dead son or servant does not protect oneself, since there is no relation to oneself; therefore ‘by renunciation’—this alone is the Vedic meaning.
The common lexical sense of √bhuj, ‘enjoy/consume’, is not Śaṅkara’s chosen sense here. He glosses bhuñjīthāḥ with pālayethāḥ and explains the protection of oneself through renunciation.
→ bhuñjīthāḥ = ‘you should protect/maintain yourself’, not ‘you should enjoy’
isup [9]bhuñjīthāḥ pālayethāḥ /
‘bhuñjīthāḥ’ means ‘you should protect/maintain’.
isup [10]evaṃ tyaktaiṣaṇastvaṃ
Thus you, having renounced desires...
Śaṅkara first takes gṛdh as covetous desire directed to wealth and treats svid as an expletive particle, so kasya svid dhanam means the wealth of anyone, another’s or one’s own. He also offers an alternative: the phrase has rhetorical force, since, when everything has been renounced as the Self, there is no one’s wealth to covet.
→ mā gṛdhaḥ kasya svid dhanam = ‘do not covet anyone’s wealth’, with an alternative rhetorical sense ‘whose wealth is it?’
isup [10]mā gṛdhaḥ gṛdhimākāṅkṣāṃ mā kārṣīrdhanaviṣayām kasyasviddhanaṃ kasyacitparasya svasya vā dhanaṃ mā kāṅkṣīrityarthaḥ /
‘Do not covet’: do not make greed, desire, whose object is wealth; ‘kasya svid dhanam’ means do not desire anyone’s wealth, whether another’s or your own.
isup [11]svidityanarthako nipātaḥ /
svid is a particle without semantic force.
isup [12, 13]athavā mā gṛdhaḥ / kasmāt? kasyasviddhanamityākṣepārtho na kasyaciddhanamasti yadgṛdhyeta /
Or: ‘do not covet.’ Why? ‘Whose wealth?’ has the force of an objection: there is no wealth belonging to anyone that might be coveted.
isup [14]ātmaivedaṃ sarvamitīśvarabhāvanayā sarvaṃ tyaktamata ātmana evedaṃ sarvamātmaiva ca sarvamato mithyāviṣayāṃ gṛdhiṃ mā kārṣīrityarthaḥ
By the contemplation ‘the Self alone is all this’ everything has been renounced; therefore all this belongs to the Self alone, and the Self alone is all; hence do not make greed whose object is false.
