Īśopaniṣad with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 9

andhaṃ tamaḥ praviśanti ye 'vidyām upāsate | tato bhūya iva te tamo ya uvidyāyāṃ ratāḥ

Translation. Into blind darkness enter those who devote themselves to avidyā—ritual action alone. Into a darkness still greater than that, indeed, enter those who, however, delight in vidyā—deity-knowledge alone.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The phrase is not taken as a separate noun ‘the blind’ plus ‘darkness’, nor specifically as a named hell, but as a neuter accusative object: darkness whose nature is non-seeing/obscuration.
→ andhaṃ tamaḥ as ‘blind/sight-obscuring darkness’
isup [15]tatra andhaṃ tamaḥ adarśanātmakaṃ tamaḥ praviśanti
Here, ‘blind darkness’ is darkness whose nature is non-seeing; they enter it.
In this verse Śaṅkara does not allow avidyā to mean ordinary ignorance alone; he explicitly defines it as karma, because it is other than vidyā and opposed to it.
→ avidyā = karma, especially Agnihotra and similar rites
isup [16]ye 'vidyāṃ vidyāyā anyā avidyā tāṃ karma ityarthaḥ
Those who [pursue] avidyā: avidyā is what is other than vidyā; by that, karma is meant.
isup [17]karmaṇo vidyāvirodhitvāt ; tāmavidyāmagnihotrādilakṣaṇāmeva kevalāmupāsate
Because karma is opposed to vidyā; that avidyā, characterized by Agnihotra and the like, alone they upāsate.
Because its object is karma, Śaṅkara explains upāsate by tatparāḥ santaḥ anutiṣṭhanti: being intent upon it, they perform it. The translation therefore avoids the misleading ‘worship ignorance’.
→ upāsate as ‘devote themselves to / practise’
isup [17]tāmavidyāmagnihotrādilakṣaṇāmeva kevalāmupāsate tatparāḥ santo 'nutiṣṭhantītyabhiprāyaḥ
They upāsate that avidyā alone, characterized by Agnihotra and the like; the intention is: being intent upon it, they perform it.
The vidyā condemned here when isolated is not supreme Self-knowledge. Śaṅkara identifies it as deity-directed knowledge connected with karma and expressly excludes paramātma-jñāna.
→ vidyā = devatā-jñāna, knowledge concerning deities
isup [5]yaddaivaṃ vittaṃ devatāviṣayaṃ jñānaṃ karmasambandhitvenopanyastaṃ na paramātmajñānam
That divine wealth is knowledge whose object is deities, presented as connected with karma, not knowledge of the supreme Self.
isup [18]ye u ye tu vidyāyāmeva devatājñāna eva ratāḥ abhiratāḥ
ye u: those, however, who are delighted—attached—in vidyā alone, that is, in deity-knowledge alone.
The second relative clause is contrastive: ‘those, however’. Ratāḥ is taken as ‘delighted/attached’, reinforced by Śaṅkara’s abhiratāḥ.
→ u as contrastive tu, and ratāḥ as abhiratāḥ
isup [18]ye u ye tu vidyāyāmeva devatājñāna eva ratāḥ abhiratāḥ
ye u means ‘those, however’; ratāḥ means ‘attached/delighted’ in deity-knowledge alone.
The ablatival tatas refers back to the blind darkness, while bhūya iva is glossed as bahutaram eva; the second clause also carries the verb praviśanti supplied in the commentary.
→ tato bhūya iva ... tamaḥ as ‘a darkness still greater than that, indeed’
isup [18]tatastasmādandhātmakāttamaso bhūya iva bahutarameva te tamaḥ praviśanti
From/than that blind darkness, into a still more abundant darkness indeed, they enter.
The translation’s ‘alone’ is justified by Śaṅkara’s explanation that the verse censures the separate performance of each member in order to motivate a combination of karma and deity-knowledge; it is not an absolute rejection of scripture-enjoined rites.
→ the condemnation targets isolated practice, not scripturally enjoined karma or vidyā as such
isup [4]iha tu samuccicīṣayā avidvadādinindā kriyate
Here, however, censure of the ignorant and so on is made with the desire for combination.
isup [9]tayorjñānakarmaṇoriha ekaikānuṣṭhānanindā samuccicīṣayā na nindāparaiva ekaikasya pṛthakphalaśravaṇāt
Here there is censure of the separate performance of each of the two, knowledge and karma, for the sake of combining them; it is not mere censure, since a separate result is heard for each.
isup [14]na hi śāstravihitaṃ kiñcidakartavyatāmiyāt
For nothing enjoined by scripture can become something not to be done.
isup [19]tatrāvāntaraphalabhedaṃ vidyākarmaṇoḥ samuccayakāraṇamāha
There he states the distinction of subsidiary results of vidyā and karma as the reason for their combination.
