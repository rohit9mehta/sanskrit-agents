Īśopaniṣad with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2

kurvann eveha karmāṇi jijīviṣecchataṃ samāḥ | evaṃ tvayi nānyatheto 'sti na karma lipyate nare

Translation. Only doing here the ritual works (karmāṇi), one should seek to live a hundred years. Thus, for you—one who would live, taking yourself as just a man (nara)— there is no other way than this: action (karma) does not cling to the man.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The object karmāṇi should not be translated as any and all activity in a secular sense. Śaṅkara identifies it as Agnihotra and other prescribed rites, and repeats that one who wishes to live should do precisely those śāstra-enjoined works.
→ karmāṇi = śāstra-prescribed ritual works, especially Agnihotra and the like; kurvan eva = only while doing them.
isup [1]karmāṇyagnihotrādīni
“works, Agnihotra and the like.”
isup [5]ataḥ śāstravihitāni karmāṇyagnihotrādīni kurvanneva jijīviṣet
“Therefore one should wish to live only while doing the works enjoined by scripture, Agnihotra and the like.”
The optative jijīviṣet is glossed as ‘should wish to live,’ and samāḥ as ‘years.’ Śaṅkara adds that the wish to live a hundred years is a prāptānuvāda, a restatement of a natural fact; what is enjoined is ‘doing works alone.’ The translation therefore avoids making desire for longevity an independent Upaniṣadic duty.
→ jijīviṣet śataṃ samāḥ = one should seek/wish to live a hundred years, but the real injunction is to do works while living.
isup [1]jijīviṣejjīvitumicchecchataṃ śatasaṅkhyākāḥ samāḥ saṃvatsarān
“jijīviṣet means ‘should wish to live’; śatam means ‘numbered by a hundred’; samāḥ means ‘years.’”},{
The words evam, anyathā, and itaḥ are construed around prakāra: ‘in this manner,’ ‘a different manner,’ and ‘from this present manner.’ This rules out a merely spatial reading such as ‘not otherwise from here.’
→ evam … nānyathā itaḥ asti = in this manner there is no other way than this present mode of doing the prescribed works.
isup [4]evamevamprakāreṇa ... etasmādagnihotrādīni karmāṇi kurvato vartamānātprakārādanyathā prakārāntaraṃ nāsti
“evam means ‘in this very manner’ ... apart from this present manner of doing Agnihotra and other works, there is no other different manner.”
Śaṅkara construes tvayi with nare and supplies jijīviṣati and naramātrābhimānini. The translation therefore treats the second half as addressed to the person on the action-path, not to the renunciant knower described in the previous mantra.
→ tvayi and nare are locatives in apposition: ‘in/for you, as a man,’ specifically one wishing to live and identifying merely as a man.
isup [4]tvayi jijīviṣati nare naramātrābhimāninīta
“in you, in a man wishing to live, [read from the unsegmented text as naramātrābhimānini itaḥ:] identifying merely as a man; from this ...”
Although karma is morphologically the neuter nominative/accusative singular form in the mantra, Śaṅkara explains the intended sense as non-taint by karma and even specifies aśubha karma. Thus ‘does not cling/taint’ is preferable to a flat passive ‘karma is not smeared in the man.’
→ na karma lipyate nare = action does not cling to/taint the man; idiomatically, he is not tainted by karma.
isup [4]yena prakāreṇāśubhaṃ karma na lipyate karmaṇā na lipyata ityarthaḥ
“by which manner evil karma does not cling; the meaning is, ‘he is not tainted by karma’ [lipyata is taken as the unproofread lipyate].”
Śaṅkara explicitly frames mantra 1 as sannyāsin-jñānaniṣṭhā and mantra 2 as karmaniṣṭhā for the one incapable of that, insisting on the firm opposition between knowledge and action and on two distinct paths.
→ The mantra teaches karmaniṣṭhā for one unable to take the renunciant knowledge-path; it is not a doctrine of combining knowledge and ritual action.
isup [6]pūrveṇa saṃnyāsino jñānaniṣṭhoktā dvitīyena tadaśaktasya karmaniṣṭheti
“By the previous [mantra] the knowledge-discipline of the renunciant was taught; by the second, the action-discipline of one incapable of that.”
isup [7]jñānakarmaṇorvirodhaṃ parvatavadakampyaṃ
“the opposition of knowledge and action, unshakable like a mountain.”
isup [11]imau dvāveva panthānāvanuniṣkrāntatarau bhavataḥ kriyāpathaścaiva purastātsaṃnyāsaścottareṇa
“These two paths alone stand out: the path of ritual action first, and renunciation thereafter.”
