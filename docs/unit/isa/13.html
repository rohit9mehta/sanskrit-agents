Īśopaniṣad with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 13

anyad evāhuḥ saṃbhavād anyad āhur asaṃbhavāt | iti śuśruma dhīrāṇāṃ ye nas tad vicacakṣire

Translation. Quite different, they say, is the fruit from saṃbhava (sambhūti: worship of the effect-Brahman, kārya-brahman); different, they say, is the fruit from asaṃbhava (asambhūti: worship of the unmanifest, avyākṛta). Thus have we heard from the wise, who explained that to us.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The neuter anyat is not left as an unspecified ‘something else’, nor is saṃbhavāt taken as the object of a simple comparative ‘other than saṃbhava’. Śaṅkara supplies phalam and glosses anyad eva with pṛthag eva, so the verse contrasts distinct results.
→ anyat eva = ‘quite/separately different is the fruit’
isup [1]anyadeva pṛthagevāhuḥ phalaṃ sambhavātsambhūteḥ kāryabrahmopāsanādaṇimādyaiśvaryalakṣaṇaṃ vyākhyātavanta ityarthaḥ
‘Different indeed’ means ‘separate indeed’; they explained the fruit from saṃbhava/sambhūti, from worship of the effect-Brahman, characterized by lordly powers beginning with aṇimā.
isup [3]ye nastadvicacakṣire vyākṛtāvyākṛtopāsanaphalaṃ vyākhyātavanta ityarthaḥ
Those who explained that to us explained the fruit of worship of the manifest and the unmanifest.
Śaṅkara identifies saṃbhava with sambhūti and with kārya-brahmopāsanā. Thus the ablative denotes the source/cause, namely that upāsanā, not mere ‘birth’, ‘coming into being’, or an abstract metaphysical ‘existence’.
→ saṃbhavāt = ‘from saṃbhava/sambhūti, worship of the effect-Brahman’
isup [1]sambhavātsambhūteḥ kāryabrahmopāsanādaṇimādyaiśvaryalakṣaṇaṃ
From saṃbhava, from sambhūti, from worship of the effect-Brahman, whose mark/result is lordly power beginning with aṇimā.
isup [3]vyākṛtāvyākṛtopāsanaphalaṃ
The fruit of worship of the manifest and the unmanifest.
The commentary takes the second ablative as the counterpart of saṃbhava: asambhūti/avyākṛta-upāsanā. Line 2 is visibly unproofread around ‘asambhūtekhyākṛtād’; I do not rely on that corrupt-looking segment alone, but the following avyākṛtopāsanāt and line 3’s vyākṛtāvyākṛtopāsanaphalam fix the intended sense.
→ asaṃbhavāt = ‘from asaṃbhava/asambhūti, worship of the unmanifest’
isup [2]tathā cānyadāhuḥ asambhavādasambhūtekhyākṛtād avyākṛtopāsanāt
Likewise they said another [fruit] from asambhava/asambhūti … from worship of the unmanifest; the printed middle segment appears corrupt/unsegmented.
isup [3]vyākṛtāvyākṛtopāsanaphalaṃ vyākhyātavanta ityarthaḥ
They explained the fruit of worship of the manifest and the unmanifest.
The genitive dhīrāṇām is construed with an understood vacanam in the bhāṣya. Therefore the verse says that this teaching was received from the wise, not merely that ‘we heard wise men’.
→ śuśruma dhīrāṇām = ‘we have heard the teaching/from the wise’
isup [3]ityevaṃ śuśruma dhīraṇāṃ vacanaṃ
Thus we heard the speech/teaching of the wise. The printed dhīraṇāṃ is an unproofread spelling for dhīrāṇām.
Although vi-√cakṣ can mean ‘see distinctly’, Śaṅkara glosses it as vyākhyātavanta, ‘they explained’. The object is tat, and naḥ is the recipient, ‘to us’. The scope of tat is the just-mentioned fruit of worship of the manifest and unmanifest.
→ ye naḥ tad vicacakṣire = ‘who explained that to us’
isup [3]ye nastadvicacakṣire vyākṛtāvyākṛtopāsanaphalaṃ vyākhyātavanta ityarthaḥ
Those who explained that to us—i.e. explained the fruit of worship of the manifest and the unmanifest.
