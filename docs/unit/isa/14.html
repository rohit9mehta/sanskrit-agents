Īśopaniṣad with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 14

saṃbhūtiṃ ca vināśaṃ ca yas tad vedobhayaṃ saha | vināśena mṛtyuṃ tīrtvā saṃbhūtyāmṛtam aśnute

Translation. He who knows that pair together— asambhūti, the unmanifest (the text’s saṃbhūti read with a-elision), and vināśa, the perishable effect— having crossed death (mṛtyu) by vināśa, that is, by its worship, attains the deathless (amṛta), the state marked by dissolution into prakṛti, by asambhūti.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Translate the term as ‘asambhūti, the unmanifest’, while recording the printed morphology as sambhūti. This is not a generic ‘birth/production’ here.
→ The two printed forms saṃbhūtim/saṃbhūtyā are understood, in Śaṅkara’s interpretation, as asambhūtim/asambhūtyā: the unmanifest, the avyākṛta, and its worship.
isup [2]sambhūtiṃ ca vināśaṃ cetyatrāvarṇalopena nirdeśo draṣṭavyaḥ prakṛtilayaphalaśrutyanurodhāt
In the expression ‘sambhūtiṃ ca vināśaṃ ca’, the statement is to be seen as made with elision of the vowel a, because that agrees with the scriptural statement of a fruit consisting in dissolution into prakṛti.
isup [1]asambhūtyā avyākṛtopāsanayā amṛtaṃ prakṛtilayalakṣaṇamaśnute
By asambhūti, that is, by worship of the unmanifest, he attains the deathless, whose mark is dissolution into prakṛti.
Do not translate vināśena as ‘by destruction’ in the abstract. In Śaṅkara’s construal, the thing called vināśa is the kārya whose property is destruction; the instrumental is upāsanā-instrumental.
→ vināśa denotes the perishable effect/dharmin, and vināśena means ‘by worship of that’, identified in the commentary with Hiraṇyagarbha-upāsana.
isup [1]vināśo dharmo yasya kāryasya sa tena dharmiṇā abhedena ucyate vināśa iti, tena tadupāsanena
The effect whose property is destruction is, by non-difference from the possessor of that property, called ‘vināśa’; by that, by worship of it...
isup [1]hiraṇyagarbhopāsanenāpti hyāṇimādiprāptiḥ phalam
The line is unproofread here; understood in the standard segmentation as ‘by worship of Hiraṇyagarbha, indeed, attainment of aṇimā and the like is the fruit.’
Translate mṛtyu as ‘death’ but make clear from the commentary that ordinary biological death is not the whole sense here. The crossing is an overcoming of anaiśvarya and related defects.
→ mṛtyu is the state of non-lordship and defect-born limitation; tīrtvā means ‘having passed beyond’.
isup [1]anaiśvaryamadharmakāmādidoṣajātaṃ ca mṛtyuṃ tīrtvā
Having crossed death consisting in non-lordship and the mass of defects such as adharma and desire...
isup [1]tenānaiśvaryādimṛtyumatītya
Thereby having passed beyond death such as non-lordship...
Do not read amṛtam here as unqualified final liberation. Śaṅkara glosses it as a specific result: dissolution into prakṛti through worship of the unmanifest.
→ amṛtam is the fruit characterized as prakṛti-laya; aśnute is ‘attains/enjoys’.
isup [1]asambhūtyā avyākṛtopāsanayā amṛtaṃ prakṛtilayalakṣaṇamaśnute
By asambhūti, by worship of the unmanifest, he attains the deathless, marked by dissolution into prakṛti.
isup [2]prakṛtilayaphalaśrutyanurodhāt
Because it accords with the scriptural statement of the fruit as dissolution into prakṛti.
The relative yaḥ is the subject of veda; tad ubhayam is the neuter accusative object summarizing the two preceding accusatives; saha is adverbial ‘together’.
→ yas ... tad ubhayam saha veda is construed as ‘who knows that pair, both together’.
