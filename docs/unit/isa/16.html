Īśopaniṣad with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 16

pūṣann ekarṣe yama sūrya prājāpatya vyūha raśmīn samūha tejaḥ | yat te rūpaṃ kalyāṇatamaṃ tat te paśyāmi yo 'sāv asau puruṣaḥ so 'ham asmi

Translation. O Pūṣan (nourisher), O ekarṣi (sole traverser), O Yama (restrainer), O Sūrya, O Prājāpatya (offspring of Prajāpati): disperse your rays; draw together your scorching brilliance. That form of yours which is most auspiciously beautiful—through your own favor—I behold. That person (puruṣa) who is yonder, yonder in the solar disk: that am I.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Translate as “O Pūṣan (nourisher), O ekarṣi (sole traverser), O Yama (restrainer), O Sūrya, O Prājāpatya (offspring of Prajāpati),” not as unrelated deities or as a separate “one sage.”
→ The opening five forms are vocatives to the one solar deity, each rendered by Śaṅkara’s etymological sense.
isup [1]he pūṣan! jagataḥ poṣaṇātpūṣā ravistathaika eva ṛṣati gacchati ityekarṣiḥ-- he ekarṣe! tathā sarvasya saṃyamanādyamaḥ-- he yama! tathā raśmīnāṃ prāṇānāṃ rasānāñca svīkaraṇāt sūryaḥ-- he sūrya! prajāpaterapatyaṃ prājāpatyaḥ-- he prājāpatya!
“O Pūṣan: because he nourishes the world he is Pūṣan, the Sun. Likewise, because he alone moves/goes, he is ekarṣi—O ekarṣi. Likewise, because of restraining all, he is Yama—O Yama. Likewise, because of taking in rays, vital breaths, and essences, he is Sūrya—O Sūrya. The offspring of Prajāpati is Prājāpatya—O Prājāpatya.”
Take raśmīn as the accusative object of the imperative vyūha, with vyūha in the sense of vigamaya, “make depart/remove,” rather than “arrange” or “marshal.”
→ vyūha raśmīn means “disperse/remove your own rays.”
isup [1]vyūha vigamaya raśmīnsvān
“Disperse—make go away—your own rays.”
Take tejaḥ as neuter accusative singular, the object of samūha; the commentary glosses samūha by ekīkuru and apparently by upasaṃhara, and defines tejas as heat-making light. The transmitted line is under-segmented around upasaṃharate tejastāpakaṃ, so the segmentation is noted rather than silently normalized.
→ samūha tejaḥ means “draw together/withdraw the burning light.”
isup [2]samūha ekīkuru upasaṃharate tejastāpakaṃ jyotiḥ
“Samūha: make one/collect; withdraw your heat-making light.”
Render kalyāṇatamam according to the gloss atyantaśobhanam, and construe te as genitive (“of yours/your own”), not as an unrelated dative.
→ The “form” is the sun’s most auspicious/beautiful form, beheld through his own favor.
isup [3]yatte tava rūpaṃ kalyāṇatamam atyantaśobhanaṃ tatte tavātmanaḥ prasādāt paśyāmi
“That form of yours which is kalyāṇatama, exceedingly beautiful, that I behold through the favor of your own self.”
Translate puruṣa as “person (puruṣa)” with the solar location supplied by the commentary; Śaṅkara supports the term by person-shapedness and by the traditional “lying in the city” derivation.
→ puruṣaḥ is the yonder person in the solar disk, not a generic human being.
isup [4]yo 'sāvādityamaṇḍalastho
“Who is yonder, situated in the solar disk.”
isup [4]puruṣaḥ puruṣākāratvāt
“He is puruṣa because he has the form of a person.”
isup [4]puri śayanādvā puruṣaḥ
“Or he is puruṣa because he lies in the city.”
Do not read the speaker as merely begging a favor like a servant; the commentary explicitly rejects that framing before glossing so 'ham asmi by bhavāmi.
→ so 'ham asmi is an identity assertion: “that am I / I am he.”
isup [4]kiñcāhaṃ na tu tvāṃ bhṛtyavadyāce
“Moreover, I do not beg you like a servant.”
isup [4]so 'hamasmi bhavāmi
“That I am; I am/become that.”
