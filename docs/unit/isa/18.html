Īśopaniṣad with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 18

agne naya supathā rāye asmān viśvāni deva vayunāni vidvān | yuyodhy asmaj juhurāṇam eno bhūyiṣṭhāṃ te nama uktiṃ vidhema

Translation. O Agni, lead us—qualified by the fruit of the dharma already taught—by the fair path, the path free from repeated going and coming, to wealth, that is, enjoyment of action’s fruit; O god, knowing all the vayunāni, all actions or forms of knowledge. Remove from us the crooked, deceitful sin (enas); to you may we render a most abundant utterance of obeisance (nama-ukti).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The opening is a direct prayer to Agni, not a descriptive sentence about fire.
→ agne is a vocative address and naya is the imperative “lead/conduct.”
isup [1]he agne! naya gamaya supathā śobhanena mārgeṇa /
“O Agni! naya means gamaya, ‘lead/conduct’; supathā means ‘by the fair path.’”
The compound is not merely “by a path well” or an unspecified route; Śaṅkara makes it a qualified path opposed to the dakṣiṇa-mārga characterized by repeated going and coming.
→ supathā is the instrumental “by the fair/good path,” specifically the path excluding the southern path of return; formally it is analyzed from the compound stem supath.
isup [1]he agne! naya gamaya supathā śobhanena mārgeṇa /
“Lead/conduct by the fair path, by the beautiful/good road.”
isup [2]supratheti viśeṣaṇaṃ dakṣiṇamārganivṛttyartham /
“The adjective—printed here as supratheti, evidently unproofread for supatheti—is for excluding the southern path.”
isup [3]nirviṇṇo 'haṃ dakṣiṇena mārgeṇa gatāgatalakṣaṇenāto yāce tvāṃ punaḥ punargamanāgamanavarjitena śobhanena pathā naya /
“I am weary of the southern path, characterized by going and returning; therefore I ask you: lead by the good path free from repeated coming and going.”
The dative rāye is not taken in an unrelated sense such as “sound/noise”; in this bhāṣya it is the desired karmic fruit.
→ rāye means “for wealth,” explained as enjoyment of the fruit of action; asmān is “us” as qualified by the fruit of the dharma already taught.
isup [4]rāye dhanāya karmaphalabhogāyetyarthaḥ, asmānyathoktadharmaphalaviśiṣṭān viśvāni sarvāṇi he deva! vayunāni karmāṇi prajñānāni vā vidvāñjānan /
“rāye means ‘for wealth,’ that is, for enjoyment of the fruit of action; asmān means ‘us, qualified by the fruit of the dharma stated above.’”
The object of Agni’s knowing is not another “path” but the totality of vayunāni as glossed by the commentator.
→ viśvāni qualifies vayunāni, and vayunāni are “actions, or cognitions/knowledges”; vidvān means “knowing.”
isup [4]rāye dhanāya karmaphalabhogāyetyarthaḥ, asmānyathoktadharmaphalaviśiṣṭān viśvāni sarvāṇi he deva! vayunāni karmāṇi prajñānāni vā vidvāñjānan /
“viśvāni means ‘all’; O god, vayunāni are actions, or cognitions; vidvān means knowing.”
juhurāṇam is construed adjectivally with enas, and asmat is separative: the sin is to be removed from us.
→ yuyodhi asks Agni to separate/remove or destroy from us the crooked, deceitful sin (enas).
isup [5]kiñca yuyodhi viyojaya vināśaya asmadasmatto juhurāṇaṃ kuṭilaṃ vañcanātmakamenaḥ pāpam /
“Further, yuyodhi means separate, destroy; asmat means from us; juhurāṇam means crooked, deceitful; enas means sin.”
isup [6]tato vayaṃ viśuddhāḥ santa iṣṭaṃ prāpsyāma ityabhiprāyaḥ /
“Then, being purified, we shall obtain what is desired—this is the intention.”
The final pāda is not “your salutation and speech we make” but a promise/prayer to perform verbal homage to Agni, since fuller service is not now possible.
→ te is dative “to you”; nama uktiṃ is a single “utterance of obeisance”; vidhema is optative, “may we render/serve with salutation.”
isup [7]kintu vayamidānīṃ te na śaknumaḥ paricaryāṃ kartum /
“But now we are not able to perform service to you.”
isup [8]bhūyiṣṭhāṃ bahutarāṃ te tubhyaṃ nama uktiṃ namaskāravacanaṃ vidhema namaskāreṇa paricarema ityarthaḥ /
“bhūyiṣṭhām means more/very abundant; te means to you; nama uktiṃ means an utterance of obeisance; vidhema means we would serve with salutation.”
