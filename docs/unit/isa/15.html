Īśopaniṣad with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 15

hiraṇmayena pātreṇa satyasyāpihitaṃ mukham / tat tvaṃ pūṣann apāvṛṇu satyadharmāya dṛṣṭaye

Translation. By a golden-bright, light-filled vessel is covered the face—the doorway—of Truth (satya). That, O Pūṣan, uncover; remove it for me, whose dharma (dharma) is truth, for the seeing, the realization, of your true Self.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The instrumental hiraṇmayena qualifies the covering as gold-like or radiant. Śaṅkara explicitly glosses hiraṇmaya as jyotirmaya, so the translation includes “light-filled.”
→ “golden-bright, light-filled,” not merely a physical golden object
isup [1]hiraṇmayamiva hiraṇmayaṃ jyotirmayamityetat /
“Hiraṇmaya means as though made of gold; that is, made of light.”
Pātra is not just an ordinary cup here but something functioning as a cover; mukha is glossed as dvāra, “doorway.” The printed commentary has brāhmaṇo, likely an unproofread form for brahmaṇo; I take the reference as Brahman located in the sun’s disk, in line with the syntax satyasya ... ādityamaṇḍalasthasya.
→ “the face—the doorway—of Truth/Brahman in the solar disk, covered by a vessel-like cover”
isup [2]tena pātreṇeva apidhānabhūtena satyasyaivādityamaṇḍalasthasya brāhmaṇo 'pihitam ācchāditaṃ mukhaṃ dvāram /
“By that, as by a vessel, being a covering, the face/doorway of Satya—Brahman [reading brāhmaṇo as likely brahmaṇo] located in the solar disk—is covered, concealed.”
The participle apihitam is a passive predication of mukham. Śaṅkara glosses it directly with ācchāditam, “covered/concealed.”
→ “is covered/concealed”
isup [2]apihitam ācchāditaṃ mukhaṃ dvāram
“The covered/concealed face, the doorway.”
The second hemistich asks Pūṣan to remove the covering from the previously mentioned doorway. The commentary line is unsegmented and likely defective at apāvṛṇvapasāraya; I read it contextually as apāvṛṇu apasāraya, because apasāraya is the explicit gloss.
→ tat refers back to the covered face/doorway; apāvṛṇu means “uncover/remove”
isup [3]tattvaṃ he pūṣannapāvṛṇvapasāraya
“That—do you, O Pūṣan—apāvṛṇu, remove.”
Satyadharmāya is dative singular masculine, not “for true dharma” as an abstract dative. Śaṅkara’s primary analysis is a bahuvrīhi: satyaṃ dharmaḥ yasya mama. He also offers an athavā: yathābhūtasya dharmasya anuṣṭhātre.
→ “for me, whose dharma is truth,” with Śaṅkara’s alternative “for the performer of dharma as it really is” recorded
isup [3]satyasya upāsanātsatyaṃ dharmo yasya mama so 'haṃ satyadharmā tasmai mahyamathavā yathābhūtasya dharmasyānuṣṭhātre
“Through worship of Satya, truth is the dharma of me; I am satyadharma—for that me; or else, for the performer of dharma as it really is.”
Dṛṣṭaye is not merely a request for physical sight. Śaṅkara glosses it as upalabdhi, apprehension/realization, and specifies the object as Pūṣan’s satyātman, true Self.
→ “for seeing/realization of your true Self”
isup [3]dṛṣṭaye tava satyātmana upalabdhaye
“For dṛṣṭi, that is, for the apprehension of your true Self.”
