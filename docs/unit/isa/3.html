Īśopaniṣad with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 3

asuryā nāma te lokā andhena tamasāvṛtāḥ | tāṃste pretyābhigacchanti ye ke cātmahano janāḥ

Translation. Asuric—nāma being only an expletive—are those worlds, the fruits of action, births, covered by blind darkness, by ignorance. To those, down even to immobile forms, after death, having left this body, go whatever ignorant people are killers of the Self (ātma-hanaḥ).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The first word is not rendered 'sunless' or 'spiritual/divine'; Śaṅkara derives the sense through asura, saying that, relative to the non-dual state of the supreme Self, even gods and others are asuras, and their worlds are asuryāḥ.
→ asuryāḥ = asuric/belonging to asuras, qualifying lokāḥ
isup [1]asuryāḥ paramātmabhāvamadvayamapekṣya devādayo 'pyasurāsteṣāñca svabhūtā lokā asuryā nāma /
‘Asuryāḥ’: in comparison with the non-dual state of the supreme Self, even gods and the rest are asuras; and the worlds belonging to them are called/as such asuryāḥ.
The particle nāma should not make the clause mean 'those worlds are named/called ...' in any strong way, because Śaṅkara expressly calls it an anarthaka nipāta, a semantically empty particle.
→ nāma is left as an expletive, at most lightly represented by 'indeed'
isup [2]nāmaśabdo 'narthako nipātaḥ /
The word nāma is a meaningless particle.
Rather than taking lokāḥ only as spatial worlds or heavens/hells, the commentary glosses them as karmaphalāni and then as janmāni, because they are seen and experienced.
→ lokāḥ are understood as karmic fruits, specifically births/existences
isup [3]te lokāḥ karmaphalāni /
Those worlds are the fruits of action.
isup [4]lokyante dṛśyante bhujyanta iti janmāni /
They are births, because they are ‘looked at,’ seen, and experienced. The printed bhujyanta appears unproofread, probably for bhujyante.
The darkness is not merely physical darkness. Śaṅkara glosses andha as adarśanātmaka and tamas as ajñāna, and glosses āvṛtāḥ with ācchāditāḥ.
→ andhena tamasā = by blind darkness, i.e. non-seeing ignorance; āvṛtāḥ = covered/concealed
isup [5]andhenādarśanātmakenājñānena tamasāvṛtā ācchāditāḥ
Covered by blind, non-perceptive ignorance as darkness—concealed.
Śaṅkara explicitly glosses pretya as leaving this body and takes the object tān as extending to births down to immobile ones; thus the movement is post-mortem attainment according to karma, not merely ordinary approaching in life.
→ pretya = after death, having left this body; abhigacchanti = they go to/attain those births
isup [5]tāṃsthāvarāntānpretya tyaktvemaṃ dehamabhigacchanti yathākarma yathāśrutam /
Having died—having abandoned this body—they go to those [births/worlds], down to immobile ones, according to karma and according to what has been heard.
The compound is explained as ‘those who kill the Self,’ but Śaṅkara immediately makes this metaphorical: the Self is existent and eternal; through ignorance its proper fruit—awareness of deathlessness and the like—is concealed as though killed. The people meant are avidvāṃsaḥ.
→ ātmahanaḥ janāḥ are the ignorant who obscure the Self, not literal suicides
isup [6]ātmānaṃ ghnantītyātmahanāḥ /
They are ‘self-killers’ because they kill the Self. The form ātmahanāḥ is printed in the commentary, but the checked mūla has ātmahano = pausal ātmahanaḥ.
isup [7]ke te janāḥ ye 'vidvāṃsaḥ /
Who are those people? Those who are ignorant.
isup [8, 9]kathaṃ ta ātmānaṃ nityaṃ hiṃsanti / avidyādoṣeṇa vidyamānasyātmanaḥ tiraskaraṇāt vidyamānasya ātmano yatkāryaṃ phalamajarāmaratvādisaṃvedanalakṣaṇaṃ taddhatasyeva tirobhūtaṃ bhavatīti prākṛtāvidvāṃso janāḥ janāḥ ātmahana ucyante /
How do they constantly injure the Self? Because, through the fault of ignorance, they conceal the existent Self; the fruit of the existent Self, marked by awareness of freedom from old age and death, becomes hidden as though killed. Thus ordinary ignorant people are called self-killers.
isup [10]tena hyātmahananadoṣeṇa saṃsaranti te
For by that fault of self-killing they transmigrate.
The pronouns are construed correlatively with te as the subject of abhigacchanti; the commentary’s question ‘Who are those people?’ and answer ‘the ignorant’ confirms that te is the people mentioned by the relative clause, not a second-person or possessive pronoun.
→ ye ke ca ... janāḥ is an indefinite relative subject: ‘whatever people / all who’
isup [7]ke te janāḥ ye 'vidvāṃsaḥ /
Who are those people? Those who are ignorant.
isup [10]saṃsaranti te
They transmigrate.
