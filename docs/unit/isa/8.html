Īśopaniṣad with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 8

sa paryagāc chukram akāyam avraṇam asnāviraṃ śuddham apāpaviddham | kavir manīṣī paribhūḥ svayambhūr yāthātathyator 'thān vyadadhāc chāśvatībhyaḥ samābhyaḥ

Translation. He—the Self just described—has gone all around, pervading like space: bright-pure, bodiless, woundless, without sinews, stainless, unpierced by pāpa; the all-seeing seer, ruler of mind, above all, self-existent— from reality-as-it-is he ordained, and suitably apportioned, the matters to be done, for the eternal Years, the Prajāpatis called Saṃvatsara.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The opening pronoun is not an unspecified ‘he’ who attains an object. Śaṅkara makes the subject the yathokta ātmā and takes paryagāt intransitively/pervasively, glossing it as ‘went all around’ and ‘pervading like space’.
→ sa = the previously taught Self; paryagāt = went all around, i.e. pervaded like space
isup [1]sa yathokta ātmā paryagāt pari samantādagādgatavānākāśavadvyāpī ityarthaḥ /
‘He, the Self as just stated, paryagāt: he went completely on all sides, went; the meaning is: pervading like space.’
The six -am descriptors are not taken as a neuter accusative object of paryagāt. Śaṅkara explicitly says the words beginning with śukram must be converted to masculine, because the passage begins with masculine sa and concludes with masculine kaviḥ, manīṣī, etc.
→ śukram through apāpaviddham are predicates of the Self, with masculine construal despite transmitted -am forms
isup [9, 10]śukramityādīni vacāṃsi pulliṅgatvena pariṇeyāni / sa paryagādityupakramya kavirmanīṣītyādinā pulliṅgatvenopasaṃhārāt /
‘The words beginning with śukram are to be converted to masculine gender; for, having begun with sa paryagāt, it concludes in the masculine with kaviḥ, manīṣī, and so on.’
Śaṅkara assigns specific force to each descriptor: śukra is pure/luminous; akāya denies the subtle body; avraṇa and asnāvira deny the gross body; śuddha denies causal-body impurity from avidyā; apāpaviddha denies pāpa such as dharma and adharma.
→ The descriptors deny all bodily/impure limitation of the Self
isup [2]śukraṃ śuddhaṃ jyotiṣmaddīptimānityarthaḥ /
‘śukra means pure, luminous, shining.’
isup [3]akāyamaśarīro liṅgaśarīravarjita ityarthaḥ /
‘akāya means bodiless, devoid of the subtle body.’
isup [4, 5, 6]avraṇam akṣatam / asnāviraṃ snāvāḥ śirā yasminna vidyanta ityasnāviram / avraṇamasnāviramityābhyāṃ sthūlaśarīrapratiṣedhaḥ /
‘avraṇa means uninjured. asnāvira means that in which sinews/vessels are not present. By avraṇa and asnāvira the gross body is denied.’
isup [7]śuddhaṃ nirmalamavidyāmalarahitamiti kāraṇaśarīrapratiṣedhaḥ /
‘śuddha means stainless, free from the impurity of avidyā; this denies the causal body.’
isup [8]apāpaviddhaṃ dharmādharmādipāpavarjitam /
‘apāpaviddha means free from pāpa such as dharma and adharma.’
The commentary rejects a merely literary or ordinary sense. kavi is glossed as krāntadarśī and sarvādṛk, while manīṣī is parsed as manasa īṣitā and equated with sarvajña īśvara.
→ kaviḥ = all-seeing seer; manīṣī = ruler of mind, omniscient Lord
isup [11]kaviḥ krāntadarśī sarvādṛk /
‘kavi means one whose seeing has gone beyond, the seer of all.’
isup [15]maniṣī manasa īṣitā sarvajña īśvara ityarthaḥ /
‘manīṣī means ruler of the mind; the meaning is the omniscient Lord.’
Śaṅkara glosses paribhūḥ by being above all, and svayambhūḥ first as self-existent, then expands it: that over which he is and that as which he is above is all himself.
→ paribhūḥ = above all; svayambhūḥ = self-existent, with all dependent reality being himself
isup [16]paribhūḥ sarveṣāṃ paryupari bhavatīti paribhūḥ /
‘paribhūḥ: he is above and over all; therefore paribhūḥ.’
isup [17, 18]svayambhūḥ svayameva bhavatīti / yeṣāmupari bhavati yaścopari bhavati sa sarvaḥ svayameva bhavatīti svayambhūḥ /
‘svayambhūḥ: he exists by himself. Those over whom he is, and that as which he is above, all that is himself alone; hence svayambhūḥ.’
The adverbial ablative yāthātathyataḥ is explained as arising from true being-as-it-is, grounded in omniscience. arthān are not arbitrary ‘objects’ but kartavya-padārthān, matters to be done, understood as means to karmic fruits. vyadadhāt is both ‘ordained’ and ‘apportioned suitably’.
→ yāthātathyataḥ ... arthān vyadadhāt = from/according to reality he ordained and suitably apportioned the matters to be done
isup [19]sa nityamukta īśvaro yāthātathyataḥ sarvajñatvādyathātathābhāvo yāthātathyaṃ tasmādyathābhūtakarmaphalasādhanator'thān karttavyapadārthān vyadadhādvihitavān yathānurūpaṃ vyabhajadityarthaḥ
‘He, the ever-liberated Lord: yāthātathyataḥ—because, from omniscience, there is being exactly as it is; that is yāthātathya, and from that. He vyadadhāt, ordained, the arthas, the matters to be done, as they truly are means to the fruits of action; the meaning is that he apportioned them suitably.’
Although the phrase can look like an adverbial ‘for ever’, Śaṅkara construes the -bhyaḥ forms as datives and identifies the samāḥ as Prajāpatis called Saṃvatsara. The translation therefore preserves ‘Years’ and the commentarial identification.
→ śāśvatībhyaḥ samābhyaḥ = to/for the eternal Years, the Prajāpatis called Saṃvatsara
isup [19]śāśvatībhyo nityābhyaḥ samābhyaḥ saṃvatsarākhyebhyaḥ prajāpatibhya ityarthaḥ
‘For the śāśvatīḥ, eternal, samāḥ, years: the meaning is, for the Prajāpatis called Saṃvatsara.’
