Īśopaniṣad with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 17

vāyur anilam amṛtam athedaṃ bhasmāntaṃ śarīram | oṃ krato smara kṛtaṃ smara krato smara kṛtaṃ smara

Translation. May my breath (vāyu), as I am dying, leaving its individual limitation, attain the immortal cosmic Air (anila); [may this subtle body (liṅga), shaped by knowledge and action, depart.] Then may this body, consigned to fire, end in ashes. Om—the symbol of Brahman called Agni. O Agni-as-Resolve (kratu), remember; remember what has been done. O Agni-as-Resolve, remember; remember what has been done.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The opening is not a bare juxtaposition ‘wind, air, immortal’; it is an elliptical wish at death. I translate it as the dying speaker’s breath attaining the immortal Anila after giving up its individual limitation.
→ vāyuḥ is the speaker’s prāṇa; anilam amṛtam is the immortal cosmic Air/sūtrātman; an optative sense ‘may attain’ is supplied.
isup [1]athedānīṃ mama mariṣyato vāyuḥ prāṇo 'dhyātmaparicchedaṃ hitvādhidaivatātmānaṃ sarvātmakamanilamamṛtaṃ sūtrātmānaṃ pratipadyatāmiti vākyaśeṣaḥ /
‘Now, of me as I am dying, vāyu—the breath—having abandoned its individual limitation, may attain the immortal Anila, the adhidaivata self, the all-self, the sūtrātman’: this is the completion of the sentence.
I include this in brackets in the translation because the commentator explicitly says it is to be understood, while distinguishing it from the gross body that becomes ash.
→ An implicit clause is understood: the subtle body (liṅga), shaped by knowledge and action, departs.
isup [2]liṅgaṃ cedaṃ jñānakarmasaṃskṛtamutkrāmatviti draṣṭavyam, mārgayācanasāmarthyāt /
And this subtle body, conditioned by knowledge and action, should be understood as departing, because of the force of the request for a path.
isup [3]athedaṃ śarīramagnau hutaṃ bhasmāntaṃ bhūyāt /
Then may this body, offered in the fire, come to have ashes as its end.
bhasmāntam is construed as a bahuvrīhi predicate adjective, not as an accusative object or a mere appositional noun.
→ idaṃ bhasmāntaṃ śarīram is a nominative optative sentence: ‘may this body become ash-ending’.
isup [3]athedaṃ śarīramagnau hutaṃ bhasmāntaṃ bhūyāt /
Then may this body, offered in the fire, come to have ashes as its end.
I leave Om as Om in the translation but gloss it as the symbol of Brahman called Agni, since the bhāṣya does not treat it as a mere exclamation.
→ oṃ invokes Brahman called Agni through the oṃ-symbol.
isup [4]om iti yathopāsanam oṃpratīkātmakatvātsatyātmakamagnyākhyaṃ brahmābhedenocyate /
‘Om’—according to the meditation, because it has oṃ as its symbol, truth-natured Brahman called Agni is spoken of as non-different [from it].
The translation ‘O Agni-as-Resolve (kratu)’ follows Śaṅkara’s saṅkalpātmaka gloss and his explicit agne in explaining kṛtaṃ smara.
→ krato is a vocative to Agni as resolve/will, not ‘sacrifice’.
isup [5]he krato! saṅkalpātmaka! smara yanmama smartavyaṃ tasya kālo 'yaṃ pratyupasthito 'taḥ smara /
O kratu! O one whose nature is resolve! Remember what is to be remembered for me; the time for it has now arrived, therefore remember.
isup [6]etāvantaṃ kālaṃ bhāvitaṃ kṛtamagne smara yanmayā bālyaprabhṛtyanuṣṭhitaṃ karma tacca smara /
Remember, O Agni, what has been done and cultivated for all this time—the action performed by me from childhood onward; remember that too.
I translate kṛtaṃ smara as ‘remember what has been done’, with kṛtam as a substantive neuter accusative object of smara.
→ kṛtam means the action/deed performed by the speaker, especially from childhood onward.
isup [6]etāvantaṃ kālaṃ bhāvitaṃ kṛtamagne smara yanmayā bālyaprabhṛtyanuṣṭhitaṃ karma tacca smara /
Remember, O Agni, what has been done and cultivated for all this time—the action performed by me from childhood onward; remember that too.
I do not collapse the repeated words into a single command, because the bhāṣya states that the repetition is for ādara.
→ The repetition krato smara kṛtaṃ smara is retained as reverent insistence.
isup [7]krato smara kṛtaṃ smareti punarvacanamādarārtham
The repetition ‘O kratu, remember; remember what has been done’ is for the sake of reverence/earnest regard.
