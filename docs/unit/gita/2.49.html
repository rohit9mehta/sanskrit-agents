Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.49

dūreṇa hy avaraṃ karma buddhi-yogād dhanaṃjaya | buddhau śaraṇam anviccha kṛpaṇāḥ phala-hetavaḥ

Translation. Indeed, action done by one who seeks its fruit is far inferior to action joined with the insight of equanimity (buddhi-yoga), O Dhanaṃjaya. Seek refuge in insight (buddhi); wretched are those whose motive is fruit.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The verse is not merely saying that ‘action’ in general is low. Śaṅkara glosses dūreṇa as an extreme separation, avaram as low/inferior, and specifies karma as action performed by one who seeks fruit.
→ dūreṇa hy avaraṃ karma = ‘action done for fruit is indeed far inferior’
gita [1]dūreṇa ativiprakarṣeṇa atyantam eva hy avaram adhamaṃ nikṛṣṭaṃ karma phalārthinā kriyamāṇaṃ
‘dūreṇa’ means ‘by an extreme remoteness, utterly’; ‘avaram’ means ‘lowest, inferior’; the action is one performed by a person seeking its fruit.
The ablative is comparative with avaram. Śaṅkara does not take buddhi-yoga here as merely abstract intellectual concentration; he glosses it as karma endowed with samatva-buddhi, the insight of equanimity.
→ buddhi-yogāt = ‘than action joined with the insight of equanimity’
gita [1]buddhi-yogāt samatva-buddhi-yuktāt karmaṇaḥ
‘from buddhi-yoga’ means ‘from action endowed with the buddhi of equanimity.’
The locative buddhau is the locus in which refuge is to be sought; śaraṇam is not a physical refuge but āśraya, the cause of attaining fearlessness. Śaṅkara further identifies the relevant buddhi as either yoga-oriented buddhi or the Sāṃkhya-buddhi born from its maturation, culminating in refuge in ultimate knowledge.
→ buddhau śaraṇam anviccha = ‘seek refuge in insight’
gita [2]yoga-viṣayāyāṃ buddhau tat-paripāka-jāyāṃ vā sāṃkhya-buddhau śaraṇam āśrayam abhaya-prāpti-kāraṇam anviccha prārthayasva, paramārtha-jñāna-śaraṇo bhavety arthaḥ
In the buddhi whose object is yoga, or in the Sāṃkhya-buddhi born from its maturation, seek refuge—an āśraya, the cause for obtaining fearlessness; the meaning is: become one whose refuge is knowledge of ultimate reality.
The compound phala-hetavaḥ is taken substantively of people impelled by desire for fruit, not as ‘causes that produce fruits.’ Śaṅkara glosses kṛpaṇāḥ as dīnāḥ and phala-hetavaḥ as those prompted by thirst for results.
→ kṛpaṇāḥ phala-hetavaḥ = ‘wretched are those whose motive is fruit’
gita [3]kṛpaṇāḥ dīnāḥ phala-hetavaḥ phala-tṛṣṇā-prayuktāḥ santaḥ
‘kṛpaṇāḥ’ means ‘wretched’; ‘phala-hetavaḥ’ means ‘being impelled by thirst for fruit.’
gita [3]yo vā etad akṣaraṃ gārgy aviditvāsmāl lokāt praiti sa kṛpaṇaḥ [bau 3.8.10] iti śruteḥ
Śruti says: ‘Whoever, O Gārgī, departs from this world without knowing this imperishable is kṛpaṇa.’
The vocative force is confirmed explicitly by Śaṅkara’s he dhanaṃjaya.
→ dhanaṃjaya is vocative address to Arjuna
gita [2]he dhanaṃjaya
O Dhanaṃjaya.
