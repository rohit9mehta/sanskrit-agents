Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.28

avyaktādīni bhūtāni vyakta-madhyāni bhārata | avyakta-nidhanāny eva tatra kā paridevanā

Translation. Beings (bhūtāni) are unmanifest (avyakta) before their arising, manifest in the middle, O Bhārata; at their ending (nidhana) they are unmanifest alone— what lamentation (paridevanā) is there in regard to them?

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
I translate avyakta as ‘unmanifest’ in the sense ‘unseen, not apprehended.’ This is why the first and last states can be rendered as before arising and after death, rather than as an independent metaphysical substance.
→ avyakta means non-appearance/non-apprehension, not primarily a technical Sāṃkhya Prakṛti here.
gita [1]avyaktādīny avyaktam adarśanam anupalabdhiḥ ādir yeṣāṃ bhūtānāṃ ... tāni avyaktadīni bhūtāni prāg-utpatteḥ
‘Avyaktādīni: avyakta is non-seeing, non-apprehension; beings whose beginning is that ... those avyaktādīni beings are so before production.’
gita [2, 3]avyakta-nidhanāny eva punar avyaktam adarśanaṃ nidhanaṃ maraṇaṃ yeṣāṃ tāni avyakta-nidhanāni | maraṇād ūrdhvam apy avyaktatām eva pratipadyante ity arthaḥ
‘Avyakta-nidhanāni: again, avyakta is non-seeing; nidhana is death; those whose ending is that are avyakta-nidhanāni. The meaning is: after death also they attain only unmanifestness.’
The three compounds describe the states of the beings across time: before production they have an unmanifest beginning; after production and before death they have a manifest middle; with death/after death they have an unmanifest end. The commentary’s printed avyaktadīni in line 1 is taken as an unproofread typo for avyaktādīni, since the same line begins with the correct form and gives the proper vigraha. The verifier’s rejection of vyakta-madhyāni and avyakta-nidhanāni was a tool_error/unsupported-compound report, not a successful contrary derivation; the regular neuter nominative plural forms and the commentary-based bahuvrīhi relations are therefore retained.
→ avyaktādīni, vyakta-madhyāni, and avyakta-nidhanāni are bahuvrīhi predicates of bhūtāni with a temporal force: beginning, middle, and end.
gita [1]ādir yeṣāṃ bhūtānāṃ ... tāni avyaktadīni bhūtāni prāg-utpatteḥ, utpannāni ca prāṅ-maraṇāt vyakta-madhyāni
‘Beings whose beginning is [unmanifest] ... those beings are avyakta-beginning before production; and, once produced, before death they are manifest-middle.’
gita [2]nidhanaṃ maraṇaṃ yeṣāṃ tāni avyakta-nidhanāni
‘Those whose nidhana—death—is [unmanifest] are avyakta-nidhanāni.’
I keep the simple English ‘beings,’ but the apparatus construes them in Śaṅkara’s sense as son/friend/etc. aggregates of effect and instrument—precisely the kind of things over which grief arises.
→ bhūtāni refers to beings/entities as perceptible aggregates such as sons and friends, not to the immutable Self as such.
gita [1]bhūtānāṃ putra-mitrādi-kārya-karaṇa-saṃghātātmakānāṃ
‘of beings, consisting in aggregates of effect and instrument, such as son, friend, and so on.’
I render avyakta-nidhanāny eva as ‘at their ending they are unmanifest alone,’ because the bhāṣya repeats the restriction with avyaktatām eva.
→ eva restricts the final condition: after death they attain only unmanifestness.
gita [3]maraṇād ūrdhvam apy avyaktatām eva pratipadyante ity arthaḥ
‘The meaning is: after death too they attain only unmanifestness.’
tatra is construed as ‘with respect to such beings,’ and paridevanā as lamenting speech/wailing. The question implies that grief is pointless for things that pass from unseen to seen and vanish again.
→ tatra kā paridevanā is a rhetorical denial: ‘what lamentation is there in regard to them?’
gita [8]tatra kā paridevanā ko vā pralāpo 'dṛṣṭa-dṛṣṭa-pranaṣṭa-bhrānti-bhūteṣu bhūteṣv ity arthaḥ
‘There, what lamentation—what prattle—is there with regard to beings whose nature is delusion: unseen, seen, and vanished? This is the meaning.’
gita [5, 6]adarśanād āpatitaḥ punaś cādarśanaṃ gataḥ | nāsau tava na tasya tvaṃ vṛthā kā paridevanā ||
‘He has come from non-seeing and gone again to non-seeing. He is not yours, nor are you his: vainly, what lamentation?’
