How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The instrumental yadṛcchayā is taken adverbially to mark that the battle has come without being sought.
→ yadṛcchayā = “unsolicitedly / of itself,” not primarily “by one’s own will.”
gita [1]yadṛcchayā ca aprārthitatayā
“yadṛcchayā—i.e. by not having been asked for / unsolicitedly.”
The participle is not translated here as “fitting” or “reasonable,” but as describing the battle as having presented itself.
→ upapannam = “come, arrived.”
gita [1]upapannam āgataṃ
“upapannam—come/arrived.”
svargadvāram is a tatpuruṣa, ‘gate of/to heaven,’ and apāvṛtam means ‘opened.’ The commentary explicitly glosses apāvṛtam by udghāṭitam; the precise compound relation is grammatical rather than explicitly explained by Śaṅkara.
→ svargadvāram apāvṛtam = “an opened gate to heaven.”
gita [1]svarga-dvāram apāvṛtam udghāṭitaṃ
“the gate to heaven, opened—unbarred/opened up.”
Śaṅkara recasts the syntax as a relative sentence: those kṣatriyas who obtain such a battle—are they not sukhinaḥ? This prevents reading sukhinaḥ merely as an attributive adjective, ‘happy kṣatriyas obtain…’.
→ sukhinaḥ is predicative: “fortunate/happy are the kṣatriyas who obtain…”
gita [1]ya etad īdṛśaṃ yuddhaṃ labhante kṣatriyāḥ he pārtha, kiṃ na sukhinas te ?
“Those kṣatriyas who obtain this such battle, O Pārtha—are they not happy/fortunate?”
The commentary’s order etad īdṛśaṃ yuddhaṃ labhante makes yuddham the object and confirms īdṛśam as its qualifier; upapannam, svarga-dvāram, and apāvṛtam are construed with the same accusative object phrase.
→ yuddham īdṛśam is the direct object of labhante; the preceding accusative neuters describe that battle.
gita [1]ya etad īdṛśaṃ yuddhaṃ labhante kṣatriyāḥ
“the kṣatriyas who obtain this such battle.”
