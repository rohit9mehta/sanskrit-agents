Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.58

yadā saṃharate cāyaṃ kūrmo 'ṅgānīva sarvaśaḥ | indriyāṇīndriyārthebhyas tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā

Translation. And when this one—the ascetic intent on knowledge—withdraws, like a tortoise its limbs, on every side, the senses (indriyāṇi) from the sense-objects (indriyārtha), his wisdom (prajñā) stands established.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Translate ayam as 'this one—the ascetic intent on knowledge' and take kūrmaḥ as the comparison: 'as a tortoise ...'.
→ ayam refers to the disciplined seeker, not to the tortoise; kūrmaḥ belongs to the simile.
gita [1]cāyaṃ jñāna-niṣṭhāyāṃ pravṛtto yatiḥ
‘and this’ means the ascetic who has set out in steadfastness in knowledge.
gita [1]evaṃ jñāna-niṣṭhaḥ indriyāṇi indriyārthebhyaḥ sarva-viṣayebhya upasaṃharate
So the one steadfast in knowledge withdraws the senses from the sense-objects, from all objects.
Render saṃharate as 'withdraws,' not as 'destroys' or merely 'collects.'
→ saṃharate means 'withdraws / draws in completely.'
gita [1]yadā saṃharate samyag upasaṃharate
When he saṃharate: he properly withdraws.
gita [1]indriyāṇi indriyārthebhyaḥ sarva-viṣayebhya upasaṃharate
He withdraws the senses from the sense-objects, from all objects.
Translate kūrmo ’ṅgānīva as 'as a tortoise [withdraws] its limbs.'
→ The tortoise withdraws its own limbs; aṅgāni is the object within the simile.
gita [1]kūrmo 'ṅgāni iva yathā kūrmo bhayāt svāny aṅgāni upasaṃharati
‘Like a tortoise its limbs’: just as a tortoise, from fear, withdraws its own limbs.
Render sarvaśaḥ as 'on every side' and let the corresponding withdrawal of the senses extend to all sense-objects.
→ sarvaśaḥ means 'on every side / completely' and supports total withdrawal from the range of objects.
gita [1]sarvaśaḥ sarvataḥ
sarvaśaḥ means ‘from every side / in every respect.’
gita [1]indriyārthebhyaḥ sarva-viṣayebhya
from the sense-objects, from all objects.
Take indriyārthebhyaḥ with upasaṃharate/saṃharate as the source from which the senses are withdrawn, and analyze indriyārtha as indriyāṇām arthāḥ.
→ indriyārthebhyaḥ is ablative plural: 'from the sense-objects'; indriyārtha is 'object of the senses.'
gita [1]indriyāṇi indriyārthebhyaḥ sarva-viṣayebhya upasaṃharate
He withdraws the senses from the sense-objects, from all objects.
Construe tasya with the yati/jñāna-niṣṭha of the first half and take prajñā pratiṣṭhitā as a nominative subject plus predicate adjective.
→ The concluding clause is the refrain: 'his wisdom (prajñā) is established.'
gita [2]tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā ity uktārthaṃ vākyam
The sentence ‘his wisdom is established’ has the meaning already stated.
Translate pratiṣṭhitā as 'established / standing firm,' not as the abstract noun pratiṣṭhā, 'stability.'
→ pratiṣṭhitā is a feminine nominative singular participial adjective agreeing with prajñā.
