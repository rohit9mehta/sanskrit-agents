Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.70

āpūryamāṇam acala-pratiṣṭhaṃ samudram āpaḥ praviśanti yadvat | tadvat kāmā yaṃ praviśanti sarve sa śāntim āpnoti na kāma-kāmī

Translation. As waters enter the ocean, which is being filled yet fixed in unmoving stability, so all desires enter that man, all of them— dissolving in himself without unsettling him. He attains peace, liberation (śānti); not the craver of objects of desire (kāma-kāmī).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
yadvat/tadvat is rendered as the correlative “as/so,” with samudram as the object of the first praviśanti and yam puruṣam as the object of the second.
→ The first half is an ocean simile: waters enter an ocean that is being filled yet remains unmoved; the second half applies this to desires entering a person.
gita [1]samudram āpaḥ sarvato gatāḥ praviśanti svātmastham avikriyam eva santaṃ yadvat, tadvat kāmāḥ ... yaṃ puruṣam --- samudram iva āpaḥ -- ... praviśanti
“As waters, come from all sides, enter the ocean, which remains established in itself and unchanged, so desires enter that person—like waters the ocean.”
I render it “fixed in unmoving stability,” qualifying samudram.
→ acala-pratiṣṭham is a bahuvrīhi: “whose stability/abiding is unmoving,” not a separate adjective plus noun and not “founded on a mountain.”
gita [1]acala-pratiṣṭham acalatayā pratiṣṭhā avasthitiḥ yasya tam acala-pratiṣṭhaṃ
“acala-pratiṣṭha: that whose pratiṣṭhā, abiding/stability, is by immovability.”
gita [1]svātmastham avikriyam eva santaṃ
“remaining in its own self, unchanged.”
I do not translate the clause as indulgence or satisfaction of desires; I render it as desires entering/dissolving without unsettling him.
→ The kāmāḥ that enter the person are particular desires arising even amid sense-objects; their “entering” means dissolving in the self without disturbing or enslaving him.
gita [1]kāmāḥ viṣaya-saṃnidhāv api sarvataḥ icchā-viśeṣāḥ yaṃ puruṣam ... avikurvantaḥ praviśanti sarve ātmany eva pralīyante na svātma-vaśaṃ kurvanti
“The desires—particular wishes from all sides, even in the presence of sense-objects—enter that person without altering him; all dissolve in the self and do not bring him under their control.”
I translate śāntim āpnoti as “he attains peace—liberation.” The bhāṣya plaintext reads śāntir mokṣam, apparently an unproofread form for śāntiṃ/mokṣam; I cite it only for the explicit śānti = mokṣa gloss.
→ śāntim means not merely psychological calm but liberation (mokṣa) in Śaṅkara’s gloss.
gita [1]sa śāntir mokṣam āpnoti
“He attains peace, i.e. liberation.”
I render na kāma-kāmī as “not the craver of objects of desire,” not as “not one who desires desires.”
→ kāma-kāmī is the person who habitually desires objects of desire; he does not attain peace/liberation.
gita [1]netaraḥ kāma-kāmī, kāmyanta iti kāmāḥ viṣayāḥ tān kāmayituṃ śīlaṃ yasya saḥ kāma-kāmī, naiva prāpnotīty arthaḥ
“Not the other, the kāma-kāmī: kāmāḥ are objects, because they are desired; one whose habit is to desire them is a kāma-kāmī. The meaning is: he certainly does not attain it.”
