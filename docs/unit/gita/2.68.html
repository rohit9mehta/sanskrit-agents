Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.68

tasmād yasya mahābāho nigṛhītāni sarvaśaḥ | indriyāṇīndriyārthebhyas tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā

Translation. Therefore, O mighty-armed one, the wisdom (prajñā) of that ascetic whose senses are restrained in every way from the objects of the senses is firmly established.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
tasmāt is an inferential connective grounded in the fault already shown in the activity of the senses.
→ “Therefore,” not merely “from that.”
gita [1]indriyāṇāṃ pravṛttau doṣa upapādito yasmāt tasmāt
Because the defect in the activity of the senses has been demonstrated, therefore...
The genitive relative-correlative construction is to be understood as “of the ascetic whose ... of that one.”
→ yasya/tasya refer to a yati, “ascetic/disciplined person.”
gita [1]yasya yateḥ he mahābāho, nigṛhītāni ... tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā
of whose—the ascetic’s—O mighty-armed one, [the senses] are restrained ... that one’s wisdom is established.
indriyārthebhyaḥ is ablative plural with nigṛhītāni; it means sense-objects, not “purposes” in the abstract.
→ “restrained from the objects of the senses,” i.e. from sound and the rest.
gita [1]nigṛhītāni ... indriyāṇi indriyārthebhyaḥ śabdādibhyas
the senses restrained from the sense-objects, from sound and the rest.
sarvaśaḥ modifies the restraint by specifying all modes or manners, not simply “all the senses.”
→ “in every way,” with modes including the mental.
gita [1]sarvaśaḥ sarva-prakāraiḥ mānasādi-bhedaiḥ
sarvaśaḥ: by all modes, with distinctions beginning with the mental.
The word is not syntactically part of the yasya phrase; it directly addresses Arjuna.
→ mahābāho is a vocative address, “O mighty-armed one.”
gita [1]he mahābāho
O mighty-armed one.
The verse’s formula tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā is read in the sthita-prajña context, where Śaṅkara characterizes the person as one in whom discriminative knowledge has arisen.
→ prajñā is “wisdom/discriminative knowledge,” and pratiṣṭhitā means “established, firmly settled.”
gita [1]tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā
that one’s wisdom is established.
gita [2]utpanna-viveka-jñānasya sthita-prajñasya
of the person of established wisdom, whose discriminative knowledge has arisen.
