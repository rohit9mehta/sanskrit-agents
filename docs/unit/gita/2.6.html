Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.6

na caitad vidmaḥ kataran no garīyo yad vā jayema yadi vā no jayeyuḥ | yān eva hatvā na jijīviṣāmas te 'vasthitāḥ pramukhe dhārtarāṣṭrāḥ

Translation. Nor do we know this: which of the two is the weightier course for us— whether we should conquer [them], or whether they should conquer us. Those very sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra (dhārtarāṣṭrāḥ), after slaying whom we would not wish to live, stand arrayed at the front.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
katarat and garīyaḥ are neuter singular forms forming the embedded question governed by vidmaḥ; the first naḥ/no is dative plural ‘for us’, not the negative particle no and not a genitive ‘of us’ in translation.
→ katarat no garīyaḥ = ‘which of the two is the weightier/better course for us’
The paired vā-particles and the parallel optatives jayema/jayeyuḥ make the first yad function conjunctively with yadi, not as an ordinary relative pronoun needing its own nominal antecedent.
→ yad vā ... yadi vā introduces alternatives: ‘whether ... or whether’
The verb ji ‘conquer’ can take an object; here naḥ supplies that object for jayeyuḥ. Taking no as indeclinable ‘not’ would leave the clause semantically and syntactically distorted after the already present yadi vā.
→ no before jayeyuḥ = naḥ, accusative plural ‘us’
Both forms express the uncertain alternatives being weighed: ‘we should conquer’ and ‘they should conquer’. This optative force is required by the indirect deliberative question, not a simple present ‘we conquer / they conquer’.
→ jayema and jayeyuḥ are optatives of √ji
yān is masculine accusative plural governed by hatvā, while te dhārtarāṣṭrāḥ is masculine nominative plural subject of avasthitāḥ. The construction identifies the very men whom Arjuna would not wish to live after slaying as the Dhārtarāṣṭras standing before him.
→ yān eva ... te is a relative-correlative construction
The reduplicated stem jijīviṣa- is desiderative from √jīv. Thus na jijīviṣāmaḥ is not merely ‘we do not live’ but ‘we do not desire to live’.
→ jijīviṣāmaḥ means ‘we wish to live’
The nominative plural masculine agrees with te and avasthitāḥ; in this epic context it is best taken substantively as Dhṛtarāṣṭra’s sons, not merely as the adjective ‘belonging to Dhṛtarāṣṭra’.
→ dhārtarāṣṭrāḥ as substantive patronymic, ‘sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra’
