Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.7

kārpaṇya-doṣopahata-svabhāvaḥ pṛcchāmi tvāṃ dharma-saṃmūḍha-cetāḥ | yac chreyaḥ syān niścitaṃ brūhi tan me śiṣyas te 'haṃ śādhi māṃ tvāṃ prapannam

Translation. With my own disposition stricken by the fault of weakness (kārpaṇya), with my mind bewildered about duty (dharma), I ask you: what would be the higher good (śreyas)—tell that to me decisively. I am your disciple; instruct me, who have taken refuge in you.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The compound is masculine nominative singular and suits the first-person subject of pṛcchāmi/aham, not an independent subject ‘nature’. The internal relation is instrumental/causal: the svabhāva is upahata by the kārpaṇya-doṣa. MW supports svabhāva as ‘nature, innate disposition’ and upa-√han as ‘hurt, damage, afflict, impede, spoil’.
→ kārpaṇya-doṣopahata-svabhāvaḥ is a bahuvrīhi: ‘one whose own disposition has been impaired by the fault of kārpaṇya’.

Among the MW senses—poverty, pitiful circumstances, weakness/poorness of spirit, parsimony—the psychological sense best fits Arjuna’s confession of a damaged svabhāva and bewildered cetas.
→ kārpaṇya is rendered ‘weakness’ or ‘poor-spiritedness’, not literal poverty or miserliness.

The compound is masculine nominative singular and qualifies the speaker. The natural vigraha is dharme saṃmūḍhaṃ ceto yasya: cetas is the inner head, saṃmūḍha describes the mind’s bewilderment, and dharma marks the matter about which the mind is confused. MW supports dharma as duty/right/prescribed conduct and sam-√muh as being stupefied or bewildered.
→ dharma-saṃmūḍha-cetāḥ is a bahuvrīhi: ‘one whose mind is bewildered concerning dharma’.

yat is nominative neuter singular in the relative clause with syāt, śreyaḥ is the neuter predicate/substantive ‘the better thing, higher good’, and tat is the neuter accusative object of brūhi. me is the dative recipient, ‘to me’.
→ yac chreyaḥ syāt … tan me brūhi is relative-correlative: ‘what would be the higher good, tell that to me’.

Although niścitam could formally be read as a neuter accusative adjective, MW explicitly gives niścitam as an indeclinable meaning ‘decidedly, positively’. Its placement before brūhi naturally gives the force ‘tell for certain’.
→ niścitam is adverbial with brūhi: ‘tell decisively/for certain’.

te is genitive singular with śiṣyaḥ. prapannam is masculine accusative singular agreeing with mām, while tvām is the object governed by pra-√pad, ‘resort to, take refuge with’ (MW s.v. pra-√pad). śādhi, from √śās, is therefore best translated ‘instruct’ here rather than merely ‘rule’.
→ śiṣyas te ’ham means ‘I am your disciple/pupil’, and śādhi māṃ tvāṃ prapannam means ‘instruct me, who have taken refuge in you’.
