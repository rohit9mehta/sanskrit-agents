Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.54

arjuna uvāca sthita-prajñasya kā bhāṣā samādhi-sthasya keśava | sthita-dhīḥ kiṃ prabhāṣeta kim āsīta vrajeta kim

Translation. Arjuna said: O Keśava, what is the defining description (bhāṣā) of one whose wisdom is established (sthita-prajña), of one abiding in samādhi (samādhi-stha)? What would the steady-minded one (sthita-dhī) himself say? How would he sit? How would he walk?

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
sthita-prajña is not translated as an abstract “steady wisdom,” but as a person whose prajñā is firmly established, with Śaṅkara specifying that prajñā as the cognition “I am the supreme Brahman.”
→ bahuvrīhi: “one whose wisdom is established.”
gita [1]sthitā pratiṣṭhitā aham asmi paraṃ brahma iti prajñā yasya saḥ sthita-prajñas tasya sthita-prajñasya
“He whose wisdom—‘I am the supreme Brahman’—is steady, established: he is a sthita-prajña; of that sthita-prajña …”
bhāṣā is taken as the verbal description or defining characteristic by which such a person is spoken of, not merely as “language.”
→ “defining description/expression” for bhāṣā.
gita [1]kā bhāṣā kiṃ bhāṣaṇaṃ vacanaṃ katham asau parair bhāṣyate
“What is his bhāṣā? What speaking, what utterance? How is he spoken of by others?”
gita [4]sthita-prajñasya lakṣaṇam
“the characteristic of the sthita-prajña.”
samādhi-sthasya is construed as samādhau sthitasya, with samādhi as the locus in which the person is established.
→ locative tatpuruṣa: “one established in samādhi.”
gita [1]samādhi-sthasya samādhau sthitasya he keśava
“O Keśava, of one established in samādhi—standing in samādhi.”
The second half continues to ask about the same realized person; sthita-dhīḥ is not a separate abstract “fixed thought.”
→ sthita-dhīḥ is the same person as the sthita-prajñaḥ, a masculine nominative bahuvrīhi.
gita [2]sthita-dhīḥ sthita-prajñaḥ svayaṃ vā kiṃ prabhāṣeta
“sthita-dhīḥ means sthita-prajñaḥ; or, what would he himself say?”
The prabhāṣeta question is taken as concerning the sthita-prajña’s own utterance, contrasted with how he is spoken of by others in the bhāṣā question.
→ “What would he himself say?”
gita [1]katham asau parair bhāṣyate
“how is he spoken of by others?”
gita [2]svayaṃ vā kiṃ prabhāṣeta
“or what would he himself say?”
For āsīta and vrajeta, kim is not taken as an object but as asking the manner or character of the person’s sitting and walking.
→ “How would he sit? How would he walk?”
gita [3]kim āsīta vrajeta kim āsanaṃ vrajanaṃ vā tasya katham ity arthaḥ
“‘How would he sit? How would he walk?’ The meaning is: what is his sitting or walking like?”
prabhāṣeta, āsīta, and vrajeta are parsed as third singular VidhiLiṅ forms and rendered with English “would,” rather than as plain present indicatives.
→ optative/potential: “would.”
