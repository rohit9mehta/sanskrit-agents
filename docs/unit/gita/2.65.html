Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.65

prasāde sarva-duḥkhānāṃ hānir asyopajāyate | prasanna-cetaso hy āśu buddhiḥ paryavatiṣṭhate

Translation. When serenity (prasāda) is present, the destruction of all sorrows arises for this yati. For, in the serene-minded one, the intellect (buddhi) quickly becomes firmly established, immovable as Self-nature itself.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The locative is taken as the circumstance in which the result arises; Śaṅkara frames the verse as praise of prasannatā, serenity, and glosses the related prasanna-cetasaḥ by a well-composed inner organ.
→ prasāde = in/when there is mental serenity (prasāda), not divine favor or mere stylistic clarity.
gita [4]seyaṃ prasannatā stūyate --
This serenity is being praised.
gita [2]prasanna-cetasaḥ svasthāntaḥ-karaṇasya
Of one whose mind is serene, whose inner instrument is well-composed.
The compound is read as “all sorrows,” not “sorrows of all [people/things].” Śaṅkara glosses the genitive plural with ādhyātmikādīnām.
→ sarva-duḥkhānām = of all sorrows, specifically including the ādhyātmika and other classes.
gita [1]sarva-duḥkhānām ādhyātmikādīnāṃ
Of all sorrows, beginning with the internal/psychophysical ones.
Although hāni can mean decrease, Śaṅkara explicitly glosses it by vināśa, destruction.
→ hāniḥ = destruction/cessation, not mere loss or diminution.
gita [1]hānir vināśo
hāniḥ means destruction.
The pronoun is not left as a vague “of this”; Śaṅkara supplies yateḥ as its referent.
→ asya = of this yati, this disciplined ascetic/practitioner.
gita [1]vināśo 'sya yater upajāyate
Destruction arises for this yati.
The form could be read superficially as “of a serene mind,” but Śaṅkara treats it as qualifying the person: svasthāntaḥ-karaṇasya, and then speaks of the prasanna-cetas as having an established buddhi.
→ prasanna-cetasaḥ = of the serene-minded person, one whose inner organ is composed.
gita [2]prasanna-cetasaḥ svasthāntaḥ-karaṇasya
Of one whose mind is serene, whose inner instrument is well-composed.
gita [3]evaṃ prasanna-cetaso 'vasthita-buddhiḥ
Thus, for the serene-minded one, the intellect is established.
Śaṅkara glosses hi by yasmāt and āśu by śīghram, so the second hemistich explains the first rather than merely adding emphasis.
→ hi is causal, “for/because,” and āśu is adverbial, “quickly.”
gita [2]hi yasmāt āśu śīghraṃ
For/because; quickly, swiftly.
Śaṅkara expands the verb as complete, all-around abiding and then states the intended sense as niścalībhavati, becoming motionless, with ātma-svarūpa. The translation therefore uses “becomes firmly established,” not merely “stands” or “pervades.”
→ buddhiḥ paryavatiṣṭhate = the intellect quickly becomes firmly established, immovable as Self-nature itself.
gita [2]buddhiḥ paryavatiṣṭhate ākāśam iva pari samantāt avatiṣṭhate, ātma-svarūpeṇaiva niścalībhavatīty arthaḥ
The intellect becomes established; it abides all around, as it were like space; the meaning is that it becomes motionless as Self-nature itself.
