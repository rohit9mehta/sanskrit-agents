Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.52

yadā te moha-kalilaṃ buddhir vyatitariṣyati | tadā gantāsi nirvedaṃ śrotavyasya śrutasya ca

Translation. When your intellect (buddhi) shall pass beyond this delusion-impurity (moha-kalila), then you will attain dispassion (nirveda) toward what is to be heard and what has been heard.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The enclitic te is construed as genitive singular dependent on buddhiḥ: ‘your intellect.’
→ te = tava, ‘your/of you’
gita [1]te tava
‘te’ means ‘your.’
The compound is taken as a karmadhāraya-like ‘delusion-impurity,’ not merely a physical ‘thicket’ or an unspecified difficulty. It is the accusative object crossed over by the intellect.
→ moha-kalilaṃ = an impurity/turbidity consisting in delusion and non-discrimination
gita [1]moha-kalilaṃ mohātmakam aviveka-rūpaṃ kāluṣyaṃ
‘moha-kalila’ is an impurity/turbidity whose nature is delusion, whose form is non-discrimination.
gita [1]yena ātmānātma-viveka-bodhaṃ kaluṣīkṛtya viṣayaṃ pratyantaḥ-karaṇaṃ pravartate
By it, after the cognition discriminating self and not-self has been defiled, the inner organ turns toward objects.
The verb is not a mere motion verb but means that the intellect will pass beyond the delusive impurity, reaching a highly purified condition.
→ vyatitariṣyati = ‘will pass beyond/transcend’
gita [1]tat tava buddhiḥ vyatitariṣyati vyatikramiṣyati, atiśuddha-bhāvam āpātryate ity arthaḥ
That, your intellect, will pass beyond; that is, it will overstep it; the sense is that it comes to a state of great purity.
nirveda is translated as dispassion/detachment, not as depression, despair, or simple disgust.
→ nirvedaṃ = vairāgya, ‘dispassion’
gita [2]gantāsi prāpsyasi nirvedaṃ vairāgyaṃ
‘You will go/come to’ means ‘you will attain’; ‘nirveda’ means ‘dispassion.’
The two genitives depend on nirveda/vairāgya and are neuter substantives. Śaṅkara further explains that both the not-yet-heard and the already-heard appear fruitless to the seeker at that point.
→ śrotavyasya śrutasya ca = ‘toward what is to be heard and what has been heard’
gita [2]nirvedaṃ vairāgyaṃ śrotavyasya śrutasya ca
Dispassion with respect to what is to be heard and what has been heard.
gita [2]tadā śrotavyaṃ śrutaṃ ca te niṣphalaṃ pratibhātīty abhiprāyaḥ
The purport is: then what is to be heard and what has been heard appear fruitless to you.

Śaṅkara's commentary on this unit

1yadā yasmin kāle te tava moha-kalilaṃ mohātmakam aviveka-rūpaṃ kāluṣyaṃ yena ātmānātma-viveka-bodhaṃ kaluṣīkṛtya viṣayaṃ pratyantaḥ-karaṇaṃ pravartate, tat tava buddhiḥ vyatitariṣyati vyatikramiṣyati, atiśuddha-bhāvam āpātryate ity arthaḥ |
2tadā tasmin kāle gantāsi prāpsyasi nirvedaṃ vairāgyaṃ śrotavyasya śrutasya ca, tadā śrotavyaṃ śrutaṃ ca te niṣphalaṃ pratibhātīty abhiprāyaḥ ||bhgs_2.52||
