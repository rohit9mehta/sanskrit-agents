Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.8

na hi prapaśyāmi mamāpanudyād yac chokam ucchoṣaṇam indriyāṇām | avāpya bhūmāv asapatnam ṛddhaṃ rājyaṃ surāṇām api cādhipatyam

Translation. For I do not see what could drive away my grief (śoka), which parches the senses (indriyāṇi)— even were I to obtain on earth an unrivalled, prosperous kingdom, and even sovereignty over the gods.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The form yat is formally ambiguous between nominative and accusative neuter singular. Since apanudyāt is active and transitive with śokam as its object, the relative pronoun most naturally supplies the subject: “what would remove…”.
→ yat is nominative neuter singular, subject of apanudyāt; an omitted correlative tad is understood as the object of prapaśyāmi.

The genitive pronoun is best taken with the nearby object śokam. The verb apanudyāt already takes śokam as its direct object, so a separate genitive complement “for me/from me” is less direct.
→ mama is possessive genitive with śokam: “my grief.”

Although ucchoṣaṇam could be read as a neuter noun in isolation, agreement with masculine accusative śokam favors an attributive adjective. MW s.v. ucchoṣaṇa gives the mfn sense “making dry, parching,” which fits the construction.
→ ucchoṣaṇam is an accusative masculine adjective qualifying śokam; indriyāṇām is its objective genitive: “parching the senses.”

The accusative neuter nouns rājyam and ādhipatyam are the objects of avāpya. In the larger negative sentence—“I do not see what would remove…”—the absolutive gives a circumstance that would still not remove the sorrow; English idiom therefore calls for a concessive rendering.
→ avāpya is an absolutive governing both rājyam and ādhipatyam, rendered concessively in English: “even were I to obtain…”.

The three accusative neuter singular forms asapatnam, ṛddham, and rājyam form one noun phrase, while bhūmau locates the kingdom. MW s.v. rājya supports “kingdom/sovereignty,” and MW s.v. asapatna supports “without a rival/adversary.”
→ bhūmau construes with rājyam: “a kingdom on earth”; asapatnam and ṛddham are attributes of rājyam.

A genitive with a sovereignty term naturally marks the domain or object of lordship. The placement of api after surāṇām heightens the claim from earthly kingdom to lordship even over gods, while ca links that object with rājyam under avāpya.
→ surāṇām depends on ādhipatyam, api intensifies it, and ca coordinates ādhipatyam with rājyam: “and even sovereignty over the gods.”
