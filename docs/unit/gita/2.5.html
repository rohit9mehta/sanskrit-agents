Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.5

gurūn ahatvā hi mahānubhāvāñ śreyo bhoktuṃ bhaikṣyam apīha loke | hatvārtha-kāmāṃs tu gurūn ihaiva bhuñjīya bhogān rudhira-pradigdhān

Translation. For, without killing the teachers, men of great dignity, it is better to eat even alms here in this world. But, having killed the teachers who desire gain, I would enjoy, right here, pleasures smeared with blood.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The a is construed as the negative prefix attached to the absolutive hatvā from √han, not as a separate vocative particle. This is required by the contrast with the second half’s positive hatvā, “having killed.” Commentary is unavailable, so the decision rests on grammar and the MW entries for a and √han.
→ ahatvā = “without/not having killed”
gita []
The compound is taken as a bahuvrīhi: mahān anubhāvo yeṣām, “those whose dignity/authority is great.” MW gives mahat as “great” and anubhāva as “dignity, authority, consequence.” Commentary is unavailable.
→ mahānubhāvān qualifies gurūn as “of great dignity/nobility”
gita []
śreyaḥ is construed as neuter nominative singular with an implied asti and the infinitive bhoktum: “it is better to eat.” It is not the object of bhoktum; bhaikṣyam fills that object role. Commentary is unavailable; MW s.v. śreyas supports “better, preferable.”
→ śreyaḥ is a neuter predicate, “better/preferable”
gita []
api most naturally scopes over the immediately preceding object bhaikṣyam, so the half-verse says that even alms are preferable to killing the teachers. Commentary is unavailable; the decision rests on word order and syntax.
→ bhaikṣyam api = “even begged food/alms”
gita []
The compound is taken adjectivally with kāma in its common final-member sense “desirous of”: arthaḥ kāmo yeṣām. It qualifies gurūn. It is not taken as a separate object “wealth and pleasures,” since its accusative masculine plural agreement and position fit gurūn. Commentary is unavailable; MW s.v. artha gives “advantage/use/aim,” and MW s.v. kāma gives “wish, desire” and supports desire-construals. The verifier’s report did not derive an alternative form, but only gave a tool_error/unsupported-compound notice, so this grammatical decision is retained.
→ artha-kāmān = “desiring gain/wealth”
gita []
eva strengthens iha, so the second half contrasts immediate worldly enjoyment with the prior alternative of alms in this world. Commentary is unavailable; MW s.v. eva supports emphatic “just, very, only,” and MW s.v. iha gives “here; in this world.”
→ ihaiva = “right here / in this very world or life”
gita []
The finite verb is VidhiLiṅ, first singular, with Arjuna as subject and bhogān as object. The optative is translated “I would enjoy,” expressing the unacceptable consequence of killing the teachers. Commentary is unavailable; the decision rests on verbal morphology.
→ bhuñjīya = first-person optative, “I would enjoy”
gita []
The compound is an instrumental tatpuruṣa: rudhireṇa pradigdhāḥ, “smeared with blood,” qualifying bhogān. It is not merely “blood-red.” Commentary is unavailable; MW s.v. rudhira includes “blood/bloody,” and MW s.v. pra-√dih gives “to smear over, besmear.” The verifier’s report did not derive an alternative form, but only gave a tool_error/unsupported-compound notice, so this grammatical decision is retained.
→ rudhira-pradigdhān = “smeared with blood”
