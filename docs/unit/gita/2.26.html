Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.26

atha cainaṃ nityajātaṃ nityaṃ vā manyase mṛtam | tathāpi tvaṃ mahābāho naivaṃ śocitum arhasi

Translation. Now, even if—granting the point— you think this self (ātman) born again and again, or dead again and again, still, O mighty-armed one, you should not grieve in this way.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Force of atha ca at the opening.
→ ‘Now/even if, granting the point’ rather than a merely sequential ‘now/and’.
gita [1]atha ca iti abhyupagamārthaḥ |
‘atha ca’ has the sense of an admission or concession.
Referent of enam.
→ enam refers to the already introduced self (ātman), not simply to the body or to Arjuna as an empirical person.
gita [2]enaṃ prakṛtam ātmānaṃ
‘this’, the self under discussion.
Sense of nitya-jātam and its compound construal.
→ nityajātam means ‘born again and again/continually born’ in connection with repeated bodily origination, according to ordinary worldly belief.
gita [2]enaṃ prakṛtam ātmānaṃ nitya-jātaṃ loka-prasiddhayā praty aneka-śarīrotpatti jāto jāta iti manyase |
You think this self under discussion to be nitya-jāta: according to worldly understanding, with the origination of many bodies, ‘born, born’.
Construe nityam with mṛtam.
→ nityaṃ ... mṛtam is taken adverbially: ‘dead again and again/continually dead’ at each destruction, not as a single static eternal death.
gita [3]tathā prati-tat-tad-vināśaṃ nityaṃ vā manyase mṛtaṃ mṛto mṛta iti |
Likewise, with respect to each such destruction, you think [it] constantly dead—‘dead, dead’.
Scope of tathāpi and force of na evaṃ śocitum arhasi.
→ Even on the conceded supposition that such birth and death belong to the self, grief is still not warranted; śocitum arhasi is therefore ‘you should/ought to grieve’, negated by na.
gita [4]tathāpi tathābhāve 'py ātmani tvaṃ mahābāho, na evaṃ śocitum arhasi, janmavato nāśo nāśavato janma cety etāv avaśyaṃbhāvināv iti ||bhgs_2.26||
Even so, even if such were the case in the self, you, O mighty-armed, should not grieve thus; for destruction for what is born and birth for what has been destroyed are both inevitable.
Compound relation in mahābāho.
→ mahābāho is a bahuvrīhi vocative: ‘O one whose arms are great’, idiomatically ‘O mighty-armed one’.
