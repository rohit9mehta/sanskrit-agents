Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.9

saṃjaya uvāca evam uktvā hṛṣīkeśaṃ guḍākeśaḥ parantapaḥ | na yotsya iti govindam uktvā tūṣṇīṃ babhūva ha

Translation. Sañjaya said: Having spoken thus to Hṛṣīkeśa, Guḍākeśa, the foe-scorcher (parantapaḥ), having said to Govinda, “I shall not fight,” became silent indeed.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The printed yotsya before iti is explained by final e of yotsye becoming ay before initial i; iti marks the preceding words as quoted speech. The verb is therefore first-person singular future from yudh, negated by na.
→ na yotsya iti is understood as sandhi for na yotsye iti, a direct quotation: “I shall not fight.”

The supplied text has parantapaḥ, and the analyzer reads it as nominative singular. In the absence of commentary, it is construed with the subject Guḍākeśaḥ rather than as a vocative address.
→ parantapaḥ is taken as nominative singular, an epithet agreeing with guḍākeśaḥ.

With the absolutive of vac, the accusative names mark the person to whom speech is directed: first “having spoken thus to Hṛṣīkeśa,” then “having said ‘I shall not fight’ to Govinda.” Both names refer to Kṛṣṇa.
→ hṛṣīkeśam and govindam are accusatives of the person addressed with the two occurrences of uktvā.

tūṣṇīm is an indeclinable meaning “silently, in silence,” construed with babhūva from bhū; ha is an emphatic or narrative particle, lightly represented by “indeed.”
→ tūṣṇīṃ babhūva ha means “became silent indeed.”

The dictionary entries identify the former three as names of Kṛṣṇa or Arjuna, while paraṃtapa is a transparent descriptive adjective, “destroying foes.” Since commentary is absent, no special etymological interpretation of the names is built into the translation.
→ Hṛṣīkeśa, Guḍākeśa, and Govinda are retained as proper names; parantapaḥ is translated as the descriptive epithet “foe-scorcher.”
