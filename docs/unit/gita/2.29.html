Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.29

āścaryavat paśyati kaścid enam āścaryavad vadati tathaiva cānyaḥ | āścaryavac cainam anyaḥ śṛṇoti śrutvāpy enaṃ veda na caiva kaścit

Translation. As a marvel some rare one beholds this Self (ātman); as a marvel, just so, another speaks of it. As a marvel, too, another hears of it; even after hearing, not one at all knows it.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
I take āścaryavat in all three clauses as a comparison to a marvel, not as ‘wonderfully’ in a merely adverbial manner and not as a possessive ‘wonder-having’.
→ āścaryavat = ‘as/like a marvel’.
gita [1]āścaryavat āścaryam adṛṣṭa-pūrvam adbhutam akasmād dṛśyamānaṃ tena tulyaṃ āścaryavat āścaryam iva enam ātmānaṃ paśyati kaścit |
‘Āścaryavat’: a wonder, something never seen before, astonishing, suddenly appearing; equal to that—āścaryavat, like a wonder—someone sees this Self.
The pronoun is not left as an unspecified ‘him/this’; it is translated as ‘this Self’ on first occurrence and then ‘it’.
→ enam/enaṃ refers to the Self (ātman).
gita [1]āścaryavat āścaryam iva enam ātmānaṃ paśyati kaścit |
Someone sees this, the Self, as like a wonder.
gita [4]śrutvā dṛṣṭvā uktvāpi enam ātmānaṃ veda na caiva kaścit |
Even after hearing, seeing, and speaking, no one knows this, the Self.
The three positive clauses distribute seeing, speaking, and hearing among different rare persons; tathā eva carries the same ‘as a marvel’ construal into the speaking clause.
→ ‘Another likewise speaks of it; another hears of it.’
gita [2]āścaryavad enaṃ vadati tathaiva cānyaḥ |
Another, just so, speaks of it as a marvel.
gita [3]āścaryavac ca enam anyaḥ sṛṇoti |
And another hears it as a marvel. The line prints sṛṇoti, evidently for the verse’s śṛṇoti.
Although the first construal translates kaścit/anyaḥ straightforwardly, the English ‘some rare one’ reflects Śaṅkara’s stated purport, not a mere random ‘someone’.
→ The persons are rare, and the point is the Self’s difficulty of comprehension.
gita [5]athavā yo 'yam ātmānaṃ paśyati sa āścarya-tulyaḥ, yo vadati ya ca sṛṇoti so 'neka-sahasreṣu kaścid eva bhavati |
Or: the one who sees this Self is wonder-like; the one who speaks and the one who hears is only some one among many thousands. The printed sṛṇoti is treated as a plaintext error for śṛṇoti.
gita [6]ato durbodha ātmā ity abhiprāyaḥ ||bhgs_2.29||
Therefore the purport is: the Self is hard to comprehend.
api is not taken as a simple additive ‘also’; the final pāda says that even after the relevant acts of hearing—and, in Śaṅkara’s expansion, seeing and speaking—knowledge still does not arise.
→ śrutvā api is concessive: ‘even after hearing’.
gita [4]śrutvā dṛṣṭvā uktvāpi enam ātmānaṃ veda na caiva kaścit |
Even after hearing, seeing, and speaking, no one knows this Self.
The final verb is translated as knowing/understanding the Self, and the negation is taken strongly; this matches Śaṅkara’s explicit explanation that the Self is durbodha, hard to comprehend.
→ veda na caiva kaścit = ‘not one at all knows it’.
gita [4]enam ātmānaṃ veda na caiva kaścit |
No one at all knows this Self.
gita [6]ato durbodha ātmā ity abhiprāyaḥ ||bhgs_2.29||
Thus the intended meaning is that the Self is difficult to understand.
Morphologically veda is a special liṭ/perfect form of √vid, but in this usage it has present value; hence the translation is not ‘has known’ as a past perfect.
→ veda is rendered with present sense, ‘knows’.
