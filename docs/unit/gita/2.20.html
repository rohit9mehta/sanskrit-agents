Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.20

na jāyate mriyate vā kadācin nāyaṃ bhūtvā bhavitā vā na bhūyaḥ | ajo nityaḥ śāśvato 'yaṃ purāṇo na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre

Translation. He is not born; he does not die, ever. Nor, after having been, will this one fail to be again; nor, after not having been, will he come to be again. Unborn, eternal, undecaying, ancient-yet-ever-new is this Self (ātman); he is not transformed (vipariṇāma) when the body is being transformed.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The verse’s masculine pronoun is not taken as the body or the empirical person; Śaṅkara repeatedly identifies the subject as ayam ātmā and says all worldly transformations are denied in the ātmā.
→ ayam refers to the ātmā, so the translation says ‘this Self (ātman)’.
gita [6]yasmāt ayam ātmā bhūtvā bhavana-kriyām anubhūya paścāt abhavitā abhāvaṃ gantā na bhūyaḥ punaḥ, tasmāt na mriyate |
Because this is ‘this ātman’, Śaṅkara identifies the subject of the clause as the Self.
gita [26, 27]asmin mantre ṣaḍ-bhāva-vikārā laukika-vastu-vikriyā ātmani pratiṣidhyante | sarva-prakāra-vikriyā-rahita ātmā iti vākyārthaḥ |
In this mantra the six modes of worldly change are denied in the ātman; the sentence-meaning is that the ātman is free from every kind of transformation.
The first vā is rendered conjunctively, not as a genuine alternative ‘or’; kadācit is construed with the denials of birth and death, indeed with the denials of all transformations.
→ ‘He is not born, and he does not die, ever.’
gita [1, 2, 3, 4]na jāyate notpadyate, jani-lakṣaṇā vastu-vikriyā na ātmano vidyate ity arthaḥ | tathā na mriyate vā | vā-śabdaḥ cārthe | na mriyate ca iti antyā vināśa-lakṣaṇā vikriyā pratiṣidhyate |
‘He is not born’ means no birth-type modification exists in the Self; likewise he does not die. The word vā has the sense of ca, ‘and’, and the final destruction-type modification is denied.
gita [5]kadācic chabdaḥ sarva-vikriyā-pratiṣedhaiḥ saṃbadhyate -- na kadācit jāyate, na kadācit mriyate, ity evam |
The word kadācit is connected with all the denials of transformation: never is he born, never does he die, and so on.
The compact second pāda is expanded following Śaṅkara into a twofold denial: the Self is not something that, after being, goes to non-being; nor is it like a body, something that, after non-being, comes into being. Bhūyaḥ is taken as ‘again’ (punaḥ).
→ ‘Nor, after having been, will this one fail to be again; nor, after not having been, will he come to be again.’
gita [6]yasmāt ayam ātmā bhūtvā bhavana-kriyām anubhūya paścāt abhavitā abhāvaṃ gantā na bhūyaḥ punaḥ, tasmāt na mriyate |
Śaṅkara explains the death-denial: the Self is not one that, after having been, later goes to non-existence; bhūyaḥ is glossed as punaḥ, ‘again’.
gita [8, 9, 10]vā-śabdāt na-śabdāc ca ayam ātmā abhūtvā vā bhavitā dehavat na bhūyaḥ | tasmāt na jāyate | yo hi abhūtvā bhavitā sa jāyata ity ucyate |
From vā and na Śaṅkara also supplies the birth-denial: the Self is not, like a body, one that after not having been will come to be again; one that does so is said to be born.
The adjectives are not mere epithets. Śaṅkara correlates them with the denial of specific bhāva-vikāras: birth, death, decline, and growth. Purāṇa therefore cannot mean ‘old/worn out’ in the ordinary sense; it is ‘old’ while remaining ever new, because the partless Self does not increase.
→ ajaḥ, nityaḥ, śāśvataḥ, purāṇaḥ are rendered as ‘unborn, eternal, undecaying, ancient-yet-ever-new’.
gita [13]yasmād evaṃ tasmāt ajaḥ, yasmāt na mriyate tasmāt nityaś ca |
Because the Self is not born, it is ajaḥ; because it does not die, it is nityaḥ.
gita [14, 15, 16, 17, 18]yadyapi ādy-antayor vikriyayoḥ pratiṣedhe sarvā vikriyāḥ pratiṣiddhā bhavanti, tathāpi madhya-bhāvinīnāṃ vikriyāṇāṃ sva-śabdair eva pratiṣedhaḥ kartavyo 'nuktānām api yauvanādi-samasta-vikriyāṇāṃ pratiṣedho yathā syāt ity āha-- śāśvata ity ādinā | śāśvata iti apakṣaya-lakṣaṇā vikriyā pratiṣidhyate | śaśvad-bhavaḥ śāśvataḥ | na apakṣīyate svarūpeṇa, niravayavatvāt | nāpi guṇa-kṣayeṇa apakṣayaḥ, nirguṇatvāt |
Although denying beginning and end already denies all transformations, the middle transformations are denied by their own words. Śāśvata denies the transformation called decline; the Self does not decline in its own nature or through loss of qualities.
gita [19, 20, 21]apakṣaya-viparītāpi vṛddhi-lakṣaṇā vikriyā pratiṣidhyate --- purāṇa iti | yo hi avayavāgamena upacīyate sa vardhate abhinava iti ca ucyate | ayaṃ tu ātmā niravayavatvāt purāpi nava eveti purāṇaḥ na vardhate ity arthaḥ |
Purāṇa denies the opposite of decline, namely growth. What grows by the addition of parts is called new; but this partless Self is even formerly new—purāṇa—and so does not grow.
Although the surface verb is from √han, Śaṅkara explicitly says that here √han must be understood in the sense of vipariṇāma in order to avoid repetition after mriyate. The locative absolute therefore means ‘when the body is being transformed’.
→ hanyate and hanyamāne are translated by ‘is transformed / being transformed’, not by literal ‘is slain / being slain’.
gita [22, 23, 24]tathā na hanyate | hantiḥ atra vipariṇāmārthe draṣṭavyo 'punaruktatāyai | na vipariṇamyate ity arthaḥ |
Similarly, ‘he is not hanyate’; here han is to be seen as having the meaning of transformation, to avoid repetition. The meaning is: he is not transformed.
