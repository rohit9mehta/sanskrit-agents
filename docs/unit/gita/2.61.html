Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.61

tāni sarvāṇi saṃyamya yukta āsīta mat-paraḥ | vaśe hi yasyendriyāṇi tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā

Translation. Having brought all those [senses] under control, let him remain collected (yuktaḥ), with Me as the Highest (mat-paraḥ)— [knowing,] “I am not other than Vāsudeva, the inner Self of all.” For the ascetic whose senses, through the strength of practice, remain under control: his wisdom (prajñā) is established.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
tāni sarvāṇi is taken as the accusative object of the absolutive saṃyamya, and saṃyamya is not merely “holding together” but “bringing under control.”
→ “having brought all those [senses] under control”
gita [1]tāni sarvāṇi saṃyamya saṃyamanaṃ vaśīkaraṇaṃ kṛtvā
“Having restrained all those: having done restraining, that is, bringing under control.”
yuktaḥ is translated according to Śaṅkara’s gloss samāhitaḥ, and āsīta is an optative injunction, not a simple past or present statement.
→ “let him remain/sit collected”
gita [1]yuktaḥ samāhitaḥ san āsīta
“Being yukta, that is, collected/concentrated, he should sit/remain.”
mat-paraḥ is read as a bahuvrīhi: one for whom Kṛṣṇa/Vāsudeva, the inner Self of all, is para. Śaṅkara also makes the required standpoint non-difference from that Vāsudeva.
→ “with Me as the Highest (mat-paraḥ), [knowing] ‘I am not other than Vāsudeva, the inner Self of all’”
gita [1]mat-paro 'haṃ vāsudevaḥ sarva-pratyag-ātmā paro yasya sa mat-paraḥ, nānyo 'haṃ tasmāt ity āsīta ity arthaḥ
“mat-paraḥ: he for whom I—Vāsudeva, the inner Self of all—am the Highest; the meaning is that he should abide [thinking], ‘I am not other than that.’”
vaśe is construed as a locative state of control with indriyāṇi as the nominative subject of an implied vartante. Śaṅkara identifies the person as the yati who abides thus and adds abhyāsa-balāt as the means by which the senses remain controlled.
→ “whose senses remain under control, by the strength of practice”
gita [2]evam āsīnasya yater vaśe hi yasyendriyāṇi vartante abhyāsa-balāt
“For the ascetic who abides thus, whose senses remain in control by the strength of practice.”
yasya and tasya form a relative-correlative pair referring to the supplied yati; prajñā is the feminine nominative subject and pratiṣṭhitā its predicate adjective.
→ “his wisdom is established”
gita [2]tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā
“His wisdom is established.”
hi is taken causally, making the second hemistich give the reason for the injunction in the first.
→ “for”
