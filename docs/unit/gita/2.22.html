Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.22

vāsāṃsi jīrṇāni yathā vihāya navāni gṛhṇāti naro 'parāṇi | tathā śarīrāṇi vihāya jīrṇāni anyāni saṃyāti navāni dehī

Translation. As a man, having cast off worn-out garments, takes up other, new ones, so too the embodied one (dehī), the self (ātman), remaining unchanged, having cast off worn-out bodies, goes to other, new ones.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The first half describes an ordinary man discarding old garments, not merely separating from something or dealing with ‘old’ in some other sense such as ‘digested’.
→ vāsāṃsi means ‘garments/clothes’; jīrṇāni means ‘worn out, grown weak’; vihāya means ‘having cast off/abandoned’.
gita [1]vāsāṃsi vastrāṇi jīrṇāni durbalatāṃ gatāni yathā loke vihāya parityajya
‘vāsāṃsi’ means garments; ‘jīrṇāni’ means those that have become weak; ‘vihāya’ means having abandoned, as in ordinary life.
The man takes up other new garments after abandoning the old ones; aparāṇi is not ‘later’ here but equivalent to anyāni, ‘other’.
→ gṛhṇāti is ‘takes up’; navāni are ‘new’; aparāṇi are ‘other’.
gita [1]navāni abhinavāni gṛhṇāti upādatte naraḥ puruṣo 'parāṇi anyāni
‘navāni’ means quite new; ‘gṛhṇāti’ means takes up; ‘naraḥ’ is a man/person; ‘aparāṇi’ means other.
The scope of the comparison is the whole action of abandoning worn-out coverings and going to/taking up other new ones; tathā is ‘just so’.
→ yathā … tathā states a strict analogy: as a man does this with clothes, so the embodied self does this with bodies.
gita [1]yathā loke
‘as in ordinary life’.
gita [1]tathā tadvad eva
‘so, just in that very same way’.
gita [1]puruṣavat
‘like the man/person’.
The subject of the second half is not the physical body or merely an empirical person, but the self that has a body; Śaṅkara explicitly adds avikriya, ‘unchanged’.
→ dehī is the embodied self, i.e. the ātman, and the intended purport includes its remaining unchanged.
gita [1]dehī ātmā puruṣavat avikriya evety arthaḥ
The dehī is the self; the meaning is that, like the man, it is indeed unchanged.
The accusative śarīrāṇi jīrṇāni is governed by vihāya, while anyāni navāni supplies the new bodies reached by saṃyāti.
→ In the second half, the dehī abandons worn-out bodies and goes to other new bodies; saṃyāti is ‘goes to / comes together with’.
gita [1]śarīrāṇi vihāya jīrṇāni anyāni saṃyāti saṃgacchati navāni dehī
The dehī, having abandoned worn-out bodies, goes to / comes together with other new ones.
