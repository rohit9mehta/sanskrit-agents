Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.4

kathaṃ bhīṣmam ahaṃ saṃkhye droṇaṃ ca madhusūdana | iṣubhiḥ pratiyotsyāmi pūjārhāv arisūdana

Translation. How shall I, in battle, fight with arrows against Bhīṣma and Droṇa, O Madhusūdana—those two worthy of reverence, O slayer of foes?

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The supplied commentary is silent. Grammatically katham is an indeclinable interrogative; MW s.v. katham, 247,2 gives ‘how? in what manner?’ and also notes that it can simply introduce an interrogation. With the future first singular pratiyotsyāmi, the natural construal is ‘How shall I fight …?’ rather than an indefinite ‘somehow’.
→ katham is an interrogative of manner/possibility: ‘How shall I …?’
The supplied commentary is silent. Although saṃkhya can relate to counting, MW s.v. saṃkhya, 1128,1 gives the neuter sense ‘conflict, battle, war’ and notes this sense as occurring ‘only in loc.’; the locative saṃkhye therefore means ‘in battle’.
→ saṃkhye is locative singular meaning ‘in battle’.
The supplied commentary is silent. MW s.v. pratiyudh, 669,2 glosses prati-√yudh as ‘to fight against’ with an accusative object; hence bhīṣmam and droṇam are governed by pratiyotsyāmi. MW s.v. iṣu, 168,3 gives ‘an arrow’, so iṣubhiḥ is ‘with arrows’. The masculine accusatives and the later accusative dual pūjārhau also show that Bhīṣma and Droṇa are personal names here, not common nouns such as ‘terrible’ or ‘vessel/measure’ from the abbreviated dictionary excerpts.
→ bhīṣmam and droṇam are coordinated accusative objects of pratiyotsyāmi; iṣubhiḥ is the instrumental means.
The supplied commentary is silent. The printed pūjārhāv is explained by external sandhi from pūjārhau before a- in arisūdana. MW s.v. pūjā, 641,1 gives ‘honour, worship, respect, reverence’, and MW s.v. arha, 93,3 gives ‘meriting, deserving, worthy of’. Since the verse has two accusative singular names, bhīṣmam and droṇam, pūjārhau is best taken as masculine accusative dual apposition to them: ‘the two worthy of reverence’.
→ pūjārhau is an accusative dual compound adjective: ‘the two worthy of reverence’.
The supplied commentary is silent. Both forms are masculine vocative singulars in -a. MW s.v. madhusūdana, 780,3 includes both the literal ‘destroyer of honey’/‘a bee’ and the epic name ‘destroyer of the demon Madhu, N. of Viṣṇu-Kṛṣṇa’; in this address to Kṛṣṇa the latter is required. MW s.v. sūdana, 1242,2 gives the ifc. sense ‘killing, destroying’, so ari-sūdana is ‘slayer of foes’.
→ madhusūdana and arisūdana are vocative epithets addressed to Kṛṣṇa.
