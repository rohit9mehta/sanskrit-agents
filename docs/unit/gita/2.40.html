Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.40

nehābhikrama-nāśo 'sti pratyavāyo na vidyate | svalpam apy asya dharmasya trāyate mahato bhayāt

Translation. Here, on the path to liberation (mokṣa-mārga), in karma-yoga, there is no loss of an undertaking (abhikrama); no adverse consequence (pratyavāya) is found. Even a little of this discipline (dharma), when practiced, protects one from the great fear of saṃsāra.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The locative/adverbial scope of iha is not ‘in this world’ in general but the presently taught karma-yoga as a mokṣa-path.
→ iha = ‘here’ in the path to liberation, specifically in karma-yoga
gita [1]neha mokṣa-mārge karma-yoge
‘Here’ means ‘on the path to liberation, in karma-yoga.’
The compound is taken as a ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa: ‘destruction/loss of the abhikrama,’ where abhikrama is glossed as prārambha, a beginning or undertaking. The point is that in yoga the initiated effort is not fruitlessly lost as in ordinary ventures such as agriculture.
→ abhikrama-nāśaḥ = loss/destruction of the undertaking or beginning
gita [1]abhikrama-nāśo 'bhikramaṇam abhikramaḥ prārambhas tasya nāśo nāsti yathā kṛṣyādeḥ
Abhikrama is the act of beginning, a commencement; there is no destruction of that, unlike agriculture and the like.
gita [2]yoga-viṣaye prārambhasya na anaikāntika-phalatvam ity arthaḥ
The meaning is: with respect to yoga, the beginning does not have uncertain fruit.
The term is not merely ‘diminution’ or ‘annoyance’; Śaṅkara’s comparison with medical treatment points to a harmful adverse result, which is denied in karma-yoga.
→ pratyavāyaḥ = adverse consequence or harmful reverse result
gita [3]kiṃ ca nāpi cikitsāvat pratyavāyo vidyate bhavati
Moreover, nor is there an adverse consequence, as in medical treatment; it does not exist/occur.
Śaṅkara identifies dharmasya as yoga-dharmasya and supplies anuṣṭhitam, so the subject of trāyate is not an abstract ‘smallness’ but even a slight practiced portion of this discipline.
→ svalpam api asya dharmasya = even a little of this yoga-dharma, when practiced
gita [4]kiṃ tu svalpam api asya dharmasya yoga-dharmasya anuṣṭhitaṃ trāyate
But even a little of this dharma, the dharma of yoga, when practiced, protects.
The verb trāyate is glossed by rakṣati, ‘protects,’ and mahad bhayam is specified as saṃsāra-bhaya characterized by birth, death, and so on.
→ trāyate mahato bhayāt = protects from the great fear of saṃsāra
gita [4]trāyate rakṣati mahataḥ bhayāt saṃsāra-bhayāt janma-maraṇādi-lakṣaṇāt
Trāyate means ‘protects’; from the great fear, from the fear of saṃsāra, characterized by birth, death, and the like.
