Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.63

krodhād bhavati saṃmohaḥ saṃmohāt smṛti-vibhramaḥ | smṛti-bhraṃśād buddhi-nāśo buddhi-nāśāt praṇaśyati

Translation. From anger arises bewilderment (saṃmoha); from bewilderment, the lapse of memory (smṛti-vibhrama). From the loss of memory comes the destruction of discernment (buddhi-nāśa); from the destruction of discernment, he is lost—unfit for the human end (puruṣārtha).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Translate saṃmoha as a moral-practical bewilderment, not merely as generic unconsciousness or mental confusion.
→ saṃmohaḥ is “bewilderment” specifically as aviveka, non-discrimination about kārya and akārya.
gita [4, 5]krodhād bhavati saṃmoho 'vivekaḥ kāryākārya-viṣayaḥ | kruddho hi saṃmūḍhaḥ san gurum apy ākrośati |
From anger there arises saṃmoha—non-discrimination whose object is what should and should not be done. For an angry man, being bewildered, abuses even a teacher.
Render smṛti-vibhramaḥ as “lapse of memory,” not as mere agitation, and not as confusion about the Smṛti textual corpus.
→ smṛti-vibhramaḥ is a lapse/derangement of the memory generated by scriptural and teacherly instruction; vibhrama is glossed as bhraṃśa and as non-arising when the occasion for memory is present.
gita [6]saṃmohāt smṛti-vibhramaḥ śāstrācāryopadeśāhita-saṃskāra-janitāyāḥ smṛte syāt vibhramo bhraṃśaḥ smṛty-utpatti-nimitta-prāptau anutpattiḥ |
From bewilderment comes smṛti-vibhrama: for the memory produced by saṃskāras deposited by scriptural and teacherly instruction, there is vibhrama, a bhraṃśa—when the cause for the arising of memory is present, it does not arise. The printed smṛte is understood as the genitive smṛteḥ in this construction.
Take buddhi-nāśa as loss of discriminative fitness, not literal annihilation of the intellect-substance.
→ smṛti-bhraṃśāt and buddhi-nāśaḥ are linked as loss of memory causing the destruction of buddhi; buddhi-nāśa is the incapacity of the inner faculty for kāryākārya-viveka.
gita [7, 8]tataḥ smṛti-bhraṃśāt buddhi-nāśaḥ buddhi-nāśaḥ | kāryākārya-viṣaya-vivekāyogyatā antaḥ-karaṇasya buddher nāśa ucyate |
Thereupon, from the loss of memory, comes buddhi-nāśa. The destruction of the buddhi is said to be the unfitness of the inner faculty, the buddhi, for discriminating what is to be done and not done.
Translate “he is lost/ruined,” with Śaṅkara’s explanation that this means becoming unfit for the human end, rather than simply “he dies.”
→ praṇaśyati means that the implied person becomes lost in the sense of becoming unfit for puruṣārtha.
gita [9, 10, 11, 12]buddher nāśāt praṇaśyati | tāvad eva hi puruṣaḥ yāvad antaḥkaraṇaṃ tadīyaṃ kāryākārya-viṣaya-viveka-yogyam | tad-ayogyatve naṣṭa eva puruṣo bhavati | ataḥ tasyāntaḥ-karaṇasya buddher nāśāt praṇaśyati puruṣārthāyogyo bhavatīty arthaḥ ||bhgs_2.62-63||
Because of the destruction of buddhi he perishes. A person is such only so long as his inner faculty is fit for discriminating what is to be done and not done. When it is unfit for that, the person is indeed lost. Therefore, from the destruction of that inner faculty, the buddhi, he perishes—meaning, he becomes unfit for puruṣārtha.
Render the ablatives with “from” in the sense of cause/source in an ordered psychological chain.
→ The repeated pañcamī forms express a causal succession: from anger comes bewilderment; from bewilderment, memory-lapse; from memory-loss, buddhi-destruction; from that, ruin.
gita [4, 6, 7, 9, 12]krodhād bhavati saṃmoho 'vivekaḥ kāryākārya-viṣayaḥ | saṃmohāt smṛti-vibhramaḥ śāstrācāryopadeśāhita-saṃskāra-janitāyāḥ smṛte syāt vibhramo bhraṃśaḥ smṛty-utpatti-nimitta-prāptau anutpattiḥ | tataḥ smṛti-bhraṃśāt buddhi-nāśaḥ buddhi-nāśaḥ | buddher nāśāt praṇaśyati | ataḥ tasyāntaḥ-karaṇasya buddher nāśāt praṇaśyati puruṣārthāyogyo bhavatīty arthaḥ ||bhgs_2.62-63||
From anger arises non-discrimination; from bewilderment, lapse of memory; then from the loss of memory, destruction of buddhi; from the destruction of buddhi, he perishes; therefore, from the destruction of that inner faculty, he becomes unfit for puruṣārtha.
