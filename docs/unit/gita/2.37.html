Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.37

hato vā prāpsyasi svargaṃ jitvā vā bhokṣyase mahīm | tasmād uttiṣṭha kaunteya yuddhāya kṛta-niścayaḥ

Translation. If slain, you will attain heaven (svarga); or, having conquered, you will enjoy the earth. Therefore arise, O son of Kuntī (Kaunteya), for battle, with your resolve (niścaya) made.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Śaṅkara explicitly glosses hato as hataḥ san, ‘being slain,’ and repeats svargaṃ prāpsyasi. His later paraphrase of the resolve includes mariṣyāmi vā, ‘or I shall die,’ confirming the death-alternative rather than an active sense such as ‘having killed.’
→ hataḥ means ‘slain,’ predicated of the addressed warrior; svargam is the heaven attained after that death.
gita [1]hato vā prāpsyasi svargam, hataḥ san svargaṃ prāpsyasi |
‘Slain, you will attain heaven’—being slain, you will attain heaven.
gita [4]jeṣyāmi śatrūn, mariṣyāmi vā iti niścayaṃ kṛtvety arthaḥ
The meaning is: having made the resolve, ‘I shall conquer the enemies, or I shall die.’
Śaṅkara supplies the object of jitvā as karṇādīn śūrān, ‘brave men such as Karṇa,’ and keeps mahīm as the object of bhokṣyase. Thus the sequence is defeat the enemy heroes, then enjoy the earth—not simply ‘conquer the earth.’
→ jitvā refers to conquering enemy warriors, while bhokṣyase mahīm means enjoying or possessing the earth after victory.
gita [2]jitvā vā karṇādīn śūrān bhokṣyase mahīm |
Or, having conquered brave men such as Karṇa, you will enjoy the earth.
Śaṅkara states the intended force directly: in both ways there is only gain for you. This controls the rhetoric of the verse as consolation/encouragement, not as a neutral prediction of two possibilities.
→ The paired vā ... vā presents two alternatives, both advantageous to Arjuna.
gita [3]ubhayathāpi tava lābha evety abhiprāyaḥ |
The intention is: in either case, there is only gain for you.
The commentary gives the correlative reasoning yata evaṃ tasmāt, ‘since this is so, therefore,’ so tasmāt is not a spatial/source ablative in the translation but the conclusion drawn from the preceding alternatives.
→ tasmāt is inferential ‘therefore,’ introducing the command uttiṣṭha, ‘arise.’
gita [4]yata evaṃ tasmāt uttiṣṭha kaunteya yuddhāya
Since this is so, therefore stand up, O Kaunteya, for battle.
The commentary’s line is textually noisy at ‘kṛtāni ca yaḥ,’ which I take as a corruption around kṛta-niścayaḥ; the immediately following niścayaṃ kṛtvā, ‘having made the resolve,’ fixes the sense. Śaṅkara also supplies the content of that resolve: ‘I shall conquer the enemies, or I shall die.’ For morphology I record the unhyphenated compound stem kṛtaniścaya; the hyphen is only the printed representation in the source.
→ kṛta-niścayaḥ is a bahuvrīhi: ‘one whose resolve has been made,’ rendered ‘with your resolve made / having made your resolve.’
gita [4]yata evaṃ tasmāt uttiṣṭha kaunteya yuddhāya kṛtāni ca yaḥ jeṣyāmi śatrūn, mariṣyāmi vā iti niścayaṃ kṛtvety arthaḥ ||bhgs_2.37||
Since this is so, therefore stand up, O Kaunteya, for battle—the meaning is: having made the resolve, ‘I shall conquer the enemies, or I shall die.’
The commentary merely repeats kaunteya, so the choice rests on grammar and lexicon: the -a stem vocative singular is kaunteya, and MW identifies kaunteya as a metronymic from Kuntī, a name of Arjuna.
→ kaunteya is a vocative metronym, ‘O son of Kuntī.’
