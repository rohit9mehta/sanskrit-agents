Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.35

bhayād raṇād uparataṃ maṃsyante tvāṃ mahārathāḥ | yeṣāṃ ca tvaṃ bahumato bhūtvā yāsyasi lāghavam

Translation. Out of fear of Karṇa and the others, not from compassion, the great chariot-warriors—Duryodhana and the rest— will think you withdrawn from battle; and, having been highly esteemed in their eyes— regarded as possessed of many virtues— you will come to lowliness (lāghava), the state of being slight.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The ablative bhayāt is causal, and Śaṅkara explicitly supplies karṇādibhyaḥ as the object/source of fear and excludes kṛpā as the motive. Therefore the translation should not leave the withdrawal as noble compassion.
→ “Out of fear of Karṇa and the others,” not “from compassion.”
gita [1]bhayāt karṇādibhyaḥ raṇāt yuddhāt uparataṃ nivṛttaṃ maṃsyante cintayiṣyanti na kṛpayeti
“Out of fear—of Karṇa and the others; from raṇa, that is, battle; uparata, withdrawn; they will think, i.e. they will consider—not through pity.”
Śaṅkara glosses raṇāt as yuddhāt, uparatam as nivṛttam, and maṃsyante as cintayiṣyanti. Thus tvām is the object of “they will think,” with uparatam as its predicate complement.
→ “Withdrawn from battle” as the predicate thought by the mahārathas.
gita [1]raṇāt yuddhāt uparataṃ nivṛttaṃ maṃsyante cintayiṣyanti
“From raṇa, from battle; uparata, withdrawn; maṃsyante, they will think.”
The commentary identifies the subject mahārathāḥ not as literal “great chariots” but as persons, specifically Duryodhana and others.
→ mahārathāḥ are the Kaurava-side great warriors, Duryodhana and the rest.
gita [1]tvāṃ mahārathāḥ duryodhana-prabhṛtayaḥ
“You—the mahārathas, Duryodhana and the rest.”
Rather than taking bahumataḥ as merely “thought by many” or socially popular, Śaṅkara explains the content of the esteem: Arjuna is regarded as endowed with many qualities.
→ bahumataḥ means highly esteemed as possessing many virtues/qualities.
gita [2]yeṣāṃ ca tvaṃ duryodhanādīnāṃ bahu-mato bahubhir guṇair yuktaḥ ity evaṃ mataḥ bahumato bhūtvā
“And for whom—you, among Duryodhana and the others—bahu-mata: thought thus, ‘endowed with many qualities’; having become bahumata.”
The object of yāsyasi is not speed, dexterity, or physical lightness; Śaṅkara glosses lāghava as laghu-bhāva, a fall into being ‘light’ or slight in esteem.
→ lāghavam is lowliness/insignificance, the state of being slight.
gita [2]punar yāsyasi lāghavaṃ laghu-bhāvam
“Then you will go to lāghava, the state of being slight.”
