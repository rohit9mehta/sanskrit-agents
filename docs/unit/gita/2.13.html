Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.13

dehino 'smin yathā dehe kaumāraṃ yauvanaṃ jarā | tathā dehāntara-prāptir dhīras tatra na muhyati

Translation. As, for the embodied self, in this body there are childhood, youth, and old age, so too there is the attainment of another body by that same unchanging self. The wise one is not deluded about this.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The genitive dehinaḥ is not translated merely as ‘of the embodied person’ or ‘of a living creature’; Śaṅkara identifies the dehin here as the body-possessing ātman, the self enveloped in a body.
→ dehinaḥ = ‘of/for the embodied self’
gita [1]deho 'sya astīti dehī, tasya dehino dehavataḥ ātmano
‘One who has a body is dehin; of that dehin, of the body-possessing self ...’
gita [4]avikriyasyaiva ātmana ity arthaḥ
‘The meaning is: of the self alone, which is changeless.’
The three nouns are rendered as ‘childhood, youth, and old age’, not as generic juvenility/youthfulness/decay, because Śaṅkara defines them as successive avasthās: childhood, middle/youthful condition, and decrepit old age.
→ kaumāram, yauvanam, jarā = three distinct bodily life-states
gita [1]kaumāraṃ kumāra-bhāvo bālyāvasthā, yauvanaṃ yūno bhāvo madhyamāvasthā, jarā vayo-hāniḥ jīrṇāvasthā, ity etāḥ tisro 'vasthāḥ anyonya-vilakṣaṇāḥ
‘Kaumāra is the condition of a child, the state of childhood; yauvana is the condition of a youth, the middle state; jarā is loss of age/vitality, the worn-out state. These three states are mutually distinct.’
The yathā/tathā structure is taken to mean: just as the self is not destroyed or produced when bodily states perish or arise, so too its attainment of another body does not imply change in the self. The translation therefore includes ‘by/for that same unchanging self’.
→ The analogy is about attainment of states and then another body by the changeless self
gita [2]tāsāṃ prathamāvasthā-nāśe na nāśaḥ, dvitīyāvasthopajane na upajana ātmanaḥ
‘When the first of those states is destroyed, there is no destruction of the self; when the second state arises, there is no arising of the self.’
gita [3]avikriyasyaiva dvitīya-tṛtīyāvasthā-prāptiḥ ātmano dṛṣṭā
‘Rather, the attainment of the second and third states is seen to belong to the self alone, which is changeless.’
gita [4]tathā tadvad eva ... dehāntara-prāptiḥ avikriyasyaiva ātmana ity arthaḥ
‘So, just in that same way ... the attainment of another body belongs to the self alone, which is changeless—that is the meaning.’
The compound is not taken as ‘attainment between bodies’ or as a vague ‘bodily transformation’; Śaṅkara gives a two-step analysis: dehāntara is a body other than the present body, and dehāntara-prāpti is the attainment of that.
→ dehāntaraprāptiḥ = ‘attainment of another body’
gita [4]dehād anyo deho dehāntaram, tasya prāptiḥ dehāntara-prāptiḥ
‘A body different from the body is dehāntara, another body; the attainment of it is dehāntara-prāpti.’
Dhīraḥ is rendered ‘wise one’, following the gloss dhīmān, rather than primarily ‘firm’ or ‘brave’. Tatra is taken situationally (‘in this matter, when this is so’), not spatially. Muhyati is active intransitive: ‘falls into delusion / is bewildered’.
→ dhīraḥ tatra na muhyati = ‘the wise one is not deluded about this / when this is so’
gita [5]dhīro dhīmān tatraivaṃ sati na muhyati na moham āpadyate
‘The dhīra is the intelligent/wise one; when this is so, he is not deluded, does not fall into delusion.’
