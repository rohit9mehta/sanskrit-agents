Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.69

yā niśā sarva-bhūtānāṃ tasyāṃ jāgarti saṃyamī | yasyāṃ jāgrati bhūtāni sā niśā paśyato muneḥ

Translation. What is night to all beings—the ultimate reality (paramārtha-tattva), out of reach for the ignorant— in that the self-controlled one (saṃyamī), the yogin of conquered senses, keeps awake. In what beings are said to be awake—the night of ignorance (avidyā) marked by the grasper–grasped division (grāhya-grāhaka-bheda)— that is night to the sage (muni) who sees the ultimate reality.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Render the opening as a metaphor: what is night to all beings is the ultimate reality, inaccessible to them.
→ The first niśā is not merely physical night; it is the ultimate reality (paramārtha-tattva) as ‘night’ for ignorant beings because it is outside the range of their cognition.
gita [1]yā niśā rātriḥ sarva-padārthānām aviveka-karī tamaḥ-svabhāvatvāt sarva-bhūtānāṃ sarveṣāṃ bhūtānām
‘The night’ is night/darkness, causing nondiscrimination of all objects because it has the nature of darkness; ‘of all beings’ means of all beings.
gita [2]kiṃ tat paramārtha-tattvaṃ sthita-prajñasya viṣayaḥ
What is that? The ultimate reality, the object of the one whose wisdom is steady.
gita [3]niśā paramārtha-tattvam, agocaratvād atad-buddhīnām
The ‘night’ is the ultimate reality, because it is not within the range of those whose understanding is not turned to that.
Translate jāgarti as cognitive/spiritual wakefulness, and saṃyamī as the self-controlled, sense-conquering yogin.
→ tasyām jāgarti means that the self-controlled yogin is awake in that ultimate-reality locus, having awakened from the sleep of ignorance.
gita [4]tasyāṃ paramārtha-tattva-lakṣaṇāyām ajñāna-nidrāyāḥ prabuddho jāgarti saṃyamī saṃyamavān, jitendriyo yogīty arthaḥ
In that which is characterized by the ultimate reality, awakened from the sleep of ignorance, the self-controlled one keeps awake; ‘self-controlled’ means restrained, a yogin whose senses are conquered.
Render yasyāṃ jāgrati bhūtāni as beings being ‘said’ to be awake within ignorance, while in truth they are asleep like dreamers.
→ The sphere in which beings are ‘awake’ is the night of ignorance (avidyā-niśā), characterized by the division between grasped object and grasper.
gita [5]yasyāṃ grāhya-grāhaka-bheda-lakṣaṇāyām avidyā-niśāyāṃ prasuptāny eva bhūtāni jāgrati iti ucyante, yasyāṃ niśāyāṃ prasuptā iva svapna-dṛśaḥ
In the night of ignorance characterized by the distinction between grasped and grasper, beings, though really asleep, are said to be awake; in that night they are as if asleep, like dream-seers.
Translate paśyato muneḥ as ‘to/for the sage who sees the ultimate reality,’ not as an ablative or as seeing ordinary objects.
→ sā niśā paśyato muneḥ means that this ignorance-sphere is night to the sage who sees the ultimate reality; paśyataḥ is genitive agreeing with muneḥ.
gita [5]sā niśā avidyā-rūpatvāt paramārtha-tattvaṃ paśyato muneḥ
That is night, because it has the form of ignorance, for the sage who sees the ultimate reality.
Use ‘all beings’ rather than ‘all elements,’ ‘all past things,’ or an abstract ‘all existents.’
→ sarvabhūtānām is ‘of all beings,’ with sarva qualifying bhūta; in context Śaṅkara treats these as the ignorant ordinary beings for whom the ultimate reality is inaccessible.
gita [1]sarva-bhūtānāṃ sarveṣāṃ bhūtānām
‘Of all beings’ means ‘of all beings.’
gita [3]ajñānāṃ sarva-bhūtānāṃ
Of all beings, the ignorant.

Śaṅkara's commentary on this unit

3yathā naktaṃ-carāṇām ahar eva sad anyeṣāṃ niśā bhavati, tadvat naktaṃ-cara-sthānīyānām ajñānāṃ sarva-bhūtānāṃ niśeva niśā paramārtha-tattvam, agocaratvād atad-buddhīnām |

5yasyāṃ grāhya-grāhaka-bheda-lakṣaṇāyām avidyā-niśāyāṃ prasuptāny eva bhūtāni jāgrati iti ucyante, yasyāṃ niśāyāṃ prasuptā iva svapna-dṛśaḥ, sā niśā avidyā-rūpatvāt paramārtha-tattvaṃ paśyato muneḥ |
6ataḥ karmāṇi avidyāvasthāyām eva codyante, na vidyāvasthāyām |
