Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.16

nāsato vidyate bhāvo nābhāvo vidyate sataḥ | ubhayor api dṛṣṭo 'ntas tv anayos tattva-darśibhiḥ

Translation. Of the unreal (asat, the non-Self) there is no being (bhāva); of the real (sat, the Self) there is no non-being (abhāva). But the settled conclusion (anta) about both these has been seen by seers of reality (tattva), Brahman’s true nature.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
asataḥ is not taken as moral ‘bad’ or mere false speech, nor as an unrelated absolute nothing; Śaṅkara glosses it as avidyamāna and applies it to caused empirical dualities and modifications.
→ asat = the unreal/non-existent non-Self, especially dependent modifications such as the body and the heat-cold dualities.
gita [1]na asato 'vidyamānasya śītoṣṇādeḥ sa-kāraṇasya na vidyate nāsti bhāvo bhavanam astitā ||
Of the unreal, the non-existent—such as cold and heat together with their causes—there is no being; there is not existence, becoming, actuality.
gita [5]tathā sarvo vikāraḥ kāraṇa-vyatirekeṇānupalabdher asat |
So every modification is unreal, because it is not apprehended apart from its cause.
gita [30]tasmād dehāder dvandvasya ca sa-kāraṇasya asato na vidyate bhāva iti |
Therefore, of the body and the duality and so on, together with their causes, being unreal, there is no being.
The first pāda denies existence of asat, and the second denies non-existence of sat; ‘state’ or ‘emotion’ for bhāva would miss Śaṅkara’s explicit gloss.
→ bhāva = being/existence; abhāva = non-being/non-existence; vidyate is translated existentially as ‘is found/exists’.
gita [1]na asato 'vidyamānasya śītoṣṇādeḥ sa-kāraṇasya na vidyate nāsti bhāvo bhavanam astitā ||
Of the unreal ... there is no bhāva: there is no becoming, existence, actuality.
gita [31]tathā sataś ca ātmano 'bhāvo 'vidyamānatā na vidyate, sarvatrāvyabhicārāt ity avocāma ||
Likewise, of the real, the Self, non-being—non-existence—is not found, because it is invariable everywhere, as we have said.
sataḥ is construed as the real whose absence is never found, not as any ordinary empirical existent; Śaṅkara identifies it with the ātman and grounds it in avyabhicāra.
→ sat = the real, the Self, recognized by invariability of cognition.
gita [10, 11]yad-viṣayā buddhir na vyabhicarati, tat sat | yad-viṣayā vyabhicarati, tad asat |
That whose cognition does not deviate is real; that whose cognition deviates is unreal.
gita [17, 18, 19]na tu sad-buddhiḥ | tasmāt ghaṭādi-buddhi-viṣayo 'san, vyabhicārāt | na tu sad-buddhi-viṣayaḥ, avyabhicārāt ||
But not the cognition of being. Therefore the object of pot-cognition and the like is unreal, because it deviates; but not the object of being-cognition, because it does not deviate.
gita [31]tathā sataś ca ātmano 'bhāvo 'vidyamānatā na vidyate, sarvatrāvyabhicārāt ity avocāma ||
Likewise, of the real, the Self, non-being—non-existence—is not found, because it is invariable everywhere, as we have said.
The genitive duals do not refer to bhāva and abhāva as abstract terms, but to the two ontological sides already stated: the Self/real and non-Self/unreal.
→ ubhayoḥ api ... anayoḥ = ‘of both these’, namely ātman/anātman, sat/asat.
gita [32]evam ātmānātmanoḥ sad-asatoḥ ubhayor api dṛṣṭaḥ upalabdho 'nto nirṇayaḥ sat sad eva, asat asad eveti, tv anayoḥ yathoktayoḥ tattva-darśibhiḥ |
Thus, regarding both—the Self and non-Self, the real and unreal—the end, the determination, has been seen, apprehended: the real is just real, the unreal just unreal; by the seers of reality, regarding these two as stated.
anta is not a physical end or destruction here; Śaṅkara explicitly glosses it as nirṇaya, and dṛṣṭa as upalabdha.
→ dṛṣṭaḥ antaḥ = ‘the settled conclusion has been seen/apprehended’.
gita [32]evam ātmānātmanoḥ sad-asatoḥ ubhayor api dṛṣṭaḥ upalabdho 'nto nirṇayaḥ sat sad eva, asat asad eveti, tv anayoḥ yathoktayoḥ tattva-darśibhiḥ |
Thus, regarding both ... the end, the determination, has been seen, apprehended: the real is just real, the unreal just unreal.
The compound is taken with tattva as the object of seeing/realization, and tattva is not Sāṃkhya categories here but the real nature of Brahman according to Śaṅkara.
→ tattva-darśibhiḥ = ‘by seers of reality (tattva)’, where tattva is Brahman’s true nature.
gita [33]tad iti sarva-nāma sarvaṃ ca brahma, tasya nāma tad iti, tad-bhāvas tattvam, brahmaṇo yāthātmyam |
‘Tat’ is a pronoun, and everything is Brahman; its name is ‘tat’. The state of That is tattva, the real nature of Brahman.
gita [34]tad draṣṭuṃ śīlaṃ yeṣāṃ te tattva-darśinaḥ, tais tattva-darśibhiḥ |
Those whose nature is to see That are tattva-darśins; by those tattva-darśins.

Śaṅkara's commentary on this unit

2na hi śītoṣṇādi sa-kāraṇaṃ pramāṇair nirūpyamāṇaṃ vastu sambhavati |
3vikāro hi saḥ, vikāraś ca vyabhicarati |
