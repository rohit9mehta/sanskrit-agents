Translation. For the wise person, O best among men, whom these [cold-and-heat and the like] do not shake, equal in pain and pleasure— he is fit for immortality (amṛtatva), that is, liberation.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The compound qualifies the accusative puruṣam/dhīram; it is not a neuter object meaning “equal pain-and-pleasure” as a thing.
→ sama-duḥkha-sukhaṃ is a bahuvrīhi: “one for whom pain and pleasure are the same,” specifically free from elation and dejection at their occurrence.
gita [1]same duḥkha-sukhe yasya taṃ sama-duḥkha-sukhaṃ sukha-duḥkha-prāptau harṣa-viṣāda-rahitaṃ
“sama-duḥkha-sukha” means him for whom pain and pleasure are the same, free from joy and despondency when pleasure and pain are obtained.
Śaṅkara explicitly glosses dhīra as dhīmān, so the translation foregrounds wisdom/intelligence rather than courage alone.
→ dhīraṃ is rendered “wise,” with steadiness implied by understanding, not merely “brave.”
gita [1]dhīraṃ dhīmantaṃ
“dhīra” means “possessed of understanding,” wise.
The subject is not “these people” or an unspecified set; Śaṅkara identifies it as yathoktāḥ śītoṣṇādayaḥ and glosses vyathayanti by cālayanti.
→ ete refers to the previously mentioned cold-and-heat and the like; vyathayanti means “disturb/shake.”
gita [1]na vyathayanti na cālayanti nityātma-darśanāt ete yathoktāḥ śītoṣṇādayaḥ
They do not disturb, do not shake, because of the vision of the eternal self; these are the aforementioned cold-and-heat and so on.
kalpate is not taken as “imagines” or simply “becomes”; Śaṅkara glosses it as samartho bhavati. amṛtatva is not mere long life or divine nectar but mokṣa.
→ amṛtatvāya kalpate means “is fit/capable for immortality,” where immortality is liberation (mokṣa).
gita [1]amṛtatvāya amṛta-bhāvāya mokṣāyety arthaḥ kalpate samartho bhavati
“For immortality” means for the state of being immortal, that is, for liberation; “kalpate” means becomes capable/fit.
The commentary is silent on this address; grammar and dictionary decide it. MW s.v. ṛṣabha, 226,3 gives “a bull” and “the best or most excellent of any kind or race,” explicitly comparing forms such as puruṣarṣabha.
→ puruṣarṣabha is a vocative tatpuruṣa, “O bull/best among men.”

Śaṅkara's commentary on this unit
