Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.38

sukha-duḥkhe same kṛtvā lābhālābhau jayājayau | tato yuddhāya yujyasva naivaṃ pāpam avāpsyasi

Translation. Making pleasure and pain alike—without forming attachment and aversion (rāga-dveṣa)— and likewise gain and loss, victory and defeat alike, then apply yourself to battle; fighting thus, you will not incur sin (pāpa).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
same is not merely ‘same’ in a loose lexical sense: Śaṅkara glosses it as tulye, and explains the point of this equalizing as not producing rāga and dveṣa toward pleasure and pain.
→ “Having made pleasure and pain alike—without forming attachment and aversion.”
gita [1]sukha-duḥkhe same tulye kṛtvā, rāga-dveṣāv apy akṛtvety etat |
Having made pleasure and pain the same, equal; that is, also not having made attachment and aversion.
The verse has same only with the neuter dual sukha-duḥkhe, but Śaṅkara explicitly supplies samau kṛtvā for the two masculine dual pairs lābhālābhau and jayājayau. The translation therefore repeats ‘alike/equal’ for both pairs.
→ “and likewise gain and loss, victory and defeat alike.”
gita [2]tathā lābhālābhau jayājayau ca samau kṛtvā tato yuddhāya yujyasva ghaṭāsva |
Likewise, having made gain-and-loss and victory-and-defeat equal, then apply yourself for battle; strive.
yuddhāya is construed as the dative goal/purpose of yujyasva, and yujyasva is translated idiomatically from Śaṅkara’s ghaṭāsva, ‘strive/apply yourself,’ rather than as a bare literal ‘be yoked’.
→ “then apply yourself to battle.”
gita [2]tathā lābhālābhau jayājayau ca samau kṛtvā tato yuddhāya yujyasva ghaṭāsva |
Likewise, having made gain-and-loss and victory-and-defeat equal, then apply yourself for battle; strive.
Śaṅkara expands evam as modifying the manner of fighting—evaṃ yuddhaṃ kurvan—not as an adjective to pāpam (‘such sin’). pāpam is the accusative object of avāpsyasi.
→ “fighting thus, you will not incur sin (pāpa).”
gita [3]na evaṃ yuddhaṃ kurvan pāpam avāpsyasi |
Fighting in this way, you will not incur sin.
This contextual scope does not change the bare sentence meaning, but it prevents over-reading the verse as Śaṅkara’s main tātparya; he calls this upadeśa prāsaṅgika and says that paramārtha-darśana is the present topic.
→ Treat the verse as an incidental practical instruction in Śaṅkara’s sequence, not the final doctrinal purport.
gita [4, 6]ity eṣa upadeśaḥ prāsaṅgikaḥ ||bhgs_2.38|| paramārtha-darśanam iha prakṛtam |
Thus this instruction is incidental. The vision of ultimate reality is what is under discussion here.
