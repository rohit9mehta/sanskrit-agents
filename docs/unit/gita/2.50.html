Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.50

buddhi-yukto jahātīha ubhe sukṛta-duṣkṛte | tasmād yogāya yujyasva yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam

Translation. Joined with the understanding (buddhi) whose field is equanimity, one abandons here in this world both merit and demerit. Therefore apply yourself to yoga—the discipline of equal understanding (samatva-buddhi-yoga); yoga is skillfulness (kauśala) in actions, the actions of one’s own duty.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The compound buddhi-yuktaḥ is not a generic epithet. Śaṅkara construes it as one joined with a buddhi whose specific viṣaya is karma-samatva, equality/equanimity regarding action; line 4 restates the verse’s instruction as becoming samatva-buddhi-yukta.
→ “Joined with the understanding whose object is equanimity,” not merely “intelligent” or “wise.”
gita [1]buddhi-yuktaḥ karma-samatva-viṣayayā buddhyā yukto buddhi-yuktaḥ
“buddhi-yuktaḥ: joined with buddhi whose object is equality with respect to action—that is buddhi-yuktaḥ.”
gita [4]tasmāt samatva-buddhi-yukto bhava tvam
“Therefore become one joined with the understanding of equanimity.”
Śaṅkara glosses jahāti by parityajati, iha by asmin loke, and sukṛta-duṣkṛte by puṇya-pāpe. The abandonment is therefore not simply “stopping good and bad deeds,” but relinquishing both karmic opposites, through purification and attainment of knowledge.
→ The object abandoned is both merit and demerit/sin, here in this world.
gita [1]saḥ jahāti parityajati iha asmin loke ubhe sukṛta-duṣkṛte puṇya-pāpe sattva-śuddhi-jñāna-prāpti-dvāreṇa
“He abandons, gives up, here in this world, both sukṛta and duṣkṛta—merit and sin—through the means of purification of sattva and attainment of knowledge.”
The dative yogāya is not left as an undefined “to yoga.” Śaṅkara expands it to samatva-buddhi-yogāya and glosses yujyasva as ghaṭāsva, so the imperative is rendered idiomatically as “apply yourself/strive,” not as a merely physical “be yoked.”
→ “Apply yourself to yoga,” with yoga specified as samatva-buddhi-yoga.
gita [1]tasmāt samatva-buddhi-yogāya yujyasva ghaṭāsva
“Therefore apply yourself, strive, for the yoga of the understanding of equanimity.”
Śaṅkara prevents reading kauśalam as ordinary clever efficiency. He defines it as kuśala-bhāva consisting in samatva-buddhi toward siddhi and asiddhi, in the context of sva-dharma actions and īśvarārpita-cetastā.
→ “Yoga is skillfulness in actions,” where skillfulness is equanimity in success and failure while performing one’s own-duty actions with the mind offered to Īśvara.
gita [2]yogo hi karmasu kauśalam sva-dharmākhyeṣu karmasu vartamānasya yā siddhy-asiddhyoḥ samatva-buddhir īśvarārpita-cetastayā tat kauśalaṃ kuśala-bhāvaḥ
“For yoga is skillfulness in actions: for one engaged in actions called one’s own duty, the equanimity of understanding toward success and failure, through the state of having the mind offered to Īśvara—that is skillfulness, the state of being skillful.”
Line 3 explains why this is kauśalam: actions that naturally bind are made to turn away from that nature through samatva-buddhi. The printed svabhāvān in the supplied text appears likely to be an unproofread/corrupt form for expected svabhāvāt; the translation follows that evident sense while quoting the input verbatim.
→ The “skill” is non-binding skill: it makes even bondage-natured actions cease from their binding nature.
gita [3]tad dhi kauśalaṃ yad bandhana-svabhāvāny api karmāṇi samatva-buddhyā svabhāvān nivartante
“For that is skill by which even actions whose nature is bondage turn away from their nature through evenness-understanding; svabhāvān as printed is likely for svabhāvāt.”
