Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.2

Translation. Whence has this taint of dejection come upon you in this crisis— resorted to by the ignoble, not leading to heaven, and bringing disgrace, O Arjuna?

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The supplied bhāṣya is silent. Grammatically kutaḥ is an interrogative indeclinable; the dictionary supports ‘from where? whence?’ [MW s.v. kutas, 290,2]. I therefore render the opening as ‘Whence …?’ rather than as a causal ‘why’ alone.
→ kutaḥ = ‘whence/from what source?’ introducing the question

No commentary evidence supplied; grammar and dictionary only.
The neuter forms kaśmalam, idam, and samupasthitam are construed as Prathama Eka. tvā is Dvitiya Eka governed by the sense of sam-upa-√sthā, for which the dictionary gives both ‘to go to, approach (acc.)’ and ‘to occur, arise, befall’ [MW s.v. samupasthā, 1169,3]. Thus the clause is not ‘you have approached this dejection’ but ‘this dejection has come upon you’.
→ idam kaśmalam samupasthitam as the nominative subject-predicate: ‘this taint/dejection has come upon’

No commentary evidence supplied; grammar and dictionary only.
The locative singular viṣame is taken substantively, not as merely ‘on uneven ground’. The dictionary senses of viṣama include ‘uneven, rugged’ and more broadly ‘irregular, dissimilar, inconstant’ [MW s.v. viṣama, 996,3]; in this battlefield rebuke, the idiomatic force is ‘at a difficult/critical juncture’.
→ viṣame = ‘in a critical/difficult situation’

No commentary evidence supplied; grammar and dictionary only.
The compound is read as anāryaiḥ juṣṭam, with anārya as ‘not noble’ and juṣṭa as a participial member from √juṣ. This avoids taking the initial a(n)- as negating the whole ārya-juṣṭa (‘not practised by nobles’). ārya denotes a respectable or honourable man and is opposed to an-ārya [MW s.v. ārya, 152,2].
→ anāryajuṣṭam = ‘resorted to by the ignoble / not-noble’

asvargya is taken as a nañ-compound adjective qualifying kaśmalam, not as ‘heavenless’ in a locative sense. The supplied dictionary explicitly gives a-svargya as ‘not leading to heaven’ [MW s.v. asvargya, 124,1].
→ asvargyam = ‘not leading to heaven’

The final-member adjective kara means ‘doer, maker, causer … causing, producing’ especially in compounds [MW s.v. kara, 253,1], and akīrti means ‘ill-fame, disgrace’ [MW s.v. akīrti, 2,2]. Hence akīrti-karam is causative in sense: ‘producing disgrace’, not merely ‘disgraceful’ as a static quality.
→ akīrtikaram = ‘bringing/causing disgrace’
