Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.12

na tv evāhaṃ jātu nāsaṃ na tvaṃ neme janādhipāḥ | na caiva na bhaviṣyāmaḥ sarve vayam ataḥ param

Translation. Yet indeed, never at any time did I not exist, nor you, nor these lords of men; and indeed, after this [body’s destruction], we all shall never not exist—in Self-nature (ātma-svarūpa).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The first na takes scope over the whole past non-existence assertion, while jātu is glossed as kadācit, ‘at any time.’ The commentary explicitly turns na ... nāsam into kintv āsam eva, ‘rather I existed indeed,’ and supplies the parallel predicates for tvam and janādhipāḥ. Line 4 is textually awkward as printed, but its clear positive gloss and the parallel with line 3 support the same construal for the kings.
→ The past clauses express negated non-existence, hence positive past existence: ‘never did I not exist; nor you; nor these lords of men.’
gita [1]na tv eva jātu kadācid ahaṃ nāsam kintv āsam eva |
‘Not indeed ever, at any time, was I not; rather, I existed indeed.’
gita [2]atīteṣu dehotpatti-vināśeṣu ghaṭādiṣu viyad iva nitya eva aham āsam ity abhiprāyaḥ |
‘The intention is: through past productions and destructions of bodies, like space in pots and the like, I was eternal indeed.’
gita [3]tathā na tvaṃ nāsīḥ, kintv āsīr eva |
‘Likewise, not were you not; rather, you were indeed.’
gita [4]tathā neme janādhipā āsan
‘Likewise, these lords of men were ...’
Śaṅkara glosses na caiva na bhaviṣyāmaḥ with kintu bhaviṣyāma eva, so bhaviṣyāmaḥ is existential ‘we shall be,’ not merely ‘we shall become.’ He also expands ataḥ param as ato 'smād deha-vināśāt param uttara-kāle 'pi, fixing the sense as the later time after this body’s destruction.
→ The future clause denies future non-existence: ‘we shall all never not exist’; ataḥ param means ‘after this,’ specified as after the body’s destruction.
gita [5]tathā na caiva na bhaviṣyāmaḥ, kintu bhaviṣyāma eva, sarve vayam ato 'smād deha-vināśāt param uttara-kāle 'pi |
‘Likewise, not indeed shall we not be; rather, we shall be indeed, all of us, after this—after this destruction of the body—even in the later time.’
The translation includes ‘in Self-nature (ātma-svarūpa)’ because Śaṅkara states this as the artha of the verse. The plural is retained grammatically, but its doctrinal force is restricted by line 7: it follows empirical identification with bodies, not an intention to assert Self-difference.
→ The verse is to be understood with respect to eternal Self-nature, while the plural ‘we all’ does not imply real division of the Self.
gita [6]triṣv api kāleṣu nityā ātma-svarūpeṇa ity arthaḥ |
‘The meaning is: eternal in all three times, by Self-nature.’
gita [7]dehābhedānuvṛttyā bahu-vacanam, nātma-bhedābhiprāyeṇa ||bhgs_2.12||
‘The plural is because of the continuance of identification/non-difference with bodies, not with an intention of difference in the Self.’
The commentary does not unpack the compound. On grammar, ime janādhipāḥ is a masculine nominative plural noun phrase, and janādhipa is best analysed as janānām adhipaḥ, a genitive tatpuruṣa. It is not vocative and not taken as a proper name.
→ janādhipāḥ is a nominative plural ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa, ‘lords/rulers of men.’
