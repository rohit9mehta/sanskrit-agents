Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.27

jātasya hi dhruvo mṛtyur dhruvaṃ janma mṛtasya ca | tasmād aparihārye 'rthe na tvaṃ śocitum arhasi

Translation. For one who is born, death is certain; and for one who has died, birth is certain. Therefore, in this unavoidable matter (artha), you should not grieve.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The genitive is not taken as an abstract “of birth” but as a substantivized participle referring to a born person or being.
→ jātasya = “of one who has obtained birth / one born”
gita [1]jātasya hi labdha-janmanaḥ
“jātasya, that is, of one who has obtained birth.”
Both occurrences of dhruva are translated as “certain,” not merely “fixed” in a physical sense, because Śaṅkara glosses dhruvaḥ with avyabhicārī, “not deviating/failing.”
→ dhruvaḥ/dhruvam = “certain, invariable”
gita [1]dhruvo 'vyabhicārī mṛtyur maraṇaṃ dhruva janma mṛtasya ca
“dhruvaḥ means invariable; mṛtyuḥ is death; and birth is certain for one who has died.”
The verse’s mṛtyuḥ is ordinary death/dying, not Death personified, since the commentary glosses it by maraṇam.
→ mṛtyuḥ = “death,” specifically maraṇam
gita [1]mṛtyur maraṇaṃ
“mṛtyuḥ is maraṇam, death.”
artha is not rendered as “purpose” or “wealth,” but as the matter/state of affairs constituted by birth and death; the locative is construed with the following prohibition of grief: in such an unavoidable matter, grief is not appropriate.
→ aparihārye arthe = “in/regarding this unavoidable matter”
gita [2, 3]tasmād aparihāryo 'yaṃ janma-maraṇa-lakṣaṇo 'rthaḥ | tasminn aparihārye 'rthe na tvaṃ śocitum arhasi
“Therefore this matter, characterized by birth and death, is unavoidable. In that unavoidable matter you ought not to grieve.”
arhasi with the infinitive śocitum is modal: “you are not entitled/fit/required to grieve,” idiomatically “you should not grieve.”
→ na tvaṃ śocitum arhasi = “you should not grieve”
gita [3]na tvaṃ śocitum arhasi
“you ought not to grieve.”
The particle introduces the reason for the preceding teaching: birth entails death and death entails birth. This is decided from ordinary grammar and usage rather than an explicit gloss in the commentary.
→ hi = “for”
