Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.55

yo hy ādita eva saṃnyasya karmāṇi jñāna-yoga-niṣṭhāyāṃ pravṛttaḥ, yaś ca karma-yogena, tayoḥ prajahāti ity ārabhya ā adhyāya-parisamāpteḥ sthita-prajña-lakṣaṇaṃ sādhanaṃ copadiśyate | sarvatraiva hi adhyātma-śāstre kṛtārtha-lakṣaṇāni yāni tāny eva sādhanāni upadiśyante, yatna-sādhyatvāt | yāni yatna-sādhyāni sādhanāni lakṣaṇāni ca bhavanti tāni śrī-bhagavān uvāca-- prajahāti yadā kāmān sarvān pārtha mano-gatān | ātmany evātmanā tuṣṭaḥ sthita-prajñas tadocyate

Translation. The Blessed Lord said: When, O Pārtha, one utterly abandons all desires lodged in the mind, and is content in the Self alone, by the Self itself— then he is called one of steady wisdom (sthita-prajña).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The verb is translated with force because Śaṅkara glosses pra- as prakarṣeṇa and then gives parityajati, ‘abandons/renounces.’
→ prajahāti = “utterly abandons / gives up,” not merely “leaves” in a weak sense
gita [1]prajahāti prakarṣeṇa jahāti parityajati
‘Prajahāti’ means ‘abandons emphatically, leaves, gives up.’
The object is not translated as ‘pleasures’ or external objects, because Śaṅkara defines kāmān as icchā-bhedān, varieties of desire.
→ kāmān = all desires, specifically differentiated forms of wishing
gita [1]sarvān samastān kāmān icchā-bhedān
‘All’ means ‘entire’; ‘desires’ means ‘varieties of wishing.’
The compound is construed as manasi gatān, ‘gone into the mind,’ and Śaṅkara adds hṛdi praviṣṭān; hence ‘mind-borne’ alone would be too vague, and ‘mentally conceived’ would understate the sense of lodged internal desire.
→ manogatān = entered into / lodged in the mind, i.e. in the heart
gita [1]mano-gatān manasi praviṣṭāan hṛdi praviṣṭān
‘Mano-gatān’ means ‘entered into the mind, entered into the heart.’
The locative ātmani is read not as the body or empirical personality but as the pratyag-ātman, the inward Self, because Śaṅkara glosses it as pratyag-ātma-svarūpa.
→ ātmani eva = in the Self alone, specifically the inner Self’s own nature
gita [2]ātmany eva pratyag-ātma-svarūpe eva
‘In the Self alone’ means ‘in the very nature of the inward Self alone.’
The instrumental is construed as svenaiva, ‘by his own [self] alone,’ and the following phrase makes the contentment independent of any external gain.
→ ātmanā = by/with his own self alone; not by external acquisition
gita [2]ātmanā svenaiva bāhya-lābha-nirapekṣas tuṣṭāḥ
‘By the self’ means ‘by his own alone, content without dependence on external acquisition.’
Śaṅkara answers the implied objection that, after abandoning all desires, there would be no cause of satisfaction; he explains a positive ground of satisfaction in realization, not mere dull passivity.
→ tuṣṭaḥ = content through the nectar-like savor of realizing the highest truth
gita [2]sarva-kāma-parityāge tuṣṭi-kāraṇābhāvāt ... ity ata ucyate --- ātmany eva ... bāhya-lābha-nirapekṣas tuṣṭāḥ paramārtha-darśanāmṛta-rasa-lābhena
‘Since, when all desires are abandoned, there would seem to be no cause for satisfaction ... therefore it is said: content in the Self alone ... independent of external gain, through attaining the nectar-like savor of the vision of ultimate reality.’
The compound is a bahuvrīhi, not a simple karmadhāraya meaning ‘steady wisdom’ as an abstract quality. Śaṅkara defines it as a person possessing established wisdom born from discrimination of Self and non-Self, and identifies him as vidvān.
→ sthitaprajñaḥ = the wise person whose discriminative wisdom is established
gita [2]sthita-prajñaḥ sthitā pratiṣṭhitā ātmānātma-vivekajā prajñā yasya saḥ sthita-prajñaḥ vidvān tadā ucyate
‘Sthita-prajña’ is he whose wisdom, born of discrimination between Self and non-Self, is steady, established; he is then called a wise person.’
The preceding Śaṅkara prose states that from this verse to the end of the chapter both the marks of the sthita-prajña and the means are being taught, since in spiritual scripture the marks of the fulfilled person are also taught as disciplines because they are attainable by effort.
→ The verse describes both the accomplished mark and the discipline to be cultivated
gita [2, 3]tyakta-putra-vitta-lokaiṣaṇaḥ saṃnyāsī ātmārāma ātma-krīḍāḥ sthita-prajña ity arthaḥ
‘The meaning is: the renunciant who has abandoned desire for sons, wealth, and worlds, delighting in the Self, sporting in the Self, is the sthita-prajña.’
