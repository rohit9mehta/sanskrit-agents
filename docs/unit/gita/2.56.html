Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.56

duḥkheṣv anudvigna-manāḥ sukheṣu vigata-spṛhaḥ | vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhaḥ sthita-dhīr munir ucyate

Translation. When sufferings arise, his mind unagitated; when pleasures arise, his craving gone; with attachment, fear, and anger gone from him— he is then called a sage-renunciate (muni), one of steady wisdom (sthita-dhī).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The locative duḥkheṣu is not merely an abstract setting; Śaṅkara supplies prāpteṣu and duḥkha-prāptau, so the sense is 'when sufferings are encountered.' The compound anudvigna-manāḥ is a bahuvrīhi: the mind of this person is not udvigna, glossed as not prakṣubhita, 'not disturbed/shaken.'
→ duḥkheṣu ... anudvigna-manāḥ = 'in sufferings, when they arise, one whose mind is unagitated.'
gita [1]duḥkheṣv ādhyātmikādiṣu prāpteṣu na udvignaṃ na prakṣubhitaṃ duḥkha-prāptau mano yasya so 'yam anudvigna-manāḥ |
In sufferings such as the bodily/inner and the like, when they have arrived: the mind of whom is not agitated, not disturbed at the arrival of sorrow—this one is anudvigna-manāḥ.
Śaṅkara again supplies prāpteṣu for the locative and explicitly glosses spṛhā as tṛṣṇā, 'craving/thirst.' The fire simile rules out taking the phrase as mere indifference of mood: the point is that pleasures do not cause craving to grow.
→ sukheṣu vigata-spṛhaḥ = 'in pleasures, when they arise, one whose craving has gone.'
gita [2]tathā sukheṣu prāpteṣu vigatā spṛhā tṛṣṇā yasya, na agnir iva indhanādy-ādhāne sukhāny anu vivardhate sa vigata-spṛhaḥ |
Likewise, when pleasures have arrived, he whose spṛhā—craving—has gone; he does not grow along with pleasures like fire when fuel and the like are put on: he is vigata-spṛhaḥ.
The compound is construed as a bahuvrīhi with three separate factors—rāga, bhaya, and krodha—gone from the person, not as a chain such as 'anger caused by fear and attachment.' The cited plaintext appears unproofread: rāga/krodha lack expected visarga and vītā/vigatā likely stand for plural vītāḥ/vigatāḥ, but the yasmāt gloss makes the relation clear.
→ vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhaḥ = 'from whom attachment, fear, and anger have gone.'
gita [3]vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhaḥ rāga ca bhayaṃ ca krodha ca vītā vigatā yasmāt sa vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhaḥ |
Vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhaḥ: attachment and fear and anger have gone, have departed, from whom—he is vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhaḥ.
Śaṅkara identifies sthita-dhīḥ with sthita-prajñaḥ and glosses muniḥ as saṃnyāsī. Therefore muni is not translated merely as 'silent one' or generic inspired seer; in this bhāṣya it has the renunciate sense, and sthita-dhī is the same technical condition as stable wisdom/understanding.
→ sthita-dhīḥ muniḥ = 'a sage/renunciate of steady wisdom.'
gita [4]sthita-dhīḥ sthita-prajño muniḥ saṃnyāsī tadā ucyate ||bhgs_2.56||
Sthita-dhīḥ means sthita-prajñaḥ; muniḥ means saṃnyāsī; then he is so called.
The form ucyate is present passive third singular from √vac; the nominatives describe the designation applied to the person meeting the stated conditions.
→ ucyate = 'is called/is said to be.'
