Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.62

dhyāyato viṣayān puṃsaḥ saṅgas teṣūpajāyate | saṅgāt saṃjāyate kāmaḥ kāmāt krodho 'bhijāyate

Translation. For a person dwelling on sense-objects (viṣaya), attachment (saṅga) to them arises; from attachment desire (kāma) is born; from desire anger (krodha) is born.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
With no commentary on this unit, the syntax is decided by agreement and government. dhyāyataḥ agrees with puṃsaḥ in masculine genitive singular, while viṣayān supplies the object of the mental act. The phrase is therefore rendered “for a person dwelling on the sense-objects,” not “from meditating.” The masculine puṃsaḥ is rendered generically as “person.”
→ dhyāyataḥ and puṃsaḥ as genitive singulars: “for/of a person contemplating”; viṣayān as the accusative object of the participle

The dictionary range of viṣaya includes sphere/range, including the range of eyes, ears, mind, etc. In this psychological sequence of contemplation, attachment, desire, and anger, “sense-objects” is the appropriate contextual rendering, rather than “territories” or merely “topics.”
→ viṣayān = “sense-objects”

saṅga is construed with the locative teṣu, yielding “attachment to/in regard to them.” The ablatives saṅgāt and kāmāt mark the source or cause in the sequence: desire arises from attachment, anger from desire.
→ saṅgaḥ teṣu = “attachment to them”; saṅgāt and kāmāt as source/causal ablatives
gita []
The three ātmanepada finite verbs are read as intransitive kartari forms of √jan with prefixes upa, saṃ, and abhi. The translation therefore presents a causal arising, not a passive construction requiring an external agent: attachment arises, desire is born, anger is born.
→ upajāyate, saṃjāyate, abhijāyate as intransitive kartari forms of √jan: “arises/is born”
