Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.46

yāvān artha udapāne sarvataḥ saṃplutodake | tāvān sarveṣu vedeṣu brāhmaṇasya vijānataḥ

Translation. Whatever purpose/result (artha) there is in a water-source—a well or tank— [all that] is fulfilled in water flooded on every side; just that much [fruit], in all the Vedas—that is, in Veda-enjoined rites— is [included] for the Brāhmaṇa (brāhmaṇa), the renouncer (saṃnyāsin), who knows ultimate reality (paramārtha-tattva).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The verse is not read temporally (“as long as ... so long”) but quantitatively, since Śaṅkara glosses both correlatives with parimāṇa, measure/extent.
→ yāvān ... tāvān expresses correlative measure: “as much ... just so much.”
gita [1]yāvān yāvat-parimāṇaḥ
yāvān means “of whatever measure/extent.”
gita [2]tāvān tāvat-parimāṇa eva
tāvān means “just of that measure/extent.”
Śaṅkara glosses artha in the water example as phala and prayojana, and in the Vedic analogue as karma-phala and vijñāna-phala.
→ artha is translated as “purpose/use/result,” not as semantic “meaning,” wealth, or object.
gita [1]snāna-pānādir arthaḥ phalaṃ prayojanaṃ
the artha such as bathing and drinking is fruit, purpose.
gita [2]yo 'rthaḥ yat karma-phalaṃ
the artha there is the fruit of action.
gita [2]yo 'rthaḥ yat vijñāna-phalaṃ
the artha there is the fruit of knowledge.
The singular udapāna is not treated narrowly as one particular well; Śaṅkara explicitly expands it to many such limited-water sources.
→ udapāne denotes a limited water-source, exemplified by wells and tanks.
gita [1]kūpa-taḍāgādy-anekasmin udapāne paricchinnodake
in many water-sources such as wells and tanks, with limited water.
Śaṅkara says that all the limited purposes of wells and tanks are fulfilled in, and included within, the all-around flooded water. This rules out a commentary-blind rendering in which the point is merely that wells become useless during a flood.
→ sarvataḥ saṃplutodake is “in water flooded on every side,” the inclusive counterpart to the limited well/tank water.
gita [1]sa sarvo 'rthaḥ sarvataḥ saṃplutodake 'pi yo 'rthaḥ tāvān eva saṃpadyate, tatra antarbhavatīty arthaḥ
all that purpose is fulfilled as just that much also in the water flooded on every side; the meaning is that it is included there.
gita [2]vijñāna-phalaṃ sarvataḥ saṃplutodaka-sthānīyaṃ
the fruit of knowledge occupies the position of the all-around flooded water.
Śaṅkara construes the locative not merely as the textual Vedas or sacred lore in general, but as the karmas taught by the Vedas and their fruits.
→ sarveṣu vedeṣu is interpreted as “in all Veda-enjoined rites,” and their artha as karma-phala.
gita [2]sarveṣu vedeṣu vedokteṣu karmasu yo 'rthaḥ yat karma-phalaṃ
the artha in all the Vedas, that is, in the rites taught by the Vedas, is the fruit of action.
Śaṅkara identifies the brāhmaṇa here with a saṃnyāsin and specifies the object of knowing as paramārtha-tattva, ultimate reality.
→ brāhmaṇasya vijānataḥ means “for/of the Brāhmaṇa, the renouncer, who knows ultimate reality.”
gita [2]brāhmaṇasya saṃnyāsinaḥ paramārtha-tattvaṃ vijānataḥ
of the Brāhmaṇa, the renouncer, who knows the truth of ultimate reality.
Although the knower’s knowledge-fruit includes the fruits of Veda-enjoined rites, Śaṅkara adds that before eligibility for steadfast knowledge, one who is qualified for karma must still perform karma.
→ The verse is not a blanket rejection of karma for everyone.
gita [5]tasmāt prāk jñāna-niṣṭhādhikāra-prāpteḥ karmaṇy adhikṛtena kūpa-taḍāgādy-artha-sthānīyam api karma kartavyam
therefore, before attaining eligibility for steadfastness in knowledge, karma—though occupying the place of the purposes of wells, tanks, and the like—must be done by one qualified for karma.
