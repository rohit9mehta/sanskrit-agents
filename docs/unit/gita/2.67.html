Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.67

indriyāṇāṃ hi caratāṃ yan mano 'nuvidhīyate | tad asya harati prajñāṃ vāyur nāvam ivāmbhasi

Translation. For the mind (manas) that follows after the senses (indriyāṇi), as they range in their own objects, that mind carries away this striver’s discriminative wisdom (prajñā), as the wind carries a boat on the waters.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The verse is introduced as giving the reason for the preceding point, not merely as an emphatic “indeed.”
→ hi is causal: “for/because.”
gita [1]indriyāṇāṃ hi yasmāt
“indriyāṇām hi — because...”
The genitive plural participle caratām modifies indriyāṇām, and Śaṅkara specifies the field of their movement as sva-sva-viṣayeṣu.
→ indriyāṇāṃ caratām means “of the senses as they range/proceed in their own respective objects.”
gita [1]caratāṃ sva-sva-viṣayeṣu pravartamānānām
“moving/proceeding in their own respective objects.”
Although the form is passive, the commentary glosses its contextual sense with anupravartate, so the translation should not be a bare “is regulated” or “is assigned.”
→ anuvidhīyate is translated by sense as “follows after / is led to conform to.”
gita [1]yat mano 'nuvidhīyate anupravartate
“the mind which is anuvidhīyate — follows along after.”
Śaṅkara explicitly expands tat as the mind set in motion by sense-object construction, and later says that the mind, having carried away self-directed wisdom, makes it sense-object-directed.
→ tat refers to the mind, not to a sense or sense-object; that mind is the subject of harati.
gita [1]tat indriya-viṣaya-vikalpanena pravṛttaṃ mano 'sya yateḥ harati prajñām
“that mind, set in motion by the construction of sense-objects, carries away this striver’s wisdom.”
gita [2]evam ātma-viṣayāṃ prajñāṃ hṛtvā mano viṣaya-viṣayāṃ karoti
“so the mind, having carried away the wisdom whose object is the Self, makes it have sense-objects as its object.”
The pronoun is not left as a vague “his”; Śaṅkara supplies yateḥ as the possessor whose wisdom is carried away.
→ asya means “of this striver/ascetic.”
gita [1]asya yateḥ
“of this yati/striver.”
Śaṅkara glosses prajñā not as general intelligence but as ātmānātma-vivekajā and ātma-viṣayā.
→ prajñā is discriminative wisdom concerning the Self, born from self/not-self discrimination.
gita [1]prajñām ātmānātma-vivekajāṃ
“wisdom born of discrimination between Self and not-Self.”
gita [2]ātma-viṣayāṃ prajñāṃ
“wisdom whose object is the Self.”
The main verb preserves the boat simile’s “carry away” sense, but Śaṅkara adds nāśayati and explains the carrying away as a redirection of wisdom toward sense-objects.
→ harati is “carries away,” with the destructive effect of ruining/diverting wisdom.
gita [1]harati prajñām ātmānātma-vivekajāṃ nāśayati
“it carries away the wisdom born of self/not-self discrimination — destroys it.”
gita [2]prajñāṃ hṛtvā mano viṣaya-viṣayāṃ karoti
“the mind, having carried away the wisdom, makes it directed to sense-objects.”
Śaṅkara glosses ambhasi as udake and expands the simile: wind takes a boat from the desired path and propels it onto a wrong path.
→ The simile is a wind driving a boat on water off its intended course.
gita [2]vāyuḥ nāvam iva ambhasi udake jigamiṣatāṃ mārgāduddhṛtya unmārge yathā vāyuḥ nāvaṃ pravartayati
“as the wind, in water, taking a boat away from the route of those wishing to go, propels the boat onto a wrong course.”
