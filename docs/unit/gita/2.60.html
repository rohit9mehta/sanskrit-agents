Translation. For, O Kaunteya, the turbulent senses (indriyāṇi) forcibly carry off the mind (manas) of a striving man— even of a discerning one.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The form is not taken as ablative or as an unattached adverbial 'while striving'; Śaṅkara glosses it with the genitive participial expression prayatnaṃ kurvataḥ and places it in the same genitive chain as puruṣasya vipaścitaḥ.
→ yatataḥ is a genitive singular participle qualifying puruṣasya: 'of a man who is striving'.
gita [1]yatataḥ prayatnaṃ kurvataḥ hi yasmāt kaunteya puruṣasya vipaścitaḥ medhāvino 'pi iti vyavahitena saṃbandhaḥ
yatataḥ means 'of one making effort'; for, because, O Kaunteya, [it is] of the man, of the wise one; 'api' is connected despite separation.
The commentary explicitly supplies yasmāt for hi, so the verse is introduced as the reason for the preceding instruction.
→ hi is causal: 'for' or 'because'.
gita [1]hi yasmāt
hi means 'because'.
Although api is printed near yatataḥ, Śaṅkara states that it is connected across intervening words with medhāvinaḥ, his gloss for vipaścitaḥ. The translation therefore makes the concessive force fall on the person's discernment, not only on the fact of striving.
→ api has scope with vipaścitaḥ/medhāvinaḥ: 'even of a discerning/wise one'.
gita [1]puruṣasya vipaścitaḥ medhāvino 'pi iti vyavahitena saṃbandhaḥ
[It is] of the man, of the wise one even; this is the connection despite intervening words.
Śaṅkara glosses vipaścitaḥ as medhāvinaḥ and further describes the mind as connected with discriminative knowledge; this supports an epistemic-discriminative sense.
→ vipaścitaḥ is rendered 'discerning', i.e. wise/intelligent, not merely 'learned'.
gita [1]vipaścitaḥ medhāvinaḥ
vipaścit means 'wise/intelligent'.
gita [2]prakāśam eva paśyato viveka-vijñāna-yuktaṃ manaḥ
the mind endowed with discriminative knowledge, of one who is plainly seeing.
The bhāṣya defines pramāthīni as pramathana-śīlāni and explains their action as vikṣobhayanti and ākulīkurvanti, so the adjective is active and disruptive, not a neutral 'restless'.
→ pramāthīni means 'turbulent/agitating', senses whose nature is to churn or disturb.
gita [2]indriyāṇi pramāthīni pramathana-śīlāni viṣayābhimukhaṃ hi puruṣaṃ vikṣobhayanti ākulīkurvanti
The senses are pramāthīni, having the nature of agitation; they disturb and throw into confusion a person turned toward objects.
The commentary repeats the syntactic frame with the senses as those that disturb and carry off, and with manaḥ as what is carried off. This fixes the otherwise formally ambiguous neuter manaḥ as accusative.
→ indriyāṇi pramāthīni is the nominative subject of haranti, and manaḥ is the accusative object.
gita [2]indriyāṇi pramāthīni pramathana-śīlāni ... ākulīkṛtya ca haranti ... manaḥ
The senses, being agitating by nature, having thrown [him] into confusion, carry off the mind.
Śaṅkara glosses prasabham directly with prasahya, so the translation uses 'by force/forcibly', not a weaker 'suddenly' or 'openly'.
→ prasabham is an adverb meaning 'forcibly'.
gita [2]haranti prasabhaṃ prasahya
They carry off prasabham, that is, forcibly.
