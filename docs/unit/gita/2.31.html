How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Translate svadharmam not as an abstract ‘personal religion’ but as Arjuna’s own class-duty: for a kṣatriya, battle. The accusative is the object of avekṣya, ‘having regard to/considering.’
→ svadharma = the kṣatriya’s own duty, namely battle
gita [1]svadharmam api svo dharmaḥ kṣatriyasya yuddhaṃ tam api avekṣya
‘svadharmam api’: one’s own dharma; for a kṣatriya it is battle—considering even that.
The finite arhasi with the infinitive has normative force; the negation is not a simple prediction but ‘you ought not / it is not proper for you.’ Vikampitum is taken as ‘to waver, be shaken,’ glossed by pracalitum.
→ na vikampitum arhasi = you ought not to waver
gita [1]tvaṃ na vikampituṃ pracalitum nārhasi
You ought not to waver, to be unsettled.
The reason svadharma bears on Arjuna is that it is the kṣatriya’s natural/innate dharma. This supports ‘your own duty’ rather than merely a chosen or private obligation.
→ the duty is rooted in kṣatriya nature
gita [1]kṣatriyasya svābhāvikād dharmād ātma-svābhāvyād ity abhiprāyaḥ
The purport is: because of the kṣatriya’s natural dharma, because of his own nature.
Dharmya qualifies yuddha and is not a mere label for warfare generally: this battle is ‘not departed from dharma’ because it serves dharma and the protection of subjects through conquest of the earth.
→ dharmya yuddha = righteous battle, not any battle as such
gita [2]tac ca yuddhaṃ pṛthivī-jaya-dvāreṇa dharmārthaṃ prajā-rakṣaṇārthaṃ ceti dharmād anapetaṃ paraṃ dharmyam
And that battle, through the conquest of the earth, is for the sake of dharma and for the protection of subjects; thus it has not fallen away from dharma and is supremely righteous.
The second half means ‘no other better good exists for a kṣatriya than righteous battle.’ The paired ablatives dharmyāt yuddhāt supply the comparison governed by śreyaḥ/anyat, and kṣatriyasya is the genitive of the person for whom this is so.
→ dharmyād yuddhāt is the ablative standard of comparison for śreyaḥ anyat
gita [3]tasmād dharmyād yuddhāc chreyo 'nyat kṣatriyasya na vidyate hi yasmāt
For, than that righteous battle, no other better thing is found for a kṣatriya.
The particle hi introduces the reason for the preceding injunction not to waver.
→ hi = for/because
gita [3]hi yasmāt
hi: because/for.
