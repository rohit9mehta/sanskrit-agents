Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.42

yām imāṃ puṣpitāṃ vācaṃ pravadanty avipaścitaḥ | veda-vāda-ratāḥ pārtha nānyad astīti vādinaḥ

Translation. That flowery speech—pleasing to hear—which the undiscerning proclaim, O Pārtha: delighting in Veda-speech (veda-vāda), saying, “There is nothing else [than works that bring fruits such as heaven].”

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The relative-demonstrative phrase is not taken as an indefinite ‘whatever speech,’ but as pointing to the specific doctrine described here and in the following verse.
→ yām imām refers to ‘this speech which is about to be stated,’ with vācam as the object of pravadanti.
gita [1]yām imāṃ vakṣyamāṇāṃ puṣpitāṃ puṣpita iva vṛkṣaḥ śobhamānāṃ śrūyamāṇa-ramaṇīyāṃ vācaṃ vākya-lakṣaṇāṃ pravadanti |
‘This, which is about to be stated, flowery—shining like a flowering tree, lovely when heard—speech, characterized as sentences, they proclaim.’
The adjective ‘flowery’ is interpreted rhetorically as showy and pleasing to hear; vāc is specified as statement/discourse.
→ puṣpitāṃ vācam means attractive, ornate, pleasant-sounding discourse, not literally a flowered physical voice.
gita [1]puṣpitāṃ puṣpita iva vṛkṣaḥ śobhamānāṃ śrūyamāṇa-ramaṇīyāṃ vācaṃ vākya-lakṣaṇāṃ
‘Flowery: shining like a flowering tree, delightful to hear; speech: having the nature of sentences.’
The word is a privative adjective: ‘not vipaścit,’ glossed by Śaṅkara as lacking intelligence and discrimination.
→ avipaścitaḥ means the undiscerning or unintelligent.
gita [2]ke ? avipaścito 'medhaso 'vivekina ity arthaḥ |
‘Who? The avipaścits—unintelligent, undiscriminating—that is the meaning.’
The compound is not ‘fond of debate about the Veda’; Śaṅkara glosses veda-vāda as veda-vākya, Vedic sentences, and ratāḥ as attached to them.
→ veda-vāda-ratāḥ means those attached to Vedic statements, especially passages proclaiming ritual fruits and means.
gita [3]veda-vāda-ratāḥ bahvy-artha-vāda-phala-sādhana-prakāśakeṣu veda-vākyeṣu ratāḥ he pārtha
‘Veda-vāda-ratāḥ: attached to Vedic sentences that reveal abundant arthavāda, fruit, and means to fruit, O Pārtha.’
The scope of ‘nothing else’ is supplied by the commentary: it is not an absolute metaphysical denial, but an insistence on karmic means to heavenly and similar fruits as all that matters.
→ nānyad astīti vādinaḥ means those who habitually say, ‘There is nothing else,’ namely nothing beyond works that are means to fruits such as heaven.
gita [3]na anyat svarga-prāpty-ādi-phala-sādhanebhyaḥ karmabhyo 'sti ity evaṃ vādinaḥ vadana-śīlāḥ
‘There is nothing other than actions that are means to fruits such as attaining heaven—those speaking thus, habitually saying so.’
