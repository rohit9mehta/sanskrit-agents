Bhagavad-Gītā with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya · 2.21

Translation. He who knows this [Self] as imperishable, eternal, unborn, and undecaying— how could that knowing person, O Pārtha, cause anyone to be killed, or kill anyone?

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Śaṅkara's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The semantic value is not ‘has known’ in a temporally prior sense, but ‘knows/understands.’
→ veda is translated as present ‘knows,’ while retained morphologically as a perfect form of √vid with present value.
gita [1]veda vijānāti avināśinam antya-bhāva-vikāra-rahitaṃ nityaṃ vipariṇāma-rahitaṃ yo veda iti saṃbandhaḥ
‘veda’ means ‘understands’; the construction is ‘he who knows [it as] imperishable, free from the final state-change, eternal, free from transformation.’
Translate ‘this [Self]’ for enam, because Śaṅkara identifies it as the entity with the characteristics stated in the prior mantra/verse and later explicitly treats the subject as the avikriya ātmā.
→ enam refers to the Self characterized in the immediately preceding teaching, not to the body or to an ordinary person.
gita [2]enaṃ pūrveṇa matreṇokta-lakṣaṇam ajaṃ janma-rahitam avyayam apakṣaya-rahitaṃ
‘this one,’ having the characteristics stated by the previous mantra/verse, unborn—free from birth—and undecaying—free from diminution.
gita [13]ataḥ pāriśeṣyāt asaṃhata ātmā vidvān avikriyaḥ iti tasya viduṣaḥ karmāsaṃbhavāt ākṣepo yuktaḥ kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ iti
Therefore, by elimination, the uncompounded Self is the knower and is changeless; since action is impossible for that knower, the rhetorical objection ‘how could that person…?’ is fitting.
Śaṅkara glosses each epithet by specifying the absence of a particular transformation or loss.
→ avināśinam, nityam, ajam, and avyayam are technical negations of change: imperishable, eternal, unborn, undecaying.
gita [1]avināśinam antya-bhāva-vikāra-rahitaṃ nityaṃ vipariṇāma-rahitaṃ
Imperishable: free from the final change of state; eternal: free from transformation.
gita [2]ajaṃ janma-rahitam avyayam apakṣaya-rahitaṃ
Unborn: free from birth; undecaying: free from diminution.
Render ‘how could…?’ with the force ‘in no way can…,’ because Śaṅkara says the question-sense is impossible and that ākṣepa is intended in both halves.
→ katham introduces a rhetorical denial, not an information-seeking question.
gita [3]na kathaṃcit kaṃcit hanti, na kathaṃcit kaṃcit ghātayati iti ubhayatra ākṣepaḥ evārthaḥ praśnārthāsaṃbhavāt
He does not in any way kill anyone, nor in any way cause anyone to be killed; in both places the meaning is rhetorical denial, since a question-sense is impossible.
Śaṅkara distinguishes hanti as doing the act of killing from ghātayati as instigating a killer; he later names this as hetu-kartṛtva, causative agency.
→ ghātayati is causative: ‘causes to kill’ or ‘causes to be killed,’ not merely a second synonym of hanti.
gita [2]hanti hanana-kriyāṃ karoti, kathaṃ vā ghātayati hantāraṃ prayojayati
‘hanti’ means he performs the act of killing; or how does he ‘ghātayati,’ that is, instigate a killer?
gita [40, 43]atra ca ātmanaḥ hanana-kriyāyāḥ kartṛtvaṃ karmatvaṃ hetu-kartṛtvaṃ ca ajñāna-kṛtaṃ darśitam | tad etat aviśeṣeṇa viduṣaḥ sarva-kriyāsu kartṛtvaṃ hetu-kartṛtvaṃ ca pratiṣedhati bhagavān vāsudevo viduṣaḥ karmādhikārābhāva-pradarśanārthaṃ vedāvināśinaṃ, kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ ity ādinā
Here agency, objecthood, and causative agency regarding the Self in the act of killing are shown to be produced by ignorance. By ‘vedāvināśinam… kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ…’ Bhagavān Vāsudeva prohibits, without distinction, the knower’s agency and causative agency in all actions, to show the absence of eligibility for ritual/action.
The subject of the second half is not any man whatsoever but the person who knows the Self under these descriptions.
→ puruṣaḥ is the vidvān puruṣaḥ, ‘the knowing person,’ correlated with yaḥ.
gita [2]kathaṃ kena prakāreṇa sa vidvān puruṣo 'dhikṛtaḥ hanti hanana-kriyāṃ karoti, kathaṃ vā ghātayati hantāraṃ prayojayati
How, in what manner, does that knowing person, the one under discussion, perform the act of killing; or how does he instigate a killer?
Although formally interrogative ‘whom?’, Śaṅkara’s paraphrase kaṃcit in both halves supports ‘anyone’ in English after the rhetorical negative force.
→ kaṃ is masculine ‘anyone’ in both occurrences under the denial.
gita [3]na kathaṃcit kaṃcit hanti, na kathaṃcit kaṃcit ghātayati
He does not in any way kill anyone; he does not in any way cause anyone to be killed.
Do not translate the verse as licensing killing or as concerning only violence; in the bhāṣya, hanti is an example used to show the impossibility of agency and causative agency for the knower generally.
→ The immediate example is killing, but Śaṅkara takes the intended scope to be the knower’s non-agency in all actions.
gita [4, 5]hetv-arthasya ca avikriyatvasya tulyatvāt viduṣaḥ sarva-karma-pratiṣedha eva prakaraṇārtho 'bhipreto bhagavatā | hantes tu ākṣepaḥ udāharaṇārthatvena kathitaḥ
Because the reason, changelessness, is the same, the contextual meaning intended by Bhagavān is precisely the prohibition of all action for the knower; the rhetorical denial concerning ‘killing’ is stated as an example.
gita [43]tad etat aviśeṣeṇa viduṣaḥ sarva-kriyāsu kartṛtvaṃ hetu-kartṛtvaṃ ca pratiṣedhati bhagavān vāsudevo viduṣaḥ karmādhikārābhāva-pradarśanārthaṃ vedāvināśinaṃ, kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ ity ādinā
Thus Bhagavān Vāsudeva, by ‘vedāvināśinam… kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ…,’ prohibits without distinction the knower’s agency and causative agency in all actions, to show the knower’s lack of eligibility for action.

Śaṅkara's commentary on this unit

1veda vijānāti avināśinam antya-bhāva-vikāra-rahitaṃ nityaṃ vipariṇāma-rahitaṃ yo veda iti saṃbandhaḥ |
2enaṃ pūrveṇa matreṇokta-lakṣaṇam ajaṃ janma-rahitam avyayam apakṣaya-rahitaṃ kathaṃ kena prakāreṇa sa vidvān puruṣo 'dhikṛtaḥ hanti hanana-kriyāṃ karoti, kathaṃ vā ghātayati hantāraṃ prayojayati |
3na kathaṃcit kaṃcit hanti, na kathaṃcit kaṃcit ghātayati iti ubhayatra ākṣepaḥ evārthaḥ praśnārthāsaṃbhavāt |
4hetv-arthasya ca avikriyatvasya tulyatvāt viduṣaḥ sarva-karma-pratiṣedha eva prakaraṇārtho 'bhipreto bhagavatā |
5hantes tu ākṣepaḥ udāharaṇārthatvena kathitaḥ |
6viduṣaḥ kaṃ karmāsaṃbhave hetu-viśeṣaṃ paśyan karmāṇy ākṣipati bhagavān kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ iti |
7nanu ukta evātmano 'vikriyatvaṃ sarva-karmāsaṃbhava-kāraṇa-viśeṣaḥ |
8satyam uktaḥ |
9na tu sa kāraṇa-viśeṣaḥ, anyatvāt viduṣo 'vikriyād ātmanaḥ |
10na hi avikriyaṃ sthāṇuṃ viditavataḥ karma na saṃbhavati iti cet, na |
11viduṣaḥ ātmatvāt |
12na dehādi-saṃghātasya vidvattā |
13ataḥ pāriśeṣyāt asaṃhata ātmā vidvān avikriyaḥ iti tasya viduṣaḥ karmāsaṃbhavāt ākṣepo yuktaḥ kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ iti |
14yathā buddhyādy āhṛtasya śabdādy-arthasya avikriya eva san buddhi-vṛtty-aviveka-vijñānena avidyayā upalabdhā ātmā kalpyate, evam eva ātmānātma-viveka-jñānena buddhi-vṛttyā vidyā asatya-rūpayaiva paramārthato 'vikriya eva ātmā vidvān ucyate |
15viduṣaḥ karmāsaṃbhava-vacanāt yāni karmāṇi śāstreṇa vidhīyante tāni aviduṣo vihitāni iti bhagavato niścayo 'vagamyate ||
16nanu vidyāpi aviduṣa eva vidhīyate, vidita-vidyasya piṣṭa-peṣaṇavat vidyā-vidhānānarthakyāt |
17tatra aviduṣaḥ karmāṇi vidhīyante na viduṣaḥ iti viśeṣo nopapadyate iti cet, na |
18anuṣṭheyasya bhāvābhāva-viśeṣopapatteḥ |
19agnihotrādi-vidhy-artha-jñānottara-kālam agnihotrādi-karma aneka-sādhanopasaṃhāra-pūrvakam anuṣṭheyaṃ kartā aham, mama kartavyam ity evaṃ prakāra-vijñānavato 'viduṣaḥ yathā anuṣṭheyaṃ bhavati, na tu tathā na jāyate ity ādy ātma-svarupa-vidhy-artha-jñānottara-kāla-bhāvi kiṃcid anuṣṭheyaṃ bhavati |
20kintu nāhaṃ kartā, nāhaṃ bhoktā ity ādy ātmaikatvākartṛtvādi-viṣaya-jñānāt nānyad utpadyate iti eṣa viśeṣa upapadyate |
21yaḥ punaḥ kartā aham iti vetti ātmānam, tasya mama idaṃ kartavyam iti avaśyaṃbhāvinī buddhiḥ syāt |
22tad-apekṣayā so 'dhikriyate iti taṃ prati karmāṇi saṃbhavanti |
23sa ca avidvān ubhau tau na vijānītaḥ iti vacanāt viśeṣitasya ca viduṣaḥ karmākṣepa-vacanāt kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ iti |
24tasmāt viśeṣitasya avikriyātma-darśinaḥ viduṣaḥ mumukṣo ca sarva-karma-saṃnyāse eva adhikāraḥ |
