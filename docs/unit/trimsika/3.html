asaṃviditakopādisthānavijñaptikañ ca tat sadā sparśamanaskāravitsaṃjñācetanānvitam

Translation. And that storehouse consciousness (ālayavijñāna) has unapprehended appropriation (upādi) and an unapprehended place-presentation (sthānavijñapti); for as long as it is, it is accompanied by contact (sparśa), attention (manaskāra), feeling (vedanā), recognition (saṃjñā), and volition (cetanā).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Do not read kopādi as kopa + ādi (‘anger etc.’). Read -ko- as the sandhi of asaṃviditaka + upādi, and let asaṃviditaka qualify both upādi and sthānavijñapti as Sthiramati states.
→ asaṃviditakopādisthānavijñaptikam is a possessive/bahuvrīhi description of ālayavijñāna: it has an unapprehended upādi and an unapprehended sthānavijñapti.
trbh [203, 204]asaṃviditaka upādir yasmin asaṃviditakā ca sthānavijñaptir yasmin tad ālayavijñānam asaṃviditakopādisthānavijñaptikam
‘That ālaya-consciousness in which the upādi is unapprehended and in which the place-presentation is unapprehended is asaṃviditakopādisthānavijñaptikam.’
Use Sthiramati’s upādāna-gloss rather than dictionary guesses such as ‘anger’, ‘affliction’, or a merely spatial ‘basis’.
→ upādi is translated as ‘appropriation’ or ‘taking-up’.
trbh [205, 206, 207, 208]upādānam upādiḥ sa punar ātmādivikalpavāsanā rūpādidharmavikalpavāsanā ca tatsadbhāvād ālayavijñānenātmādivikalpo rūpādivikalpaś ca kāryatvenopātta iti tad vāsanātmādivikalpānāṃ rūpādivikalpānāñ copādir ity ucyate
‘Upādi is upādāna, taking-up/appropriation. It is the latent impression of constructions of self and so on, and the latent impression of constructions of form and so on; because these exist, the constructions of self and form are taken up by ālaya-consciousness as effects. Therefore it is called the upādi of those constructions.’
trbh [212, 213, 214]āśrayopādānañ copādiḥ āśraya ātmabhāvaḥ sādhiṣṭhānam indriyarūpaṃ nāma ca tasya punar yad upādānam upagamanam ekayogakṣematvena tad upādiḥ
‘Appropriation of the basis is also upādi. The basis is the embodied stream, with its seat, material sense-faculties, and name; its taking-up, its entering into a common risk-and-security, is upādi.’
The verse does not deny that ālayavijñāna functions; it says that its appropriation and place-presentation are not apprehended in the relevant reflective/determinate way.
→ asaṃvidita is rendered ‘unapprehended’ or ‘not consciously apprehended’.
trbh [210, 211]tad iti pratisaṃvedanākāreṇāsaṃvidita ity atas tad asaṃviditakopādir ity ucyate
‘Therefore, since it is not apprehended in the mode of reflexive experience as “that”, it is called asaṃviditakopādi.’
trbh [220, 221]tat punar upādānam idantayā pratisaṃvedayitum aśakyam ity ato 'saṃvidita ity ucyate
‘That appropriation cannot be reflexively apprehended as “this”; therefore it is called unapprehended.’
trbh [223, 224]sāpy aparicchinnālambanākārapravṛttatvād asaṃviditety ucyate
‘That too is called unapprehended because it proceeds with an undetermined object-aspect.’
Do not reduce sthāna to a generic ‘place’ or vijñapti to a bare act of knowing; Sthiramati identifies the compound with bhājanalokasaṃniveśavijñapti.
→ sthānavijñapti is translated as ‘place-presentation’, specifically the presentation of the receptacle-world’s configuration.
trbh [222]sthānavijñaptir bhājanalokasaṃniveśavijñaptiḥ
‘The place-presentation is the presentation of the arrangement/configuration of the receptacle-world.’
trbh [223, 224]sāpy aparicchinnālambanākārapravṛttatvād asaṃviditety ucyate
‘That too is called unapprehended because it proceeds with an undetermined object-aspect.’
Supply the antecedent ālayavijñāna in the translation; the commentary explicitly makes it the subject of the verse’s predicates.
→ tat is ‘that storehouse consciousness (ālayavijñāna)’.
trbh [203, 204]asaṃviditaka upādir yasmin asaṃviditakā ca sthānavijñaptir yasmin tad ālayavijñānam asaṃviditakopādisthānavijñaptikam
‘That ālaya-consciousness in which the upādi is unapprehended and in which the place-presentation is unapprehended is asaṃviditakopādisthānavijñaptikam.’
trbh [231, 232, 233, 234, 235, 236, 237]tatrālayākhyaṃ vijñānam ity uktaṃ vijñānañ cāvaśyaṃ caittaiḥ saṃprayuktam ity ato vaktavyaṃ katamaiḥ katibhiś ca tebhyaḥ saṃprayujyate tathā kiṃ taiḥ sarvadā saṃprayujyate uta nety ata āha sadā sparśamanaskāravitsaṃjñācetanānvitam
‘Here the consciousness called ālaya has been stated. Since consciousness is necessarily associated with mental factors, it must be said with which and with how many it is associated, and whether it is always associated with them or not; therefore he says: “always accompanied by contact, attention, feeling, recognition, and volition.”’
Render ‘always’ with its commentary-given scope, and take the final compound as answering which caittas accompany ālayavijñāna.
→ sadā means throughout the duration of ālayavijñāna’s occurrence; anvitam means accompanied/associated with five universal mental factors.
trbh [239, 240, 241]sadeti yāvad ālayavijñānaṃ tāvad ebhiḥ sparśamanaskāravedanāsaṃjñācetanākhyaiḥ pañcabhiḥ sarvatragair dharmair anvitam
‘“Always” means: for as long as there is ālaya-consciousness, for that long it is accompanied by these five universal factors called contact, attention, feeling, recognition, and volition.’
trbh [497, 498, 499, 500]tathā hi sadāsparśamanaskāravitsaṃjñācetanānvitam iti prathamato nirdiṣṭāḥ sparśa eṣām ādir iti sparśādayaḥ
‘For thus, “always accompanied by contact, attention, feeling, recognition, and volition”: they were first stated; since contact is their first, they are “contact and the rest.”’
Translate the member vit by the technical factor vedanā, because Sthiramati explicitly equates them and then discusses which of the three vedanās is meant.
→ vit is ‘feeling (vedanā)’, not ‘knower’ or ‘knowledge’.
trbh [242]vedanā vit
‘vit is vedanā.’
trbh [302, 303]tatrālayavijñāne vid iti sāmānyopadeśena na vijñāyate tisṝṇāṃ vedanānāṃ katamā vedanā
‘Here, in the case of ālaya-consciousness, because “vid” is a general statement, it is not known which of the three feelings is the feeling meant.’
trbh [268, 269, 270, 271, 272, 273, 274]vedanā anubhavasvabhāvā sā punar viṣayasyāhlādakaparitāpakatadubhayākāraviviktasvarūpasākṣātkaraṇabhedāt tridhā bhavati sukhā duḥkhā aduḥkhāsukhā ca
‘Feeling has the nature of experience. It is threefold according to the direct realization of the object’s pleasant, painful, or neither-of-the-two character: pleasant, painful, and neither-painful-nor-pleasant.’
Use English terms guided by the bhāṣya’s functional definitions of the mental factors, not merely by broad dictionary meanings.
→ The five terms are rendered as contact (sparśa), attention (manaskāra), feeling (vedanā), recognition (saṃjñā), and volition (cetanā).
trbh [243, 244, 245, 246, 247, 248, 249]tatra sparśas trikasaṃnipāte indriyavikāraparicchedaḥ vedanāsaṃniśrayadānakarmakaḥ indriyaviṣayavijñānāni trīṇy eva trikam tasya kāryakāraṇabhāvena samavasthānaṃ trikasaṃnipātaḥ tasmin sati tatsamakālam viṣayasya sukhādivedanīyākāraparicchedo yaḥ sa sparśaḥ
‘Contact, when the triad has come together, is the discernment of a modification of the faculty and has the function of providing the support for feeling. The triad is precisely faculty, object, and consciousness; their standing together as cause and effect is the conjunction of the triad. When that occurs, the contemporaneous discernment of the object’s character as to-be-felt as pleasant and so on is contact.’
trbh [258, 259, 260, 261, 262, 263, 264]manaskāraś cetasa ābhogaḥ ābhujanam ābhogaḥ ālambane yena cittam abhimukhīkriyate sa punar ālambane cittadhāraṇakarmakaḥ cittadhāraṇaṃ punas tatraivālambane punaḥ punaś cittasyāvarjanam
‘Attention is the mind’s turning-toward; turning is that by which the mind is made face-to-face with the object. It has the function of holding the mind on the object, that is, repeatedly drawing the mind back to that very object.’
trbh [291, 292, 293, 294, 295, 296]saṃjñā viṣayanimittodgrahaṇam viṣaya ālambanam nimittaṃ tadviśeṣo nīlapītādyālambanavyavasthākāraṇam tasyodgrahaṇaṃ nirūpaṇaṃ nīlam etan na pītam iti
‘Recognition is the grasping of the object’s sign. The object is the ālambana; the sign is its particular feature, the basis for determining an object as blue, yellow, and so on. Grasping it is determination: “this is blue, not yellow.”’
trbh [297, 298]cetanā cittābhisaṃskāro manasaś ceṣṭā yasyāṃ satyām ālambanaṃ prati cetasaḥ praspanda iva bhavaty ayaskāntavaśād ayaḥpraspandavat
‘Volition is the mind’s conditioning activity, the movement of manas: when it is present, there is as it were a stirring of the mind toward the object, like the stirring of iron under the power of a magnet.’
