Triṃśikā (Vasubandhu) with Sthiramati's bhāṣya · 11

alobhāditrayaṃ vīryaṃ praśrabdhiḥ sāpramādikā | āhiṃsā kuśalāḥ kleśā rāgapratighamūḍhayaḥ

Translation. The triad beginning with non-greed (alobha)—non-greed, non-hatred (adveṣa), non-delusion (amoha)—energy in the wholesome (vīrya), pliancy (praśrabdhi), equanimity (upekṣā) together with heedfulness (apramāda), and non-harming (ahiṃsā, glossed avihiṃsā): these complete the wholesome factors (kuśala). The afflictions (kleśa) are attachment (rāga), aversion (pratigha), and delusion (mūḍhi).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
I do not take kuśalāḥ as an isolated adjective meaning skilled, nor as forming a phrase with kleśāḥ. Sthiramati explicitly supplies dharmāḥ and later closes the section as the eleven kuśalas.
→ kuśalāḥ means the wholesome dharmas/factors, with dharmāḥ supplied; the listed singular items are part of an eleven-factor class.
trbh [577]eta ekādaśa dharmā iti vākyaśeṣaḥ
The sentence is to be completed: these are eleven dharmas.
trbh [679]uktā ekādaśa kuśalāḥ
The eleven wholesome ones have been stated.
The compound is not left as a vague etcetera; the commentary’s sequence defines the three members immediately and distinctly.
→ alobhāditrayaṃ is the triad beginning with non-greed: alobha, adveṣa, and amoha.
trbh [601, 602, 603, 604, 605]alobho lobhapratipakṣaḥ lobho nāma bhave bhavopakaraṇeṣu ca yāsaktiḥ prārthanā ca tatpratipakṣo 'lobho bhave bhavopakaraṇeṣu vānāsaktiḥ vaimukhyañ ca
Non-greed is the antidote to greed. Greed is attachment and longing for existence and the equipment of existence; its opposite, non-greed, is non-attachment and turning away from them.
trbh [607, 608, 609, 610, 611]adveṣo dveṣapratipakṣo maitrī dveṣo hi sattveṣu duḥkhe duḥkhasthānīyeṣu ca dharmeṣv āghātaḥ adveṣo dveṣapratipakṣatvāt sattveṣu duḥkhe duḥkhasthānīyeṣu ca dharmeṣv anāghātaḥ
Non-hatred is the antidote to hatred, friendliness. Since hatred is hostility toward beings, suffering, and things connected with suffering, non-hatred is non-hostility toward them.
trbh [613, 614, 615, 616, 617]amoho mohapratipakṣo yathābhūtasaṃpratipattiḥ mohaḥ karmaphalasatyaratneṣv ajñānam mohapratipakṣatvād amohas teṣv eva karmaphalasatyaratneṣu saṃpratipattiḥ
Non-delusion is the antidote to delusion, comprehension as things really are. Delusion is ignorance regarding karma, its fruits, the truths, and the jewels; non-delusion is comprehension regarding those very things.
I translate vīrya as energy in the wholesome, not as mere strength, heroism, or any kind of zeal, because Sthiramati excludes zeal in defiled states.
→ vīrya is wholesome energy or exertion of mind toward the wholesome.
trbh [619, 620, 621, 622, 623, 624]vīryaṃ kausīdyapratipakṣaḥ kuśale cetaso 'bhyutsāhaḥ na tu kliṣṭe kliṣṭe tūtsāhaḥ kutsitatvāt kausīdyam eva etac ca kuśalapakṣaparipūraṇapariniṣpādanakarmakam
Energy is the antidote to laziness, the mind’s ardor in the wholesome, not in the defiled; ardor in the defiled is itself laziness because it is blameworthy. Its function is to complete and accomplish the wholesome side.
The translation avoids reducing praśrabdhi to simple calm or relaxation; Sthiramati defines it as the antidote to dauṣṭhulya and as kāya-citta-karmaṇyatā.
→ praśrabdhi is pliancy, the serviceability/workability of body and mind.
trbh [625, 626, 627, 628, 629, 630, 631, 632, 633]praśrabdhir dauṣṭhulyapratipakṣaḥ kāyacittakarmaṇyatā dauṣṭhulyaṃ kāyacittayor akarmaṇyatā sāṃkleśikadharmabījāni ca tadapagame laghusamutthānatā yato bhavati cittakarmaṇyatā samyaṅmanasikārasaṃprayuktasya cittasyāhlādalāghavanimittaṃ yac caitasikaṃ dharmāntaraṃ yadyogāc cittam avyāhatam ālambane pravartate 'tas tac cittakarmaṇyatety ucyate
Pliancy is the antidote to heaviness, the workability of body and mind. Heaviness is the unworkability of body and mind and the seeds of defiled dharmas. When that is removed, light arising occurs. The distinct mental factor that causes joy and lightness in a mind joined with correct attention, by virtue of which the mind proceeds unobstructed toward its object, is called workability of mind.
I do not construe sāpramādikā as an adjective of praśrabdhiḥ alone or as a stand-alone name for apramāda only. Sthiramati asks what the feminine referent is and answers upekṣā, then separately defines apramāda and upekṣā.
→ sāpramādikā points to upekṣā, equanimity, occurring together with apramāda, heedfulness.
trbh [638, 639, 640, 641, 642, 643, 644, 645]sāpramādikā sahāpramādena pravartata iti sāpramādikā kā punar asau upekṣā kuta etat ekāntakuśalatvāt sarvakuśalānāṃ ceha nirdeśādhikārāc chraddhādivat sākṣād anirdeśāt tadvyatiriktānyakuśalābhāvāc copekṣaiva vijñāyate
Sāpramādikā: it occurs together with apramāda, hence sāpramādikā. What is that? Equanimity. Why so? Because it is exclusively wholesome, because all the wholesome factors are the topic here, because it is not directly mentioned like faith and the others, and because there is no other wholesome factor apart from these; therefore only equanimity is understood.
trbh [646, 647, 648, 649, 650]tatrāpramādaḥ pramādapratipakṣaḥ alobhād yāvad vīryam apramādaḥ yān alobhādīn niśrityākuśalān dharmān prajahāti tatpratipakṣāṃś ca kuśalān dharmān bhāvayati te 'lobhādayo 'pramādaḥ
There apramāda is the antidote to heedlessness. Apramāda is the factors from non-greed through energy: relying on those beginning with non-greed, one abandons unwholesome dharmas and cultivates the wholesome dharmas opposed to them; those beginning with non-greed are apramāda.
trbh [654]upekṣā cittasamatā cittapraśaṭhatā cittānābhogatā
Equanimity is evenness of mind, straightness of mind, and non-effort of mind.
The translation follows Sthiramati’s avihiṃsā gloss and does not treat the printed āhiṃsā as a separate lexical item with a different meaning.
→ ahiṃsā, glossed avihiṃsā, is non-harming, compassion toward beings.
trbh [670, 671, 672, 673, 674, 675, 676, 677, 678]avihiṃsā vihiṃsāpratipakṣaḥ vadhabandhanādibhiḥ sattvānām aviheṭhanam avihiṃsā sattveṣu karuṇā kaṃ ruṇaddhīti karuṇā kam iti sukhasyākhyā sukhaṃ ruṇaddhīty arthaḥ kāruṇiko hi paraduḥkhaduḥkhī bhavatīti iyaṃ cāviheṭhanakarmikā
Non-harming is the antidote to harming: the non-injuring of beings by killing, binding, and the like. Non-harming is compassion toward beings. It is called karuṇā because it blocks happiness; the compassionate person suffers at another’s suffering. Its function is non-injury.
The pāda boundary in subject matter comes after the eleven kuśalas. Sthiramati explicitly says the immediately following items are kleśas and introduces them with kleśā rāgapratighamūḍhayaḥ.
→ kleśāḥ begins a new category; rāga, pratigha, and mūḍhi are afflictions, not wholesome factors.
trbh [679, 680, 681]uktā ekādaśa kuśalāḥ tadanantaroddiṣṭās tu kleśā ity atas tān adhikṛtyāha kleśā rāgapratighamūḍhayaḥ
The eleven wholesome ones have been stated. But immediately after them the kleśas are listed; therefore, taking those as the topic, he says: the afflictions are rāga, pratigha, and mūḍhi.
