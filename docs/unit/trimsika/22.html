Translation. For just this reason, it is neither different nor non-different from the other-dependent (paratantra); as with impermanence and the rest, it is to be so stated. When this perfected nature (pariniṣpanna) has not been seen, that other-dependent nature is not seen.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Translate the first half as a statement that the perfected is neither different nor non-different from the other-dependent, not as a statement about the dependent being different from something else or about mere ‘dependence’ in the abstract.
→ The first saḥ is the perfected nature (pariniṣpannaḥ), and paratantrataḥ means ‘from / in relation to the other-dependent nature (paratantraḥ).’
trbh [1342, 1343, 1344, 1345, 1346, 1347, 1348]ata eva sa naiveti parikalpitena svabhāvena paratantrasya sadā rahitatā pariniṣpannaḥ rahitatā ca dharmatā sā ca dharmatā dharmān nānyā nānanyā yujyate pariniṣpannaś ca paratantradharmatety ataḥ paratantrāt pariniṣpanno nānyo nānanya iti boddhavyaḥ
On ‘for just this reason he is not ...’: the perfected is the constant absence, in the other-dependent, of the imagined nature. Absence is dharmatā, and that dharmatā is not properly either different or non-different from the dharmas. And since the perfected is the dharmatā of the other-dependent, the perfected should be understood as neither different nor non-different from the other-dependent.
Preserve the double denial rather than resolving it into simple identity, simple difference, or a vague ‘indescribability.’
→ Neither ‘different’ nor ‘non-different’ is to be affirmed without qualification.
trbh [1349, 1350, 1351, 1352, 1353, 1354, 1355]yadi hi pariniṣpannaḥ paratantrād anyaḥ syād evaṃ na parikalpitena paratantraḥ śūnyaḥ syāt athānanya evam api pariniṣpanno na viśuddhālambanaḥ syāt paratantravat saṃkleśātmakatvāt evaṃ paratantraś ca na kleśātmakaḥ syāt pariniṣpannād ananyatvāt pariniṣpannavat
If the perfected were different from the other-dependent, then the other-dependent would not be empty of the imagined. But if it were simply non-different, then the perfected would not be a purified object, because, like the other-dependent, it would have the nature of defilement; and likewise the other-dependent would not have the nature of defilement, because, being non-different from the perfected, it would be like the perfected.
Take vācyo with an explicit ellipsis supplied by Sthiramati, not as ‘expressible like impermanence’ in a loose lexical sense.
→ anityatādivat vācyo means ‘is to be stated [as neither different nor non-different], as with impermanence and the rest.’
trbh [1356, 1357]anityatādivad vācyo nānyo nānanya iti vākyaśeṣaḥ
‘Is to be stated as with impermanence and the rest’: the remainder of the sentence is ‘neither different nor non-different.’
trbh [1358, 1359, 1360, 1361, 1362, 1363, 1364, 1365]yathā hy anityatā duḥkhatānātmatā ca saṃskārādibhyo nānyā nānanyā yadi saṃskārebhyo 'nityatā anyā evaṃ tarhi saṃskārā nityāḥ syuḥ athānanyā evam api saṃskārāḥ pradhvaṃsābhāvarūpāḥ syur anityatāvat evaṃ duḥkhatādiṣv api vācyam
For just as impermanence, sufferingness, and selflessness are neither different nor non-different from formations and the like: if impermanence were different from formations, then formations would be permanent; but if it were non-different, then formations would have the nature of destruction-absence, like impermanence. The same is to be said for sufferingness and the rest.
Translate the last pāda as a locative-absolute condition: ‘when this perfected nature has not been seen, that other-dependent nature is not seen.’
→ In nādṛṣṭe ’smin sa dṛśyate, asmin refers to the perfected nature, while saḥ refers to the other-dependent nature.
trbh [1366, 1367, 1368, 1369, 1370]yadi grāhyagrāhakabhāvarahitaḥ paratantraḥ katham asau gṛhyate agṛhyamāṇo vā katham astīti vijñāyate ata āha nādṛṣṭe 'smin sa dṛśyate nādṛṣṭe 'sminn iti pariniṣpannasvabhāve sa dṛśyata iti paratantraḥ svabhāvaḥ
Objection: if the other-dependent is devoid of the condition of grasper and grasped, how is it apprehended? Or, if it is not apprehended, how is it known to exist? Therefore he says: ‘When this has not been seen, it is not seen.’ In ‘when this has not been seen’ the reference is to the perfected nature; in ‘it is seen’ the reference is to the other-dependent nature.
trbh [1371, 1372, 1373, 1374]nirvikalpalokottarajñānadṛśye pariniṣpanne svabhāve adṛṣṭe apratividdhe asākṣātkṛte tatpṛṣṭhalabdhaśuddhalaukikajñānagamyatvāt paratantro 'nyena jñānena na gṛhyate ataḥ pariniṣpanne 'dṛṣṭe paratantro na dṛśyate
When the perfected nature, which is seen by non-conceptual supramundane knowledge, has not been seen, penetrated, or directly realized, the other-dependent is not grasped by any other knowledge, because it is accessible to the pure worldly knowledge obtained subsequently to that. Therefore, when the perfected has not been seen, the other-dependent is not seen.
Maintain the restricted scope: it is not seen by ‘another’ cognition before the perfected is realized; it is seen by subsequently obtained pure worldly knowledge.
→ The negation in the final pāda is not a denial that the other-dependent can ever be seen.
trbh [1375, 1376]na punar lokottarajñānapṛṣṭhalabdhenāpi jñānena na dṛśyate
But it is not the case that it is not seen even by the knowledge obtained subsequent to supramundane knowledge.
trbh [1378, 1379, 1380, 1381, 1382, 1383, 1384, 1385]tat pṛṣṭhalabdhena jñānena māyāmarīcisvapnapratiśrutkodakacandranirmitasamān sarvadharmān pratyetīti atra ca dharmāḥ paratantrasaṃgṛhītā abhipretāḥ pariniṣpannaś cākāśavad ekarasaḥ jñānañ ca yathoktaṃ nirvikalpena jñānenākāśasamatayā tān sarvadharmān paśyatīti paratantradharmāṇāṃ tathatāmātradarśanāt
With the knowledge obtained subsequently to that, one understands all dharmas to be like illusion, mirage, dream, echo, moon in water, and magical creation. Here the dharmas intended are those included in the other-dependent. And the perfected is of one taste, like space. And knowledge is as stated: by non-conceptual knowledge one sees all those dharmas as equal to space, because one sees only the suchness of other-dependent dharmas.
