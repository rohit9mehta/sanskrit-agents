Triṃśikā (Vasubandhu) with Sthiramati's bhāṣya · 4

upekṣā vedanā tatrānivṛtāvyākṛtañ ca tat tathā sparśādayaḥ tac ca vartate srotasaughavat

Translation. There, in the store-consciousness (ālayavijñāna), the feeling (vedanā) is equanimity (upekṣā); and that [store-consciousness] is unobscured-indeterminate (anivṛtāvyākṛta). Contact and the rest (sparśādayaḥ) are likewise; and this [store-consciousness] goes on, in a stream-continuum (srotas), like a flood (ogha).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The locative adverb tatra is not a vague ‘there’ or a reference to the immediately preceding noun vedanā; it is explicitly connected to ālayavijñāna, the topic already under discussion.
→ tatra = ‘there, in the ālayavijñāna’
trbh [308]tatrety ālayavijñānam eva prakṛtatvāt saṃbadhyate
‘Tatra’ connects with ālayavijñāna alone, because that is the topic.
upekṣā is the technical neutral feeling associated with ālayavijñāna, not ordinary negligence or contempt; the commentary excludes sukha and duḥkha for this ālaya-feeling.
→ upekṣā vedanā = the feeling is equanimity, i.e. neither pleasure nor pain
trbh [309, 310, 311]upekṣaivālayavijñāne vedanā na sukhā na duḥkhā tayoḥ paricchinnālambanākāratvād rāgadveṣānuśayitatvāc ca
In ālayavijñāna, the feeling is only upekṣā; it is not pleasure and not pain, because those two have a delimited object-aspect and are accompanied by latent attachment and aversion.
The compound is parsed as the technical moral classification of ālayavijñāna: anivṛta excludes nivṛta, and avyākṛta excludes wholesome and unwholesome. It is not merely ‘unexplained’.
→ anivṛtāvyākṛtam = unobscured and indeterminate; predicated of ālayavijñāna
trbh [312, 313]anivṛtāvyākṛtañ ca tat ālayavijñānam iti prakṛtam
‘And that is anivṛtāvyākṛta’: ālayavijñāna is what is under discussion.
trbh [314, 315, 316, 317, 318]tatrānivṛtagrahaṇaṃ nivṛtavyavacchedārtham avyākṛtagrahaṇaṃ kuśalākuśalavyavacchedārtham manobhūmikair āgantukair upakleśair anāvṛtatvād anivṛtam vipākatvād vipākaṃ prati kuśalākuśalatvenāvyākaraṇād avyākṛtam
The mention of anivṛta serves to exclude nivṛta; the mention of avyākṛta serves to exclude wholesome and unwholesome. It is anivṛta because it is not covered by adventitious defilements belonging to the mental plane; it is avyākṛta because, being maturation, it is not classified as wholesome or unwholesome with respect to maturation.
tathā does not merely add ‘also contact, etc.’; Sthiramati spells out the analogy: just as ālayavijñāna is a maturation, has an undelimited object-aspect, is always conjoined with sparśa etc., and has neutral feeling and anivṛtāvyākṛta status, so too sparśa and the rest share the corresponding features.
→ tathā sparśādayaḥ = contact and the rest are likewise, with the same relevant ālaya-properties
trbh [319, 320, 321, 322, 323, 324, 325]tathā sparśādayaḥ yathālayavijñānam ekāntena vipāko 'paricchinnālambanākāraṃ sadā sparśādibhir anvitaṃ tatra copekṣaiva vedanānivṛtāvyākṛtañ ca tathā sparśādayo 'py ekāntena vipākā evāparicchinnālambanākārāś ca ātmānaṃ hitvetaraiś caturbhir ālayavijñānena ca nityam anugatās teṣu copekṣaiva vedanānivṛtāvyākṛtāś cālayavijñānavat
‘Likewise contact and the rest’: as ālayavijñāna is exclusively maturation, has an undelimited object-aspect, is always accompanied by contact and the rest, and in it the feeling is only upekṣā and it is anivṛtāvyākṛta, so too contact and the rest are exclusively maturations, have undelimited object-aspects, are always accompanied by the other four and by ālayavijñāna, and in them too the feeling is upekṣā and they are anivṛtāvyākṛta, just like ālayavijñāna.
trbh [326, 327]na hi vipākena saṃprayuktānām avipākatvam aparicchinnālambanākāreṇa ca paricchinnālambanākāratvaṃ saṃbhavati
For what is conjoined with a maturation cannot be non-maturation; nor can what is conjoined with an undelimited object-aspect have a delimited object-aspect.
The second tat again refers to ālayavijñāna. The commentary explicitly rejects the view that one identical ālayavijñāna persists throughout saṃsāra; its continuing is by continuum/series, in keeping with momentariness.
→ tac ca vartate = this ālayavijñāna continues, but not as one unchanged entity
trbh [330, 331, 332, 333, 334, 335]kiṃ punas tad ālayavijñānam ekam abhinnam āsaṃsāram anuvartate uta saṃtānena na hi tad ekam abhinnam anuvartate kṣaṇikatvāt kiṃ tarhi tac ca vartate srotasaughavat tac cety ālayavijñānam eva saṃbadhyate
The question is whether that ālayavijñāna continues throughout saṃsāra as one undivided thing or by continuum. It certainly does not continue as one undivided thing, because it is momentary. What then? ‘And this goes on, like a stream/flood.’ In ‘tac ca’, ālayavijñāna alone is connected.
The printed srotasaughavat is segmented as srotasā + oghavat. Morphologically oghavat is an inflected neuter nominative singular adjective-form, but the commentary defines srotas as uninterrupted cause-effect continuity and ogha as a continuous water-flow, then makes the yathā/evam simile explicit. Thus its force here is comparative, ‘flood-like, like a flood,’ not the MW possessive sense ‘having a strong stream’.
→ srotasā oghavat = by way of a stream/continuum, flood-like
trbh [336, 337, 338, 339]tatra sroto hetuphalayor nairantaryaprabandhena pravṛttiḥ udakasamūhasya pūrvāparabhāgāvicchedena pravāha ogha ity ucyate
Here ‘stream’ is the occurrence of cause and result in an uninterrupted continuum. The flow of a mass of water without a break between earlier and later parts is called ‘flood’.
trbh [340, 341, 342]yathā hy oghas tṛṇakāṣṭhagomayādīn ākarṣayaṅ gacchaty evam ālayavijñānam api puṇyāpuṇyāneñjyakarmavāsanānugataṃ sparśamanaskārādīn ākarṣan srotasāsaṃsāram avyuparataṃ pravartata iti
As a flood goes along drawing grass, wood, cow-dung and the like, so too ālayavijñāna, followed by the impressions of meritorious, unmeritorious and unchanging karma, draws contact, attention and the rest and proceeds without stopping by way of a stream throughout saṃsāra.
