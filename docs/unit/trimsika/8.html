Triṃśikā (Vasubandhu) with Sthiramati's bhāṣya · 8

dvitīyaḥ pariṇāmo 'yam tṛtīyaḥ ṣaḍvidhasya yā | viṣayasyopalabdhiḥ sā kuśalākuśalādvayā

Translation. This is the second transformation (pariṇāma). The third consciousness-transformation (vijñāna-pariṇāma) is whatever apprehension (upalabdhi) there is of the sixfold object (viṣaya): that is wholesome (kuśala), unwholesome (akuśala), or neither—the undetermined (advayā/avyākṛta).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The verse is not saying that ‘this second transformation is third’; it closes the second transformation and then begins the third. The masculine tṛtīyaḥ is construed with the supplied masculine vijñānapariṇāmaḥ.
→ Take dvitīyaḥ pariṇāmo 'yam as a concluding sentence about the second transformation, and take tṛtīyaḥ as introducing the third consciousness-transformation with supplied vijñānapariṇāmaḥ.
trbh [473, 474]uddiṣṭo nidiṣṭaś ceti nigamayati dvitīyapariṇāmāntaraṃ tṛtīyaḥ pariṇāmo vaktavya ity ata āha
He concludes that what was stated has also been explained. Since, after the second transformation, the third transformation remains to be stated, therefore he says...
trbh [475, 476, 477]tṛtīyaḥ ṣaḍvidhasya yā viṣayasyopalabdhiḥ sā tṛtīyo vijñānapariṇāma iti vākyaśeṣaḥ
‘The third: whatever apprehension there is of the sixfold object, that...’ The remainder of the sentence is: ‘the third consciousness-transformation.’
The genitive phrase is ‘of the sixfold object,’ not ‘of a sixfold apprehension’ or an unspecified sixfold consciousness.
→ ṣaḍvidhasya qualifies viṣayasya: the object is sixfold, namely the domains whose nature is form, sound, smell, taste, tangible, and dharma.
trbh [478, 479]ṣaḍvidhasyeti ṣaṭprakārasya rūpaśabdagandharasaspraṣṭavya dharmātmakasya viṣayasya yā upalabdhir grahaṇaṃ
‘Sixfold’ means ‘of six kinds’: whatever apprehension, grasping, there is of an object whose nature is form, sound, smell, taste, tangible, and dharma...
The bhāṣya glosses upalabdhi with grahaṇa and pratipatti, so the relevant sense is perceptual/cognitive taking-up, not acquisition or possession.
→ Translate upalabdhiḥ as ‘apprehension’ and construe viṣayasya as its objective genitive: apprehension of the object.
trbh [479, 480]rūpaśabdagandharasaspraṣṭavya dharmātmakasya viṣayasya yā upalabdhir grahaṇaṃ pratipattir ity arthaḥ
Whatever apprehension of the object whose nature is form, sound, smell, taste, tangible, and dharma—‘apprehension’ means grasping; the sense is cognition/recognition.
The pronouns frame the apprehension as the subject subsequently classified ethically.
→ Read yā ... sā as a relative-correlative construction whose referent is the feminine upalabdhiḥ.
trbh [475, 476, 481]tṛtīyaḥ ṣaḍvidhasya yā viṣayasyopalabdhiḥ sā sā punaḥ kiṃ kuśalākuśalāvyākṛtety ata āha
‘Whatever apprehension there is of the sixfold object, that...’ Again, is that wholesome, unwholesome, or undetermined? Therefore he says...
Sthiramati first restates the question as the standard triad kuśala/akuśala/avyākṛta, then glosses advayā as avyākṛtā and as ‘not wholesome, not unwholesome.’
→ Translate kuśalā akuśalā advayā as ‘wholesome, unwholesome, or neither,’ with advayā meaning the ethically undetermined (avyākṛta), not metaphysical non-duality.
trbh [481, 482, 483]sā punaḥ kiṃ kuśalākuśalāvyākṛtety ata āha kuśalākuśalādvayā kuśalākuśalādvayety avyākṛtāpi
Again, is it wholesome, unwholesome, or undetermined? Therefore he says: ‘wholesome, unwholesome, or neither.’ By saying ‘wholesome, unwholesome, or neither,’ the undetermined too is included.
trbh [484, 485, 486, 487]alobhādveṣāmohaiḥ saṃprayuktā kuśalā lobhadveṣamohaiḥ saṃprayuktākuśalā kuśalākuśalair asaṃprayuktādvayā na kuśalā nākuśalety arthaḥ
Conjoined with non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion, it is wholesome. Conjoined with greed, hatred, and delusion, it is unwholesome. Unconjoined with wholesome and unwholesome factors, it is neither: the meaning is ‘not wholesome and not unwholesome.’
