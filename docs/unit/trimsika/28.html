Triṃśikā (Vasubandhu) with Sthiramati's bhāṣya · 28

yadā tv ālambanaṃ jñānaṃ naivopalabhate tadā | sthitaṃ vijñaptimātratve grāhyābhābhāve tadagrahāt

Translation. When cognition (jñāna), however, does not at all apprehend an object-support (ālambana) outside mind, then it stands established in representation-only (vijñaptimātratva): for, when the graspable-appearance (grāhyābhā) is absent, there is no grasping of that.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The commentary explicitly expands the pair with yasmin kāle and tasmin kāle, so I keep the temporal structure rather than making yadā causal or conditional in the main translation.
→ yadā ... tadā as a temporal correlative: “when ... then.”
trbh [1486, 1488]yasmin kāle deśanālambanaṃ avavādālambanaṃ prākṛtaṃ vā rūpaśabdādyālambanaṃ ... tasmin kāle vijñānagrāhasya prahāṇaṃ
“At the time when” there is such an object-support ... “at that time” there is abandonment of grasping cognition.
I translate “cognition does not apprehend an object-support,” not “one does not apprehend cognition” or “cognition is the object.” The commentary repeats exactly this construal and supplies examples of ālambana.
→ jñānam is the subject, and ālambanam is the apprehended object-support.
trbh [1486, 1487]yasmin kāle deśanālambanaṃ avavādālambanaṃ prākṛtaṃ vā rūpaśabdādyālambanaṃ jñānaṃ bahiś cittān nopalabhate
At the time when cognition does not apprehend, outside mind, a teaching-support, an instruction-support, or an ordinary support such as form, sound, and so on.
The translation uses “apprehend” and specifies “outside mind,” because the commentary glosses nopalabhate with seeing, grasping, and fixating, and places the denied object bahiś cittāt/cittān.
→ upalabhate means apprehends/perceives/graspingly notices, not obtains or acquires.
trbh [1487]jñānaṃ bahiś cittān nopalabhate na paśyati na gṛhṇāti nābhiniviśate
Cognition does not apprehend outside mind, does not see, does not grasp, does not fixate.
trbh [1488]yathābhūtārthadarśanān na tu jātyandhavat
This is because of seeing reality as it is, not like someone blind from birth.
I take sthitam as a predicative nominative neuter referring to cognition/mind, not as an adjective modifying ālambanam. The locative is not a physical location but the Yogācāra state/own-nature of mind.
→ sthitaṃ vijñaptimātratve means “is established in representation-only,” glossed as being established in the own-nature of mind.
trbh [1488, 1489]tasmin kāle vijñānagrāhasya prahāṇaṃ svacittadharmatāyāṃ ca pratiṣṭhito bhavati
At that time there is abandonment of grasping cognition, and one/it is established in the own-nature of mind.
trbh [1495, 1496]grāhyagrāhakābhiniveśānuśayāḥ prahīyante svacittadharmatāyāṃ ca cittam avasthitaṃ bhavati
The latent tendencies of fixation on grasped and grasper are abandoned, and mind becomes settled in the own-nature of mind.
The apparatus does not silently replace the bhāṣya’s jñānam/vijñaptimātratve with the vulgate vijñānam/vijñānamātratve. The commentary’s own prose continues with jñāna-language and glosses the state as svacittadharmatā.
→ The bhāṣya reading jñānam and vijñaptimātratve is followed, with vijñaptimātratva rendered “representation-only.”
trbh [1484, 1485]yadā tv ālambanaṃ jñānaṃ naivopalabhate tadā | sthitaṃ vijñaptimātratve grāhyābhābhāve tadagrahāt
When cognition does not apprehend an object-support, then it is established in representation-only, because of the non-grasping of that when the graspable-appearance is absent.
trbh [1494]samasamālambyālambakaṃ nirvikalpaṃ lokottaraṃ jñānam utpadyate
A nonconceptual supramundane cognition arises, with ālambya and ālambaka on the same footing.
Morphologically I retain the supplied bhāṣya form grāhya-ābhā-abhāva, but I do not translate it as mere optical darkness or “lack of splendour.” The commentary explains the reason through the absence of grāhya and consequent absence of grāhaka.
→ grāhyābhābhāve tadagrahāt is causal: when the graspable or its appearance is absent, there is no grasping of it and no grasper.
trbh [1490, 1491]atraiva kāraṇam āha grāhyābhāve tadagrahād iti
He states the reason for just this: because, when the graspable is absent, there is non-grasping of that.
trbh [1492, 1493]grāhye sati grāhako bhavati na tu grāhyābhāva iti grāhyābhāve grāhakābhāvam
When the graspable exists, there is a grasper, but not when there is absence of the graspable; in the absence of the graspable, there is absence of the grasper.
