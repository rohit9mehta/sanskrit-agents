Triṃśikā (Vasubandhu) with Sthiramati's bhāṣya · 25

dharmāṇāṃ paramārthaś ca sa yatas tathatāpi saḥ sarvakālaṃ tathābhāvāt saiva vijñaptimātratā

Translation. Since that [perfected nature (pariniṣpanna-svabhāva)] is the ultimate reality (paramārtha) of dharmas (dharma), it too is suchness (tathatā); for at every time it is just so— that very suchness is representation-only-ness (vijñaptimātratā).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Do not translate paramārthaḥ merely as an ordinary “highest purpose/meaning.” Sthiramati gives a technical nirukti and then identifies it with pariniṣpanna-svabhāva, specifically as the paramārtha of all dharmas whose nature is dependent.
→ paramārthaḥ means the ultimate object/reality: the perfected nature as the object of supreme supramundane cognition and as the ultimate of dependent-nature dharmas.
trbh [1414]paramaṃ hi lokottarajñānaṃ niruttaratvāt tasyārthaḥ paramārthaḥ
For supreme supramundane cognition, because it is unsurpassed, is ‘parama’; its object/referent is ‘paramārtha’.
trbh [1415, 1416, 1417]atha vākāśavat sarvatraikarasārthena vaimalyāvikārārthena ca pariniṣpannaḥ svabhāvaḥ paramārtha ucyate
Or, the perfected nature is called paramārtha in the sense that it is uniform everywhere like space, and in the sense of purity and non-alteration.
trbh [1418, 1419, 1420]sa yasmāt pariniṣpannaḥ svabhāvaḥ sarvadharmāṇāṃ paratantrātmakānāṃ paramārthaḥ taddharmateti kṛtvā
Because that perfected nature is the paramārtha of all dharmas, which have dependent nature, being their dharmatā.
The verse’s sa yatas is to be construed through the commentary’s sa yasmāt ... tasmāt, not as a spatial “whence.” The masculine pronoun is not referring to tathatā but to the perfected nature.
→ yatas is causal, “since/because,” and saḥ refers to pariniṣpannaḥ svabhāvaḥ.
trbh [1418, 1419, 1420, 1421]sa yasmāt pariniṣpannaḥ svabhāvaḥ sarvadharmāṇāṃ paratantrātmakānāṃ paramārthaḥ taddharmateti kṛtvā tasmāt pariniṣpanna eva svabhāvaḥ paramārthaniḥsvabhāvātā pariniṣpannasyābhāvasvabhāvatvāt
Because that perfected nature is the paramārtha of all dependent-nature dharmas, being their dharmatā, therefore the perfected nature itself is ultimate naturelessness, since the perfected has absence as its nature.
Translate tathatā as the technical term “suchness,” but do not make api concessive (“although”) or limit the point to one additional synonym. Sthiramati says api indicates that all dharmadhātu-synonyms may designate it.
→ tathatā api means “also suchness,” with api marking that this is not the only applicable designation.
trbh [1426, 1427, 1428, 1429]tathatāpi saḥ apiśabdān na kevalaṃ tathatāśabdenaivābhidhātavyaḥ kiṃ tarhi yāvanto dharmadhātuparyāyāḥ sarvais tair apy abhidhātavya iti
‘It is also suchness.’ From the word api: it is not to be designated only by the word suchness; rather, by as many synonyms of dharmadhātu as there are, by all those too it is to be designated.
The compound is tathā-bhāva, “being so,” in the ablative of cause; it is not tathā-abhāva, “non-being so.” This line explains the name tathatā.
→ sarvakālaṃ tathābhāvāt means “because at all times it is just so.”
trbh [1430, 1431, 1432, 1433]sarvakālaṃ tathābhāvāt tathatā tathā hi pṛthagjanaśaikṣāśaikṣāvasthāsu sarvakālaṃ tathaiva bhavati nānyatheti tathatety ucyate
‘Because of being so at all times—suchness.’ For in the states of ordinary person, trainee, and adept, at all times it is just so, not otherwise; therefore it is called suchness.
Do not take vijñaptimātratā as a different entity or merely as the earlier ordinary thesis of cognition-only. Sthiramati explicitly poses the alternative—whether it is the perfected itself like tathatā, or something else—and answers with sā eva; he adds that this statement teaches realization (abhisamaya).
→ sā eva vijñaptimātratā identifies that very suchness/perfected nature with representation-only-ness.
trbh [1434, 1435, 1436, 1437]kiṃ punas tathatāvat pariniṣpanna eva vijñaptimātratā utānyā vijñaptimātratā ata āha saiva vijñaptimātratā
Is vijñaptimātratā, like tathatā, just the perfected, or is vijñaptimātratā something else? Therefore he says: ‘that very is vijñaptimātratā.’
trbh [1444]saiva vijñaptimātratety anena vacanenābhisamaya uktaḥ
By the statement ‘that very is vijñaptimātratā,’ realization has been taught.
