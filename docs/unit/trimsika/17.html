Triṃśikā (Vasubandhu) with Sthiramati's bhāṣya · 17

vijñānapariṇāmo 'yaṃ vikalpo yad vikalpyate | tena tan nāsti tenedaṃ sarvaṃ vijñāptimātrakam

Translation. This transformation of consciousness (vijñānapariṇāma) is conceptual construction (vikalpa): whatever object is imagined by it—that is not there. Therefore all this is representation-only (vijñaptimātraka), ‘all’ meaning the three realms and the unconditioned.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
vijñānapariṇāmo 'yam is not a generic ‘change of knowledge’; Sthiramati identifies it with the threefold transformation just taught.
→ ‘This transformation of consciousness’ is the immediately preceding threefold vijñānapariṇāma, and it is predicated as vikalpa.
trbh [1096]yo 'yaṃ vijñānapariṇāmas trividho 'nantaram abhihitaḥ so 'yaṃ vikalpaḥ
‘This threefold transformation of consciousness that was just stated is this vikalpa.’
trbh [1100]tena trividhena vikalpenālayavijñānakliṣṭamanaḥpravṛttivijñānasvabhāvena
‘By that threefold vikalpa, whose nature is storehouse-consciousness, afflicted mind, and active consciousness …’
The commentary defines vikalpa through adhyāropa and identifies it with abhūtaparikalpa, the false construction constituted by three-realm cittas and caittas.
→ vikalpa is ‘conceptual construction/imagination’ bearing superimposed object-aspects, not ‘option’ or ‘alternative.’
trbh [1097, 1098, 1099]adhyāropitārthākārās traidhātukāś cittacaittā vikalpa ucyate yathoktam abhūtaparikalpas tu cittacaittās tridhātukā iti
‘The cittas and caittas belonging to the three realms, having the aspects of superimposed objects, are called vikalpa; as it has been said: “False construction consists of cittas and caittas of the three realms.”’
tena is construed instrumentally with vikalpyate, not as the causal ‘therefore’; the relative yad is filled out by Sthiramati as bhājana, ātman, skandhas, dhātus, āyatanas, rūpa, śabda, etc.
→ The first tena means ‘by it,’ i.e. by the threefold vikalpa; yad/tat refers to the imagined object (vastu).
trbh [1100, 1101, 1102]tena trividhena vikalpenālayavijñānakliṣṭamanaḥpravṛttivijñānasvabhāvena sasaṃprayogeṇa yad vikalpyate bhājanam ātmāskandhadhātvāyatanarūpaśabdādikaṃ vastu tan nāstīty ataḥ sa vijñānapariṇāmo vikalpa ucyate
‘By that threefold vikalpa, whose nature is storehouse-consciousness, afflicted mind, and active consciousness, together with its associates, whatever object is imagined—receptacle-world, self, aggregates, elements, sense-bases, form, sound, and so on—that object does not exist; therefore that transformation of consciousness is called vikalpa.’
trbh [1125, 1126]yasmāt pariṇāmātmakena vikalpena yad vikalpyate tena tan nāsti tasmād viṣayābhāvāt sarvaṃ vijñaptimātrakam
‘Because what is imagined by the vikalpa whose nature is transformation—by it, that does not exist; therefore, because of the absence of an object-domain, everything is representation-only.’
Sthiramati argues for non-existent objects as supports: cognition can arise in illusion, dream, eye-disease, etc.; it arises from a prior same-kind consciousness, not from an external object. Thus the non-existence is targeted at the superimposed object.
→ tan nāsti denies the existence of the imagined object/ālambana/viṣaya, not the occurrence of consciousness itself.
trbh [1103]asadālambanatvāt
‘Because it has a non-existent object/support.’
trbh [1108, 1109, 1110, 1111, 1112, 1113, 1114]māyāgandharvanagarasvapnatimirādāv asaty apyālambane jāyate yadi ca vijñānasyālambanapratibaddha utpādaḥ syād evaṃ sati māyādiṣv arthābhāvān na vijñānam utpadyate tasmāt pūrvakān niruddhāt tajjātīyavijñānād vijñānam utpadyate na bāhyād arthāt tasminn asaty api bhāvāt
‘Consciousness arises in illusion, a gandharva-city, dream, eye-disease, and the like, even when the object/support is unreal. If the arising of consciousness were bound to an object-support, then in illusions and the like, because there is no object, consciousness would not arise. Therefore consciousness arises from a preceding, ceased consciousness of the same kind, not from an external object, since it exists even when that is absent.’
trbh [1119, 1120]tasmād adhyāropitarūpatvād vikalpasyālambanam asad iti pratipattavyam
‘Therefore, because it has the nature of superimposition, the object/support of vikalpa is to be understood as non-existent.’
Sthiramati explicitly glosses tena as tasmāt and frames the sentence as avoiding the nihilistic extreme after rejecting superimposition.
→ The second tena, in tenedaṃ, means ‘therefore’ (= tasmāt), leading to vijñaptimātrakam because the object-domain is absent.
trbh [1121, 1122, 1123, 1124, 1125, 1126]anena tāvat samāropāntaṃ parihṛtyāpavādāntaparijihīrṣayā āha tenedaṃ sarvaṃ vijñāptimātrakam iti teneti tasmāt yasmāt pariṇāmātmakena vikalpena yad vikalpyate tena tan nāsti tasmād viṣayābhāvāt sarvaṃ vijñaptimātrakam
‘By this, having first rejected the extreme of superimposition, and wishing to avoid the extreme of denial, he says: “Therefore all this is representation-only.” Here tena means “therefore.” Because what is imagined by the vikalpa whose nature is transformation—by it, that does not exist; therefore, because of the absence of an object-domain, everything is representation-only.’
The scope of ‘all’ and the force of mātrakam are fixed by explicit glosses: sarvam = traidhātukam plus asaṃskṛtam; mātra excludes what is over and above that, while ka fills the pāda.
→ sarvam includes the three realms and the unconditioned; mātra means ‘only’ by excluding any additional object-domain, and -ka is only metrical.
trbh [1127, 1128, 1129, 1130, 1131]sarvam iti traidhātukam asaṃskṛtañ ca mātraśabdas tadadhikaviṣayavyavacchedārthaḥ kakāraḥ pādapūraṇārthaḥ
‘“All” means the three realms and the unconditioned. The word mātra serves to exclude an object-domain over and above that. The syllable ka is for filling out the pāda.’
