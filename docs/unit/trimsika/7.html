Triṃśikā (Vasubandhu) with Sthiramati's bhāṣya · 7

yatrajas tanmayair arhato na tat | na nirodhasamāpattau mārge lokottare na ca

Translation. Wherever it is born, it is associated only with factors of that same realm (dhātu) and level (bhūmi); for an arhat, that defiled mind (kliṣṭaṃ manas) is not. Nor is it present in cessation-attainment (nirodha-samāpatti), nor on the supramundane path (lokottara-mārga).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The first word is not an independent yatra plus jaḥ in the verse; it is a compound whose force Sthiramati explains by a relative-location paraphrase.
→ yatrajaḥ = ‘born wherever’, specifically born in a given dhātu or bhūmi.
trbh [427]yatra jāto yatrajaḥ
‘Born where’ is ‘where-born’.
trbh [428]tanmayair iti yatra dhātau bhūmau vā jātas
By ‘tanmayaiḥ’: born in whatever element/realm or level.
The instrumental tanmayaiḥ is construed with an understood saṃprayujyate and is restrictive: same realm/element and same level, not others.
→ tanmayaiḥ means ‘with those belonging to that same dhātu and bhūmi’, and the supplied predicate is saṃprayujyate ‘is associated/conjoined’.
trbh [428, 429, 430, 431]tanmayair iti yatra dhātau bhūmau vā jātas taddhātukais tadbhūmikair eva ca saṃprayujyate nānyadhātukair anyabhūmikair vā
By ‘tanmayaiḥ’: born in whatever element/realm or level, it is associated only with those belonging to that element and that level, not with those belonging to another element or another level.
trbh [441, 442]etair api yatra jātas tanmayair eva saṃprayujyate nānyadhātubhūmikaiḥ
With these too, wherever it is born, it is associated only with those of that same kind, not with those of another element or level.
The words anyaiḥ sparśādyaiś ca are not entered as verse-words in this apparatus because the user-specified bhāṣya reading omits them; nevertheless Sthiramati explicitly comments them, so their force is recorded as contextual textual evidence.
→ The shorter supplied bhāṣya surface is analyzed, while Sthiramati’s comments on anyaiḥ sparśādyaiś ca are treated as evidence for the longer/vulgate wording and for the semantic field of association.
trbh [432, 433, 434, 435, 436, 437, 438]kiṃ punaś caturbhir eva kleśaiḥ saṃprayujyate nety āha anyaiḥ sparśādyaiś ca saṃprayujyata iti saṃbadhyate caśabdaḥ samuccayārthaḥ sparśādyair iti sparśamanaskāravedanāsaṃjñācetanābhiḥ
‘But is it associated only with the four afflictions?’ No, he says: ‘and with other factors beginning with contact.’ ‘Is associated’ is to be connected; the word ca has the sense of addition. ‘Beginning with contact’ means contact, attention, feeling, conception, and volition.
trbh [443, 444, 445, 446]atha vānyair iti mūlavijñānasaṃprayuktebhyo vyavacchedārtham mūlavijñāne hy anivṛtāvyākṛtāḥ sparśādayaḥ kliṣṭe tu manasi manovan nivṛtāvyākṛtāḥ
Alternatively, ‘other’ serves to exclude those associated with root-consciousness: in root-consciousness, contact and the rest are unobstructed-undetermined; but in the defiled mind, as in mental consciousness, they are obstructed-undetermined.
arhataḥ is a genitive singular noun ‘of/for an arhat’, and tat refers to the kliṣṭaṃ manaḥ, not to an unspecified action or object of √arh.
→ arhato na tat = ‘for an arhat, that defiled mind is not’.
trbh [447, 448, 449, 450, 451, 452, 453, 454]yadi tat kliṣṭaṃ manaḥ kuśalakliṣṭāvyākṛtāvasthāsv aviśeṣeṇa pravartate na tasya tarhi nivṛttir asti anivṛtte ca tasmin kuto mokṣa iti kathaṃ na mokṣābhāvaḥ prasajyate na prasajyate yasmād arhato na tat na nirodhasamāpattau mārge lokottare na ca arhatas tāvad aśeṣakleśaprahāṇāt kliṣṭaṃ mano naivāsti
If that defiled mind operated indiscriminately in wholesome, afflicted, and undetermined states, then it would have no cessation; and if it did not cease, how could there be liberation? Would the absence of liberation not follow? It does not follow, because: ‘for an arhat that is not; nor in cessation-attainment, nor on the supramundane path.’ First, for an arhat, because all afflictions have been abandoned, the defiled mind simply does not exist.
trbh [456, 457, 458]bhāvāgrikabhāvanāprahātavyakleśavad arhattvaprāptyānantaryamārgeṇaiva prahīyate tad anyakleśavad arhattvāvasthāyān naiva vidyate
Like the afflictions of the summit of existence that are to be abandoned by cultivation, it is abandoned precisely by the uninterrupted path of attaining arhatship; like the other afflictions, it simply is not present in the state of arhatship.
The locative is the technical state nirodha-samāpatti, and the negation indicates non-operation/cessation there, not merely absence from a list.
→ na nirodhasamāpattau means that the defiled mind is stopped in the state of cessation-attainment.
trbh [459, 460, 461, 462]ākiñcanyāyatanavītarāgasyāpy anāgāmino nirodhasamāpattilābhino mārgabalena nirodhasamāpatter labhyatvāṃ mārgavan nirodhasamāpattyavasthāyām api nirudhyate nirodhāc ca vyutthitasya punar ālayavijñānād eva pravartate
Even for a non-returner, dispassionate toward the sphere of nothingness and one who has obtained cessation-attainment, because cessation-attainment is obtained by the power of the path, it is stopped in the state of cessation-attainment as on the path; and when one has arisen from cessation, it operates again from the store-consciousness alone.
lokottare restricts mārge: Sthiramati explicitly says the mention of ‘supramundane’ excludes the worldly path, where the defiled mind does operate.
→ mārge lokottare na ca = ‘nor on the supramundane path’, specifically excluding the worldly path.
trbh [463, 464, 465, 466]mārge lokottare na ceti lokottaragrahaṇaṃ laukikavyavacchedārtham laukike tu mārge kliṣṭaṃ manaḥ pravartata eva
As for ‘and not on the supramundane path’: the word ‘supramundane’ is for excluding the worldly. But on the worldly path the defiled mind does indeed operate.
trbh [467, 468, 469, 470]nairātmyadarśanasyātmadarśanapratipakṣatvān na lokottaramārge pravartitum utsaheta vipakṣapratipakṣayor ayaugapadyāl lokottaramārge tan nirudhyate
Because the vision of selflessness is the antidote to the view of self, it cannot manage to operate on the supramundane path. Since opponent and antidote cannot occur simultaneously, it is stopped on the supramundane path.
