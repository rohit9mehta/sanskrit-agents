yāvad vijñaptimātratve vijñānaṃ nāvatiṣṭhate | grāhadvayasyānuśayas tāvan na vinivartate

Translation. So long as the yogin’s consciousness (vijñāna) does not abide in representation-only (vijñaptimātratva), so long the latent seed (anuśaya) of the twofold grasping (grāhadvaya)— grouting grasped object and grasper—does not cease.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The first pāda sets the duration of non-establishment, and the third pāda gives the matching duration of non-cessation; it is not a quantitative “as much as ... so much.”
→ yāvat ... tāvat is a temporal correlative: “so long as ... so long.”
trbh [1460, 1461, 1462]yāvad advayalakṣaṇe vijñaptimātratve yoginaś cittaṃ na pratiṣṭhitaṃ bhavati tāvad grāhyagrāhakānuśayo na vinivartate na prahīyata ity arthaḥ
“So long as the yogin’s mind has not become established in representation-only, whose mark is non-duality, so long the underlying tendency of grasped-and-grasper does not cease, is not abandoned: this is the meaning.”
The locative is not “in mere information/request,” nor a vague “in consciousness-only” without the commentary’s ontological specification; Sthiramati glosses it with cittadharmatā and advayalakṣaṇa.
→ vijñaptimātratve means “in representation-only,” specifically the dharmatā of mind marked by non-duality.
trbh [1454, 1455]yāvac cittadharmatāyāṃ vijñaptimātrasaṃśabditāyāṃ vijñānaṃ nāvatiṣṭhate
“So long as consciousness does not abide in the dharmatā of mind that is designated ‘representation-only’ ...”
trbh [1460]yāvad advayalakṣaṇe vijñaptimātratve yoginaś cittaṃ na
“So long as the yogin’s mind is not [established] in representation-only, whose mark is non-duality ...”
The bhāṣya’s ātmanepada avatiṣṭhate is supported by the commentary’s own nāvatiṣṭhate and by the paraphrase pratiṣṭhitaṃ bhavati. The subject is not an accusative object but nominative neuter vijñānam, paraphrased as yoginaś cittam.
→ vijñānaṃ nāvatiṣṭhate is “consciousness/the yogin’s mind does not abide or become established.”
trbh [1454, 1455]vijñānaṃ nāvatiṣṭhate kiṃ tarhi grāhyagrāhakopalambhe carati
“Consciousness does not abide. What then? It moves in apprehension of grasped and grasper.”
trbh [1460, 1461]yoginaś cittaṃ na pratiṣṭhitaṃ bhavati
“The yogin’s mind has not become established.”
The compound is not ‘two graspers’ or the two entities themselves; Sthiramati defines it as two forms of grāha/upalambha, external and internal.
→ grāhadvaya is the twofold grasping: grasping of the grasped and grasping of the grasper.
trbh [1455, 1456, 1457]kiṃ tarhi grāhyagrāhakopalambhe carati grāhadvayaṃ grāhyagrāho grāhakagrāhaś ca
“What then? It moves in apprehension of grasped and grasper. The twofold grasping is grasping of the grasped and grasping of the grasper.”
trbh [1463, 1464]atra ca bahir upalambhāprahāṇenādhyātmikopalambhāprahāṇaṃ darśitam iti
“And here, by the non-abandonment of external apprehension, the non-abandonment of internal apprehension is shown.”
The translation uses “latent seed,” not merely “attachment,” “remorse,” or an unspecified consequence, because the commentary explicitly defines anuśaya as bīja in ālayavijñāna.
→ anuśaya is a latent seed in the store-consciousness for the future arising of the twofold grasping.
trbh [1458, 1459]tasyānuśayas tadāhitam anāgatagrāhadvayotpattaye bījam ālayavijñāne
“Its underlying tendency is the seed, deposited by that, in the store-consciousness for the future arising of the twofold grasping.”
trbh [1449, 1450, 1451]atha vā yās tāḥ karmavāsanā grāhadvayavāsanāsahitāḥ kṣīṇe pūrvavipāke 'nyaṃ vipākaṃ janayantīty uktaṃ tasmāt kathaṃ prahāṇam aprahāṇaṃ cety ata āha
“Alternatively, those karmic impressions accompanied by impressions of the twofold grasping were said to produce another result when the former result is exhausted; therefore, [to explain] how there is abandonment and non-abandonment, he says ...”
Although vi-ni-vṛt can mean ‘turn back/return,’ Sthiramati glosses the negative here by na prahīyate, tying it to prahāṇa, abandonment.
→ na vinivartate means “does not cease / is not abandoned,” not “does not return.”
trbh [1461, 1462]tāvad grāhyagrāhakānuśayo na vinivartate na prahīyata ity arthaḥ
“So long the underlying tendency of grasped-and-grasper does not cease, is not abandoned: this is the meaning.”
trbh [1451]tasmāt kathaṃ prahāṇam aprahāṇaṃ cety ata āha
“Therefore, [to explain] how there is abandonment and non-abandonment, he says ...”
