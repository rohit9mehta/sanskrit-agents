Triṃśikā (Vasubandhu) with Sthiramati's bhāṣya · 2

vipāko mananākhyaś ca vijñaptir viṣayasya ca tatrālayākhyaṃ vijñānaṃ vipākaḥ sarvabījakam

Translation. The transformation is threefold: maturation-result (vipāka), the one called mentation (manana), and presentation of an object (viṣaya-vijñapti). Among these, the consciousness called the storehouse (ālaya) is the maturation-result, all-seeded (sarvabījaka).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
vipāko mananākhyaś ca vijñaptir viṣayasya ca is not a list of unrelated items; it continues the previous verse’s trividhaḥ pariṇāmaḥ. The genitive viṣayasya is construed with vijñaptiḥ as the technical expression viṣaya-vijñapti.
→ The first half names the three kinds of vijñāna-pariṇāma: vipāka, mananākhya, and viṣayavijñapti.
trbh [169]sa eṣa trividhaḥ pariṇāmo vipākākhyo mananākhyo viṣayavijñaptyākhyaś ca
This very threefold transformation is called vipāka, called manana, and called viṣaya-vijñapti.
Translate vipāka as ‘maturation-result’ rather than merely ‘ripening’ or ‘cooking,’ because Sthiramati defines it through the ripening of wholesome and unwholesome karmic impressions and the production of their fruit.
→ vipāka = karmic maturation-result/fruition.
trbh [170, 171]tatra kuśalākuśalakarmavāsanāparipākavaśād yathākṣepaṃ phalābhinirvṛttir vipākaḥ
Here, vipāka is the arising of a fruit according to projection, through the force of the ripening of impressions of wholesome and unwholesome karma.
trbh [188]sarvadhātugatiyonijātiṣu kuśalākuśalakarmavipākatvād vipākaḥ
It is vipāka because, in all realms, destinies, wombs, and classes of birth, it is the maturation-result of wholesome and unwholesome karma.
The compound is possessive, ‘having the name manana’; the term is not an adjective meaning simply ‘thoughtful’ and not a neutral ‘meditation.’
→ mananākhya = ‘called mentation,’ referring to the afflicted manas characterized by constant mentation.
trbh [172]kliṣṭaṃ mano nityaṃ mananātmakatvān mananākhyam
The afflicted mind is called manana because its nature is constant mentation.
The word vijñapti is not used here in the common sense ‘request/report’; Sthiramati defines it as the sixfold eye-and-so-on consciousness insofar as objects such as form appear.
→ vijñaptir viṣayasya = ‘presentation/manifestation of an object.’
trbh [173, 174]rūpādiviṣayapratyavabhāsatvāc cakṣurādivijñānaṃ ṣaṭprakāram api viṣayavijñaptiḥ
Because objects such as form appear, the sixfold consciousness beginning with eye-consciousness is also viṣaya-vijñapti.
Translate tatra as ‘among these’ rather than simply ‘there.’ The ālaya-named consciousness is identified as the vipāka-pariṇāma.
→ tatra points back to the just-mentioned threefold pariṇāma; the second hemistich identifies the vipāka-pariṇāma.
trbh [179, 180, 181]tatreti yo 'yam anantaroktas trividhaḥ pariṇāmaḥ ālayākhyam ity ālayavijñānasaṃjñakaṃ yad vijñānaṃ sa vipākapariṇāmaḥ
‘Tatra’ means this just-mentioned threefold transformation. ‘Ālayākhyam’ means: the consciousness that has the designation ālaya-vijñāna is the vipāka-transformation.
The English ‘storehouse/repository’ is chosen because Sthiramati glosses ālaya as sthāna, ‘place/support,’ and explains it as that in which all dharmas are bound as effects and which is bound to all dharmas as cause.
→ ālaya = repository/storehouse, not just a house or dwelling.
trbh [183, 184]sarvasāṃkleśikadharmabījasthānatvād ālayaḥ ālayaḥ sthānam iti paryāyau
It is ālaya because it is the place of the seeds of all defiled dharmas. Ālaya and sthāna are synonyms.
trbh [185, 186]atha vālīyante upanibadhyante 'smin sarvadharmāḥ kāryabhāvena tad vālīyate upanibadhyate kāraṇabhāvena sarvadharmeṣv ity ālayaḥ
Or, all dharmas are lodged/bound in it in the mode of effects, and it is lodged/bound in all dharmas in the mode of cause; therefore it is ālaya.
The translation ‘consciousness’ is supported by Sthiramati’s verbal gloss vijānāti iti vijñānam.
→ vijñāna = consciousness/discernment.
trbh [187]vijānātīti vijñānam
It is vijñāna because it cognizes/discerns.
Do not translate sarvabījakam as the noun ‘every seed’ or as merely ‘seed-like’; it qualifies ālayākhyam vijñānam as possessing/supporting all seeds.
→ sarvabījakam = all-seeded, i.e. the support of the seeds of all dharmas.
trbh [189]sarvadharmabījāśrayatvāt sarvabījakam
It is sarvabījaka because it is the support of the seeds of all dharmas.
