Triṃśikā (Vasubandhu) with Sthiramati's bhāṣya · 29

acitto 'nupalambo 'sau jñānaṃ lokottarañ ca tat āśrayasya parāvṛttir dvidhādauṣṭhulyahānitaḥ

Translation. It is without grasping mind (acitta), non-apprehension (anupalamba); and that is world-transcending knowledge (lokottara-jñāna). Then comes the turning-about of the basis (āśraya-parāvṛtti), through the abandonment of the twofold unwieldiness (dauṣṭhulya).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Translate acitta not as “inconceivable” or merely “unconscious,” but as lacking grasper-mind; translate anupalamba as non-apprehension of the grāhya object.
→ acitta = absence of apprehender-mind; anupalamba = non-apprehension of the grasped object
trbh [1507, 1508]grāhakacittābhāvād grāhyārthānupalambhāc cācitto 'nupalambho 'sau
Because of the absence of apprehender-mind and because of non-apprehension of the object-to-be-grasped, ‘that is acitta and anupalambha.’
Render lokottara as “world-transcending/supramundane,” not merely “extraordinary,” because Sthiramati derives it from having crossed beyond the world through non-worldly, non-conceptual occurrence.
→ lokottara = world-transcending supramundane knowledge
trbh [1509, 1510]anucitatvāl loke samudācārābhāvān nirvikalpatvāc ca lokād uttīrṇam iti jñānaṃ lokottarañ ca tad iti
Because it is not customary, because it does not occur in the world, and because it is non-conceptual, it has crossed beyond the world; therefore, ‘that is supramundane knowledge.’
Take jñānam, lokottaram, and tat as nominatives: ‘that is supramundane knowledge,’ not as an accusative object.
→ jñānam lokottaram ca tat is a neuter nominative predication
trbh [1510, 1511]jñānaṃ lokottarañ ca tad iti tasya jñānasyānantaraṃ āśrayasya parāvṛttir bhavatīti jñāpanārtham āha
‘And that is supramundane knowledge’; to make known that after that knowledge the turning-about of the basis occurs, he says ...
Translate āśrayasya parāvṛttiḥ as the turning-about of the basis, with the basis specifically identified as the all-seed ālaya-vijñāna, and construe it as occurring after the lokottara jñāna.
→ āśraya = sarvabījaka ālayavijñāna; parāvṛtti follows that knowledge
trbh [1511, 1513]tasya jñānasyānantaraṃ āśrayasya parāvṛttir bhavatīti jñāpanārtham āha āśrayasya parāvṛttir iti āśrayo 'tra sarvabījakam ālayavijñānam
To indicate that after that knowledge the turning-about of the basis occurs, he says ‘the turning-about of the basis.’ Here ‘basis’ is the all-seed ālaya-vijñāna.
Render parāvṛtti as Yogācāra ‘turning-about/transformation,’ not merely physical turning or reversal.
→ parāvṛtti is a transformative turning involving cessation of the old dispositions and presence of fitness, dharmakāya, and nondual knowledge
trbh [1514, 1516]tasya parāvṛttir yā dauṣṭhulyavipākadvayavāsanābhāvena nivṛttau satyāṃ karmaṇyatādharmakāyādvayajñānabhāvena parāvṛttiḥ
Its turning-about is the turning-about in which, when cessation has occurred through the absence of the latent impressions of the two—dauṣṭhulya and vipāka—there is the presence of fitness, dharma-body, and nondual knowledge.
Construe dvidhā dauṣṭhulya-hānitaḥ as ‘through abandonment of the twofold dauṣṭhulya,’ not as a vague twofold abandonment or merely twofold parāvṛtti.
→ dvidhā qualifies dauṣṭhulya: the twofold dauṣṭhulya of the kleśa- and jñeya-obstructions
trbh [1519, 1521]dvidhādauṣṭhulyahānitaḥ dvidheti kleśāvaraṇadauṣṭhulyaṃ jñeyāvaraṇadauṣṭhulyañ ca dauṣṭhulyam
‘Through abandonment of twofold dauṣṭhulya.’ ‘Twofold’ means the dauṣṭhulya of the afflictive obstruction and the dauṣṭhulya of the obstruction concerning what is to be known.
trbh [1529]dvidhā āvaraṇabhedena sottarā niruttarā cāśrayaparāvṛttir uktā
By the distinction of the obstructions, āśraya-parāvṛtti is said to be twofold: with a higher remainder and unsurpassed.
Translate dauṣṭhulya with the technical sense ‘unwieldiness/unfitness,’ not merely ‘grossness’ or ‘badness.’
→ dauṣṭhulya = the basis’s akarmaṇyatā and the seeds of the two obstructions
trbh [1521, 1523]dauṣṭhulyam āśrayasyākarmaṇyatā tat punaḥ kleśajñeyāvaraṇayor bījam
Dauṣṭhulya is the basis’s lack of fitness/workability; that, again, is the seed of the afflictive and knowable-obstructions.
Take hānitaḥ as ablatival-causal: the turning-about is obtained through the abandonment of the twofold dauṣṭhulya.
→ hānitaḥ = because of/from abandonment
trbh [1517, 1519]sā punar āśrayaparāvṛttiḥ kasya prahāṇāt prāpyate ata āha dvidhādauṣṭhulyahānitaḥ
Again, by the abandoning of what is that turning-about of the basis obtained? Therefore he says: ‘through abandonment of twofold dauṣṭhulya.’
trbh [1525, 1528]āśrayaparāvṛttiḥ śrāvakādigatadauṣṭhulyahānitaś ca prāpyate yad āha vimuktikāya iti bodhisattvagatadauṣṭhulyahānitaś ca prāpyate yad āha dharmākhyo 'yaṃ mahāmuner iti
Āśraya-parāvṛtti is obtained through abandonment of dauṣṭhulya belonging to śrāvakas and so on—hence ‘liberation-body’; and it is obtained through abandonment of dauṣṭhulya belonging to bodhisattvas—hence ‘this is called dharma [body] of the Great Sage.’
