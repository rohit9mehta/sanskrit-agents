Triṃśikā (Vasubandhu) with Sthiramati's bhāṣya · 21

paratantrasvabhāvas tu vikalpaḥ pratyayodbhavaḥ niṣpannas tasya pūrveṇa sadā rahitatā tu yā

Translation. But the dependent nature (paratantra-svabhāva) is construction (vikalpa), born of conditions (pratyaya). The perfected nature (pariniṣpanna-svabhāva) is its always being devoid of the former— the imagined grasper-and-grasped.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Translate the opening as ‘the dependent nature is construction.’ The bhāṣya says that vikalpa states the paratantra’s own form and pratyayodbhava states the reason for the designation paratantra.
→ paratantrasvabhāvaḥ is the dependent nature being defined as vikalpa, not merely ‘a construction whose nature is dependent.’
trbh [1320, 1321, 1322]atra ca vikalpa iti paratantrasvarūpam āha pratyayodbhava ity anenāpi paratantrābhidhānapravṛttinimittam āha
Here, by ‘vikalpa’ he states the dependent’s own form; by ‘born from conditions’ too he states the basis for applying the designation ‘dependent.’
Render vikalpa as ‘construction’ and identify its scope as citta-caittas, not as ordinary ‘alternative/option.’
→ vikalpa is Yogācāra constructive imagination/false construction, concretely the mind and mental factors of the three realms.
trbh [1323, 1324, 1325, 1326]tatra parikalpaḥ kuśalākuśalāvyākṛtabhedabhinnās traidhātukāś cittacaittāḥ yathoktam abhūtaparikalpas tu cittacaittās tridhātukā iti
There, construction is the mind and mental factors belonging to the three realms, differentiated as wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate. As has been said: ‘False construction is the mind and mental factors of the three realms.’
Read pratyaya in its Buddhist causal sense, because Sthiramati glosses it with hetupratyaya and explains paratantra as having its self-attainment bound to other causes and conditions.
→ pratyayodbhava means ‘born/produced from causes and conditions.’
trbh [1327, 1328, 1329]parair hetupratyayais tantryata iti paratantra utpādyata ity arthaḥ svato 'nyahetupratyayapratibaddhātmalābha iti yāvaduktaṃ bhavati
It is paratantra because it is governed by other causes and conditions; the meaning is ‘produced.’ In short, its acquisition of its own being is bound to causes and conditions other than itself.
Translate niṣpannaḥ as ‘the perfected nature,’ since the commentary introduces the question about pariniṣpanna and concludes with pariniṣpannasvabhāvaḥ; its mode is avikārapariniṣpatti, unchanging consummation.
→ niṣpannaḥ is the pariniṣpanna/pariniṣpannasvabhāva, the perfected nature, not an ordinary produced result.
trbh [1331, 1332, 1333]pariniṣpannaḥ katham ity ata āha niṣpannas tasya pūrveṇa sadā rahitatā tu yā avikārapariniṣpattyā sa pariniṣpannaḥ
How is the perfected to be stated? Therefore he says: ‘The perfected is whatever is its always being devoid of the former.’ Because of unchanging consummation, it is perfected.
trbh [1340]sa pariniṣpannasvabhāvaḥ
That is the perfected nature.
Translate ‘its being devoid of the former’ as the dependent nature’s being devoid of the imagined subject–object duality, not as a temporal phrase ‘by/before the previous one.’
→ tasya refers to the paratantra; pūrveṇa refers to the former parikalpita, specifically the imagined grasper–grasped structure in vikalpa.
trbh [1334, 1335, 1336, 1337, 1338]tasyeti paratantrasya pūrveṇeti parikalpitena tasmin vikalpe grāhyagrāhakabhāvaḥ parikalpitaḥ tathā hi tasmin vikalpe grāhyagrāhakatvam avidyamānam eva parikalpyata iti parikalpitam ucyate
‘Of that’ means of the dependent; ‘by the former’ means by the imagined. In that construction the state of grasped and grasper is imagined. For so it is: in that construction, grasped-and-grasper-hood, though precisely nonexistent, is imagined; therefore it is called imagined.
Render rahitatā as ‘being devoid/absence’ and sadā as ‘always,’ strengthened by the bhāṣya’s sarvakālam and atyantarahitatā.
→ sadā rahitatā is constant and absolute devoidness/absence of the grasper–grasped in the dependent nature.
trbh [1339, 1340]tena grāhyagrāhakeṇa paratantrasya sadā sarvakālam atyantarahitatā yā sa pariniṣpannasvabhāvaḥ
The constant, at-all-times, absolute devoidness of the dependent from that grasped-and-grasper—that is the perfected nature.
