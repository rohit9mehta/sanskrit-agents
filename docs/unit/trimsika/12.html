How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The compound is not a tatpuruṣa such as ‘doubt about a view of conceit’; Sthiramati treats māna, dṛg, and vicikitsā as separate items and then sums the preceding discussion as the six kleśas.
→ mānadṛgvicikitsāḥ is an enumerative dvandva: ‘conceit, views, and doubt’.
trbh [715]mānaḥ
‘Māna.’
trbh [752]dṛg iti sāmānyanirdeśe 'pi kleśādhikārāt pañcaiva kleśātmikāḥ
Although ‘dṛg’ is stated generally, because the topic is the afflictions, only the five that are afflictive in nature are meant.
trbh [767, 768]vicikitsā karmaphalasatyaratneṣu vimatiḥ
‘Vicikitsā’: indecision regarding karma, its fruits, the truths, and the jewels.
trbh [772]uktāḥ ṣaṭkleśās tadanantaroddiṣṭās tv idānīm upakleśā vaktavyā ity ata āha
The six afflictions have been stated; now the minor afflictions mentioned immediately after them are to be explained.
The English ‘conceit’ is chosen over generic ‘measure’, ‘opinion’, or ‘notion’ because the commentary defines māna by mental elevation and self-superiority.
→ māna = conceit/pride, specifically elevation of mind.
trbh [716, 717, 718, 719, 720, 721]māno hi nāma sarva eva satkāyadṛṣṭisamāśrayeṇa pravartate sa punaś cittasyonnatilakṣaṇaḥ tathā hy ātmātmīyabhāvaṃ skandheṣv adhyāropyāyam aham idaṃ mamety ātmānaṃ tena tena viśeṣeṇonnamayati anyebhyo 'dhikaṃ manyate
All that is called māna proceeds dependent on personality-view. It is characterized by elevation of mind: having superimposed self and what belongs to self on the aggregates, one raises oneself in this or that respect, thinking ‘I am this; this is mine,’ and thinks oneself superior to others.
Although the verse uses the general word dṛg, the commentary restricts it by the context of kleśa to the five kleśa-natured dṛṣṭis beginning with satkāyadṛṣṭi, explicitly excluding mundane right view and supramundane view.
→ dṛg = the five afflictive views, not ordinary seeing or right view.
trbh [752, 753, 754]dṛg iti sāmānyanirdeśe 'pi kleśādhikārāt pañcaiva kleśātmikāḥ satkāyadṛṣṭyādikā dṛṣṭayaḥ saṃbadhyante na laukikī samyagdṛṣṭir anāsravā vā
Although ‘dṛg’ is stated generally, since the topic is afflictions, only the five views whose nature is afflictive, beginning with personality-view, are connected here—not mundane right view or the uncontaminated one.
The commentary defines vicikitsā as vimati, indecision, specifically with respect to karma, fruits, truths, and jewels; hence not medical inquiry or mere curiosity.
→ vicikitsā = doubt/indecision regarding Buddhist doctrinal objects.
trbh [767, 768, 769, 770, 771]vicikitsā karmaphalasatyaratneṣu vimatiḥ vividhā matir vimatiḥ syān na syād iti prajñātaś ceyaṃ jātyantaram evoktā
‘Vicikitsā’: indecision regarding karma, its fruits, the truths, and the jewels. ‘Indecision’ is divided thinking—‘it may be, it may not be’; and this has been stated as a distinct kind from prajñā.
The bhāṣya line quotes īrṣyātha without r; metrically and syntactically this is the nominative īrṣyā followed by the particle atha introducing the next item.
→ īrṣyātha is parsed as īrṣyā + atha, ‘envy; then …’, not as īrṣyārtha-.
trbh [774]mrakṣaḥ pradāśa īrṣyātha mātsaryaṃ saha māyayā
Concealment, spite, envy—then stinginess together with deceit.
The commentary introduces the quoted sequence as the upakleśas now to be explained. Therefore mātsaryam, though identical in form to an accusative, is a neuter nominative in the list, and saha māyayā is comitative: ‘together with deceit’.
→ mrakṣaḥ, pradāśaḥ, īrṣyā, and mātsaryam are nominative catalogue items; māyayā is instrumental governed by saha.
trbh [772]uktāḥ ṣaṭkleśās tadanantaroddiṣṭās tv idānīm upakleśā vaktavyā ity ata āha
The six afflictions have been stated; now the minor afflictions mentioned immediately after them are to be explained.
