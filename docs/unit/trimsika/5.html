Triṃśikā (Vasubandhu) with Sthiramati's bhāṣya · 5

tasya vyāvṛttir arhattve tad āśritya pravartate | tadālambaṃ manonāma vijñānaṃ mananātmakam

Translation. Its cessation is in arhatship (arhattva). Relying on that—the storehouse consciousness (ālayavijñāna)—it proceeds. Having that as its object-support (ālambana), it is a consciousness (vijñāna) named mind (manas), whose nature is cogitation (manana).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
tasya vyāvṛttir arhattve is not translated as an abstract ‘exclusion of that from arhatship’ or as a merely logical ‘distinction’; it states that ālayavijñāna has ceased/turned away in the state of arhatship.
→ ‘Its cessation/turning-away is in arhatship’—the genitive tasya refers to ālayavijñāna, and vyāvṛtti is its cessation in the arhat-state.
trbh [345, 346, 349, 350, 351, 352]kiṃ punar arhattvam yadyogād arhann ity ucyate kṣayajñānānutpādajñānalābhāt tasyāṃ hy avasthāyām ālayavijñānāśritadauṣṭhulyaniravaśeṣaprahāṇād ālayavijñānaṃ vyāvṛttaṃ bhavati saiva cārhadavasthā
What is arhatship? It is that by connection with which one is called an arhat: by the attainment of the knowledge of destruction and the knowledge of non-arising. For in that state, because of the complete abandonment of the coarseness resting on ālayavijñāna, ālayavijñāna has turned away/ceased; that alone is the arhat-state.
The pronoun tad is not allowed to refer to arhattva, vyāvṛtti, or an unspecified prior item; Sthiramati explicitly binds it to ālayavijñāna. The verb is rendered ‘proceeds/arises’, not ‘sets itself into activity’ in a transitive sense.
→ tad in tad āśritya refers to ālayavijñāna, and pravartate means that the defiled manas arises/proceeds in a continuum relying on it.
trbh [363, 364, 365]tad āśritya pravartata iti tacchabdenālayavijñānam abhisaṃbadhyate tadvāsanāśrayo hy ālayavijñānam atas tad āśritya pravartate santānenotpadyata ity arthaḥ
In ‘relying on that it proceeds’, the word ‘that’ is connected with ālayavijñāna. For ālayavijñāna is the basis of its latent impressions; therefore ‘relying on that it proceeds’ means ‘it arises in a continuum’.
trbh [366, 367, 368]atha vā yasmin dhātau bhūmau vālayavijñānaṃ vipākas tad api kliṣṭaṃ manas taddhātukaṃ tadbhūmikaṃ ceti tatpratibaddhavṛttitvāt tad āśritya pravartate
Or else, in whatever realm or level ālayavijñāna exists as maturation, that defiled manas too belongs to that realm and that level; because its occurrence is bound to that, it proceeds relying on that.
The compound is read as an adjectival compound qualifying vijñānam/manas, and its tad again points to ālayavijñāna; ālamba is interpreted in the Yogācāra technical sense of object-support, not merely a physical prop.
→ tadālambam means ‘having ālayavijñāna as its object-support (ālambana)’.
trbh [369, 370]tadālambam ity ālayavijñānālambanam eva satkāyadṛṣṭyādibhiḥ saṃprayogād ahaṃ mamety ālayavijñānālambanatvāt
‘Having that as its object’ means precisely having ālayavijñāna as its object, because it is associated with satkāyadṛṣṭi and the others, since in thinking ‘I’ and ‘mine’ it has ālayavijñāna as its object.
trbh [371, 372, 373, 374]kathaṃ punar yata eva cittād utpadyate tad eva tadālambanaṃ bhavati yathā tad anicchatāṃ keṣāṃcit kasyāñcid avasthāyāṃ yata eva cittān manovijñānam utpadyate tadālambanam eva tad utpadyate
How, then, can the very mind from which it arises be what it has as object? Just as, for some who do not accept that, in a certain state the mental consciousness arises from a mind and arises with that very mind as its object.
manonāma is not two separate syntactic words ‘mind, indeed/name’; it is a designation of the second transformation as a consciousness called manas, distinguished from ālayavijñāna and the active cognitions.
→ manonāma vijñānam means ‘a consciousness whose name is manas’.
trbh [375, 376, 377]manonāma vijñānam iti mana iti nāma ākhyā yasya vijñānasya tad ālayavijñānam āśritya pravarte tadālambanaṃ ca manonāmety anenālayavijñānāt pravṛttivijñānāc ca vyavacchinatti
‘A consciousness named manas’ means: that consciousness whose name or appellation is ‘manas’. By saying that it relies on ālayavijñāna, has that as its object, and is named manas, he distinguishes it from ālayavijñāna and from the active consciousnesses.
The final adjective states the svabhāva/nature of this consciousness; it is not merely an incidental ‘thoughtful’ quality. The derivation of the name manas is from manana by a nirukta-style explanation.
→ mananātmakam means ‘having cogitation/reflection as its nature’, explaining why it is called manas.
trbh [378, 379, 381, 382]tat punaḥ kiṃ svabhāvam ity āha mananātmakam iti mananātmakatvān mana ity ucyate nairūktena vidhinā vijñānasvarūpatvād avaśyaṃ tac caittaiḥ saṃprayujyate
What, then, is its own nature? He says: ‘having cogitation as its nature.’ Because it has cogitation as its nature, it is called manas by the etymological method. Since it has the nature of consciousness, it is necessarily associated with mental factors.
Although this consciousness relies on and takes ālayavijñāna as object, it is not identified with ālayavijñāna; the commentary introduces it as the second transformation called manana and explicitly distinguishes it from both ālayavijñāna and pravṛttivijñāna.
→ The second half names the defiled manas as the second transformation, not ālayavijñāna itself.
trbh [353, 354]uktaḥ savibhaṅgo vipākapariṇāmaḥ idānīṃ mananākhyāṃ dvitīyaṃ pariṇāmam āha
The maturation-transformation has been explained with its divisions. Now he states the second transformation, called manana.
trbh [357, 358, 359, 360]tatra yathā cakṣurādivijñānānāṃ cakṣurādaya āśrayatvena rūpādayaś cālambanatvena prasiddhā naiva kliṣṭasya manasa āśraya ālambanaṃ vā prasiddham na ca vijñānam āśrayālambananirapekṣaṃ yujyata ity ataḥ kliṣṭasya manasa āśrayālambanapratipādanārthaṃ niruktipratipādanārthañ cāha
Just as, for eye-consciousness and the rest, the eye and so on are established as supports and forms and so on as objects, the support or object of the defiled manas is not established in that way. And a consciousness cannot be without dependence on a support and an object; therefore, in order to explain the support and object of the defiled manas, and to explain the etymology, he says...
trbh [375, 376, 377]manonāma vijñānam iti mana iti nāma ākhyā yasya vijñānasya tad ālayavijñānam āśritya pravarte tadālambanaṃ ca manonāmety anenālayavijñānāt pravṛttivijñānāc ca vyavacchinatti
