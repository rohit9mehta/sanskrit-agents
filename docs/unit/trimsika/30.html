Triṃśikā (Vasubandhu) with Sthiramati's bhāṣya · 30

sa evānāsravo dhātur acintyaḥ kuśalo dhruvaḥ | sukho vimuktikāyo 'sau dharmākhyo 'yaṃ mahāmuneḥ

Translation. That very basis-transformation (āśraya-parāvṛtti) is the uncontaminated element-source (anāsrava dhātu)— inconceivable, wholesome, constant, blissful. That, for the śrāvakas, is the liberation-body (vimuktikāya); this, of the Great Sage (mahāmuni), is called the Dharma-body (dharmakāya).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The demonstrative does not refer simply to the immediately preceding word or to an abstract ‘that’ left unspecified; Sthiramati explicitly glosses it as the āśraya-parāvṛtti-rūpa, the transformation of the basis discussed just before.
→ saḥ eva = ‘that very basis-transformation (āśraya-parāvṛtti)’
trbh [1511, 1512, 1513, 1514, 1515, 1516]tasya jñānasyānantaraṃ āśrayasya parāvṛttir bhavatīti jñāpanārtham āha āśrayasya parāvṛttir iti āśrayo 'tra sarvabījakam ālayavijñānam tasya parāvṛttir yā dauṣṭhulyavipākadvayavāsanābhāvena nivṛttau satyāṃ karmaṇyatādharmakāyādvayajñānabhāvena parāvṛttiḥ
To indicate that, immediately after that knowledge, there is a transformation of the basis, he says ‘transformation of the basis.’ Here the basis is the store-consciousness with all seeds. Its transformation is the transformation in which, when there is cessation through absence of the impressions of both badness and maturation, there is the state of fitness, the dharma-body, and non-dual cognition.
trbh [1537, 1538, 1539]sa evānāsravo dhātur iti sa evāśrayaparāvṛttirūpaḥ anāsravo dhātur ity ucyate nirdauṣṭhulyatvāt
As to ‘that very thing is the uncontaminated element’: that very thing, being of the nature of basis-transformation, is called the uncontaminated element because it is free from badness.
‘Anāsrava’ is defined as free from all āsravas, because it is free from dauṣṭhulya; ‘dhātu’ is not left as a bare cosmological element but is glossed in a causal sense, as the cause/source of noble dharmas.
→ anāsravo dhātuḥ = ‘uncontaminated element-source’
trbh [1539, 1540, 1541, 1542, 1543]anāsravo dhātur ity ucyate nirdauṣṭhulyatvāt sarvāsravavigata ity anāsravaḥ āryadharmahetutvād dhātuḥ hetvartho hy atra dhātuśabdaḥ
It is called the uncontaminated element because it is free from badness. ‘Uncontaminated’ means devoid of all outflows. It is ‘dhātu’ because it is the cause of noble dharmas; here the word dhātu has the meaning ‘cause.’
The commentary grounds acintya in three reasons: it is not accessible to reasoning, it is to be known personally, and there is no example for it.
→ acintyaḥ = ‘inconceivable’ in the sense of beyond reasoning and analogy, personally realized
trbh [1544]acintyas tarkāgocaratvāt pratyātmavedyatvād dṛṣṭāntābhāvāc ca
It is inconceivable because it is beyond the range of reasoning, because it is to be known individually/personally, and because there is no example.
The term is not rendered as ‘clever’ or merely ‘skilful’; Sthiramati explains it by purity of object, safety, and constitution out of uncontaminated dharmas.
→ kuśalaḥ = ‘wholesome’
trbh [1545, 1546]kuśalo viśuddhālambanatvāt kṣematvād anāsravadharmamayatvāc ca
It is wholesome because its object is purified, because it is secure, and because it consists of uncontaminated dharmas.
Sthiramati links dhruva with nityatva and akṣayatā, and derives sukha from that very permanence by the contrast ‘whatever is impermanent is suffering; this is permanent.’
→ dhruvaḥ and sukhaḥ = ‘constant’ and ‘blissful’ because permanent/inexhaustible
trbh [1547, 1548, 1549, 1550]dhruvo nityatvād akṣayatayā sukho nityatvād eva yad anityaṃ tad duḥkhaṃ ayaṃ ca nitya iti tasmāt sukhaḥ
It is constant because it is permanent, because it is inexhaustible. It is blissful precisely because it is permanent: whatever is impermanent is suffering, but this is permanent; therefore it is blissful.
The verse-word is not treated as a generic synonym of the Buddha’s dharma-body. Sthiramati assigns vimuktikāya to the attainment of basis-transformation through the abandonment of dauṣṭhulya in śrāvakas, specifically through abandoning the kleśāvaraṇa.
→ vimuktikāyaḥ = the liberation-body associated with śrāvakas’ abandonment of the afflictive obstruction
trbh [1525, 1526, 1527, 1528, 1529]āśrayaparāvṛttiḥ śrāvakādigatadauṣṭhulyahānitaś ca prāpyate yad āha vimuktikāya iti bodhisattvagatadauṣṭhulyahānitaś ca prāpyate yad āha dharmākhyo 'yaṃ mahāmuner iti dvidhā āvaraṇabhedena sottarā niruttarā cāśrayaparāvṛttir uktā
Basis-transformation is also attained through abandoning the badness present in śrāvakas and so on; hence he says ‘liberation-body.’ It is also attained through abandoning the badness present in bodhisattvas; hence he says ‘this, of the Great Sage, is called Dharma.’ Thus, because of the difference of obstructions, basis-transformation has been stated as twofold: with something higher still, and unsurpassed.
trbh [1551]kleśāvaraṇaprahāṇāc chrāvakāṇāṃ vimuktikāyaḥ
Because of the abandonment of the afflictive obstruction, it is the liberation-body of the śrāvakas.
Although the compound dharmākhya literally means ‘called Dharma,’ Sthiramati’s explanation supplies the understood kāya and identifies it as the Mahāmuni’s dharmakāya, attained through the abandonment of both afflictive and cognitive obstructions. Mahāmuni is explicitly glossed as the Buddha Bhagavān.
→ dharmākhyaḥ ayaṃ mahāmuneḥ = ‘this, of the Great Sage, is called the Dharma-body’
trbh [1552, 1553, 1554, 1555]sa evāśrayaparāvṛttilakṣaṇo dharmākhyo 'py ucyate mahāmuner bhūmipāramitābhāvanayā kleśajñeyāvaraṇaprahāṇāt āśrayaparāvṛttisamudāgamān mahāmuner dharmakāya ity ucyate
That same thing, characterized as basis-transformation, is also called ‘Dharma’ for the Great Sage. Because, through cultivating the stages and perfections, there is abandonment of the afflictive and cognitive obstructions and the attainment of basis-transformation, it is called the dharma-body of the Great Sage.
trbh [1556, 1557]saṃsārāparityāgāt tadanupasaṃkleśatvād bodhisattvānāṃ sarvadharmavibhutvalābhataś ca dharmakāya ity ucyate
Because there is no abandonment of saṃsāra, because there is no defilement by it, and because bodhisattvas obtain mastery over all dharmas, it is called the dharma-body.
trbh [1558]mahāmuner iti paramauneyayogād buddho bhagavān mahāmunir iti
‘Of the Great Sage’: because of possessing supreme sagehood, the Buddha, the Blessed One, is the Great Sage.
