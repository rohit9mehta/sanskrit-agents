Triṃśikā (Vasubandhu) with Sthiramati's bhāṣya · 27

vijñaptimātram evedam ity api hy upalambhataḥ | sthāpayann agrataḥ kiñcit tanmātre nāvatiṣṭhate

Translation. For even as he apprehends—grasps and pictures— “This is mere representation (vijñapti-mātra) alone,” mentally setting some support (ālambana) before him, he does not abide in that mere [representation] (tanmātra).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Read the verse as a corrective to ābhimāna based on mere hearing; this controls the concessive “even” in the translation.
→ The verse describes a conceited learner who thinks he is established in purified representation-only, not someone who has genuinely attained that state.
trbh [1472, 1473]iti atha vā yaḥ punar ābhimānikaḥ śrutamātrakeṇa jānīyad ahaṃ vijñaptimātratāyāṃ śuddhāyāṃ sthita iti tadgrahavyudāsārtham āha
Or else: someone conceited might, from mere hearing, think, “I am established in purified representation-only”; to remove that grasp, he says...
trbh [1474, 1475]vijñaptimātram evedam ity api hy upalambhata ity ādi
...the verse beginning “Even [thinking] ‘This is mere representation’—for he apprehends...”
Translate vijñaptimātram evedam as “This is mere representation alone,” not as a mere quantity of information or as a claim compatible with external objects.
→ vijñapti-mātra means objectless “mere representation,” excluding a bāhyārtha, an external object.
trbh [1476]vijñaptimātram evedaṃ artharahitaṃ na bāhyo 'rtho 'stīty evam
“This is mere representation, objectless; there is no external object”—in this way...
Render with “as he apprehends—grasps and pictures,” while recognizing that this apprehension is precisely what has not yet been abandoned.
→ upalambhataḥ denotes problematic apprehension/grasping/mental picturing and supplies the reason he does not abide in representation-only.
trbh [1477]upalambhato grahaṇataḥ citrīkaraṇata ity artho
upalambhataḥ means grahaṇataḥ, “from/by grasping,” and citrīkaraṇataḥ, “from/by making a mental image.”
trbh [1481]vijñānopalambhāprahāṇāt
Because the apprehension of consciousness has not been abandoned.
Take agrataḥ as “in front/facing,” sthāpayan as a mental act, and kiñcit as one among the many Yogācāra supports, not a real external thing placed physically before the practitioner.
→ agrataḥ sthāpayan kiñcit means mentally setting some practice-support before himself.
trbh [1477]ity artho 'grata ity abhimukhaṃ
agrataḥ means abhimukham, “facing/in front.”
trbh [1478]sthāpayann iti yathāśrutaṃ manasā
sthāpayan means mentally, just as it has been heard/taught.
trbh [1479, 1480]bahuprakāratvād yogācārālambanānāṃ kiṃcid ity āhāsthisaṃkalikaṃ vā vinīlakaṃ vā vipūyakaṃ vā vipaḍumakaṃ vā vyādhmātakādikaṃ vā
Because Yogācāra supports are of many kinds, by “something” he means, for example, a skeleton-support, a livid one, a festering one, a discolored one, a bloated one, and so on.
Connect tanmātra back to vijñaptimātratā rather than reading it as a Sāṃkhya subtle element or as “in that object alone.”
→ tanmātre is the locative “in that mere [representation],” i.e. in representation-only, and nāvatiṣṭhate is failure to be established there.
trbh [1473]vijñaptimātratāyāṃ śuddhāyāṃ sthita
Established in purified representation-only.
trbh [1480, 1481]tanmātre nāvatiṣṭhate vijñānopalambhāprahāṇāt
He does not abide in that mere [representation], because the apprehension of consciousness has not been abandoned.
Use the bhāṣya’s api hy upalambhataḥ rather than the vulgate-like hayu sequence.
→ The bhāṣya reading is api hi, yielding “for even,” with hi sandhied to hy before upalambhataḥ.
trbh [1474]vijñaptimātram evedam ity api hy upalambhata ity
“Even [thinking] ‘This is mere representation’—for he apprehends...”
