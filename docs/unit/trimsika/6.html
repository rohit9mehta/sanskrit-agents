Triṃśikā (Vasubandhu) with Sthiramati's bhāṣya · 6

kleśaiś caturbhiḥ sahitaṃ nivṛtāvyākṛtaiḥ sadā ātmadṛṣṭyātmamohātmamānātmasnehasaṃjñitaiḥ

Translation. Always, so long as it exists, this [mind (manas)] is conjoined (saṃprayukta) with four obscured-neutral afflictions (kleśa; nivṛtāvyākṛta): those called self-view (ātmadṛṣṭi), delusion about self (ātmamoha), self-conceit (ātmamāna), and self-love/attachment (ātmasneha).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Sthiramati's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Because verse 6 is elliptical, translate sahitam as predicated of the previously introduced manas/vijñāna, not of ālayavijñāna itself as the grammatical subject.
→ The supplied subject in translation is ‘this mind (manas)’, carried over from the previous verse.
trbh [417, 418, 419]āha ca avidyayā cātmadṛṣṭyā cāsmimānena tṛṣṇayā ebhiś caturbhiḥ saṃkliṣṭaṃ mananālakṣaṇaṃ manaḥ
And he says: ‘By ignorance, by self-view, by I-conceit, and by craving—by these four—the mind whose characteristic is mentation is defiled.’
The word excludes other caittas; it is not a general statement about all mental factors.
→ kleśaiḥ means ‘with afflictions’, a subset of mental factors.
trbh [386, 387, 388]caittā hi dviprakārāḥ kleśās tad anye ca tadanyebhyo vyavacchedārtham āha kleśair iti
Mental factors are of two kinds: afflictions and the others. To exclude those others, he says ‘with afflictions.’
Do not read this as a vague ‘fourfold’ epithet; it means four out of the six kleśas, since the mind is not associated with all six.
→ caturbhiḥ restricts the kleśas to four.
trbh [389, 390]kleśā api ṣaṭ na ca taiḥ saṃprayujyate 'taś caturbhir ity āha
The afflictions too are six; and it is not associated with those [all], therefore he says ‘with four.’
The commentary glosses sahita directly with saṃprayukta, so it is stronger and more technical than loose accompaniment.
→ sahitam is translated as ‘conjoined/associated’ in the technical sense of saṃprayoga.
trbh [391]sahitam iti saṃprayuktam
‘Sahitam’ means ‘associated/conjoined.’
trbh [401]yāvat tad asti tāvat taiḥ saṃprayuktam
As long as it exists, it is associated with them.
The four kleśas here are distinguished from akuśala kleśas; nivṛta is explained by being kliṣṭa, and avyākṛta by not being classed as wholesome or unwholesome.
→ nivṛtāvyākṛtaiḥ means ‘obscured-neutral’ or ‘afflicted yet ethically indeterminate’.
trbh [392, 393, 394, 395]kleśā api dvividhāḥ akuśalā nivṛtāvyākṛtāś ca akuśalebhyo viśeṣārtham āha nivṛtāvyākṛtair iti
Afflictions too are of two kinds: unwholesome and obscured-neutral. To distinguish them from the unwholesome ones, he says ‘obscured-neutral.’
trbh [396, 397, 398]na hi nivṛtena vijñānenākuśalānāṃ saṃprayogaḥ saṃbhavati nivṛtāḥ kliṣṭatvāt avyākṛtāḥ kuśalākuśalatvenāvyākaraṇāt
For there is no association of unwholesome [factors] with an obscured cognition. They are obscured because they are afflicted; they are indeterminate because they are not determined as wholesome or unwholesome.
The scope is temporal and constant: whenever that mind exists, it is associated with these four.
→ sadā is translated as ‘always, so long as it exists’.
trbh [399, 400, 401]sadeti sarvakālam yāvat tad asti tāvat taiḥ saṃprayuktam
‘Always’ means ‘at all times’: as long as it exists, it is associated with them.
The bhāṣya quotes ātmadṛṣṭi and glosses it by darśana and satkāyadṛṣṭi; the four names are self-view, delusion about self, self-conceit, and self-love/attachment.
→ Read ātmadṛṣṭi, not ātmaduṣṭi, and take the final compound as naming four specific kleśas.
trbh [402, 403]sāmānyanirdeśād viśeṣato na jñāyanta iti viśeṣato nirdiśati ātmadṛṣṭyātmamohātmamānātmasnehasaṃjñitaiḥ
Since from a general designation they are not known specifically, he states them specifically: ‘called self-view, self-delusion, self-conceit, and self-love.’
trbh [404, 405, 406, 407, 408, 409, 410]upādānaskandheṣv ātmeti darśanam ātmadṛṣṭiḥ satkāyadṛṣṭir ity arthaḥ moho 'jñānam ātmany ajñānam ātmamohaḥ ātmaviṣaye māna ātmamāno 'smimāna ity arthaḥ ātmani sneha ātmasneha ātmapremety arthaḥ
Seeing ‘self’ in the aggregates of clinging is self-view; the meaning is satkāyadṛṣṭi. Delusion is ignorance. Ignorance regarding self is self-delusion. Conceit with self as its object is self-conceit; the meaning is I-conceit. Affection/love toward self is self-love; the meaning is love for self.
Avoid a translation that implies the text affirms an ātman; the commentary frames these as confusion, self-view, conceit, and attachment concerning what is taken to be self.
→ The ‘self’ in the four names is not an accepted real self; in this context it is an object imagined as self, especially with respect to ālayavijñāna.
trbh [411, 412, 413, 414, 415, 416]tatrālayavijñānasvarūpe saṃmūḍhaḥ sann ālayavijñāne ātmadṛṣṭim utpādayati ātmadarśanād yā cittasyonnatiḥ so 'smimānaḥ etasmiṃs traye saty ātmābhimate vastuni yo 'bhiṣvaṅgaḥ sa ātmasnehaḥ
There, with regard to the own-nature of ālayavijñāna, being deluded, one produces self-view toward ālayavijñāna. The elevation of mind that arises from seeing self is I-conceit. When these three are present, the clinging to an object conceived as self is self-love.
