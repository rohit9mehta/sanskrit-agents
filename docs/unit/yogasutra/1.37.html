How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The sūtra compound is not merely ‘a passionless object’ nor ‘an object belonging to a passionless person’; Vyāsa takes the yogin’s citta to be colored by the ālambana consisting of vītarāga-citta. Thus viṣaya is read as meditative object/support, and the whole compound is an adjective of cittam.
→ vītarāgaviṣayaṃ = ‘whose object/support is the mind of one free from rāga’
ys [1]vītarāgacittālambanoparaktaṃ vā yoginaś cittaṃ sthitipadaṃ labhata iti.
Or, the yogin’s mind, tinged by the support consisting of the mind of one free from rāga, attains the state of steadiness. The printed labhata is taken as the expected labhate.
Although cittam is formally also accusative, the bhāṣya explicitly construes yoginaś cittaṃ as what attains sthitipada. Therefore the sūtra is elliptical: ‘the mind [becomes steady/attains steadiness]’, not an instruction with cittam as an object such as ‘meditate on the mind’.
→ cittam is nominative singular, the subject of the implied result
ys [1]yoginaś cittaṃ sthitipadaṃ labhata iti.
The yogin’s mind attains the state of steadiness; labhata is printed, probably for labhate.
The bhāṣya repeats vā in its paraphrase, so the particle continues the series of optional supports for stabilizing the mind.
→ vā = ‘or’, introducing another alternative means
ys [1]vītarāgacittālambanoparaktaṃ vā
Or, tinged by the support of the mind of one free from rāga.
In vītarāga, rāga is taken in its affective yogic sense, so vītarāga means ‘free from attachment/passion’, not simply ‘devoid of color/redness’. The commentary repeats the technical compound but does not define rāga here, so this decision rests on grammar and standard yogic usage rather than a fresh gloss in this line.
→ rāga = attachment/passion
