How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The adverb is not taken spatially as “elsewhere.” Vyāsa glosses the condition introduced by itaratra with vyutthāne, the state other than the restrained state of the preceding sūtra.
→ itaratra = “at other times / in the other condition,” namely vyutthāna.
ys [1]vyutthāne yāś cittavṛttayas tadaviśiṣṭavṛttiḥ puruṣaḥ.
In the emerged/active state, whatever mental modifications there are, the puruṣa’s functioning is not distinguished from those.
The first member of the compound is not rendered in the broad dictionary senses “conduct,” “livelihood,” or “usage”; Vyāsa explicitly identifies the relevant vṛtti-s as cittavṛttayaḥ.
→ vṛtti = citta-vṛtti, “mental modification.”
ys [1]vyutthāne yāś cittavṛttayas tadaviśiṣṭavṛttiḥ puruṣaḥ.
In the emerged/active state, whatever mental modifications there are, the puruṣa’s functioning is not distinguished from those.
Sārūpya is read as conformity/assimilation to those vṛtti-s, not as mere physical resemblance or as the later devotional liberation-grade. Vyāsa explains it through tad-aviśiṣṭa-vṛttiḥ, “having a functioning not distinguished from those.”
→ vṛtti-sārūpya = “conformity with the vṛtti-s,” a tatpuruṣa relation: vṛttīnām sārūpyam.
ys [1]vyutthāne yāś cittavṛttayas tadaviśiṣṭavṛttiḥ puruṣaḥ.
In the emerged/active state, whatever mental modifications there are, the puruṣa’s functioning is not distinguished from those.
The translation includes “[for the puruṣa]” because Vyāsa makes puruṣaḥ the bearer of the non-distinguished vṛtti and describes citta as the object/possession serving puruṣa, its master. This avoids making the sūtra say merely that the mind resembles its own modifications.
→ The implicit locus is the puruṣa, supplied from the commentary and context.

ys [3]cittam ayaskāntamaṇikalpaṃ saṃnidhimātropakāri dṛśyatvena svaṃ bhavati puruṣasya svāminaḥ.
Citta, like a magnet-stone, helpful by mere proximity, as something seen, becomes the own/possession of puruṣa, its master.
