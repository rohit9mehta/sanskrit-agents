tatpratiṣedhārtham ekatattvābhyāsaḥ

Translation. For the prevention of that [distraction, vikṣepa], there is cultivation (abhyāsa) of the mind resting on a single principle/object (eka-tattva).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Referent and sense of tat-pratiṣedha
→ tat in tatpratiṣedha refers to vikṣepa, and pratiṣedha is prevention/warding-off rather than logical denial or mere prohibition.
ys [1]vikṣepapratiṣedhārtham ekatattvāvalambanaṃ cittam abhyaset.
For the prevention of distraction, one should cultivate the mind resting on a single tattva.
Syntactic force of -artham
→ tatpratiṣedhārtham is an adverbial purpose accusative: ‘for the sake of preventing that [distraction].’
ys [1]vikṣepapratiṣedhārtham ekatattvāvalambanaṃ cittam abhyaset.
For the prevention of distraction, one should cultivate the mind resting on a single tattva.
Meaning and scope of eka-tattva
→ eka-tattva means a single principle/object serving as the mind’s support, not ‘the One Reality’ as a monistic doctrine.
ys [1]vikṣepapratiṣedhārtham ekatattvāvalambanaṃ cittam abhyaset.
One should cultivate the mind resting on a single tattva for the prevention of distraction.
ys [3]yadi punar idaṃ sarvataḥ pratyāhṛtyaikasminn arthe samādhīyate tadā bhavaty ekāgram ity ato na pratyarthaniyatam.
But if this [mind], withdrawn from all sides, is concentrated on one object, then it becomes one-pointed; therefore it is not fixed object-by-object.
Force of abhyāsaḥ
→ abhyāsaḥ is a practical prescription: one should cultivate/practice the citta in this one-object support.
ys [1]vikṣepapratiṣedhārtham ekatattvāvalambanaṃ cittam abhyaset.
For the prevention of distraction, one should cultivate the mind resting on a single tattva.
Commentarial background for ‘one-pointed’ practice
→ The practice presupposes one enduring mind that can be distracted or made one-pointed; it is not the automatic one-pointedness of momentary object-specific cognitions or of a mere stream.
ys [2]yasya tu pratyarthaniyataṃ pratyayamātraṃ kṣaṇikaṃ ca cittaṃ tasya sarvam eva cittam ekāgraṃ nāsty eva vikṣiptam.
For one who holds mind to be fixed to each object, mere cognition, and momentary, every mind would be one-pointed, and there would be no distracted mind at all.
ys [6]tasmād ekam anekārtham avasthitaṃ cittam iti.
Therefore the mind is one, has many objects, and is enduring.
ys [16]tasmād ekam anekārtham avasthitaṃ ca cittam.
Therefore the mind is one, has many objects, and is enduring.

Vyāsa's commentary on this unit

1vikṣepapratiṣedhārtham ekatattvāvalambanaṃ cittam abhyaset.
2yasya tu pratyarthaniyataṃ pratyayamātraṃ kṣaṇikaṃ ca cittaṃ tasya sarvam eva cittam ekāgraṃ nāsty eva vikṣiptam.
3yadi punar idaṃ sarvataḥ pratyāhṛtyaikasminn arthe samādhīyate tadā bhavaty ekāgram ity ato na pratyarthaniyatam.
4yo 'pi sadṛśapratyayapravāhena cittam ekāgraṃ manyate tasyaikāgratā yadi pravāhacittasya dharmas tadaikaṃ nāsti pravāhacittaṃ kṣaṇikatvāt.
5atha pravāhāṃśasyaiva pratyayasya dharmaḥ, sa sarvaḥ sadṛśapratyayapravāhī vā visadṛśapratyayapravāhī vā pratyarthaniyatatvād ekāgra eveti vikṣiptacittānupapattiḥ.
6tasmād ekam anekārtham avasthitaṃ cittam iti.
7yadi ca cittenaikenānanvitāḥ svabhāvabhinnāḥ pratyayā jāyerann atha katham anyapratyayadṛṣṭasyānyaḥ smartā bhavet.
8anyapratyayopacitasya ca karmāśayasyānyaḥ pratyaya upabhoktā bhavet.
9kathaṃcit samādhīyamānam apy etad gomayapāyasīyanyāyam ākṣipati.
10kiṃ ca svātmānubhavāpahnavaś cittasyānyatve prāpnoti.
11katham, yad aham adrākṣaṃ tat spṛśāmi yac cāsprākṣaṃ tat paśyāmīty aham iti pratyayaḥ sarvasya pratyayasya bhede sati pratyayiny abhedenopasthitaḥ.
12ekapratyayaviṣayo 'yam abhedātmāham iti pratyayaḥ katham atyantabhinneṣu citteṣu vartamānaḥ sāmānyam ekaṃ pratyayinam āśrayet.
13svānubhavagrāhyaś cāyam abhedātmāham iti pratyayaḥ.
14na ca pratyakṣasya māhātmyaṃ pramāṇāntareṇābhibhūyate.
15pramāṇāntaraṃ ca pratyakṣabalenaiva vyavahāraṃ labhate.
16tasmād ekam anekārtham avasthitaṃ ca cittam.
