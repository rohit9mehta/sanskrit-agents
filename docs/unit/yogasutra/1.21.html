tīvrasaṃvegānām āsannaḥ

Translation. For those whose ardor (saṃvega) is intense, the attainment of meditative concentration (samādhi), together with samādhi’s fruit, is close at hand.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The sūtra’s āsannaḥ, “near / close at hand,” is not left as a bare “it.” Vyāsa supplies what is close at hand: samādhilābhaḥ and samādhiphalam. The masculine singular āsannaḥ most directly agrees with samādhilābhaḥ, with the bhāṣya adding samādhi’s fruit as included in what comes about.
→ The implied subject/scope is “the attainment of samādhi and the fruit of samādhi.”
ys [1]samādhilābhaḥ samādhiphalaṃ ca bhavatīti.
“The attainment of samādhi and the fruit of samādhi come about.”
The genitive plural is construed as referring to practitioners, not to plural abstract “intense excitements.” This is represented morphologically as a substantivized bahuvrīhi: tīvraḥ saṃvegaḥ yeṣām. The bhāṣya’s supplied result—attainment of samādhi—fits persons possessing such ardor.
→ tīvrasaṃvegānām means “for/of those whose saṃvega is intense.”
ys [1]samādhilābhaḥ samādhiphalaṃ ca bhavatīti.
“The attainment of samādhi and the fruit of samādhi come about.”
MW allows both “violent agitation/excitement” and “vehemence/intensity” or “desire of emancipation” for saṃvega. Here Vyāsa connects it directly with samādhi-attainment and samādhi’s fruit, so “ardor” or “urgent impetus” is preferable to a merely psychological “agitation.”
→ saṃvega is rendered “ardor,” i.e. urgent soteriological impetus, not mere agitation.
