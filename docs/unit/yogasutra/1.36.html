How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The sūtra is not a bare naming of two things; the bhāṣya explicitly carries forward ‘pravṛttir utpannā manasaḥ sthitinibandhanī’ and then speaks of a singular pravṛttiḥ. Hence the translation supplies ‘cognitive activity’ and the longer predicate ‘once arisen, is a basis for the mind’s steadiness.’
→ Both viśokā and jyotiṣmatī qualify the same implied feminine subject, pravṛttiḥ, continued from 1.35.
ys [1]pravṛttir utpannā manasaḥ sthitinibandhanīty anuvartate.
‘The phrase “an arisen activity, a basis for the mind’s steadiness” is continued.’
ys [2]tatra sthitivaiśāradyāt pravṛttiḥ sūryendugrahamaṇiprabhārūpākāreṇa vikalpate.
‘There, through mastery of steadiness, the activity takes shape in the form of the radiance of sun, moon, planet, and jewel.’
Although vā stands after viśokā, the bhāṣya’s anuvṛtti of the prior pravṛtti-construction makes the whole expression one alternative in the series of sūtras, not ‘sorrowless or luminous’ as two mutually exclusive choices.
→ vā introduces this whole sorrowless, luminous activity as an alternative means of stabilizing the mind.
ys [1]pravṛttir utpannā manasaḥ sthitinibandhanīty anuvartate.
‘The phrase “an arisen activity, a basis for the mind’s steadiness” is continued.’
The bhāṣya explains the relevant cognition by saying that buddhi-sattva is shining and that the pravṛtti takes the forms of solar, lunar, planetary, and jewel-radiance. This excludes unrelated dictionary senses such as a night, brick, or metre.
→ jyotiṣmatī means ‘luminous, light-bearing.’
ys [2]buddhisattvaṃ hi bhāsvaram ākāśakalpaṃ, tatra sthitivaiśāradyāt pravṛttiḥ sūryendugrahamaṇiprabhārūpākāreṇa vikalpate.
‘For the sattva of buddhi is shining, like space; there, through mastery of steadiness, the activity takes shape in the form of the radiance of sun, moon, planet, and jewel.’
The bhāṣya locates the practice in concentration on the heart-lotus and identifies the relevant occurrence as buddhisaṃvit, ‘awareness/cognition of buddhi.’ Therefore ‘cognitive activity’ is preferable to a merely physical ‘light.’
→ The implied pravṛtti is a cognitive activity, specifically a buddhi-awareness arising in meditation.
ys [2]hṛdayapuṇḍarīke dhārayato yā buddhisaṃvit
‘The awareness of buddhi that occurs for one who is fixing attention on the lotus of the heart …’
This follows the transparent formation vi-śoka and the available lexical sense ‘exemption from grief.’ The excerpted bhāṣya does not directly gloss viśokā with an iti-definition, but its continued pravṛtti-construal prevents taking it here as a proper name.
→ viśokā is rendered ‘free from sorrow’ or ‘sorrowless.’

Vyāsa's commentary on this unit

1pravṛttir utpannā manasaḥ sthitinibandhanīty anuvartate.
2hṛdayapuṇḍarīke dhārayato yā buddhisaṃvit, buddhisattvaṃ hi bhāsvaram ākāśakalpaṃ, tatra sthitivaiśāradyāt pravṛttiḥ sūryendugrahamaṇiprabhārūpākāreṇa vikalpate.
