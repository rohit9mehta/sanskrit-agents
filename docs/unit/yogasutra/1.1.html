Yoga-sūtra with Vyāsa's bhāṣya · 1.1

ādyaṃ prabhavati jagato 'nekadhānugrahāya prakṣīṇakleśarāśir viṣamaviṣadharo 'nekavaktraḥ subhogī / sarvajñānaprasūtir bhujagaparikaraḥ prītaye yasya nityaṃ devo 'hīśaḥ sa vo 'vyāt sitavimalatanur yogado yogayuktaḥ //1// atha yogānuśāsanam

Translation. At the beginning he manifests for the world’s benefit in many ways—his mass of afflictions worn away, bearing dread poison, many-mouthed, fair-coiled; source of all knowledge, with serpents for his retinue, always for its delight. May that god, the Lord of Serpents, white and stainless in body, giver of yoga, joined to yoga, protect you. Now: the authoritative instruction on yoga (yoga, i.e. samādhi).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
Force of atha in the sūtra
→ atha is a topic-opening/undertaking marker: “Now:” in the sense of opening the adhikāra.
ys [1]athety ayam adhikārārthaḥ.
“The word atha has the sense of adhikāra.”
Scope of yogānuśāsanam
→ yogānuśāsanam denotes the śāstra of authoritative instruction on yoga now being undertaken.
ys [2]yogānuśāsanaṃ śāstram adhikṛtaṃ veditavyam.
“The yogānuśāsana is to be understood as the śāstra that has been taken up as the topic.”
Technical sense of yoga
→ Yoga (yoga) is rendered technically as samādhi, not as generic yoking or union.
ys [3]yogaḥ samādhiḥ.
“Yoga is samādhi.”
ys [4]sa ca sārvabhaumaś cittasya dharmaḥ.
“And that is a property of the mind in all its grounds.”
Technical scope of samādhi/yoga after the opening sūtra
→ The yoga to be expounded is not any distracted concentration; it is the samādhi that illumines a real object, attenuates kleśas, loosens karmic bonds, turns toward nirodha, and also the asaṃprajñāta samādhi of total cessation of vṛttis.
ys [6]tatra vikṣipte cetasi vikṣepopasarjanībhūtaḥ samādhir na yogapakṣe vartate.
“There, in a distracted mind, a samādhi subordinated to distraction does not fall within the class of yoga.”
ys [7]yas tv ekāgre cetasi sadbhūtam arthaṃ pradyotayati kṣiṇoti ca kleśān karmabandhanāni ślathayati nirodham abhimukhaṃ karoti sa saṃprajñāto yoga ity ākhyāyate.
“But that which, in a one-pointed mind, makes a real object shine forth, destroys the kleśas, loosens the bonds of karma, and turns toward cessation is called saṃprajñāta yoga.”
ys [9]sarvavṛttinirodhe tv asaṃprajñātaḥ samādhiḥ.
“But when all vṛttis have ceased, it is asaṃprajñāta samādhi.”
Prayer syntax of the maṅgala verse
→ saḥ … vaḥ avyāt is the benedictory frame: “may that god protect you,” with avyāt from √av, not √vī.
Antecedent of yasya in the verse
→ yasya prītaye is taken with jagataḥ: the deity manifests for the world’s manifold benefit and for its delight.
Compound relation in prakṣīṇakleśarāśiḥ
→ prakṣīṇakleśarāśiḥ is a bahuvrīhi epithet: “one whose mass of kleśas is exhausted.”
Gender and function of sarvajñānaprasūtiḥ
→ sarvajñānaprasūtiḥ is a feminine tatpuruṣa abstract in apposition, “source/origin of all knowledge.”

Vyāsa's commentary on this unit

1athety ayam adhikārārthaḥ.
2yogānuśāsanaṃ śāstram adhikṛtaṃ veditavyam.
3yogaḥ samādhiḥ.
4sa ca sārvabhaumaś cittasya dharmaḥ.
5kṣiptaṃ mūḍhaṃ vikṣiptam ekāgraṃ niruddham iti cittabhūmayaḥ.
6tatra vikṣipte cetasi vikṣepopasarjanībhūtaḥ samādhir na yogapakṣe vartate.
7yas tv ekāgre cetasi sadbhūtam arthaṃ pradyotayati kṣiṇoti ca kleśān karmabandhanāni ślathayati nirodham abhimukhaṃ karoti sa saṃprajñāto yoga ity ākhyāyate.
8sa ca vitarkānugato vicārānugata ānandānugato 'smitānugata ity upariṣṭān nivedayiṣyāmaḥ.
9sarvavṛttinirodhe tv asaṃprajñātaḥ samādhiḥ.
