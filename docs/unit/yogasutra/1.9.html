8 śabdajñānānupātī vastuśūnyo vikalpaḥ

Translation. Conceptual construction (vikalpa) is that which follows upon verbal cognition (śabda-jñāna), while empty of a corresponding object (vastu).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The commentary glosses the sūtra’s śabda-jñāna element by saying that the relevant vyavahāra is grounded in the efficacy of śabda-jñāna; therefore the compound is construed as a single dependence on word-cognition.
→ śabdajñānānupātī means “following upon / dependent on verbal cognition,” not “following words and knowledge” as two separate items.
ys [3]vastuśūnyatve 'pi śabdajñānamāhātmyanibandhano vyavahāro dṛśyate.
Even when there is object-emptiness, a usage is seen that is grounded in the efficacy of verbal cognition.
The bhāṣya explicitly says that vyavahāra is seen even in vastuśūnyatva. Its examples show that what is lacking is not necessarily meaningful use, but a real counterpart such as an actual relation or a real property inhering in the subject.
→ vastuśūnyaḥ means “empty of a corresponding object/reality,” while still capable of supporting linguistic usage.
ys [3]vastuśūnyatve 'pi śabdajñānamāhātmyanibandhano vyavahāro dṛśyate.
Even in object-emptiness, a usage is observed, grounded in the efficacy of verbal cognition.
ys [4, 5, 6]tad yathā caitanyaṃ puruṣasya svarūpam iti. yadā citir eva puruṣas tadā kim atra kena vyapadiśyate. bhavati ca vyapadeśe vṛttiḥ.
For example: ‘Consciousness is the own-form of puruṣa.’ When consciousness itself is puruṣa, what is here designated by what? Yet in designation an operation does occur.
ys [10, 11]tathānutpattidharmā puruṣa iti, utpattidharmasyābhāvamātram avagamyate na puruṣānvayī dharmaḥ. tasmād vikalpitaḥ sa dharmas tena cāsti vyavahāra iti.
Similarly, in ‘puruṣa has non-origination as its property,’ only the mere absence of the property of origination is understood, not a property inhering in puruṣa. Therefore that property is constructed, and by it there is usage.
Vyāsa excludes vikalpa from both pramāṇa and viparyaya, and calls the relevant dharma vikalpita, constructed. The English “conceptual construction” best preserves that it is a cognition/usage produced through verbal construction without a corresponding object.
→ vikalpaḥ is rendered “conceptual construction,” not “option/alternative” and not a pramāṇa or viparyaya.
ys [1, 2]sa na pramāṇopārohī. na viparyayopārohī ca.
It does not fall under pramāṇa. Nor does it fall under viparyaya.
ys [11]tasmād vikalpitaḥ sa dharmas tena cāsti vyavahāra iti.
Therefore that property is constructed, and by it there is usage.
The three inflected forms are nominative masculine singular, forming a compact sūtra-definition: ‘vikalpa is that which is X and Y.’
→ śabdajñānānupātī and vastuśūnyaḥ are nominative masculine singular adjectives qualifying vikalpaḥ.
