abhyāsavairāgyābhyāṃ tannirodhaḥ

Translation. Through practice (abhyāsa) and dispassion (vairāgya), there is the cessation (nirodha) of those [mental fluctuations of consciousness].

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
abhyāsavairāgyābhyām is an instrumental dual dvandva, not a loose list or an alternative pair; the cessation is dependent on both.
→ “by practice and dispassion,” construed as two jointly operative means
ys [4, 5]tatra vairāgyeṇa viṣayasrotaḥ khilīkriyate. vivekadarśanābhyāsena vivekasrota udghāṭyata ity ubhayādhīnaś cittavṛttinirodhaḥ.
There, by dispassion the stream toward objects is made barren; by the practice of discriminative seeing the stream of discrimination is opened; thus the cessation of mental fluctuations is dependent on both. The printed udghāṭyata is taken as the intended passive udghāṭyate.
tan-nirodhaḥ refers to citta-vṛtti-nirodha, the cessation of the mental fluctuations, not to the cessation of practice/dispassion or of the two streams themselves.
→ “the cessation of those [mental fluctuations of consciousness]”
ys [5]ity ubhayādhīnaś cittavṛttinirodhaḥ.
Thus the cessation of mental fluctuations is dependent on both.
The terms are translated neutrally as “practice” and “dispassion,” but their operative senses here are fixed by Vyāsa’s river metaphor: dispassion checks the viṣaya-current, while practice opens the viveka-current.
→ abhyāsa as practice oriented to discriminative seeing; vairāgya as blocking the object-current
ys [1, 2, 3]cittanadī nāmobhayatovāhinī vahati kalyāṇāya vahati pāpāya ca. yā tu kaivalyaprāgbhārā vivekaviṣayanimnā sā kalyāṇavahā. saṃsāraprāgbhārāvivekaviṣayaniṃnā pāpavahā.
The river called consciousness flows in both directions: it flows toward good and toward evil. That which slopes toward kaivalya and has discrimination as its field is good-bearing. That which slopes toward saṃsāra and has non-discrimination as its field is evil-bearing.
ys [4, 5]tatra vairāgyeṇa viṣayasrotaḥ khilīkriyate. vivekadarśanābhyāsena vivekasrota udghāṭyata
There, by dispassion the stream toward objects is made barren; by the practice of discriminative seeing the stream of discrimination is opened.
