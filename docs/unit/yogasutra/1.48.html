Yoga-sūtra with Vyāsa's bhāṣya · 1.48

47ṛtaṃbharā tatra prajñā

Translation. There, the insight (prajñā) is truth-bearing (ṛtaṃbharā). In Vyāsa’s terms: it is the prajñā that arises in that state for one whose mind is concentrated.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The sūtra is read as a nominal statement: ‘the prajñā there is ṛtaṃbharā.’ Vyāsa explicitly says that the prajñā which arises has the designation ṛtaṃbharā.
→ ṛtaṃbharā is predicated of the prajñā, not a separate substantive.
ys [1]tasmin samāhitacittasya yā prajñā jāyate tasyā ṛtaṃbhareti
The wisdom that arises in that, for one whose mind is concentrated—of it [the designation] is ‘ṛtaṃbharā.’
The adverb is locative in force. The Bhāṣya glosses tatra with tasmin and links it with the concentrated mind.
→ tatra means ‘in that [state/locus]’, not ‘thither’.
ys [1]tasmin samāhitacittasya yā prajñā jāyate
The wisdom that arises in that, for one whose mind is concentrated...
Although the sūtra is verbless, Vyāsa supplies jāyate and qualifies the possessor/context as samāhitacittasya.
→ prajñā is a cognition/insight that arises for the concentrated mind.
ys [1]samāhitacittasya yā prajñā jāyate
the wisdom that arises for one whose mind is concentrated
The Bhāṣya here identifies the epithet but does not unpack its semantics. The English ‘truth-bearing’ follows the compound ṛtaṃ-bhara and MW s.v. ṛtaṃbhara, ‘bearing the truth in one’s self’.
→ ṛtaṃbharā is rendered ‘truth-bearing’.
