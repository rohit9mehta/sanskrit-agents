Yoga-sūtra with Vyāsa's bhāṣya · 1.13

12 tatra sthitau yatno 'bhyāsaḥ

Translation. In that context, practice (abhyāsa) is the energetic effort toward steadiness (sthiti)—the tranquil flow of the mind when it is without fluctuations.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The locative sthitau is not taken as bodily posture or mere remaining in place, but as the yogic mental condition glossed by Vyāsa: the mind, free of vṛttis, has a pacified continuity.
→ “steadiness” (sthiti) means the tranquil flow of the mind when it is without fluctuations.
ys [1]cittasyāvṛttikasya praśāntavāhitā sthitiḥ.
Steadiness is the tranquil flow of the mind when it is without fluctuations.
The word yatna is interpreted through Vyāsa’s synonyms prayatna, vīrya, and utsāha, and through tadarthaḥ: it is effort for the sake of that sthiti, not effort occurring while already in it.
→ yatnaḥ is “effort/exertion,” specifically energy and resolve directed to that steadiness.
ys [2]tadarthaḥ prayatno vīryam utsāhaḥ.
For the sake of that, exertion—energy, resolve.
The predicate relation ‘yatnaḥ abhyāsaḥ’ is expanded by the commentary: practice is the performance of the means to that state, undertaken with the wish to bring it fully about. Thus it is not merely mechanical repetition.
→ abhyāsaḥ is “practice,” i.e. undertaking the means for attaining that steadiness.
ys [3]tat saṃpipādayiṣayā tat sādhanānuṣṭhānam abhyāsaḥ.
With the wish to accomplish that, the undertaking of its means is practice.
The commentary does not independently gloss tatra in this unit. Grammatically it is an indeclinable locative adverb, here introducing the definition of abhyāsa in the context established by the preceding sūtra.
→ tatra is rendered “in that context / as to that.”
