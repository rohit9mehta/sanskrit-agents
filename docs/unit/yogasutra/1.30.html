Yoga-sūtra with Vyāsa's bhāṣya · 1.30

vyādhistyānasaṃśayapramādālasyāviratibhrāntidarśanālabdhabhūmikatvānavasthitatvāni cittavikṣepās te 'ntarāyāḥ

Translation. Disease (vyādhi), mental stagnation (styāna), doubt (saṃśaya), heedlessness (pramāda), sloth (ālasya), non-abstention or craving (avirati), false seeing (bhrāntidarśana), non-attainment of a stage (alabdhabhūmikatva), and instability (anavasthitatva): these are distractions of the mind (cittavikṣepāḥ); they are the obstacles (antarāyāḥ).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The long opening expression is not to be translated as an unstructured mass or as separate syntactic clauses; it is a list of nine items, later predicated as mind-distractions and obstacles.
→ a nine-member dvandva naming the obstacles
ys [1]navāntarāyāś cittasya vikṣepāḥ.
The nine obstacles are distractions of the mind.
ys [14]ete cittavikṣepā nava yogamalā yogapratipakṣā yogāntarāyā ity abhidhīyante.
These nine distractions of the mind are called impurities of yoga, opponents of yoga, obstacles to yoga.
The genitive relation in cittavikṣepāḥ is supplied as ‘of the mind,’ and the obstacles are understood specifically as impediments to yoga, not merely obstacles in general.
→ cittavikṣepāḥ = distractions of the mind; antarāyāḥ = obstacles to yoga
ys [1]navāntarāyāś cittasya vikṣepāḥ.
The nine obstacles are distractions of the mind.
ys [14]ete cittavikṣepā nava yogamalā yogapratipakṣā yogāntarāyā ity abhidhīyante.
These nine distractions of the mind are called impurities of yoga, opponents of yoga, obstacles to yoga.
Vyādhi is rendered ‘disease,’ with the commentary specifying imbalance among bodily constituents, not simply mental unease.
→ vyādhi as disease, a physiological disorder
ys [4]vyādhir dhāturasakaraṇavaiṣamyam.
Disease is disequilibrium of the humors, fluids, and instruments/organs.
Styāna is not taken in its lexical sense of physical stiffness or density, but as the mind’s incapacity for action.
→ styāna as mental stagnation or incapacity
ys [5]styānam akarmaṇyatā cittasya.
Styāna is the mind’s incapacity for action.
Saṃśaya is translated ‘doubt,’ specifically a cognition that touches both alternatives, rather than vague uncertainty only.
→ saṃśaya as doubt between two alternatives
ys [6]saṃśaya ubhayakoṭispṛg vijñānaṃ syād idam evaṃ naivaṃ syād iti.
Doubt is cognition touching both alternatives: ‘This may be so; it may not be so.’
Pramāda is translated ‘heedlessness/negligence,’ not intoxication or madness, because Vyāsa defines it as failure to cultivate the means to samādhi.
→ pramāda as heedless non-cultivation of samādhi’s means
ys [7]pramādaḥ samādhisādhanānām abhāvanam.
Pramāda is the non-cultivation of the means to samādhi.
Ālasya is rendered ‘sloth,’ with the commentary’s explanation that both body and mind fail to engage because of heaviness.
→ ālasya as slothful non-activity due to heaviness
ys [8]ālasyaṃ kāyasya cittasya ca gurutvād apravṛttiḥ.
Sloth is non-activity of body and mind because of heaviness.
Avirati is not merely lack of restraint in the abstract; Vyāsa identifies it as craving whose nature is the mind’s conjunction with objects.
→ avirati as non-abstention, sensual craving
ys [9]aviratiś cittasya viṣayasaṃprayogātmā gardhaḥ.
Avirati is craving, having as its nature the mind’s conjunction with sense-objects.
The compound bhrāntidarśana is translated by its technical gloss ‘erroneous cognition,’ not as physical wandering of sight.
→ bhrāntidarśana as false seeing or erroneous cognition
ys [10]bhrāntidarśanaṃ viparyayajñānam.
False seeing is erroneous cognition.
The last two terms are taken specifically with respect to the stages/grounds of samādhi: first not attaining such a ground, then not remaining established in one that has been attained.
→ alabdhabhūmikatva and anavasthitatva as samādhi-stage non-attainment and instability
ys [11]alabdhabhūmikatvaṃ samādhibhūmer alābhaḥ.
Non-attainment of a stage is failure to obtain the ground of samādhi.
ys [12]anavasthitatvaṃ yal labdhāyāṃ bhūmau cittasyāpratiṣṭhā.
Instability is that non-establishment of the mind in a ground that has been attained.
ys [13]samādhipratilambhe hi sati tadavasthitaṃ syād iti.
For when samādhi has been obtained, the mind would be established in it.
