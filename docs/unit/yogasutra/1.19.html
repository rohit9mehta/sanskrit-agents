bhavapratyayo videhaprakṛtilayānām

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The compound is read as a predicate: the relevant state has bhava as its pratyaya, i.e. its condition or basis. Vyāsa explains the videha case through their own residual saṃskāras and their particular ongoing mode of existence, not through an asserted doctrine or belief.
→ bhavapratyayaḥ = ‘conditioned by birth/state of being’, not ‘belief in existence’.
ys [1, 2]videhānāṃ devānāṃ bhavapratyayaḥ. te hi svasaṃskāramātropayogena cittena kaivalyapadam ivānubhavantaḥ svasaṃskāravipākaṃ tathājātīyakam ativāhayanti.
For the bodiless gods it is bhava-conditioned. For, with a mind whose use is only their own saṃskāra, they experience as it were the state of kaivalya and pass through the ripening of their own saṃskāra of that kind.
The genitive videhānām is not left as a vague class of ‘bodiless ones’; Vyāsa explicitly glosses them as devas.
→ videha means the bodiless gods.
ys [1]videhānāṃ devānāṃ bhavapratyayaḥ.
For the bodiless gods it is bhava-conditioned.
Vyāsa first treats the videhas and then introduces prakṛtilayāḥ with tathā, showing that the sūtra names two distinct classes, not a single entity such as ‘bodiless dissolution in prakṛti’.
→ videhaprakṛtilayānām is a dvandva of two groups: videhas and prakṛtilayas.
ys [1, 3]videhānāṃ devānāṃ bhavapratyayaḥ. tathā prakṛtilayāḥ sādhikāre cetasi prakṛtilīne kaivalyapadam ivānubhavanti, yāvan na punar āvartate 'dhikāravaśāc cittam iti.
For the bodiless gods it is bhava-conditioned. Likewise, the prakṛtilayas, while the mind with its mandate remains merged in prakṛti, experience as it were the state of kaivalya, until the mind returns again under the force of its mandate.
The bhāṣya says their mind is prakṛtilīna and that they experience kaivalya only iva, ‘as it were’, until the mind returns. Thus the translation ‘absorbed in prakṛti’ should not be taken as final kaivalya.
→ prakṛtilaya denotes those absorbed/merged in prakṛti, not final liberated persons.
ys [3]tathā prakṛtilayāḥ sādhikāre cetasi prakṛtilīne kaivalyapadam ivānubhavanti, yāvan na punar āvartate 'dhikāravaśāc cittam iti.
Likewise, the prakṛtilayas, while the mind with its mandate remains merged in prakṛti, experience as it were the state of kaivalya, until the mind returns again under the force of its mandate.
