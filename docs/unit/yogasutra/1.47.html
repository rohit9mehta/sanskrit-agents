Yoga-sūtra with Vyāsa's bhāṣya · 1.47

46 nirvicāravaiśāradye 'dhyātmaprasādaḥ

Translation. When lucidity (vaiśāradya) has arisen in the nirvicāra samādhi, inner clarity (adhyātma-prasāda) comes to the yogin.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The word vaiśāradya is rendered by “lucidity” because Vyāsa defines it as an unoverpowered, transparent, steady flow of the luminous buddhisattva after impurity and covering have been removed.
→ vaiśāradya as lucid clearness/settled transparency of the cognitive sattva, not merely practical skill or expertise
ys [1]aśuddhyāvaraṇamalāpetasya prakāśātmano buddhisattvasya rajastamobhyām anabhibhūtaḥ svacchaḥ sthitipravāho vaiśāradyam.
Vaiśāradya is the transparent steady-flow of the luminous buddhisattva, free from the impurity-covering stain and not overpowered by rajas and tamas.
The compound is construed as a genitive tatpuruṣa, with nirvicāra qualifying samādhi; the sūtra’s locative is temporal/conditional, not simply a static location.
→ nirvicāravaiśāradye = ‘when lucidity in/of the nirvicāra samādhi has arisen’
ys [2]yadā nirvicārasya samādher vaiśāradyam idaṃ jāyate tadā
When this vaiśāradya of the nirvicāra samādhi arises, then ...
The second compound is not taken as ‘grace of the Supreme Spirit’ or external favor. Vyāsa says it comes to the yogin and qualifies it by its real-object scope and clarity.
→ adhyātmaprasādaḥ as the yogin’s inner clarity, a clear cognition concerning things as they are
ys [2]tadā yogino bhavaty adhyātmaprasādo bhūtārthaviṣayaḥ kramānanurodhī sphuṭaḥ
Then the yogin has adhyātmaprasāda, whose object is the real thing, not dependent on sequence, distinct/clear.
The sūtra has no finite verb; the commentary supplies jāyate for the condition and bhavati for the result, so the translation uses ‘has arisen ... comes to the yogin.’
→ supply the finite sense as ‘comes to be/arises’
ys [2]jāyate tadā yogino bhavaty adhyātmaprasādaḥ
[It] arises; then adhyātmaprasāda comes to be for the yogin.
