How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The defining compound is translated as ‘a cognition/presentation of absence.’ Vyāsa argues that sleep is a pratyayaviśeṣa, a specific pratyaya, because on waking there is retrospective awareness; without an experienced pratyaya such recollection would not occur. Thus the sūtra does not define sleep as blank non-cognition.
→ pratyaya = a cognition/presentation, not a mere absence of cognition
ys [1]sā ca saṃprabodhe pratyavamarśāt pratyayaviśeṣaḥ.
And it, because of retrospective awareness on full awakening, is a particular cognition.
ys [10]sa khalv ayaṃ prabuddhasya pratyavamarśo na syād asati pratyayānubhave
This retrospective awareness of one who has awakened would not exist if there had been no experience of a cognition.
ys [11]tasmāt pratyayaviśeṣo nidrā.
Therefore sleep is a particular cognition.
The compound is taken as a bahuvrīhi adjective: ‘that vṛtti whose ālambana is an abhāva-pratyaya.’ The commentary does not give an explicit vigraha for the compound, so the compound relation is decided grammatically; Vyāsa’s pratyayaviśeṣa discussion controls only that the relevant pratyaya is positive enough to ground later recollection.
→ abhāvapratyayālambanā qualifies vṛttiḥ as ‘having a cognition of absence as its support/object’
The nominatives are construed definitionally: ‘Sleep is a vṛtti…’. Vyāsa confirms that sleep belongs among pratyayas/vṛttis and is subject to nirodha in samādhi, so it is not excluded from the class of mental modifications.
→ nidrā is being defined as a vṛtti/pratyayaviśeṣa to be restrained like other pratyayas
ys [11]tasmāt pratyayaviśeṣo nidrā.
Therefore sleep is a particular cognition.
ys [12]sā ca samādhāv itarapratyayavan niroddhavyeti.
And in samādhi it is to be restrained like other cognitions.
The commentary here does not separately gloss abhāva. Its basic lexical sense is used, while avoiding the commentary-rejected idea that sleep is simply the absence of pratyaya.
→ abhāva = absence/non-presence
