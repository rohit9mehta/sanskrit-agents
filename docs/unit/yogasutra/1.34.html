How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The two procedures are coordinated, not a tatpuruṣa such as “retention of expulsion.” Vyāsa glosses the two members separately and then refers back to them with the dual instrumental tābhyām, which fixes both the compound relation and the case-function as means.
→ pracchardanavidhāraṇābhyām is an instrumental-dual dvandva: “by expulsion and retention.”
ys [1]vamanaṃ pracchardanam, vidhāraṇaṃ prāṇāyāmas tābhyāṃ vā manasaḥ sthitiṃ saṃpādayet.
“Expulsion is pracchardana; retention is prāṇāyāma; by those two, alternatively, one may bring about steadiness of the mind.”
Although pracchardana can mean “vomiting” in other contexts, here the commentary restricts it to the Yogic breathing context: the air located in the abdomen is expelled through the nostril-openings by a special exertion. Hence “exhalation/expulsion,” not an emetic act.
→ pracchardana means expulsion/exhalation of the abdominal air through the nostrils by a special effort.
ys [1]kauṣṭhyasya vāyor nāsikāpuṭābhyāṃ prayatnaviśeṣād vamanaṃ pracchardanam
“The expelling of the abdominal air through the nostril-openings by a particular effort is pracchardana.”
The word is not taken here in the broader senses “division” or merely “checking” in general; Vyāsa identifies the vidhāraṇa at issue as prāṇāyāma, i.e. breath-control by retention.
→ vidhāraṇa means retention of breath, identified with prāṇāyāma.
ys [1]vidhāraṇaṃ prāṇāyāmas
“Retention is prāṇāyāma.”
Vyāsa paraphrases prāṇa as vāyu, specifically kauṣṭhya-vāyu, “abdominal air,” and makes it the object/content of expulsion; the following vidhāraṇa is then the retention of that same breath/air.
→ prāṇasya is a genitive depending on both pracchardana and vidhāraṇa: “of the breath.”
ys [1]kauṣṭhyasya vāyor nāsikāpuṭābhyāṃ prayatnaviśeṣād vamanaṃ pracchardanam
“The expelling of the abdominal air through the nostril-openings by a particular effort is pracchardana.”
The bare sūtra contains only an instrumental phrase plus vā; Vyāsa supplies the predicate and the result, manasaḥ sthitim saṃpādayet, and repeats vā after tābhyām. The translation therefore must not remain a predicate-less noun phrase.
→ The sūtra’s ellipsis is filled as: “one may bring about steadiness of the mind”; vā marks this as an alternative means.
ys [1]tābhyāṃ vā manasaḥ sthitiṃ saṃpādayet.
“By those two, alternatively, one may bring about steadiness of the mind.”
