Yoga-sūtra with Vyāsa's bhāṣya · 1.24

kleśakarmavipākāśayair aparāmṛṣṭaḥ puruṣaviśeṣa īśvaraḥ

Translation. The Lord (īśvara) is a special puruṣa: untouched by afflictions (kleśa, ignorance and the rest), by actions (karman, wholesome and unwholesome), by their fruition (vipāka), and by the corresponding latent impressions/deposits (āśaya).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The initial instrumental compound is not a nested relation such as “the deposit of the fruition of afflicted action”; it is a four-member coordinate compound whose members have the bhāṣya’s Yoga-technical senses.
→ Coordinate dvandva: afflictions, actions, fruition, and latent impressions/deposits.
ys [1, 2, 3, 4]avidyādayaḥ kleśāḥ. kuśalākuśalāni karmāṇi. tatphalaṃ vipākaḥ. tadanuguṇā vāsanā āśayāḥ.
Afflictions are ignorance and the rest. Actions are wholesome and unwholesome. Fruition is the fruit of that. The āśayas are the corresponding vāsanā[s]; the printed singular vāsanā is treated collectively/as likely for plural because it glosses plural āśayāḥ.
The bhāṣya construes aparāmṛṣṭaḥ experientially. Kleśas and the rest reside in the mind but are predicated of puruṣa because puruṣa is the enjoyer/experiencer of their fruit; Īśvara is the one not touched by that bhoga.
→ “Untouched by” means not contacted by these factors through the experience of their fruits; the instrumental is the contacting/affecting factor.
ys [5, 6, 7]te ca manasi vartamānāḥ puruṣe vyapadiśyante, sa hi tatphalasya bhokteti. yathā jayaḥ parājayo vā yoddhṛṣu vartamānaḥ svāmini vyapadiśyate. yo hy anena bhogenāparāmṛṣṭaḥ sa puruṣaviśeṣa īśvaraḥ.
And these, while existing in the mind, are predicated of puruṣa, for he is the experiencer of their fruit. Just as victory or defeat, though residing in the warriors, is predicated of the lord. For he who is untouched by this experience is that special puruṣa, Īśvara.
The commentary raises the possible confusion with liberated kevalins only to distinguish Īśvara from them: liberated selves have a prior bondage-limit, and prakṛtilīnas may have a future one, but Īśvara has neither and is always free.
→ Īśvara is an eternally free distinctive puruṣa, not merely one liberated puruṣa among many kevalins.
ys [8, 9, 10, 11, 12]kaivalyaṃ prāptās tarhi santi ca bahavaḥ kevalinaḥ. te hi trīṇi bandhanāni cchittvā kaivalyaṃ prāptā īśvarasya ca tatsaṃbandho na bhūto na bhāvī. yathā muktasya pūrvā bandhakoṭiḥ prajñāyate naivam īśvarasya. yathā vā prakṛtilīnasyottarā bandhakoṭiḥ saṃbhāvyate naivam īśvarasya. sa tu sadaiva muktaḥ sadaiveśvara iti.
Objection: then there are many kevalins who have attained isolation. For they attained isolation after cutting the three bonds; but for Īśvara, relation to that [bondage] has not existed and will not exist. As a prior limit of bondage is known for a liberated one, not so for Īśvara. Or as a later limit of bondage is possible for one dissolved into prakṛti, not so for Īśvara. He, however, is always free, always Īśvara.
The bhāṣya explains īśvara through aiśvarya that is free from equality and superiority; hence the word is not merely a generic “master/capable one.”
→ Translate īśvaraḥ as “the Lord,” with unique, unsurpassed lordship, and take it in apposition to puruṣaviśeṣaḥ.
ys [18, 21, 26, 27]tac ca tasyaiśvaryaṃ sāmyātiśayavinirmuktam. tasmād yatra kāṣṭhāprāptir aiśvaryasya sa īśvara iti. tasmād yasya sāmyātiśayair vinirmuktam aiśvaryaṃ sa eveśvaraḥ. sa ca puruṣaviśeṣa iti.
And that lordship of his is free from equality and superiority. Therefore, where lordship has reached its culmination, that is Īśvara. Therefore he whose lordship is free from equals and superiors—he alone is Īśvara. And he is a special puruṣa.
The formal samāsa analysis of puruṣaviśeṣaḥ is taken as a genitival tatpuruṣa rather than a bahuvrīhi; this is a grammatical decision, while the bhāṣya supplies the semantic contrast with other puruṣas/kevalins elsewhere.
→ ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa: puruṣāṇāṃ viśeṣaḥ, rendered idiomatically “a special/distinctive puruṣa.”

Vyāsa's commentary on this unit

1avidyādayaḥ kleśāḥ.
2kuśalākuśalāni karmāṇi.
3tatphalaṃ vipākaḥ.
4tadanuguṇā vāsanā āśayāḥ.
5te ca manasi vartamānāḥ puruṣe vyapadiśyante, sa hi tatphalasya bhokteti.
6yathā jayaḥ parājayo vā yoddhṛṣu vartamānaḥ svāmini vyapadiśyate.
7yo hy anena bhogenāparāmṛṣṭaḥ sa puruṣaviśeṣa īśvaraḥ.
8kaivalyaṃ prāptās tarhi santi ca bahavaḥ kevalinaḥ.
9te hi trīṇi bandhanāni cchittvā kaivalyaṃ prāptā īśvarasya ca tatsaṃbandho na bhūto na bhāvī.
10yathā muktasya pūrvā bandhakoṭiḥ prajñāyate naivam īśvarasya.
11yathā vā prakṛtilīnasyottarā bandhakoṭiḥ saṃbhāvyate naivam īśvarasya.
12sa tu sadaiva muktaḥ sadaiveśvara iti.
13yo 'sau prakṛṣṭasattvopādānād īśvarasya śāśvatika utkarṣaḥ sa kiṃ sanimitta āhosvin nirnimitta iti.
14tasya śāstraṃ nimittam.
15śāstraṃ punaḥ kiṃnimittam, prakṛṣṭasattvanimittam.
16etayoḥ śāstrotkarṣayor īśvarasattve vartamānayor anādiḥ saṃbandhaḥ.
17etasmād etad bhavati sadaiveśvaraḥ sadaiva mukta iti.
18tac ca tasyaiśvaryaṃ sāmyātiśayavinirmuktam.
19na tāvad aiśvaryāntareṇa tad atiśayyate.
20yad evātiśayi syāt tad eva tat syāt.
21tasmād yatra kāṣṭhāprāptir aiśvaryasya sa īśvara iti.
22na ca tatsamānam aiśvaryam asti.
23kasmāt, dvayos tulyayor ekasmin yugapatkāmite 'rthe navam idam astu purāṇam idam astv ity ekasya siddhāv itarasya prākāmyavighātād ūnatvaṃ prasaktam.
24dvayoś ca tulyayor yugapatkāmitārthaprāptir nāsti.
25arthasya viruddhatvāt.
26tasmād yasya sāmyātiśayair vinirmuktam aiśvaryaṃ sa eveśvaraḥ.
27sa ca puruṣaviśeṣa iti.
