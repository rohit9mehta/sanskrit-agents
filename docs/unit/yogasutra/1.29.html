Yoga-sūtra with Vyāsa's bhāṣya · 1.29

tataḥ pratyakcetanādhigamo 'py antarāyābhāvaś ca

Translation. From that [devotion to Īśvara], there is also the attainment of inward consciousness (pratyak-cetanā)—the seeing of one’s own nature—and the absence of obstacles.

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The demonstrative ablatival indeclinable is not left as a vague “from that”; Vyāsa explicitly explains the causal source as īśvarapraṇidhāna.
→ tataḥ = from īśvarapraṇidhāna, devotion/dedication to Īśvara
ys [1]ye tāvad antarāyā vyādhiprabhṛtayas te tāvad īśvarapraṇidhānān na bhavanti.
As for those obstacles beginning with disease, they do not come to be through/from īśvarapraṇidhāna.
The head adhigama is taken as attainment/realization; the object attained is pratyak-cetanā, the inward consciousness. Vyāsa’s gloss makes the experiential sense precise: svarūpadarśana, not mere intellectual knowledge or ordinary mental awareness.
→ pratyakcetanādhigamaḥ = attainment/realization of inward consciousness, glossed as vision of one’s own nature
ys [2]svarūpadarśanam apy asya bhavati.
There is also for him a seeing of his own nature.
ys [3]yathaiveśvaraḥ puruṣaḥ śuddhaḥ prasannaḥ kevalo 'nupasargas tathāyam api buddheḥ pratisaṃvedī yaḥ puruṣa
Just as Īśvara is a puruṣa, pure, clear, isolated, untouched by adjuncts, so too is this puruṣa, the one who is aware of buddhi.
The compound is construed as a genitive tatpuruṣa, antarāyāṇām abhāvaḥ. Vyāsa identifies the antarāyas as vyādhi-prabhṛtayaḥ and paraphrases their absence by na bhavanti, “they do not arise/come to be.”
→ antarāyābhāvaḥ = absence/non-arising of obstacles
ys [1]ye tāvad antarāyā vyādhiprabhṛtayas te tāvad īśvarapraṇidhānān na bhavanti.
As for those obstacles beginning with disease, they do not come to be through/from īśvarapraṇidhāna.
Although the sūtra states pratyakcetanādhigamo 'pi first and antarāyābhāvaś ca second, Vyāsa treats the two as distinct fruits and explicitly repeats api with svarūpadarśana. Therefore the translation marks “also” with the attainment of inward consciousness and “and” with the absence of obstacles.
→ api has scope over the self-vision/attainment, while ca coordinates the absence of obstacles
ys [1]ye tāvad antarāyā vyādhiprabhṛtayas te tāvad īśvarapraṇidhānān na bhavanti.
The obstacles beginning with disease do not arise from īśvarapraṇidhāna.
ys [2]svarūpadarśanam apy asya bhavati.
