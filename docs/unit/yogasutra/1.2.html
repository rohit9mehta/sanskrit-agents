How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The sūtra’s yoga is not to be restricted to asaṃprajñāta alone. Vyāsa explicitly says that because the word sarva ‘all’ is not used, saṃprajñāta too is called yoga, and then states that this yoga is twofold.
→ yoga as a technical state/discipline with twofold scope, not only total objectless cessation
ys [1]sarvaśabdāgrahaṇāt saṃprajñāto 'pi yoga ity ākhyāyate.
Because the word ‘all’ is not taken, saṃprajñāta too is designated yoga.
ys [13]dvividhaḥ sa yogaś cittavṛttinirodha iti.
That yoga is twofold: ‘the restraint of the fluctuations of citta.’
The translation keeps citta in Sanskrit and glosses it as ‘mind-stuff’ because the commentary characterizes citta as triguṇa, marked by illumination, activity, and stability. That excludes taking it as the unchanging conscious principle itself.
→ citta as mind-stuff/inner organ, not pure consciousness or puruṣa
ys [2]cittaṃ hi prakhyāpravṛttisthitiśīlatvāt triguṇam.
For citta, since it has the character of illumination, activity, and stability, is composed of the three guṇas.
The compound is read as a genitive tatpuruṣa: nirodha is directed toward the vṛttis belonging to citta. The commentary repeats the compound but does not give an explicit vigraha; this construal follows standard grammar of dependent tatpuruṣa compounds.
→ cittavṛttinirodhaḥ = cittavṛttīnāṃ nirodhaḥ, ‘restraint of the fluctuations of citta’
‘Restraint’ best preserves Vyāsa’s scope: in the highest objectless state nothing is cognized, but the definition also covers saṃprajñāta yoga because ‘all’ is not stated. The commentator also uses the cognate verb niruṇaddhi for citta’s checking even viveka-khyāti.
→ nirodha as restraint/checking, not annihilation of mind and not necessarily cessation of every vṛtti in all yoga
ys [1]sarvaśabdāgrahaṇāt saṃprajñāto 'pi yoga ity ākhyāyate.
Because the word ‘all’ is not taken, saṃprajñāta too is designated yoga.
ys [9]atas tasyāṃ viraktaṃ cittaṃ tām api khyātiṃ niruṇaddhi.
Therefore, citta, dispassionate toward that, restrains even that discernment.
ys [12]na tatra kiṃcit saṃprajñāyata ity asaṃprajñātaḥ.
There nothing at all is distinctly cognized; therefore it is asaṃprajñāta.
Both words are nominative singular masculine, forming a definition: ‘Yoga is ...’. Vyāsa’s restatement confirms the equation.
→ nominal equation: yogaḥ = cittavṛttinirodhaḥ
ys [13]dvividhaḥ sa yogaś cittavṛttinirodha iti.
That yoga is twofold: ‘the restraint of the fluctuations of citta.’

Vyāsa's commentary on this unit

1sarvaśabdāgrahaṇāt saṃprajñāto 'pi yoga ity ākhyāyate.
2cittaṃ hi prakhyāpravṛttisthitiśīlatvāt triguṇam.
3prakhyārūpaṃ hi cittasattvaṃ rajastamobhyāṃ saṃsṛṣṭam aiśvaryaviṣayapriyaṃ bhavati.
4tad eva tamasānuviddham adharmājñānāvairāgyānaiśvaryopagaṃ bhavati.
5tad eva prakṣīṇamohāvaraṇaṃ sarvataḥ pradyotamānam anuviddhaṃ rajomātrayā dharmajñānavairāgyaiśvaryopagaṃ bhavati.
6tad eva rajoleśamalāpetaṃ svarūpapratiṣṭhaṃ sattvapuruṣānyatākhyātimātraṃ dharmameghadhyānopagaṃ bhavati.
7tat paraṃ prasaṃkhyānam ity ācakṣate dhyāyinaḥ.
8citiśaktir apariṇāminy apratisaṃkramā darśitaviṣayā śuddhā cānantā ca sattvaguṇātmikā ceyam ato viparītā vivekakhyātir iti.
9atas tasyāṃ viraktaṃ cittaṃ tām api khyātiṃ niruṇaddhi.
10tadavasthaṃ saṃskāropagaṃ bhavati.
11sa nirbījaḥ samādhiḥ.
12na tatra kiṃcit saṃprajñāyata ity asaṃprajñātaḥ.
13dvividhaḥ sa yogaś cittavṛttinirodha iti.
