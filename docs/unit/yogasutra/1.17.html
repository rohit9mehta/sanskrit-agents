Yoga-sūtra with Vyāsa's bhāṣya · 1.17

vitarkavicārānandāsmitārūpānugamāt saṃprajñātaḥ

Translation. Because of accompaniment by the forms—coarse object-engagement (vitarka), subtle consideration (vicāra), delight (ānanda), and I-am awareness (asmitā)—[the samādhi] is cognitive (saṃprajñāta).

How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The four list-members are not translated by their broad dictionary senses alone. Vyāsa gives technical glosses, so vitarka is not merely ‘conjecture’ and asmitā is not simply ‘egoism’; the translation follows these bhāṣya definitions.
→ vitarka = coarse engagement of the mind with its support; vicāra = subtle consideration/engagement; ānanda = delight; asmitā = unitary I-am awareness.
ys [1, 2, 3, 4]vitarkaś cittasyālambane sthūla ābhogaḥ. sūkṣmo vicāraḥ. ānando hlādaḥ. ekātmikā saṃvid asmitā.
Vitarka is a coarse engagement of the mind with its support. Vicāra is subtle. Ānanda is delight. Asmitā is a unitary awareness.
The bhāṣya immediately recasts the item as samādhiḥ and later as samādhayaḥ. Therefore the English supplies ‘[samādhi]’ and renders saṃprajñāta as the technical ‘cognitive’ concentration. The English ‘cognitive’ is also supported by derivation from sam-pra-√jñā, ‘to know accurately’ (MW s.v. samprajñā, 1174,1).
→ saṃprajñātaḥ qualifies an implied samādhiḥ; the sūtra defines a kind of samādhi, not an isolated predicate ‘is known’.
ys [5, 9]tatra prathamaś catuṣṭayānugataḥ samādhiḥ savitarkaḥ. sarva ete sālambanāḥ samādhayaḥ.
There the first samādhi, accompanied by the tetrad, is savitarka. All these are samādhis with a support.
Vyāsa glosses the sūtra’s anugama by anugataḥ and then distinguishes the first, second, third, and fourth samādhis through presence or absence of vitarka, vicāra, ānanda, and asmitā. Thus ‘accompaniment by the forms’ should be read in this staged technical sense.
→ anugamāt is a causal ablative: ‘because of accompaniment/being accompanied’; the accompaniment is mapped across four samādhi-modes rather than treated only as one undifferentiated co-presence.
ys [5, 6, 7, 8]tatra prathamaś catuṣṭayānugataḥ samādhiḥ savitarkaḥ. dvitīyo vitarkavikalaḥ savicāraḥ. tṛtīyo vicāravikalaḥ sānandaḥ. caturthas tadvikalo 'smitāmātra iti.
There the first samādhi, accompanied by the tetrad, is with vitarka. The second, lacking vitarka, is with vicāra. The third, lacking vicāra, is with ānanda. The fourth, lacking that, is asmitā alone.
Vyāsa does not explicitly gloss rūpa. The choice rests on grammar and dictionary: MW s.v. rūpa, 885,3 gives ‘form, shape’ and notes ifc. uses such as ‘having the form/appearance of’. In this compound, ‘forms/modes’ best connects rūpa with Vyāsa’s fourfold catuṣṭaya.
→ rūpa is rendered ‘forms/modes’.
