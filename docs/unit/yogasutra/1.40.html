How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The compound is read as a bahuvrīhi adjective of vaśīkāraḥ: ‘whose two ends are the atom and supreme magnitude.’ The bhāṣya explicitly splits the range into subtle and gross, with paramāṇu as one end and paramamahattva as the other, then calls them ‘both limits’ (ubhayī koṭi).
→ paramāṇu and parama-mahattva are the two limiting extremes of the mastery, not a single object called ‘atom-supreme-greatness’.
ys [1]sūkṣme niviśamānasya paramāṇvantaṃ sthitipadaṃ labhata iti.
When it enters into the subtle, it obtains a station of steadiness ending in the atom.
ys [2]sthūle niviśamānasya paramamahattvāntaṃ sthitipadaṃ cittasya.
When it enters into the gross, there is for the mind a station of steadiness ending in supreme magnitude.
ys [3]evaṃ tām ubhayīṃ koṭim anudhāvato yo 'syāpratīghātaḥ sa paro vaśīkāraḥ.
Thus, as it runs along both those limits, its non-obstruction is supreme mastery.
The pronoun is construed with citta/cetas from the surrounding bhāṣya. The genitive is subjective/possessive: the mind’s mastery or non-impediment, not control exercised over the mind by someone else.
→ asya means ‘of this mind/citta,’ not ‘of this yogin’ as the primary syntactic referent.
ys [2]sthūle niviśamānasya paramamahattvāntaṃ sthitipadaṃ cittasya.
When it enters into the gross, there is for the mind a station of steadiness ending in supreme magnitude.
ys [3]yo 'syāpratīghātaḥ sa paro vaśīkāraḥ.
Its non-obstruction is supreme mastery.
ys [4]tadvaśīkārāt paripūrṇaṃ yoginaś cittaṃ na punar abhyāsakṛtaṃ parikarmāpekṣata iti.
Because of that mastery, the yogin’s mind is complete and no longer depends on cultivation produced by practice.
The translation keeps ‘mastery’ but constrains it by Vyāsa’s definition: yo 'syāpratīghātaḥ sa paro vaśīkāraḥ, ‘its non-obstruction is supreme mastery.’
→ vaśīkāraḥ is ‘mastery’ understood as unobstructed capacity of the mind to range over the two extremes.
ys [3]evaṃ tām ubhayīṃ koṭim anudhāvato yo 'syāpratīghātaḥ sa paro vaśīkāraḥ.
Thus, as it runs along both those limits, its non-obstruction is supreme mastery.
ys [4]tadvaśīkārāt paripūrṇaṃ yoginaś cittaṃ na punar abhyāsakṛtaṃ parikarmāpekṣata iti.
Because of that mastery, the yogin’s mind is complete and no longer depends on cultivation produced by practice.
