How the commentary settled it

Where the verse alone is ambiguous, the reading follows Vyāsa's commentary; the deciding lines are quoted below each decision.
The genitive pronoun is not left as an unspecified ‘of that’; Vyāsa explicitly states that Īśvara is the vācya, the object denoted by praṇava. Hence the translation supplies ‘[Īśvara]’ after ‘him.’
→ tasya = ‘of him,’ i.e. of Īśvara
ys [1]vācya īśvaraḥ praṇavasya.
Īśvara is the denoted object of praṇava.
Although vācaka can mean ‘speaker’ in other contexts, here the bhāṣya repeatedly frames the term in the technical pair vācya/vācaka, ‘denoted object/designator.’ Therefore ‘designator’ is required.
→ vācakaḥ = ‘designator/signifier’
ys [2, 3]kim asya saṃketakṛtaṃ vācyavācakatvam atha pradīpaprakāśavad avasthitam iti. sthito 'sya vācyasya vācakena saha saṃbandhaḥ.
Is this relation of denoted object and designator made by convention, or is it established like lamp and light? The relation of this denoted object with its designator is established.
ys [6]sargāntareṣv api vācyavācakaśaktyapekṣas tathaiva saṃketaḥ kriyate.
Even in other creations, convention is made in the same way with regard to the powers of denoted object and designator.
Line 2 raises the alternative that the vācya-vācaka relation might be saṃketakṛta, ‘made by convention’; line 3 answers that the relation is sthita, established. The father-son analogy and the appeal to nityatva reinforce that convention manifests or recognizes an already established relation rather than inventing it.
→ The praṇava is not merely an arbitrary conventional name for Īśvara; its word-object relation is treated as established.
ys [2, 3]kim asya saṃketakṛtaṃ vācyavācakatvam atha pradīpaprakāśavad avasthitam iti. sthito 'sya vācyasya vācakena saha saṃbandhaḥ.
Is this relation of denoted object and designator made by convention, or is it established like lamp and light? The relation of this denoted object with its designator is established.
ys [5]yathāvasthitaḥ pitṛputrayoḥ saṃbandhaḥ saṃketenāvadyotyate, ayam asya pitā, ayam asya putra iti.
Just as the relation of father and son, already existing, is illuminated by convention: ‘this is his father, this is his son.’
ys [7]saṃpratipattinityatayā nityaḥ śabdārthasaṃbandha ity āgaminaḥ pratijānate.
The āgamins maintain that the relation of word and meaning is eternal because common recognition is eternal.
The identification of praṇava as the sacred syllable Oṃ is lexical/traditional, supported by MW s.v. praṇava, 660,3 (‘the mystical or sacred syllable om’). The commentary does not define the sound-form here, so this choice does not depend on the cited bhāṣya lines.
→ praṇavaḥ = ‘the praṇava (Oṃ)’
