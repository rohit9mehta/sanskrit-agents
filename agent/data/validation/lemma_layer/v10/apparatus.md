## Justifications

**J1. Read and translate the opening as ādyāḥ, ‘the first-mentioned ones,’ not as adyāḥ in any ordinary lexical sense; Sthiramati equates these first-mentioned factors with the sarvatraga class.**
- chosen: ādyāḥ = the first-mentioned sarvatraga dharmas (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [496]: `ādau nirdiṣṭatvād ādyāḥ sarvatragā ity arthaḥ` — Because they were specified at the beginning, they are ‘the first’; the meaning is ‘the universal factors’.
- trbh [498, 499]: `sadāsparśamanaskāravitsaṃjñācetanānvitam
iti prathamato nirdiṣṭāḥ` — The earlier verse passage listed contact, attention, etc.; thus they were specified first.

**J2. Analyze sparśādayaḥ as a bahuvrīhi: ‘those whose first is contact,’ referring to the five universal mental factors, not to an indefinite open-ended ‘contact and so on’ without scope.**
- chosen: bahuvrīhi sparśaḥ ādiḥ eṣām; the five dharmas beginning with sparśa (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [500]: `sparśa eṣām ādir iti sparśādayaḥ` — ‘Contact is their beginning’: thus sparśādayaḥ.
- trbh [502]: `sparśamanaskārādayaḥ pañca dharmāḥ sarvacittam anugacchantīti sarvatragāḥ` — The five dharmas beginning with contact and attention accompany every mind; therefore they are universal.
- trbh [503, 504]: `tathā hy eta ālayavijñāne kliṣṭe manasi pravṛttivijñāneṣu cāviśeṣeṇa
pravartante` — For they occur without distinction in ālayavijñāna, afflicted manas, and the active consciousnesses.

**J3. Construe chandādhimokṣasmṛtayaḥ with saha samādhidhībhyām as a set of five niyata/viniyata dharmas; samādhi and dhī are included by saha with the preceding triple.**
- chosen: chanda, adhimokṣa, smṛti, together with samādhi and dhī, are the five viniyata dharmas (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [505, 506, 507]: `viniyatān adhikṛtyāha
chandādhimokṣasmṛtayaḥ saha
samādhidhībhyāṃ niyatāḥ` — With reference to the determinate/restricted ones he says: desire, ascertainment, and mindfulness, together with concentration and discernment, are niyata.
- trbh [568, 569, 570, 571]: `ete hi pañca dharmāḥ parasparaṃ vyatiricyāpi pravartante
evañ ca
yatrādhimokṣas tatra nāvaśyam itarair api bhavitavyam
evaṃ sarvatra vācyam` — These are five dharmas, and they occur even separately from one another; where adhimokṣa is present, the others need not necessarily be present. This should be said throughout.

**J4. Translate niyatāḥ in this context as ‘determinate/restricted,’ i.e. having specific objects, not as ‘permanent,’ ‘necessary,’ or ‘always present.’**
- chosen: restricted/determinate: viśeṣe niyata, with a specific object rather than every object (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [508, 509]: `viśeṣe niyatatvād viniyatāḥ
eṣāṃ hi viśeṣa eva viṣayo na sarvaḥ` — They are ‘restricted’ because they are fixed on a specific case; for their object is only something specific, not everything.

**J5. Translate chanda as aspiration/desire toward an intended object, not as ‘metre’ or a general preference without object-restriction.**
- chosen: aspiration/desire (chanda) toward an abhipreta vastu (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [510, 511, 512, 513, 514, 515, 516]: `tatra chando 'bhiprete vastuny abhilāṣaḥ
abhiprete vastuny abhilāṣa iti
pratiniyataviṣayatvaṃ jñāpitaṃ bhavaty anabhiprete chandābhāvāt
darśanaśravaṇādikriyāviṣayatvena yad abhimataṃ vastu tad abhipretam
tatra
darśanaśravaṇādiprārthanā chandaḥ
sa ca vīryārambhasaṃniśrayadānakarmakaḥ` — Chanda is desire for an intended object; saying ‘for an intended object’ shows its specifically determined object, since chanda is absent toward what is not intended. It is the wishing for seeing, hearing, and the like, and it has the function of giving support for the undertaking of vigor.

**J6. Translate adhimokṣa as ascertainment/resolve toward a decided object, not as liberation or release.**
- chosen: ascertainment/resolve (adhimokṣa) with respect to a niścita vastu (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [517, 518, 519, 520, 521, 522, 523, 524, 525, 526]: `adhimokṣo niścite vastuni tathaivāvadhāraṇam
niścitagrahaṇam
aniścitapratiṣedhārtham
yuktita āptopadeśato vā yad vastu asaṃdigdhaṃ tan
niścitam
yenaivākāreṇa tan niścitam anityaduḥkhādyākāreṇa tenaivākāreṇa
tasya vastunaś cetasy abhiniveśanam
evam etan nānyathety avadhāraṇam
adhimokṣaḥ
sa cāsaṃhāryatākarmakaḥ` — Adhimokṣa is determination just so with respect to a decided object; ‘decided’ excludes the undecided. What is free from doubt through reasoning or authoritative instruction is decided. Fixing that object in mind in just the way it is decided—‘it is thus, not otherwise’—is adhimokṣa; its function is non-removability.

**J7. Translate smṛti as mindfulness/recollection, specifically the non-loss and mental re-expression of a familiar or previously experienced object, not as a text or sacred tradition.**
- chosen: mindfulness/recollection (smṛti) as asaṃpramoṣa and cetaso 'bhilapanatā (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [529, 530, 531, 532, 533, 534, 535]: `smṛtiḥ saṃstute vastuny asaṃpramoṣaś cetaso 'bhilapanatā
saṃstutaṃ vastu
pūrvānubhūtam
ālambanagrahaṇāvipraṇāśakāraṇatvād asaṃpramoṣaḥ
pūrvagṛhītasya vastunaḥ punaḥ punar ālambanākārasmaraṇam abhilapanatā
abhilapanam evābhilapanatā
sā punar avikṣepakarmikā` — Smṛti is non-loss with respect to a familiar object and the mind’s abhilapanatā; the familiar object is one previously experienced. It is non-loss because it causes the grasping of the object not to perish; abhilapanatā is repeatedly remembering the object-aspect of what was previously grasped. Its function is non-distraction.

**J8. Translate samādhi as concentration/single-pointedness and dhī as discernment/prajñā, not as generic ‘putting together’ and ‘thought.’**
- chosen: concentration (samādhi) and discernment (dhī/prajñā) (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [538, 539, 540, 541, 542, 543, 544]: `samādhir upaparīkṣye vastuni cittasyaikāgratā
upaparīkṣyaṃ vastu guṇato
doṣato vā
ekāgratā ekālambanatā
jñānasaṃniśrayadānakarmakaḥ
samāhite
citte yathābhūtaparijñānāt` — Samādhi is the mind’s single-pointedness with respect to an object to be examined, whether in terms of qualities or faults; single-pointedness is having one object, and it supports knowledge, since true comprehension occurs in a concentrated mind.
- trbh [545, 546, 547, 548, 549, 550]: `dhīḥ prajñā
sāpy upaparīkṣya eva vastuni pravicayo yogāyogavihito 'nyathā
veti
pravicinotīti pravicayaḥ
yaḥ samyaṅ mithyā vā
saṃkīrṇasvasāmānyalakṣaṇeṣv iva dharmeṣu vivekāvabodhaḥ` — Dhī is prajñā. It is investigation of an examinable object, produced by proper means, improper means, or otherwise. Pravicaya is what investigates; whether correct or mistaken, it is discriminating understanding among dharmas with mixed own- and common-characteristics.
- trbh [566, 567]: `eṣā ca saṃśayavyāvartanakarmikā
saṃśayavyāvartanaṃ prajñayā dharmān pravicinvato niścayalābhād iti` — Its function is the removal of doubt, since one gains certainty when investigating dharmas by prajñā.

**J9. Take śraddhā atha hrīḥ apatrapā as the beginning of the next kuśala list, not as further items governed by niyatāḥ.**
- chosen: atha introduces the immediately following kuśala dharmas, beginning with śraddhā, hrī, and apatrapā (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [572, 573, 574]: `uktā viniyatāḥ
tadanantaroddiṣṭās tv idānīṃ kuśalā vaktavyā ity ata āha
śraddhātha hrīr apatrapā` — The restricted ones have been stated. Now the wholesome factors specified immediately after them are to be stated; therefore he says: faith, then shame, apatrapā.

## Translation

> The first-mentioned—contact (sparśa) and the rest—are the universal factors (sarvatraga);
aspiration (chanda), ascertainment (adhimokṣa), and mindfulness (smṛti),
together with concentration (samādhi) and discernment (dhī), are the determinate/restricted ones (niyata/viniyata).
Then: faith (śraddhā), shame (hrī), and compunction (apatrapā).
