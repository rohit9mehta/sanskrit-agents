## Justifications

**J1. The pronouns enam and ayam refer to the dehin/ātman, not to the physical body as such.**
- chosen: ‘this embodied Self’ or ‘this Self’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `ya enaṃ prakṛtaṃ dehinaṃ vetti vijānāti` — ‘He who knows this—the embodied one under discussion—understands...’
- gita [4]: `yasmāt na ayam atmā hanti` — ‘Because this Self does not kill...’

**J2. hantāram is to be understood technically as the agent of the act of killing, not merely as a loose epithet ‘murderer.’**
- chosen: ‘as the slayer,’ i.e. as agent of the act of killing (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `hantāraṃ hanana-kriyāyāḥ kartāraṃ` — ‘hantāram: the agent of the act of killing.’
- gita [4]: `na ayam atmā hanti na hanana-kriyāyāḥ kartā bhavati` — ‘This Self does not kill; it does not become the agent of the act of killing.’

**J3. hatam and hanyate mark the Self as imagined patient/object of killing; Śaṅkara rejects that patienthood, especially as arising from identification with killing of the body.**
- chosen: ‘slain,’ with the sense ‘made the object/patient of killing’ only in the mistaken view (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `manyate hataṃ deha-hananena hato 'ham iti hanana-kriyāyāḥ karma-bhūtam` — ‘[He] thinks [this] slain: “I am slain by the killing of the body,” as having become the object of the act of killing.’
- gita [4]: `na ca hanyate na ca karma bhavatīty arthaḥ` — ‘Nor is it killed; that means, it does not become the object [of the action].’

**J4. ubhau tau na vijānītaḥ means that the two mistaken cognizers do not truly know the Self; ubhau tau are the dual subject, not an object.**
- chosen: ‘both of them do not know,’ namely they do not know the Self’s true nature (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `tau ubhau na vijānītaḥ na jñātavantau avivekena ātmānam` — ‘Those two both do not know; through non-discrimination they have not known the Self.’
- gita [3]: `yau vijānītaḥ tau ātma-svarūpānabhijñau ity arthaḥ` — ‘Those two who understand [in that way] are ignorant of the Self’s own nature—this is the meaning.’

**J5. The final pāda is a metaphysical denial of agency and patienthood in the changeless Self, not an ethical injunction or a denial that bodily killing occurs.**
- chosen: ‘This Self neither kills nor is killed,’ grounded in avikriyatva, changelessness (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `na ca karma bhavatīty arthaḥ, avikriyatvāt` — ‘Nor does it become the object [of the act]; this is the meaning, because it is changeless.’

**J6. The two yaḥ-clauses are correlative with tau ubhau and identify two mistaken standpoints, the second explicitly ‘another’ according to the commentary.**
- chosen: two mistaken cognizers: ‘whoever knows it as slayer, and whoever thinks it slain—both...’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `ya ca enam anyo manyate hataṃ` — ‘And another who thinks this one slain...’
- gita [3]: `yau vijānītaḥ tau` — ‘the two who understand [thus], those two...’

## Translation

> Whoever knows this embodied Self as the slayer,
and whoever thinks this one slain—
both of them do not know.
This Self does not kill, nor is it killed.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind rendering might take enam as the body or an ordinary person; Śaṅkara identifies it as the dehin/ātman under discussion.
- It might translate hantāram and hatam merely as ‘killer’ and ‘dead,’ missing Śaṅkara’s kartṛ/karma analysis of agency and patienthood in the act of killing.
- It might read the final statement ethically or physically, whereas the bhāṣya makes it a claim about the changeless Self’s non-agency and non-patienthood.
- It might leave na vijānītaḥ without its supplied object; Śaṅkara states that the two fail to know the Self due to non-discrimination.
