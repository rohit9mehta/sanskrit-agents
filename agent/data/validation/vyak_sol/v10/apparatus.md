- trbh [496]: `ādau nirdiṣṭatvād ādyāḥ sarvatragā ity arthaḥ` — Because they were indicated at the beginning, they are ‘the first’; the meaning is ‘the omnipresent factors.’
- trbh [502, 503, 504]: `sparśamanaskārādayaḥ pañca dharmāḥ sarvacittam anugacchantīti sarvatragāḥ

pravartante` — The five factors beginning with contact and mental application accompany every mind and are therefore omnipresent. Thus they occur without distinction in the store consciousness, the afflicted mind, and the active consciousnesses.

**J2. Analyze sparśādayaḥ as a bahuvrīhi whose referents have contact as their first member, not merely as an opaque expression meaning ‘contact, etc.’**

## Translation

> The first-listed—the omnipresent factors (sarvatraga)—are contact and the rest;
aspiration (chanda), resolve (adhimokṣa), and mindfulness (smṛti), together
with concentration (samādhi) and discernment (dhī), are the determinate factors (niyata, the viniyata class).
Next: faith (śraddhā), shame (hrī), and embarrassment (apatrapā).

## Analyzer disagreements

- For adyāḥ, Vyākaraṇī proposed a second-person singular benedictive of ad. The supplied canonical lemma adya, nominative-plural syntax, and Sthiramati’s explicit explanation ādyāḥ sarvatragā rule out that verbal parse.
- ByT5 split sparśādayaḥ into sparśa plus ādayaḥ, and Vyākaraṇī analyzed only ādayaḥ. Line 500 explicitly derives the whole bahuvrīhi sparśādayaḥ, so the adopted stem is sparśādi.
- ByT5 split chandādhimokṣasmṛtayaḥ into three members, and Vyākaraṇī analyzed only smṛtayaḥ. The commentary treats the expression as three coordinated members of a five-factor set, so the adopted lemma is the whole dvandva stem chandādhimokṣasmṛti.
- ByT5 split samādhidhībhyām into samādhi plus dhībhyām, and Vyākaraṇī assigned the latter the simple stem dhī. Its coordination under saha and the commentary’s count of five require the whole dvandva stem samādhidhī. The instrumental dual features themselves are retained.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind analysis could parse adyāḥ as a benedictive of ad (‘may you eat’) or as ‘today’s,’ missing Sthiramati’s explicit ādyāḥ, ‘first-listed,’ and its reference to the omnipresent factors.
- It could translate niyatāḥ simply as ‘restrained’ or ‘fixed,’ missing the technical viniyata class whose members occur only with particular objects.
- It could miss the two dvandvas and attach saha incorrectly; Sthiramati explicitly counts chanda, adhimokṣa, smṛti, samādhi, and dhī as five factors.
- It could render dhī merely as ‘thought’ rather than prajñā or discernment, and could fail to recognize śraddhātha as the transition to the wholesome category.

## Open questions

- The supplied verse has short-initial adyāḥ, whereas Sthiramati’s gloss and the vulgate have semantically expected ādyāḥ. The transmitted surface and canonical lemma adya are retained, but the translation follows the commentary’s explicit interpretation.
- The present commentary span identifies śraddhā, hrī, and apatrapā as the beginning of the wholesome class but does not yet define their individual functions; accordingly, ‘shame’ and ‘embarrassment’ remain conservative provisional glosses rather than a fully differentiated doctrinal account.
