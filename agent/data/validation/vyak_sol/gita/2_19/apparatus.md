## Justifications

**J1. Both occurrences of enam and the later ayam denote the embodied Self (ātman) under discussion, not the physical body.**
- chosen: the embodied Self under discussion (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `enaṃ prakṛtaṃ dehinaṃ` — ‘this embodied one under discussion’
- gita [2]: `avivekena ātmānam` — ‘the Self, through lack of discrimination’

**J2. Hantāram is a predicative accusative construed with enam and means ‘as the killer,’ specifically the agent of the act of killing.**
- chosen: predicative accusative expressing supposed agency (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `hantāraṃ hanana-kriyāyāḥ kartāraṃ` — ‘the killer, the agent of the act of killing’

**J3. Manyate is active in construction, and hatam is its predicative accusative: the second person mistakenly considers the Self to be slain because the body has been killed.**
- chosen: ‘considers this Self slain’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `ya ca enam anyo manyate hataṃ deha-hananena hato 'ham iti hanana-kriyāyāḥ karma-bhūtam` — ‘and another who considers this slain—“I have been slain” through the killing of the body—as the object of the act of killing’
- gita [3]: `hantā ahaṃ, hato 'smy aham iti deha-hananena ātmānam` — ‘[those who apprehend] the Self, through the killing of the body, as “I am the killer” or “I have been slain”’

**J4. Ubhau tau correlates with the two yaḥ clauses. The negative means that neither of those two possesses true knowledge of the Self; ātmānam is the understood object of vijānītaḥ.**
- chosen: ‘neither of those two understands the Self’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `tau ubhau na vijānītaḥ na jñātavantau avivekena ātmānam` — ‘Those two both do not understand—have not known—the Self, owing to nondiscrimination.’
- gita [3]: `yau vijānītaḥ tau ātma-svarūpānabhijñau ity arthaḥ` — ‘Those two who apprehend it thus are ignorant of the Self’s true nature: that is the meaning.’

**J5. Hanti is active and hanyate passive, but the verse denies that the immutable Self is respectively the agent or the affected object of killing.**
- chosen: denial that the Self is either killing-agent or killed object, grounded in its immutability (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `hanti na hanana-kriyāyāḥ kartā bhavati, na ca hanyate na ca karma bhavatīty arthaḥ, avikriyatvāt` — ‘It does not kill: it does not become the agent of the act of killing; nor is it killed, nor does it become the object—that is the meaning—because it is immutable.’

**J6. The lemma of ubhau is the nominal stem ubha, rather than the verbal root ubh proposed as the stem by Vyākaraṇī.**
- chosen: ubha (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> Whoever knows this Self (ātman) as the killer,
and whoever considers it slain—
neither of those two understands:
this Self does not kill, nor is it killed.

## Analyzer disagreements

- For ubhau, Vyākaraṇī gives the stem ubh. This is overridden by the supplied canonical lemma ubha; ubh is a verbal root, whereas ubhau is the masculine nominative dual of the nominal ubha.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might leave enam as an ambiguous ‘him’; Śaṅkara identifies its referent as the embodied Self, not the body.
- It might miss that hantāram and hatam are predicative accusatives assigning the Self the roles of killing-agent and killed object.
- It might treat bodily death as the Self’s death; the commentary explains ‘I have been slain’ as a mistaken identification produced by the killing of the body.
- It might render the final line merely as a paradox or ethical prohibition, whereas Śaṅkara takes it as a denial of agency and object-status based on the Self’s immutability.
