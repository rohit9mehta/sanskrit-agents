## Translation

> The first-listed—those beginning with contact (sparśa)—are universally concomitant (sarvatraga);
aspiration (chanda), determination (adhimokṣa), and mindfulness (smṛti),
together in the list with concentration (samādhi) and discernment (dhī), are the object-specific factors (viniyata);
then the wholesome (kuśala) factors begin: faith (śraddhā), moral shame (hrī), and compunction (apatrapā).

## One-shot delta

- The short-a surface adyāḥ could invite “today’s,” but Sthiramati reads ādyāḥ, derives it from prior placement, and supplies the omitted classification sarvatragāḥ.
- Sparśādayaḥ is not a vague “contact and so forth”: the commentary gives a possessive vigraha, “those whose first is contact.”
- Niyatāḥ does not mean permanent, controlled, or necessarily co-occurring. It denotes five factors restricted to particular objects, and the commentary says that they may arise separately.
- Saha is enumerative: it adds the samādhi–dhī pair to the first three factors. It does not assert that all five always occur together.
- Dhī could be rendered generically as “intelligence,” but the gloss dhīḥ prajñā and the ensuing definition require discriminative discernment.
- Atha does not merely add three more object-specific factors; it marks the beginning of the following wholesome category.

- The supplied verse has adyāḥ with short initial a, while the commentary explicitly quotes ādyāḥ and explains it from ādau nirdiṣṭatvāt. The apparatus retains the supplied surface and canonical lemma adya for formal morphology but follows the commentary’s ādya sense in translation.
- The verse-10 commentary span classifies śraddhā, hrī, and apatrapā as wholesome but does not yet define their individual functions. “Faith,” “moral shame,” and “compunction” are therefore conservative lexical renderings; their finer technical differentiation depends on the subsequent commentary.
