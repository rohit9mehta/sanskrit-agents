## Justifications

**J1. Ādyāḥ means “those stated first,” and sparśādayaḥ denotes their class; the verse therefore supplies the understood classification “universally operative” (sarvatraga), rather than the temporal adverb “today.”**
- chosen: “Those first stated—those beginning with contact—are universally operative (sarvatraga).” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [496]: `ādau nirdiṣṭatvād ādyāḥ sarvatragā ity arthaḥ` — “Ādyāḥ” means sarvatragāḥ because they were stated at the beginning.
- trbh [498, 499]: `sadāsparśamanaskāravitsaṃjñācetanānvitam
iti prathamato nirdiṣṭāḥ` — The preceding verse had stated them first.
- trbh [502, 503, 504]: `sparśamanaskārādayaḥ pañca dharmāḥ sarvacittam anugacchantīti sarvatragāḥ
tathā hy eta ālayavijñāne kliṣṭe manasi pravṛttivijñāneṣu cāviśeṣeṇa
pravartante` — The five beginning with contact accompany every mind and operate without distinction in ālayavijñāna, the afflicted manas, and the active consciousnesses.

**J2. Sparśādayaḥ is an exocentric compound: “those whose beginning is contact,” not a simple coordinate phrase “contact and beginnings.”**
- chosen: Bahuvrīhi: “those beginning with contact.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [500]: `sparśa eṣām ādir iti sparśādayaḥ` — “Sparśādayaḥ” means those for whom sparśa is the beginning.

**J3. Niyatāḥ means that the five dharmas are restricted to special, particular objects (viśeṣa), not that they are invariably present together or are simply “determined” in an unrestricted sense.**
- chosen: “Restricted to particular objects.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [505, 506, 507, 508, 509]: `viniyatān adhikṛtyāha
chandādhimokṣasmṛtayaḥ saha
samādhidhībhyāṃ niyatāḥ
viśeṣe niyatatvād viniyatāḥ
eṣāṃ hi viśeṣa eva viṣayo na sarvaḥ` — The passage concerns the viniyata dharmas: they are niyata with respect to a special object, not every object.

**J4. Chanda is rendered “aspiration,” adhimokṣa “resolute ascertainment,” and smṛti “mindfulness/recollection,” because Sthiramati gives their respective technical definitions.**
- chosen: “Aspiration (chanda), resolute ascertainment (adhimokṣa), and mindfulness (smṛti).” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [510, 515, 516]: `tatra chando 'bhiprete vastuny abhilāṣaḥ
darśanaśravaṇādiprārthanā chandaḥ
sa ca vīryārambhasaṃniśrayadānakarmakaḥ` — Chanda is desire for an intended object, specifically wishing for seeing, hearing, and the like.
- trbh [517, 522, 523, 524, 525, 526]: `adhimokṣo niścite vastuni tathaivāvadhāraṇam
yenaivākāreṇa tan niścitam anityaduḥkhādyākāreṇa tenaivākāreṇa
tasya vastunaś cetasy abhiniveśanam
evam etan nānyathety avadhāraṇam
adhimokṣaḥ
sa cāsaṃhāryatākarmakaḥ` — Adhimokṣa is unwavering ascertainment of an object precisely in its ascertained aspect.
- trbh [529, 533, 535, 537]: `smṛtiḥ saṃstute vastuny asaṃpramoṣaś cetaso 'bhilapanatā
pūrvagṛhītasya vastunaḥ punaḥ punar ālambanākārasmaraṇam abhilapanatā
sā punar avikṣepakarmikā
cittasyālambanāntara ākārāntare vā vikṣepābhāvād avikṣepakarmikā` — Smṛti is non-forgetting and repeated recollective apprehension of a previously experienced object; its function is non-distraction.

**J5. Samādhi is “concentration” and dhī is “discernment”; samādhidhībhyām is a dual dvandva governed by saha.**
- chosen: “Together with concentration (samādhi) and discernment (dhī).” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [538, 541, 542]: `samādhir upaparīkṣye vastuni cittasyaikāgratā
ekāgratā ekālambanatā
jñānasaṃniśrayadānakarmakaḥ` — Samādhi is one-pointedness of mind on an object to be examined, i.e. having one support.
- trbh [545, 546, 548, 550]: `dhīḥ prajñā
sāpy upaparīkṣya eva vastuni pravicayo yogāyogavihito 'nyathā
pravicinotīti pravicayaḥ
saṃkīrṇasvasāmānyalakṣaṇeṣv iva dharmeṣu vivekāvabodhaḥ` — Dhī is prajñā, discriminative examination and discerning awareness.

**J6. Saha groups the preceding three and the following two into a list of five, but does not state that all five must occur together.**
- chosen: Enumerative association only; the five may occur separately. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [568, 570, 571]: `ete hi pañca dharmāḥ parasparaṃ vyatiricyāpi pravartante
evañ ca
yatrādhimokṣas tatra nāvaśyam itarair api bhavitavyam
evaṃ sarvatra vācyam` — These five operate even separately from one another; where adhimokṣa occurs, the others need not necessarily occur, and the same is to be said throughout.

**J7. Atha is transitional—“then”—and śraddhā, hrī, and apatrapā begin the subsequent list of wholesome dharmas; they are not further members of the niyata group.**
- chosen: “Then: faith, shame, and moral scruple,” beginning the wholesome list. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- trbh [572, 573, 574]: `uktā viniyatāḥ
tadanantaroddiṣṭās tv idānīṃ kuśalā vaktavyā ity ata āha
śraddhātha hrīr apatrapā` — After the viniyata dharmas have been stated, the wholesome dharmas that were subsequently mentioned are now to be explained; then the verse names śraddhā, hrī, and apatrapā.

## Translation

> Those first stated—those beginning with contact—are universally operative (sarvatraga);
aspiration (chanda), resolute ascertainment (adhimokṣa), and mindfulness (smṛti),
with concentration (samādhi) and discernment (dhī), are restricted to particular objects;
then: faith (śraddhā), shame (hrī), and moral scruple (apatrapā).

## Analyzer disagreements

- adyāḥ: the tagger analyzes this as ad/adya. It is instead ādyāḥ, Prathama Bahu Pum of ādi, as required by Sthiramati's gloss “ādau nirdiṣṭatvād ādyāḥ” (trbh 496).

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind reading could misanalyse ādyāḥ as connected with adya (“today”), whereas Sthiramati explicitly takes it as “those stated first” and supplies their status as sarvatraga.
- It could render niyatāḥ vaguely as “determined” or infer that the five always co-occur. The commentary defines their restriction by their particular objects and expressly says that the five can operate separately.
- It could attach śraddhā, hrī, and apatrapā to the preceding niyata classification. The commentary marks them instead as the opening of the wholesome (kuśala) list.
- It could flatten adhimokṣa into ordinary assent and smṛti into bare memory, missing their defined functions of unshakeable ascertainment and non-forgetting, non-distraction.
