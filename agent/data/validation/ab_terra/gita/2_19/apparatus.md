## Justifications

**J1. The two occurrences of enam denote the embodied self currently under discussion; hantāram means the agent of killing, whereas hatam means that self regarded as the object killed through the body's destruction.**
- chosen: Both are predicative accusatives construed with enam: “knows this embodied self as a killer” and “thinks it killed.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `ya enaṃ prakṛtaṃ dehinaṃ vetti vijānāti hantāraṃ hanana-kriyāyāḥ kartāraṃ ya ca enam anyo manyate hataṃ deha-hananena hato 'ham iti hanana-kriyāyāḥ karma-bhūtam` — He glosses hantāram as the agent of the act of killing and hatam as what has become the object of that act, through the destruction of the body.

**J2. Tau ubhau refers jointly to the two persons introduced by the two relative clauses, not to two selves or to a killer and a separate victim.**
- chosen: “Those two” are the two persons holding the respective false views. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1, 2]: `ya ca enam anyo manyate hataṃ deha-hananena hato 'ham iti hanana-kriyāyāḥ karma-bhūtam | tau ubhau na vijānītaḥ na jñātavantau avivekena ātmānam` — The commentator identifies a second person (anyaḥ) and then calls the pair tau ubhau.

**J3. Na vijānītaḥ is not merely failure to identify a fact; it means that the two lack discriminative knowledge of the Self and are ignorant of its true nature.**
- chosen: The translation retains “do not know,” while its referent is the Self's true nature. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2, 3]: `tau ubhau na vijānītaḥ na jñātavantau avivekena ātmānam | hantā ahaṃ, hato 'smy aham iti deha-hananena ātmānam ahaṃ pratyaya-viṣayaṃ yau vijānītaḥ tau ātma-svarūpānabhijñau ity arthaḥ` — Śaṅkara glosses the expression as not having known the Self through non-discrimination, and states that the two are ignorant of the Self's own nature.

**J4. Hanti is active, “kills,” and hanyate is passive, “is killed”; both are denied of the Self respectively as agent and as object of the act of killing.**
- chosen: “This Self neither kills nor is killed.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `yasmāt na ayam atmā hanti na hanana-kriyāyāḥ kartā bhavati, na ca hanyate na ca karma bhavatīty arthaḥ, avikriyatvāt` — Hanti is glossed as not being the agent (kartṛ) of the killing-action, and hanyate as not being its object (karman), because the Self is unmodifiable.

## Translation

> He who knows this embodied self as a killer,
and he who thinks it killed—
those two do not know; this Self neither kills nor is killed.

## Analyzer disagreements

- For enam, the analyzer cites enad. I cite the nominal lemma idam, the dictionary headword supplied for this demonstrative; enam is its enclitic masculine accusative singular form.
- For ubhau, the analyzer's material links the form to verbal ubh and describes it as a kṛdanta. Here ubhau is instead the masculine nominative dual of the pronominal/adjectival stem ubha, agreeing appositionally with tau; the dual subject is confirmed by the commentary's “tau ubhau” (line 2).

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind rendering may miss that hantāram and hatam are not symmetrical labels for two people: Śaṅkara construes them as the mistaken attribution of agenthood and patienthood in the killing-action to the one embodied self.
- It may translate vijānītaḥ as merely “do not recognize,” without seeing that the stated defect is ignorance of the Self's proper nature (ātma-svarūpa).
- It may flatten “neither kills nor is killed” into a generic denial of mortality, whereas the commentary expressly contrasts the Self's non-agency (kartṛtva) and non-objecthood (karmatva), grounded in its unchangeability.
