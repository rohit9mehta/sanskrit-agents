## Justifications

**J1. The verse is not read temporally (“as long as ... so long”) but quantitatively, since Śaṅkara glosses both correlatives with parimāṇa, measure/extent.**
- chosen: yāvān ... tāvān expresses correlative measure: “as much ... just so much.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `yāvān yāvat-parimāṇaḥ` — yāvān means “of whatever measure/extent.”
- gita [2]: `tāvān tāvat-parimāṇa eva` — tāvān means “just of that measure/extent.”

**J2. Śaṅkara glosses artha in the water example as phala and prayojana, and in the Vedic analogue as karma-phala and vijñāna-phala.**
- chosen: artha is translated as “purpose/use/result,” not as semantic “meaning,” wealth, or object. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `snāna-pānādir arthaḥ phalaṃ prayojanaṃ` — the artha such as bathing and drinking is fruit, purpose.
- gita [2]: `yo 'rthaḥ yat karma-phalaṃ` — the artha there is the fruit of action.
- gita [2]: `yo 'rthaḥ yat vijñāna-phalaṃ` — the artha there is the fruit of knowledge.

**J3. The singular udapāna is not treated narrowly as one particular well; Śaṅkara explicitly expands it to many such limited-water sources.**
- chosen: udapāne denotes a limited water-source, exemplified by wells and tanks. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `kūpa-taḍāgādy-anekasmin udapāne paricchinnodake` — in many water-sources such as wells and tanks, with limited water.

**J4. Śaṅkara says that all the limited purposes of wells and tanks are fulfilled in, and included within, the all-around flooded water. This rules out a commentary-blind rendering in which the point is merely that wells become useless during a flood.**
- chosen: sarvataḥ saṃplutodake is “in water flooded on every side,” the inclusive counterpart to the limited well/tank water. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `sa sarvo 'rthaḥ sarvataḥ saṃplutodake 'pi yo 'rthaḥ tāvān eva saṃpadyate, tatra antarbhavatīty arthaḥ` — all that purpose is fulfilled as just that much also in the water flooded on every side; the meaning is that it is included there.
- gita [2]: `vijñāna-phalaṃ sarvataḥ saṃplutodaka-sthānīyaṃ` — the fruit of knowledge occupies the position of the all-around flooded water.

**J5. Śaṅkara construes the locative not merely as the textual Vedas or sacred lore in general, but as the karmas taught by the Vedas and their fruits.**
- chosen: sarveṣu vedeṣu is interpreted as “in all Veda-enjoined rites,” and their artha as karma-phala. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `sarveṣu vedeṣu vedokteṣu karmasu yo 'rthaḥ yat karma-phalaṃ` — the artha in all the Vedas, that is, in the rites taught by the Vedas, is the fruit of action.

**J6. Śaṅkara identifies the brāhmaṇa here with a saṃnyāsin and specifies the object of knowing as paramārtha-tattva, ultimate reality.**
- chosen: brāhmaṇasya vijānataḥ means “for/of the Brāhmaṇa, the renouncer, who knows ultimate reality.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `brāhmaṇasya saṃnyāsinaḥ paramārtha-tattvaṃ vijānataḥ` — of the Brāhmaṇa, the renouncer, who knows the truth of ultimate reality.

**J7. Although the knower’s knowledge-fruit includes the fruits of Veda-enjoined rites, Śaṅkara adds that before eligibility for steadfast knowledge, one who is qualified for karma must still perform karma.**
- chosen: The verse is not a blanket rejection of karma for everyone. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [5]: `tasmāt prāk jñāna-niṣṭhādhikāra-prāpteḥ karmaṇy adhikṛtena kūpa-taḍāgādy-artha-sthānīyam api karma kartavyam` — therefore, before attaining eligibility for steadfastness in knowledge, karma—though occupying the place of the purposes of wells, tanks, and the like—must be done by one qualified for karma.

## Translation

> Whatever purpose/result (artha) there is in a water-source—a well or tank—
[all that] is fulfilled in water flooded on every side;
just that much [fruit], in all the Vedas—that is, in Veda-enjoined rites—
is [included] for the Brāhmaṇa (brāhmaṇa), the renouncer (saṃnyāsin), who knows ultimate reality (paramārtha-tattva).

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render artha as “meaning” and make the verse about the semantic content of the Vedas; Śaṅkara makes it purpose/result, specifically karma-phala and vijñāna-phala.
- It might say simply, “a well is useless when there is a flood.” Śaṅkara’s point is inclusion: the purposes of limited water-sources are fulfilled within water flooded on every side.
- It might take sarveṣu vedeṣu as the Vedic texts in general. Śaṅkara narrows the comparison to Veda-enjoined rites and their fruits.
- It might take brāhmaṇa as a caste label. Śaṅkara glosses the brāhmaṇa here as a saṃnyāsin who knows paramārtha-tattva.
- It might infer a universal dismissal of karma; Śaṅkara explicitly preserves the obligation of karma before eligibility for jñāna-niṣṭhā.
