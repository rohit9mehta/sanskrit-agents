## Justifications

**J1. The enclitic te is construed as genitive singular dependent on buddhiḥ: ‘your intellect.’**
- chosen: te = tava, ‘your/of you’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `te tava` — ‘te’ means ‘your.’

**J2. The compound is taken as a karmadhāraya-like ‘delusion-impurity,’ not merely a physical ‘thicket’ or an unspecified difficulty. It is the accusative object crossed over by the intellect.**
- chosen: moha-kalilaṃ = an impurity/turbidity consisting in delusion and non-discrimination (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `moha-kalilaṃ mohātmakam aviveka-rūpaṃ kāluṣyaṃ` — ‘moha-kalila’ is an impurity/turbidity whose nature is delusion, whose form is non-discrimination.
- gita [1]: `yena ātmānātma-viveka-bodhaṃ kaluṣīkṛtya viṣayaṃ pratyantaḥ-karaṇaṃ pravartate` — By it, after the cognition discriminating self and not-self has been defiled, the inner organ turns toward objects.

**J3. The verb is not a mere motion verb but means that the intellect will pass beyond the delusive impurity, reaching a highly purified condition.**
- chosen: vyatitariṣyati = ‘will pass beyond/transcend’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `tat tava buddhiḥ vyatitariṣyati vyatikramiṣyati, atiśuddha-bhāvam āpātryate ity arthaḥ` — That, your intellect, will pass beyond; that is, it will overstep it; the sense is that it comes to a state of great purity.

**J4. nirveda is translated as dispassion/detachment, not as depression, despair, or simple disgust.**
- chosen: nirvedaṃ = vairāgya, ‘dispassion’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `gantāsi prāpsyasi nirvedaṃ vairāgyaṃ` — ‘You will go/come to’ means ‘you will attain’; ‘nirveda’ means ‘dispassion.’

**J5. The two genitives depend on nirveda/vairāgya and are neuter substantives. Śaṅkara further explains that both the not-yet-heard and the already-heard appear fruitless to the seeker at that point.**
- chosen: śrotavyasya śrutasya ca = ‘toward what is to be heard and what has been heard’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `nirvedaṃ vairāgyaṃ śrotavyasya śrutasya ca` — Dispassion with respect to what is to be heard and what has been heard.
- gita [2]: `tadā śrotavyaṃ śrutaṃ ca te niṣphalaṃ pratibhātīty abhiprāyaḥ` — The purport is: then what is to be heard and what has been heard appear fruitless to you.

## Translation

> When your intellect (buddhi) shall pass beyond
this delusion-impurity (moha-kalila),
then you will attain dispassion (nirveda)
toward what is to be heard and what has been heard.

## One-shot delta

- Without Śaṅkara, nirvedaṃ might be rendered as ‘despair’ or ‘disgust’; the commentary fixes it as vairāgya, dispassion.
- A commentary-blind translation might leave moha-kalilaṃ as a vague ‘mire/thicket of delusion’; Śaṅkara specifies it as the impurity/turbidity of non-discrimination that defiles self/not-self discernment.
- One might take śrotavyasya śrutasya ca as merely ‘scriptures heard and to be heard’; Śaṅkara’s paraphrase emphasizes their appearing niṣphala, fruitless, to the seeker at that stage.
