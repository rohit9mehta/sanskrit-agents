**J1. Translate saṃmoha as a moral-practical bewilderment, not merely as generic unconsciousness or mental confusion.**
- chosen: saṃmohaḥ is “bewilderment” specifically as aviveka, non-discrimination about kārya and akārya. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4, 5]: `krodhād bhavati saṃmoho 'vivekaḥ kāryākārya-viṣayaḥ |
kruddho hi saṃmūḍhaḥ san gurum apy ākrośati |` — From anger there arises saṃmoha—non-discrimination whose object is what should and should not be done. For an angry man, being bewildered, abuses even a teacher.

**J2. Render smṛti-vibhramaḥ as “lapse of memory,” not as mere agitation, and not as confusion about the Smṛti textual corpus.**
- chosen: smṛti-vibhramaḥ is a lapse/derangement of the memory generated by scriptural and teacherly instruction; vibhrama is glossed as bhraṃśa and as non-arising when the occasion for memory is present. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [6]: `saṃmohāt smṛti-vibhramaḥ śāstrācāryopadeśāhita-saṃskāra-janitāyāḥ smṛte syāt vibhramo bhraṃśaḥ smṛty-utpatti-nimitta-prāptau anutpattiḥ |` — From bewilderment comes smṛti-vibhrama: for the memory produced by saṃskāras deposited by scriptural and teacherly instruction, there is vibhrama, a bhraṃśa—when the cause for the arising of memory is present, it does not arise. The printed smṛte is understood as the genitive smṛteḥ in this construction.

**J3. Take buddhi-nāśa as loss of discriminative fitness, not literal annihilation of the intellect-substance.**
- chosen: smṛti-bhraṃśāt and buddhi-nāśaḥ are linked as loss of memory causing the destruction of buddhi; buddhi-nāśa is the incapacity of the inner faculty for kāryākārya-viveka. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [7, 8]: `tataḥ smṛti-bhraṃśāt buddhi-nāśaḥ buddhi-nāśaḥ |

## Translation

> From anger arises bewilderment (saṃmoha); from bewilderment, the lapse of memory (smṛti-vibhrama).
From the loss of memory comes the destruction of discernment (buddhi-nāśa); from the destruction of discernment, he is lost—unfit for the human end (puruṣārtha).
