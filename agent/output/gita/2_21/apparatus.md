## Justifications

**J1. The semantic value is not ‘has known’ in a temporally prior sense, but ‘knows/understands.’**
- chosen: veda is translated as present ‘knows,’ while retained morphologically as a perfect form of √vid with present value. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `veda vijānāti avināśinam antya-bhāva-vikāra-rahitaṃ nityaṃ vipariṇāma-rahitaṃ yo veda iti saṃbandhaḥ` — ‘veda’ means ‘understands’; the construction is ‘he who knows [it as] imperishable, free from the final state-change, eternal, free from transformation.’

**J2. Translate ‘this [Self]’ for enam, because Śaṅkara identifies it as the entity with the characteristics stated in the prior mantra/verse and later explicitly treats the subject as the avikriya ātmā.**
- chosen: enam refers to the Self characterized in the immediately preceding teaching, not to the body or to an ordinary person. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `enaṃ pūrveṇa matreṇokta-lakṣaṇam ajaṃ janma-rahitam avyayam apakṣaya-rahitaṃ` — ‘this one,’ having the characteristics stated by the previous mantra/verse, unborn—free from birth—and undecaying—free from diminution.
- gita [13]: `ataḥ pāriśeṣyāt asaṃhata ātmā vidvān avikriyaḥ iti tasya viduṣaḥ karmāsaṃbhavāt ākṣepo yuktaḥ kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ iti` — Therefore, by elimination, the uncompounded Self is the knower and is changeless; since action is impossible for that knower, the rhetorical objection ‘how could that person…?’ is fitting.

**J3. Śaṅkara glosses each epithet by specifying the absence of a particular transformation or loss.**
- chosen: avināśinam, nityam, ajam, and avyayam are technical negations of change: imperishable, eternal, unborn, undecaying. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `avināśinam antya-bhāva-vikāra-rahitaṃ nityaṃ vipariṇāma-rahitaṃ` — Imperishable: free from the final change of state; eternal: free from transformation.
- gita [2]: `ajaṃ janma-rahitam avyayam apakṣaya-rahitaṃ` — Unborn: free from birth; undecaying: free from diminution.

**J4. Render ‘how could…?’ with the force ‘in no way can…,’ because Śaṅkara says the question-sense is impossible and that ākṣepa is intended in both halves.**
- chosen: katham introduces a rhetorical denial, not an information-seeking question. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `na kathaṃcit kaṃcit hanti, na kathaṃcit kaṃcit ghātayati iti ubhayatra ākṣepaḥ evārthaḥ praśnārthāsaṃbhavāt` — He does not in any way kill anyone, nor in any way cause anyone to be killed; in both places the meaning is rhetorical denial, since a question-sense is impossible.

**J5. Śaṅkara distinguishes hanti as doing the act of killing from ghātayati as instigating a killer; he later names this as hetu-kartṛtva, causative agency.**
- chosen: ghātayati is causative: ‘causes to kill’ or ‘causes to be killed,’ not merely a second synonym of hanti. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `hanti hanana-kriyāṃ karoti, kathaṃ vā ghātayati hantāraṃ prayojayati` — ‘hanti’ means he performs the act of killing; or how does he ‘ghātayati,’ that is, instigate a killer?
- gita [40, 43]: `atra ca ātmanaḥ hanana-kriyāyāḥ kartṛtvaṃ karmatvaṃ hetu-kartṛtvaṃ ca ajñāna-kṛtaṃ darśitam | tad etat aviśeṣeṇa viduṣaḥ sarva-kriyāsu kartṛtvaṃ hetu-kartṛtvaṃ ca pratiṣedhati bhagavān vāsudevo viduṣaḥ karmādhikārābhāva-pradarśanārthaṃ vedāvināśinaṃ, kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ ity ādinā` — Here agency, objecthood, and causative agency regarding the Self in the act of killing are shown to be produced by ignorance. By ‘vedāvināśinam… kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ…’ Bhagavān Vāsudeva prohibits, without distinction, the knower’s agency and causative agency in all actions, to show the absence of eligibility for ritual/action.

**J6. The subject of the second half is not any man whatsoever but the person who knows the Self under these descriptions.**
- chosen: puruṣaḥ is the vidvān puruṣaḥ, ‘the knowing person,’ correlated with yaḥ. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `kathaṃ kena prakāreṇa sa vidvān puruṣo 'dhikṛtaḥ hanti hanana-kriyāṃ karoti, kathaṃ vā ghātayati hantāraṃ prayojayati` — How, in what manner, does that knowing person, the one under discussion, perform the act of killing; or how does he instigate a killer?

**J7. Although formally interrogative ‘whom?’, Śaṅkara’s paraphrase kaṃcit in both halves supports ‘anyone’ in English after the rhetorical negative force.**
- chosen: kaṃ is masculine ‘anyone’ in both occurrences under the denial. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `na kathaṃcit kaṃcit hanti, na kathaṃcit kaṃcit ghātayati` — He does not in any way kill anyone; he does not in any way cause anyone to be killed.

**J8. Do not translate the verse as licensing killing or as concerning only violence; in the bhāṣya, hanti is an example used to show the impossibility of agency and causative agency for the knower generally.**
- chosen: The immediate example is killing, but Śaṅkara takes the intended scope to be the knower’s non-agency in all actions. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4, 5]: `hetv-arthasya ca avikriyatvasya tulyatvāt viduṣaḥ sarva-karma-pratiṣedha eva prakaraṇārtho 'bhipreto bhagavatā | hantes tu ākṣepaḥ udāharaṇārthatvena kathitaḥ` — Because the reason, changelessness, is the same, the contextual meaning intended by Bhagavān is precisely the prohibition of all action for the knower; the rhetorical denial concerning ‘killing’ is stated as an example.
- gita [43]: `tad etat aviśeṣeṇa viduṣaḥ sarva-kriyāsu kartṛtvaṃ hetu-kartṛtvaṃ ca pratiṣedhati bhagavān vāsudevo viduṣaḥ karmādhikārābhāva-pradarśanārthaṃ vedāvināśinaṃ, kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ ity ādinā` — Thus Bhagavān Vāsudeva, by ‘vedāvināśinam… kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ…,’ prohibits without distinction the knower’s agency and causative agency in all actions, to show the knower’s lack of eligibility for action.

## Translation

> He who knows this [Self] as imperishable, eternal,
unborn, and undecaying—
how could that knowing person, O Pārtha,
cause anyone to be killed, or kill anyone?

## Analyzer disagreements

- For ghātayati, ByT5 gives the lemma as ghātay; following the instruction to give the bare verbal root and Śaṅkara’s gloss ghātayati hantāraṃ prayojayati (line 2), it is analyzed as the causative present of √han.
- For enam, ByT5 gives the lemma as enad; corrected to idam because enad as a regular stem would yield enadam, while enam is the accusative masculine singular enclitic form of idam.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might treat katham as a real question; Śaṅkara instead requires rhetorical denial: the knower cannot in any way kill or cause killing.
- It might translate ghātayati as merely ‘kills’ and miss the causative-agency distinction; Śaṅkara glosses it as hantāraṃ prayojayati, ‘instigates a killer.’
- It might not mark enam as the Self characterized previously, or might read the adjectives as loose epithets; Śaṅkara ties them to absence of birth, transformation, destruction, and diminution.
- It might confine the point to battlefield killing; Śaṅkara says killing is only an example and that the intended scope is the knower’s non-agency in all actions.
