## Justifications

**J1. atha cet ... na kariṣyasi is not merely a temporal sequence; tataḥ is read by Śaṅkara as causal/resultative, tied to tad-akaraṇāt.**
- chosen: A conditional protasis and apodosis: ‘if you will not ... then, because of that non-performance ...’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `atha cet tvam imaṃ dharmyaṃ dharmād anapetaṃ vihitaṃ saṃgrāmaṃ yuddhaṃ na kariṣyasi cet, tataḥ tad-akaraṇāt` — ‘Now/but if you will not do this dharmya battle, not departed from dharma, enjoined, namely war, then because of not doing that ...’

**J2. The adjective is not taken merely as ‘customary’ or vaguely ‘legal’; Śaṅkara explains it as both dharmād anapetam and vihitam.**
- chosen: dharmyaṃ means ‘righteous/enjoined, not apart from dharma’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `dharmyaṃ dharmād anapetaṃ vihitaṃ` — ‘dharmya: not departed from dharma, enjoined.’

**J3. The object saṃgrāma is not an assembly or troop here; the bhāṣya glosses it directly as yuddham.**
- chosen: saṃgrāmaṃ is ‘battle/war’, and kariṣyasi with it means ‘undertake/fight’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `saṃgrāmaṃ yuddhaṃ` — ‘saṃgrāma: war/battle.’

**J4. The compound relation is possessive; ‘sva’ qualifies dharma as belonging to the subject, not as a separate coordinated item.**
- chosen: svadharma is a tatpuruṣa compound: svasya dharmaḥ, ‘one’s own duty’, here ‘your own duty’. (depends on commentary: no)

**J5. The absolutive is not limited to kīrti alone; Śaṅkara repeats the pair as a single abandonment, svadharma-kīrti-parityāgaḥ.**
- chosen: hitvā governs both svadharmaṃ and kīrtiṃ: ‘abandoning your own duty and fame’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `svadharmaṃ kīrtiṃ ca mahā-devādi-samāgama-nimittāṃ hitvā` — ‘having abandoned own duty and fame, occasioned by encounters with Mahādeva and the like’
- gita [2]: `na kevalaṃ svadharma-kīrti-parityāgaḥ -` — ‘not merely the abandonment of own duty and fame ...’

**J6. Śaṅkara identifies the source or occasion of the fame; the translation therefore avoids reducing kīrti to generic ‘report’ or ‘mention’.**
- chosen: kīrti is ‘fame’, specifically the fame occasioned by encounters with Mahādeva and others. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `kīrtiṃ ca mahā-devādi-samāgama-nimittāṃ` — ‘and fame occasioned by meeting Mahādeva and the others.’

**J7. pāpam is a neuter substantive ‘sin’, not an adjective or ‘wicked man’; Śaṅkara adds kevalam, marking it as the sole result after the abandonment.**
- chosen: pāpam avāpsyasi means ‘you will incur only sin’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `hitvā kevalaṃ pāpam avāpsyasi` — ‘having abandoned [them], you will attain/incur only sin.’

## Translation

> But if you will not fight this battle—righteous, enjoined, not apart from dharma—then, abandoning your own duty (svadharma) and the fame occasioned by encounters with Mahādeva and the like, you will incur only sin (pāpa).

## One-shot delta

- Without the commentary, dharmyaṃ might be translated blandly as ‘customary’ or ‘legal’; Śaṅkara fixes it as ‘not apart from dharma’ and ‘enjoined’.
- A commentary-blind reading might take tataḥ as simply temporal ‘after that’; Śaṅkara’s tad-akaraṇāt makes the result arise from non-performance.
- The verse’s kīrtiṃ could be left as generic fame, but Śaṅkara specifies fame occasioned by encounters with Mahādeva and the like.
- The force of kevalam in the bhāṣya prevents softening pāpam avāpsyasi to ‘you will meet with misfortune’; the point is that only sin is incurred.
