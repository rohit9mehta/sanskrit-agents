## Justifications

**J1. I take āścaryavat in all three clauses as a comparison to a marvel, not as ‘wonderfully’ in a merely adverbial manner and not as a possessive ‘wonder-having’.**
- chosen: āścaryavat = ‘as/like a marvel’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `āścaryavat āścaryam adṛṣṭa-pūrvam adbhutam akasmād dṛśyamānaṃ tena tulyaṃ āścaryavat āścaryam iva enam ātmānaṃ paśyati kaścit |` — ‘Āścaryavat’: a wonder, something never seen before, astonishing, suddenly appearing; equal to that—āścaryavat, like a wonder—someone sees this Self.

**J2. The pronoun is not left as an unspecified ‘him/this’; it is translated as ‘this Self’ on first occurrence and then ‘it’.**
- chosen: enam/enaṃ refers to the Self (ātman). (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `āścaryavat āścaryam iva enam ātmānaṃ paśyati kaścit |` — Someone sees this, the Self, as like a wonder.
- gita [4]: `śrutvā dṛṣṭvā uktvāpi enam ātmānaṃ veda na caiva kaścit |` — Even after hearing, seeing, and speaking, no one knows this, the Self.

**J3. The three positive clauses distribute seeing, speaking, and hearing among different rare persons; tathā eva carries the same ‘as a marvel’ construal into the speaking clause.**
- chosen: ‘Another likewise speaks of it; another hears of it.’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `āścaryavad enaṃ vadati tathaiva cānyaḥ |` — Another, just so, speaks of it as a marvel.
- gita [3]: `āścaryavac ca enam anyaḥ sṛṇoti |` — And another hears it as a marvel. The line prints sṛṇoti, evidently for the verse’s śṛṇoti.

**J4. Although the first construal translates kaścit/anyaḥ straightforwardly, the English ‘some rare one’ reflects Śaṅkara’s stated purport, not a mere random ‘someone’.**
- chosen: The persons are rare, and the point is the Self’s difficulty of comprehension. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [5]: `athavā yo 'yam ātmānaṃ paśyati sa āścarya-tulyaḥ, yo vadati ya ca sṛṇoti so 'neka-sahasreṣu kaścid eva bhavati |` — Or: the one who sees this Self is wonder-like; the one who speaks and the one who hears is only some one among many thousands. The printed sṛṇoti is treated as a plaintext error for śṛṇoti.
- gita [6]: `ato durbodha ātmā ity abhiprāyaḥ ||bhgs_2.29||` — Therefore the purport is: the Self is hard to comprehend.

**J5. api is not taken as a simple additive ‘also’; the final pāda says that even after the relevant acts of hearing—and, in Śaṅkara’s expansion, seeing and speaking—knowledge still does not arise.**
- chosen: śrutvā api is concessive: ‘even after hearing’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `śrutvā dṛṣṭvā uktvāpi enam ātmānaṃ veda na caiva kaścit |` — Even after hearing, seeing, and speaking, no one knows this Self.

**J6. The final verb is translated as knowing/understanding the Self, and the negation is taken strongly; this matches Śaṅkara’s explicit explanation that the Self is durbodha, hard to comprehend.**
- chosen: veda na caiva kaścit = ‘not one at all knows it’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `enam ātmānaṃ veda na caiva kaścit |` — No one at all knows this Self.
- gita [6]: `ato durbodha ātmā ity abhiprāyaḥ ||bhgs_2.29||` — Thus the intended meaning is that the Self is difficult to understand.

**J7. Morphologically veda is a special liṭ/perfect form of √vid, but in this usage it has present value; hence the translation is not ‘has known’ as a past perfect.**
- chosen: veda is rendered with present sense, ‘knows’. (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> As a marvel some rare one beholds this Self (ātman);
as a marvel, just so, another speaks of it.
As a marvel, too, another hears of it;
even after hearing, not one at all knows it.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render āścaryavat as ‘wonderfully’ or ‘with amazement’; Śaṅkara explicitly glosses it as āścaryam iva, ‘like/as a marvel’ (line 1).
- It might leave enam as a vague ‘him’ or take it as the body; Śaṅkara identifies it twice as ātmānam, the Self (lines 1 and 4).
- It might miss the verse’s stress on rarity and difficulty; Śaṅkara says such a person is only ‘one among many thousands’ and concludes ato durbodha ātmā (lines 5–6).
- It might take api as merely ‘also’; Śaṅkara’s śrutvā dṛṣṭvā uktvāpi ... veda na shows the concessive force, ‘even after hearing’ (line 4).
