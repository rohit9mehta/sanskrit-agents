## Justifications

**J1. The repeated demonstrative ayam is translated as “this Self,” because Śaṅkara explicitly supplies ātmā with the predicates.**
- chosen: ayam throughout refers to the Self (ātmā), not to the body or to an unspecified person. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `avyaktaḥ sarva-karaṇāviṣayatvāt na vyajyata iti avyakto 'yam ātmā |` — Because it is not an object of any instrument, it is not manifested; therefore this Self is avyakta.
- gita [4]: `ayaṃ tv ātmā anindriya-gocaratvāt acintyaḥ |` — But this Self, because it is not within the range of the senses, is acintya.
- gita [8]: `avikriyatvāt avikāryo 'yam ātmā ucyate |` — Because of being without modification, this Self is called avikārya.

**J2. The word is not taken as the Sāṃkhya technical noun ‘the Unmanifest’ or as merely ‘undeveloped’; Śaṅkara derives the sense from na vyajyate and sarva-karaṇāviṣayatva.**
- chosen: avyakta is “unmanifest/imperceptible,” in the sense of not being objectified by any instrument of cognition. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `avyaktaḥ sarva-karaṇāviṣayatvāt na vyajyata iti avyakto 'yam ātmā |` — Avyakta: because it is not an object of any instrument; it is not manifested, hence this Self is avyakta.

**J3. The translation avoids “not to be thought about” as a prescription; Śaṅkara presents an epistemic reason for its being beyond thought.**
- chosen: acintya is “inconceivable/beyond thought,” because thought has sense-objects as its field and the Self is not a sense-object. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2, 3, 4]: `ataeva acintyo 'yam | yad dhi indriya-gocaras tac cintā-viṣayatvam āpadyate | ayaṃ tv ātmā anindriya-gocaratvāt acintyaḥ |` — Therefore this is acintya. Whatever is within the range of the senses becomes an object of thought; but this Self, because it is not within the range of the senses, is acintya.

**J4. The commentary ties avikārya to absence of vikāra, contrasting the Self with milk transformed into curd and then explaining it by niravayavatva and avikriyatva.**
- chosen: avikārya is “unmodifiable/unchangeable,” specifically because the Self is avikriya, not subject to transformation and without parts. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [5]: `ataeva avikāryaḥ, yathā kṣīraṃ dadhyātañcanādinā vikāri na tathā ayam ātmā |` — Therefore it is avikārya: as milk is transformed by curdling agents, this Self is not so.
- gita [6]: `niravayavatvāc cāvikriyaḥ |` — And because it is without parts, it is without modification.
- gita [8]: `avikriyatvāt avikāryo 'yam ātmā ucyate |` — Because of being without modification, this Self is called avikārya.

**J5. The commentary fixes both the scope of evam and the referent of enam, and it construes the result as the impropriety of grieving over agency and slaughter.**
- chosen: tasmāt evaṃ viditvā enam means “therefore, having known this Self in this way,” with evam = yathokta-prakāreṇa and enam = ātmānam. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [9]: `tasmāt evaṃ yathokta-prakāreṇa enam ātmānaṃ viditvā tvaṃ na anuśocitum arhasi hantāham eṣām, mayaite hanyanta iti ||bhgs_2.25||` — Therefore, having known this Self in this way, in the manner stated, you ought not to grieve, thinking ‘I am their slayer; by me these are slain.’

**J6. With an infinitive, arhasi has the modal sense of fitness/obligation; Śaṅkara repeats the construction directly with tvaṃ and explains the false basis of grief.**
- chosen: na anuśocitum arhasi is rendered “you ought not to grieve,” not merely “you cannot grieve.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [9]: `tvaṃ na anuśocitum arhasi hantāham eṣām, mayaite hanyanta iti` — You ought not to grieve, thinking ‘I am their slayer; by me these are slain.’

## Translation

> This Self is unmanifest (avyakta); this Self is inconceivable (acintya); this Self is said to be unmodifiable (avikārya). Therefore, having known this Self in this way, you ought not to grieve.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind rendering might take avyakta as a generic metaphysical ‘Unmanifest’ or prakṛti; Śaṅkara instead makes it the Self’s non-objectifiability by all instruments of cognition.
- It might translate acintya simply as “unthinkable” without the reason that thought depends on sense-range, from which the Self is excluded.
- It might render avikārya as only “invariable” or as a passive gerundive “not to be changed”; Śaṅkara glosses it through avikriyatva, niravayavatva, and the contrast with milk becoming curd.
- It might leave enam as a vague “him/this”; Śaṅkara identifies it as ātmānam and ties the prohibition of grief to the mistaken thought “I am the slayer; these are slain by me.”
