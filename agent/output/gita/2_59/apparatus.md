## Justifications

**J1. Although the surface noun is viṣaya, Śaṅkara explicitly glosses the verse’s viṣayāḥ as indriyāṇi, ‘senses,’ qualified by viṣayopalakṣitāni and viṣaya-śabda-vācyāni. The translation therefore says ‘the senses—called by their objects.’**
- chosen: viṣayāḥ means the senses indicated or denoted by their objects, not simply external objects. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `viṣayāḥ viṣayopalakṣitāni viṣaya-śabda-vācyāni indriyāṇi` — ‘viṣayāḥ’: the senses, indicated by objects and denoted by the word ‘object’.

**J2. Śaṅkara does not leave nirāhāra at the literal level of food-fasting; he glosses it as anāhriyamāṇa-viṣayasya, ‘whose objects are not being brought in,’ and frames it as austerity that may occur even in a fool.**
- chosen: nirāhārasya dehinaḥ is the embodied one whose sense-objects are not being brought in, especially one standing in harsh austerity. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `nirāhārasya anāhriyamāṇa-viṣayasya kaṣṭe tapasi sthitasya mūrkhasyāpi` — ‘of the nirāhāra’: of one whose objects are not being brought in, even of a fool standing in harsh austerity.

**J3. The compound/indeclinable is governed by varjam, which Śaṅkara paraphrases with varjayitvā; the thing excepted is rasa understood as rāga toward objects.**
- chosen: rasa-varjam means ‘excepting the rasa,’ where rasa is attachment/relish toward objects. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `rasa-varjaṃ raso rāgo viṣayeṣu yas taṃ varjayitvā` — ‘rasa-varjam’: leaving aside that rasa which is attachment toward objects.
- gita [2]: `rasa-śabdo rāge prasiddhaḥ` — The word rasa is well known in the sense of attachment.

**J4. The second rasaḥ is not a different literal ‘taste’; Śaṅkara continues the same sense, calling it rañjana-rūpa and sūkṣma, a subtle coloring/attachment belonging to this yati.**
- chosen: raso 'py asya is ‘even his subtle attachment/relish.’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `so 'pi raso rañjana-rūpaḥ sūkṣmo 'sya yateḥ` — Even that rasa, subtle and of the nature of coloring/attachment, of this yati.

**J5. Śaṅkara identifies param with paramārtha-tattva, brahman, and glosses dṛṣṭvā with upalabhya, adding the realization ‘aham eva tat’; the translation therefore uses ‘having seen/realized the supreme (Brahman).’**
- chosen: paraṃ dṛṣṭvā means having realized the supreme reality, Brahman, as ‘I am that.’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `paraṃ paramārtha-tattvaṃ brahma dṛṣṭvā upalabhya aham eva tat iti` — Having seen—realized—the supreme, the ultimate reality, Brahman, as ‘I myself am that.’

**J6. Śaṅkara glosses the cessation as nirbījaṃ viṣaya-vijñānaṃ saṃpadyate, and explicitly denies the destruction of rasa in the absence of samyag-darśana.**
- chosen: nivartate means that attachment ceases in the strong sense of becoming seedless; it is not destroyed without right vision. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `nivartate nirbījaṃ viṣaya-vijñānaṃ saṃpadyate ity arthaḥ` — ‘It ceases’: object-cognition becomes seedless—this is the meaning.
- gita [4]: `nāsati samyag-darśane rasasya ucchedaḥ` — When right vision is absent, there is no cutting off of rasa.

## Translation

> The senses—called by the names of their objects—withdraw
from the embodied one whose objects are not taken in,
excepting the taste, the attachment.
But even his subtle attachment ceases
when he has seen the supreme, Brahman.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation would likely render viṣayāḥ as external ‘objects’; Śaṅkara instead takes them here as the senses indicated by their objects.
- It might take nirāhārasya as simple food-fasting; Śaṅkara’s gloss makes it the withholding of sense-objects, even in severe austerity.
- It might translate rasa as literal ‘taste’ or ‘juice’; Śaṅkara explicitly defines it as rāga, subtle attachment/relish.
- It might miss that paraṃ dṛṣṭvā is realization of Brahman and that attachment is not uprooted without samyag-darśana.
