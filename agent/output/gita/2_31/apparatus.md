## Justifications

**J1. Translate svadharmam not as an abstract ‘personal religion’ but as Arjuna’s own class-duty: for a kṣatriya, battle. The accusative is the object of avekṣya, ‘having regard to/considering.’**
- chosen: svadharma = the kṣatriya’s own duty, namely battle (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `svadharmam api svo dharmaḥ kṣatriyasya yuddhaṃ tam api avekṣya` — ‘svadharmam api’: one’s own dharma; for a kṣatriya it is battle—considering even that.

**J2. The finite arhasi with the infinitive has normative force; the negation is not a simple prediction but ‘you ought not / it is not proper for you.’ Vikampitum is taken as ‘to waver, be shaken,’ glossed by pracalitum.**
- chosen: na vikampitum arhasi = you ought not to waver (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `tvaṃ na vikampituṃ pracalitum nārhasi` — You ought not to waver, to be unsettled.

**J3. The reason svadharma bears on Arjuna is that it is the kṣatriya’s natural/innate dharma. This supports ‘your own duty’ rather than merely a chosen or private obligation.**
- chosen: the duty is rooted in kṣatriya nature (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `kṣatriyasya svābhāvikād dharmād ātma-svābhāvyād ity abhiprāyaḥ` — The purport is: because of the kṣatriya’s natural dharma, because of his own nature.

**J4. Dharmya qualifies yuddha and is not a mere label for warfare generally: this battle is ‘not departed from dharma’ because it serves dharma and the protection of subjects through conquest of the earth.**
- chosen: dharmya yuddha = righteous battle, not any battle as such (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `tac ca yuddhaṃ pṛthivī-jaya-dvāreṇa dharmārthaṃ prajā-rakṣaṇārthaṃ ceti dharmād anapetaṃ paraṃ dharmyam` — And that battle, through the conquest of the earth, is for the sake of dharma and for the protection of subjects; thus it has not fallen away from dharma and is supremely righteous.

**J5. The second half means ‘no other better good exists for a kṣatriya than righteous battle.’ The paired ablatives dharmyāt yuddhāt supply the comparison governed by śreyaḥ/anyat, and kṣatriyasya is the genitive of the person for whom this is so.**
- chosen: dharmyād yuddhāt is the ablative standard of comparison for śreyaḥ anyat (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `tasmād dharmyād yuddhāc chreyo 'nyat kṣatriyasya na vidyate hi yasmāt` — For, than that righteous battle, no other better thing is found for a kṣatriya.

**J6. The particle hi introduces the reason for the preceding injunction not to waver.**
- chosen: hi = for/because (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `hi yasmāt` — hi: because/for.

## Translation

> And even considering your own duty (svadharma), you ought not to waver;
for, for a kṣatriya, no other better good is found than a righteous battle.

## Analyzer disagreements

- ByT5 segmented sva-dharmam as sva + dharmam; I analyze the printed hyphenated expression as the compound svadharmam, following Śaṅkara’s compound gloss ‘svo dharmaḥ ... tam’ (line 1).
- ByT5 gives prefixed lemmas avekṣ and vikamp; the required verbal format uses bare roots with prefixes, so these are analyzed as īkṣ with prefix ava and kamp with prefix vi.
- In sandhi parsing, cāvekṣya is represented as ca + avekṣya; hence the individual surface_in_sandhi values are cā and vekṣya, preserving the printed sandhi while giving pausal ca and avekṣya.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render svadharma as vague ‘personal duty’; Śaṅkara identifies it as the kṣatriya’s battle-duty grounded in his nature.
- It might make dharmya-yuddha mean ‘religious war’ or any war; Śaṅkara restricts it to a battle not apart from dharma, aimed at dharma and protection of subjects.
- It might translate na vikampitum arhasi as ‘you cannot tremble’; the bhāṣya gives the normative sense ‘you ought not to waver.’
- It might miss that hi is causal and that dharmyāt yuddhāt is the ablative standard for ‘no other better good.’
