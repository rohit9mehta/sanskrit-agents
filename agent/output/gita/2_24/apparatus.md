## Justifications

**J1. The repeated masculine nominative predicates and pronouns are taken as predications of the ātmā, not of a body or another unspecified object. Śaṅkara explicitly supplies ātmā as the referent.**
- chosen: ayam / this = the Self, ātmā (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `enam ātmānaṃ` — this ātman
- gita [4]: `acalo 'yam ātmā` — this ātman is immovable

**J2. The forms are read passively as impossibility predicates, not as active qualities. Śaṅkara frames the point as the incapacity of destructive agents to destroy the ātmā.**
- chosen: The negative gerundives mean that the Self cannot be cut, burned, moistened, or dried. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `enam ātmānaṃ nāśayituṃ notsahante` — they are not able to destroy this ātman

**J3. The sequence is not read as literal travel everywhere. Śaṅkara makes all-pervasion follow from eternality: because it is eternal, it is sarvagata.**
- chosen: nityaḥ = eternal; sarva-gataḥ = all-pervading (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `tasmāt nityaḥ |` — therefore it is eternal
- gita [2]: `nityatvāt sarvagataḥ |` — because it is eternal, it is all-pervading

**J4. Although sthāṇu can denote a stump, post, or pillar, Śaṅkara glosses the intended sense as sthira, “stable/firm.” The translation therefore avoids a literal noun such as “post.”**
- chosen: sthāṇuḥ = stable/firm (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `sarva-gatatvāt sthāṇur iva, sthira ity etat |` — because it is all-pervading, like a sthāṇu; that is, stable

**J5. The word is taken as a predicate of the ātmā following from stability, not as the noun “mountain.”**
- chosen: acalaḥ = immovable/unmoving (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `sthiratvāt acalo 'yam ātmā |` — because of stability, this ātman is immovable

**J6. Śaṅkara glosses sanātana as cirantana and denies origination from any cause. Hence the translation uses “primordial” rather than merely “old.” The supplied line’s final abhinava ity arthaḥ is understood under the preceding negation, not as an assertion that the Self is “new.”**
- chosen: sanātanaḥ = primordial/beginningless, not newly produced (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [5]: `ataḥ sanātanaś cirantanaḥ, na kāraṇāt kutaścin niṣpannaḥ` — therefore sanātana means ancient/enduring, not produced from any cause whatsoever

**J7. The verse’s apparent repetition is not flattened away as careless redundancy. Śaṅkara says some repetition is verbal and some semantic, because the ātman is difficult to understand and is being taught again in different words.**
- chosen: The repeated predicates are intentional restatement of the ātman’s eternality and changelessness. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [6]: `ātmanaḥ nityatvam avikriyatvaṃ coktam` — the eternality and changelessness of the ātman have been stated
- gita [8]: `kiṃcic chabdataḥ punaruktam, kiṃcid arthataḥ iti |` — some is repeated verbally, some in meaning
- gita [9]: `durbodhatvāt ātma-vastunaḥ punaḥ punaḥ prasaṅgam āpādya śabdāntareṇa tad eva vastu nirūpayati bhagavān vāsudevaḥ` — because the reality that is the ātman is hard to understand, Bhagavān Vāsudeva repeatedly brings that very reality into discussion and expounds it in other words

**J8. The commentary is silent on a special restrictive scope for eva. On grammar and MW s.v. eva, 232,2, I take it as an emphatic particle strengthening aśoṣya, with ca as the connective “and.”**
- chosen: eva ca = “and indeed” (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> This Self (ātman) cannot be cut; this Self cannot be burned; it cannot be moistened, and indeed cannot be dried. Eternal (nitya), all-pervading (sarvagata), stable (sthāṇu), immovable (acala)—this Self is primordial (sanātana).

- It might take sarva-gataḥ as motion-language, “gone everywhere”; Śaṅkara’s nityatvāt sarvagataḥ points to all-pervasion of the eternal Self.
