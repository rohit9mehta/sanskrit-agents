## Justifications

**J1. bhogaiśvarya-prasaktānām describes people whose affection is fixed on the pair bhoga and aiśvarya, not merely ‘enjoyment of power’ or possession of wealth.**
- chosen: ‘attached to enjoyment and lordly power,’ taking bhoga and aiśvarya as two objects of attachment (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `bhogaiśvarya-prasaktānāṃ bhogaḥ kartavyaḥ caiśvaryaṃ ca iti bhogaiśvaryayor eva praṇayavatāṃ tad-ātma-bhūtānām |` — “For bhogaiśvarya-prasaktānām: thinking ‘enjoyment is to be pursued, and lordly power too’; those who have affection only for enjoyment and lordly power, who have become of that nature.”

**J2. The instrumental tayā refers to the preceding vāc, and apahṛta-cetasām is explained as a covering of viveka-prajñā, discriminating wisdom.**
- chosen: tayā = ‘by that speech rich in special ritual acts’; apahṛta-cetasām = ‘whose minds are carried away/whose discriminating cognition is covered’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `tayā kriyā-viśeṣa-bahulayā vācā apahṛta-cetasām ācchādita-viveka-prajñānāṃ` — “By that speech, abundant in particular rites; of those whose minds are carried off, that is, whose discriminating cognition is covered.”

**J3. Śaṅkara construes vyavasāyātmikā buddhiḥ as the determinate intellect relevant to Sāṅkhya or Yoga, not as any ordinary worldly resolve.**
- chosen: ‘the resolute/determinate intellect, whether in Sāṅkhya or in Yoga’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `vyavasāyātmikā sāṃkhye yoge vā buddhiḥ` — “The vyavasāyātmikā intellect, in Sāṅkhya or in Yoga.”

**J4. The locative samādhau is not translated as ‘in meditative trance/concentration’; Śaṅkara defines samādhi etymologically as the inner instrument in which everything is assembled for the puruṣa’s experience.**
- chosen: samādhi here = the inner organ/intellect (antaḥkaraṇa, buddhi) (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `samādhau samādhīyate asmin puruṣopabhogāya sarvam iti samādhiḥ antaḥkaraṇaṃ buddhiḥ tasmin samādhau` — “In samādhi: because everything is placed together in it for the enjoyment of the puruṣa, it is called samādhi—the inner organ, the intellect; in that samādhi.”

**J5. Although vidhīyate can mean ‘is prescribed/enjoined,’ Śaṅkara rejects that as the operative sense here by glossing it with na bhavati.**
- chosen: na vidhīyate = ‘does not arise / does not come to be’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `na vidhīyate na bhavati ity arthaḥ` — “na vidhīyate means: it does not come to be.”

**J6. The compound relation of vyavasāyātmikā is analysed grammatically as a bahuvrīhi-like -ātmikā compound; the supplied commentary here gives scope but not a separate vigraha for the compound.**
- chosen: vyavasāyātmikā as ‘having determination as its nature,’ hence ‘resolute/determinate’ (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> For those attached to enjoyment and lordly power,
whose minds are carried away by that speech rich in special rites,
the resolute intellect (vyavasāyātmikā buddhiḥ), whether in Sāṅkhya or in Yoga,
does not arise in the samādhi—the inner organ (antaḥkaraṇa).
