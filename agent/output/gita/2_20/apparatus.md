## Justifications

**J1. The verse’s masculine pronoun is not taken as the body or the empirical person; Śaṅkara repeatedly identifies the subject as ayam ātmā and says all worldly transformations are denied in the ātmā.**
- chosen: ayam refers to the ātmā, so the translation says ‘this Self (ātman)’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [6]: `yasmāt ayam ātmā bhūtvā bhavana-kriyām anubhūya paścāt abhavitā abhāvaṃ gantā na bhūyaḥ punaḥ, tasmāt na mriyate |` — Because this is ‘this ātman’, Śaṅkara identifies the subject of the clause as the Self.
- gita [26, 27]: `asmin mantre ṣaḍ-bhāva-vikārā laukika-vastu-vikriyā ātmani pratiṣidhyante |
sarva-prakāra-vikriyā-rahita ātmā iti vākyārthaḥ |` — In this mantra the six modes of worldly change are denied in the ātman; the sentence-meaning is that the ātman is free from every kind of transformation.

**J2. The first vā is rendered conjunctively, not as a genuine alternative ‘or’; kadācit is construed with the denials of birth and death, indeed with the denials of all transformations.**
- chosen: ‘He is not born, and he does not die, ever.’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1, 2, 3, 4]: `na jāyate notpadyate, jani-lakṣaṇā vastu-vikriyā na ātmano vidyate ity arthaḥ |
tathā na mriyate vā |
vā-śabdaḥ cārthe |
na mriyate ca iti antyā vināśa-lakṣaṇā vikriyā pratiṣidhyate |` — ‘He is not born’ means no birth-type modification exists in the Self; likewise he does not die. The word vā has the sense of ca, ‘and’, and the final destruction-type modification is denied.
- gita [5]: `kadācic chabdaḥ sarva-vikriyā-pratiṣedhaiḥ saṃbadhyate -- na kadācit jāyate, na kadācit mriyate, ity evam |` — The word kadācit is connected with all the denials of transformation: never is he born, never does he die, and so on.

**J3. The compact second pāda is expanded following Śaṅkara into a twofold denial: the Self is not something that, after being, goes to non-being; nor is it like a body, something that, after non-being, comes into being. Bhūyaḥ is taken as ‘again’ (punaḥ).**
- chosen: ‘Nor, after having been, will this one fail to be again; nor, after not having been, will he come to be again.’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [6]: `yasmāt ayam ātmā bhūtvā bhavana-kriyām anubhūya paścāt abhavitā abhāvaṃ gantā na bhūyaḥ punaḥ, tasmāt na mriyate |` — Śaṅkara explains the death-denial: the Self is not one that, after having been, later goes to non-existence; bhūyaḥ is glossed as punaḥ, ‘again’.
- gita [8, 9, 10]: `vā-śabdāt na-śabdāc ca ayam ātmā abhūtvā vā bhavitā dehavat na bhūyaḥ |
tasmāt na jāyate |
yo hi abhūtvā bhavitā sa jāyata ity ucyate |` — From vā and na Śaṅkara also supplies the birth-denial: the Self is not, like a body, one that after not having been will come to be again; one that does so is said to be born.

**J4. The adjectives are not mere epithets. Śaṅkara correlates them with the denial of specific bhāva-vikāras: birth, death, decline, and growth. Purāṇa therefore cannot mean ‘old/worn out’ in the ordinary sense; it is ‘old’ while remaining ever new, because the partless Self does not increase.**
- chosen: ajaḥ, nityaḥ, śāśvataḥ, purāṇaḥ are rendered as ‘unborn, eternal, undecaying, ancient-yet-ever-new’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [13]: `yasmād evaṃ tasmāt ajaḥ, yasmāt na mriyate tasmāt nityaś ca |` — Because the Self is not born, it is ajaḥ; because it does not die, it is nityaḥ.
- gita [14, 15, 16, 17, 18]: `yadyapi ādy-antayor vikriyayoḥ pratiṣedhe sarvā vikriyāḥ pratiṣiddhā bhavanti, tathāpi madhya-bhāvinīnāṃ vikriyāṇāṃ sva-śabdair eva pratiṣedhaḥ kartavyo 'nuktānām api yauvanādi-samasta-vikriyāṇāṃ pratiṣedho yathā syāt ity āha-- śāśvata ity ādinā |
śāśvata iti apakṣaya-lakṣaṇā vikriyā pratiṣidhyate |
śaśvad-bhavaḥ śāśvataḥ |
na apakṣīyate svarūpeṇa, niravayavatvāt |
nāpi guṇa-kṣayeṇa apakṣayaḥ, nirguṇatvāt |` — Although denying beginning and end already denies all transformations, the middle transformations are denied by their own words. Śāśvata denies the transformation called decline; the Self does not decline in its own nature or through loss of qualities.
- gita [19, 20, 21]: `apakṣaya-viparītāpi vṛddhi-lakṣaṇā vikriyā pratiṣidhyate --- purāṇa iti |
yo hi avayavāgamena upacīyate sa vardhate abhinava iti ca ucyate |
ayaṃ tu ātmā niravayavatvāt purāpi nava eveti purāṇaḥ na vardhate ity arthaḥ |` — Purāṇa denies the opposite of decline, namely growth. What grows by the addition of parts is called new; but this partless Self is even formerly new—purāṇa—and so does not grow.

**J5. Although the surface verb is from √han, Śaṅkara explicitly says that here √han must be understood in the sense of vipariṇāma in order to avoid repetition after mriyate. The locative absolute therefore means ‘when the body is being transformed’.**
- chosen: hanyate and hanyamāne are translated by ‘is transformed / being transformed’, not by literal ‘is slain / being slain’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [22, 23, 24]: `tathā na hanyate |
hantiḥ atra vipariṇāmārthe draṣṭavyo 'punaruktatāyai |
na vipariṇamyate ity arthaḥ |` — Similarly, ‘he is not hanyate’; here han is to be seen as having the meaning of transformation, to avoid repetition. The meaning is: he is not transformed.

## Translation

> He is not born; he does not die, ever.
Nor, after having been, will this one fail to be again;
nor, after not having been, will he come to be again.
Unborn, eternal, undecaying, ancient-yet-ever-new is this Self (ātman);
he is not transformed (vipariṇāma) when the body is being transformed.

## Analyzer disagreements

- mriyate: ByT5 marks Pv; I analyze √mṛ mriyate as an intransitive middle present in Kartari prayoga, ‘dies’, not a passive ‘is died’.
- bhavitā: ByT5’s SFu tag is treated as too broad/misleading for this form; I specify luṭ/periphrastic future 3rd singular, supported by Śaṅkara’s gantā gloss at line 6.
- kadācit: ByT5 segments kadācid; the printed sandhi is kadācin before nāyaṃ, while the pausal/pre-sandhi form required here is kadācit.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation would likely render vā as ‘or’; Śaṅkara says the first vā has the force of ca, ‘and’.
- It might attach kadācit only to ‘dies’; Śaṅkara attaches it to all the denials of transformation.
- It might flatten nāyaṃ bhūtvā bhavitā vā na bhūyaḥ into only one existential claim; Śaṅkara extracts a twofold denial, covering both death as becoming non-existent after being and birth as coming into being after non-being.
- It might translate purāṇa simply as ‘old’; Śaṅkara requires the paradoxical sense ‘ancient yet ever new’, because the partless Self does not grow.
- It would almost certainly translate hanyate/hanyamāne as ‘is slain/when the body is slain’; Śaṅkara explicitly takes √han here as vipariṇāma, ‘transformation’, to avoid repeating the earlier denial of death.
