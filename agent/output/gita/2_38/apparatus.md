## Justifications

**J1. same is not merely ‘same’ in a loose lexical sense: Śaṅkara glosses it as tulye, and explains the point of this equalizing as not producing rāga and dveṣa toward pleasure and pain.**
- chosen: “Having made pleasure and pain alike—without forming attachment and aversion.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `sukha-duḥkhe same tulye kṛtvā, rāga-dveṣāv apy akṛtvety etat |` — Having made pleasure and pain the same, equal; that is, also not having made attachment and aversion.

**J2. The verse has same only with the neuter dual sukha-duḥkhe, but Śaṅkara explicitly supplies samau kṛtvā for the two masculine dual pairs lābhālābhau and jayājayau. The translation therefore repeats ‘alike/equal’ for both pairs.**
- chosen: “and likewise gain and loss, victory and defeat alike.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `tathā lābhālābhau jayājayau ca samau kṛtvā tato yuddhāya yujyasva ghaṭāsva |` — Likewise, having made gain-and-loss and victory-and-defeat equal, then apply yourself for battle; strive.

**J3. yuddhāya is construed as the dative goal/purpose of yujyasva, and yujyasva is translated idiomatically from Śaṅkara’s ghaṭāsva, ‘strive/apply yourself,’ rather than as a bare literal ‘be yoked’.**

**J4. Śaṅkara expands evam as modifying the manner of fighting—evaṃ yuddhaṃ kurvan—not as an adjective to pāpam (‘such sin’). pāpam is the accusative object of avāpsyasi.**

## Translation

> Making pleasure and pain alike—without forming attachment and aversion (rāga-dveṣa)—
and likewise gain and loss, victory and defeat alike,
then apply yourself to battle;
fighting thus, you will not incur sin (pāpa).
