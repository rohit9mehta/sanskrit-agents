## Justifications

**J1. The compound qualifies the accusative puruṣam/dhīram; it is not a neuter object meaning “equal pain-and-pleasure” as a thing.**
- chosen: sama-duḥkha-sukhaṃ is a bahuvrīhi: “one for whom pain and pleasure are the same,” specifically free from elation and dejection at their occurrence. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `same duḥkha-sukhe yasya taṃ sama-duḥkha-sukhaṃ sukha-duḥkha-prāptau harṣa-viṣāda-rahitaṃ` — “sama-duḥkha-sukha” means him for whom pain and pleasure are the same, free from joy and despondency when pleasure and pain are obtained.

**J2. Śaṅkara explicitly glosses dhīra as dhīmān, so the translation foregrounds wisdom/intelligence rather than courage alone.**
- chosen: dhīraṃ is rendered “wise,” with steadiness implied by understanding, not merely “brave.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `dhīraṃ dhīmantaṃ` — “dhīra” means “possessed of understanding,” wise.

**J3. The subject is not “these people” or an unspecified set; Śaṅkara identifies it as yathoktāḥ śītoṣṇādayaḥ and glosses vyathayanti by cālayanti.**
- chosen: ete refers to the previously mentioned cold-and-heat and the like; vyathayanti means “disturb/shake.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `na vyathayanti na cālayanti nityātma-darśanāt ete yathoktāḥ śītoṣṇādayaḥ` — They do not disturb, do not shake, because of the vision of the eternal self; these are the aforementioned cold-and-heat and so on.

**J4. kalpate is not taken as “imagines” or simply “becomes”; Śaṅkara glosses it as samartho bhavati. amṛtatva is not mere long life or divine nectar but mokṣa.**
- chosen: amṛtatvāya kalpate means “is fit/capable for immortality,” where immortality is liberation (mokṣa). (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `amṛtatvāya amṛta-bhāvāya mokṣāyety arthaḥ kalpate samartho bhavati` — “For immortality” means for the state of being immortal, that is, for liberation; “kalpate” means becomes capable/fit.

**J5. The commentary is silent on this address; grammar and dictionary decide it. MW s.v. ṛṣabha, 226,3 gives “a bull” and “the best or most excellent of any kind or race,” explicitly comparing forms such as puruṣarṣabha.**
- chosen: puruṣarṣabha is a vocative tatpuruṣa, “O bull/best among men.” (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> For the wise person, O best among men,
whom these [cold-and-heat and the like] do not shake,
equal in pain and pleasure—
he is fit for immortality (amṛtatva), that is, liberation.

## Analyzer disagreements

- sama-duḥkha-sukham: ByT5 presents the members separately with final sukham; the analysis here records the whole inflected compound stem sama-duḥkha-sukha, masculine accusative singular, because it qualifies puruṣam/dhīram and Śaṅkara supplies the bahuvrīhi vigraha same duḥkha-sukhe yasya.
- amṛtatvāya: ByT5 segments amṛta + tvāya; the apparatus treats the form as the single derived abstract noun amṛtatva, neuter dative singular, supported by Śaṅkara’s amṛta-bhāvāya mokṣāya.
- puruṣarṣabha: ByT5’s puruṣa + ṛṣabha segmentation is retained as compound membership, but the inflected word is recorded under the internally sandhied compound stem puruṣarṣabha.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might leave sama-duḥkha-sukha as a vague “having equal pain and pleasure”; Śaṅkara makes it an inner freedom from joy and despondency when pleasure and pain arise.
- It might render amṛtatva as literal immortality only; Śaṅkara identifies it as mokṣa, liberation.
- It might take dhīra primarily as “brave” or “stoic”; Śaṅkara glosses it as dhīmān, wise.
- It might leave ete unspecified or read it as people/enemies; Śaṅkara says it is the previously mentioned cold-and-heat and the like.
- It might translate kalpate as “becomes” or “is made”; Śaṅkara’s samartho bhavati supports “is fit/capable.”
