## Justifications

**J1. tāni sarvāṇi is taken as the accusative object of the absolutive saṃyamya, and saṃyamya is not merely “holding together” but “bringing under control.”**
- chosen: “having brought all those [senses] under control” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `tāni sarvāṇi saṃyamya saṃyamanaṃ vaśīkaraṇaṃ kṛtvā` — “Having restrained all those: having done restraining, that is, bringing under control.”

**J2. yuktaḥ is translated according to Śaṅkara’s gloss samāhitaḥ, and āsīta is an optative injunction, not a simple past or present statement.**
- chosen: “let him remain/sit collected” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `yuktaḥ samāhitaḥ san āsīta` — “Being yukta, that is, collected/concentrated, he should sit/remain.”

**J3. mat-paraḥ is read as a bahuvrīhi: one for whom Kṛṣṇa/Vāsudeva, the inner Self of all, is para. Śaṅkara also makes the required standpoint non-difference from that Vāsudeva.**
- chosen: “with Me as the Highest (mat-paraḥ), [knowing] ‘I am not other than Vāsudeva, the inner Self of all’” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `mat-paro 'haṃ vāsudevaḥ sarva-pratyag-ātmā paro yasya sa mat-paraḥ, nānyo 'haṃ tasmāt ity āsīta ity arthaḥ` — “mat-paraḥ: he for whom I—Vāsudeva, the inner Self of all—am the Highest; the meaning is that he should abide [thinking], ‘I am not other than that.’”

**J4. vaśe is construed as a locative state of control with indriyāṇi as the nominative subject of an implied vartante. Śaṅkara identifies the person as the yati who abides thus and adds abhyāsa-balāt as the means by which the senses remain controlled.**
- chosen: “whose senses remain under control, by the strength of practice” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `evam āsīnasya yater vaśe hi yasyendriyāṇi vartante abhyāsa-balāt` — “For the ascetic who abides thus, whose senses remain in control by the strength of practice.”

**J5. yasya and tasya form a relative-correlative pair referring to the supplied yati; prajñā is the feminine nominative subject and pratiṣṭhitā its predicate adjective.**

**J6. hi is taken causally, making the second hemistich give the reason for the injunction in the first.**

## Translation

> Having brought all those [senses] under control,
let him remain collected (yuktaḥ), with Me as the Highest (mat-paraḥ)—
[knowing,] “I am not other than Vāsudeva, the inner Self of all.”

For the ascetic whose senses, through the strength of practice,
remain under control:
his wisdom (prajñā) is established.
