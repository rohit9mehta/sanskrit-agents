## Justifications

**J1. The ablative bhayāt is causal, and Śaṅkara explicitly supplies karṇādibhyaḥ as the object/source of fear and excludes kṛpā as the motive. Therefore the translation should not leave the withdrawal as noble compassion.**
- chosen: “Out of fear of Karṇa and the others,” not “from compassion.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `bhayāt karṇādibhyaḥ raṇāt yuddhāt uparataṃ nivṛttaṃ maṃsyante cintayiṣyanti na kṛpayeti` — “Out of fear—of Karṇa and the others; from raṇa, that is, battle; uparata, withdrawn; they will think, i.e. they will consider—not through pity.”

**J2. Śaṅkara glosses raṇāt as yuddhāt, uparatam as nivṛttam, and maṃsyante as cintayiṣyanti. Thus tvām is the object of “they will think,” with uparatam as its predicate complement.**
- chosen: “Withdrawn from battle” as the predicate thought by the mahārathas. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `raṇāt yuddhāt uparataṃ nivṛttaṃ maṃsyante cintayiṣyanti` — “From raṇa, from battle; uparata, withdrawn; maṃsyante, they will think.”

**J3. The commentary identifies the subject mahārathāḥ not as literal “great chariots” but as persons, specifically Duryodhana and others.**
- chosen: mahārathāḥ are the Kaurava-side great warriors, Duryodhana and the rest. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `tvāṃ mahārathāḥ duryodhana-prabhṛtayaḥ` — “You—the mahārathas, Duryodhana and the rest.”

**J4. Rather than taking bahumataḥ as merely “thought by many” or socially popular, Śaṅkara explains the content of the esteem: Arjuna is regarded as endowed with many qualities.**
- chosen: bahumataḥ means highly esteemed as possessing many virtues/qualities. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `yeṣāṃ ca tvaṃ duryodhanādīnāṃ bahu-mato bahubhir guṇair yuktaḥ ity evaṃ mataḥ bahumato bhūtvā` — “And for whom—you, among Duryodhana and the others—bahu-mata: thought thus, ‘endowed with many qualities’; having become bahumata.”

**J5. The object of yāsyasi is not speed, dexterity, or physical lightness; Śaṅkara glosses lāghava as laghu-bhāva, a fall into being ‘light’ or slight in esteem.**

- gita [2]: `punar yāsyasi lāghavaṃ laghu-bhāvam` — “Then you will go to lāghava, the state of being slight.”

## Translation

> Out of fear of Karṇa and the others, not from compassion,
the great chariot-warriors—Duryodhana and the rest—
will think you withdrawn from battle;
and, having been highly esteemed in their eyes—
regarded as possessed of many virtues—
you will come to lowliness (lāghava), the state of being slight.

## Analyzer disagreements

- raṇāt: ByT5 marks raṇa as masculine; in the battle sense, Śaṅkara’s gloss raṇāt yuddhāt and the supplied MW entry support neuter raṇa here. The ablative singular form is identical either way.
- uparatam: ByT5 gives lemma uparam; for the required subanta analysis I record the inflected participial stem uparata, while noting its derivation from upa-√ram.
- mahārathāḥ: ByT5 outputs mahā + rathāḥ as compound part plus noun; I treat the printed form as a single inflected compound stem mahāratha.
- bahumato/bahumataḥ: ByT5 outputs bahu + mataḥ separately and lemmatizes mataḥ to √man; I treat the printed sandhi-form as one inflected compound stem bahumata, with mata as the kta-participle within the compound.
- tvām and tvam: ByT5 gives second-person pronoun case/number without gender; I supply masculine because the referent is Arjuna and the agreeing predicates are masculine uparatam and bahumataḥ.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might take bhayāt as vague fear and miss Śaṅkara’s explicit “fear of Karṇa and the others,” as well as the contrast “not through compassion.”
- It might render mahārathāḥ generically, or even as literal chariots, rather than as Duryodhana and the other great warriors.
- It might translate bahumataḥ as “thought by many” or simply “popular,” whereas Śaṅkara explains it as being regarded as endowed with many qualities.
- It might choose a dictionary sense of lāghava such as speed, lightness, or dexterity; the bhāṣya requires “lowliness/insignificance,” laghu-bhāva.
