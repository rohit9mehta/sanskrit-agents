## Justifications

**J1. I take aśocyān as the accusative plural object of anvaśocaḥ, “those who are not to be grieved for,” not as an abstract neuter. Śaṅkara explicitly defines aśocyāḥ as na śocyāḥ and identifies the referents as Bhīṣma, Droṇa, etc.; he further gives the reasons as good conduct and ultimate eternality.**
- chosen: aśocyān = persons not fit to be mourned, especially Bhīṣma, Droṇa, and the like (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `na śocyā aśocyāḥ bhīṣma-droṇādayaḥ |` — “Aśocyāḥ means ‘not to be grieved for’: Bhīṣma, Droṇa, and the others.”
- gita [2]: `sad-vṛttatvāt paramārtha-svarūpeṇa ca nityatvāt, tān aśocyān anvaśoco 'nuśocitavān asi` — “Because of their good conduct and because, in their ultimate nature, they are eternal, you have mourned those who are not to be mourned.”
- gita [7]: `paramārthatas tu tān nityān aśocyān anuśocasi, ato mūḍho 'si ity abhiprāyaḥ` — “But in ultimate truth you mourn them, eternal and not to be mourned; therefore you are deluded—this is the purport.”

**J2. Although formally a laṅ second singular, I render it idiomatically as “you have grieved,” following Śaṅkara’s gloss anuśocitavān asi. The following words in the bhāṣya are Arjuna’s imagined thought, marked by iti, not Śaṅkara’s doctrinal assertion.**
- chosen: anvaśocaḥ = “you have grieved/mourned” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `tān aśocyān anvaśoco 'nuśocitavān asi te mriyante man-nimittam, ahaṃ tair vinābhūtaḥ kiṃ kariṣyāmi rājya-sukhādinā iti |` — “You have mourned those not to be mourned, thinking: ‘They are dying because of me; bereft of them, what shall I do with kingdom, pleasures, and so on?’”

**J3. I do not take prajñā-vādān as “arguments about wisdom” or “philosophical doctrines.” Śaṅkara glosses it as the vādas and vacanāni of prajñāvats/buddhimats, so the translation is “pronouncements of the wise.”**
- chosen: prajñā-vādān = pronouncements/words of the wise and intelligent (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `tvaṃ prajñā-vādān prajñāvatāṃ buddhimatāṃ vādāṃś ca vacanāni ca bhāṣase |` — “You speak prajñā-vādas: the speeches and utterances of those possessed of wisdom, the intelligent.”

**J4. I add “and yet” in English to bring out the contrast between grieving and speaking like the wise. Śaṅkara says Arjuna is displaying mutually opposed delusion and learnedness, like a madman.**
- chosen: the first hemistich is a rebuke with ironic contrast (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `tad etat mauḍhyaṃ pāṇḍityaṃ ca viruddham ātmani darśayasi unmatta iva ity abhiprāyaḥ |` — “Thus you display in yourself this delusion and learnedness, mutually opposed, like a madman—such is the purport.”

**J5. The bahuvrīhi compounds are construed personally in the accusative plural: those whose life-breaths have gone and those whose life-breaths have not gone. Śaṅkara glosses them as mṛtān and jīvataḥ respectively.**
- chosen: gatāsūn / agatāsūn = the dead / the living (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [5]: `yasmāt gatāsūn gata-prāṇān mṛtān, agatāsūn agata-prāṇān jīvataś ca nānuśocanti paṇḍitāḥ ātmajñāḥ |` — “For the wise, knowers of the Self, do not mourn those whose breaths have gone, the dead, nor those whose breaths have not gone, the living.”

**J6. I translate paṇḍitāḥ as “the wise, knowers of the Self.” Śaṅkara first glosses paṇḍitāḥ as ātmajñāḥ and then derives the term through paṇḍā as an intellect whose object is the Self.**

## Translation

> The Blessed Lord said:
You have grieved for those who are not to be grieved for,
and yet you speak pronouncements of the wise (prajñā-vādān).
The wise (paṇḍitāḥ), knowers of the Self,
do not grieve for the dead, whose breaths have gone,
nor for the living, whose breaths have not gone.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render prajñā-vādān as “arguments about wisdom”; Śaṅkara instead takes it as the words or pronouncements of wise, intelligent persons.
- It might take paṇḍitāḥ as ordinary learned scholars; Śaṅkara makes the term technical: ātmajñāḥ, knowers of the Self.
- It might miss the rebuking irony of the first half; Śaṅkara explicitly says Arjuna is displaying mutually opposed delusion and learnedness.
- It might treat aśocyān as only a vague moral adjective; Śaṅkara identifies the referents as Bhīṣma, Droṇa, and the others, and gives the deeper reason that they are ultimately eternal.
