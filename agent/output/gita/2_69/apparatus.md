## Translation

> What is night to all beings—the ultimate reality (paramārtha-tattva), out of reach for the ignorant—
in that the self-controlled one (saṃyamī), the yogin of conquered senses, keeps awake.
In what beings are said to be awake—the night of ignorance (avidyā) marked by the grasper–grasped division (grāhya-grāhaka-bheda)—
that is night to the sage (muni) who sees the ultimate reality.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might make the verse a simple contrast between nocturnal sleep and daytime waking; Śaṅkara instead makes the first ‘night’ the ultimate reality and the second ‘night’ ignorance.
- It might treat jāgrati bhūtāni as genuinely positive wakefulness; the commentary says beings are only ‘said’ to be awake and are actually asleep like dreamers in avidyā.
- It might translate paśyato muneḥ vaguely as ‘of the seeing sage’; Śaṅkara specifies that the sage sees paramārtha-tattva, the ultimate reality.
- It might miss that saṃyamī is not merely restrained in a general sense but, for Śaṅkara, a jitendriya yogin awakened from ignorance-sleep.
