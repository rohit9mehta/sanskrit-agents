## Justifications

**J1. Compound relation and syntactic attachment of rāga-dveṣa-viyuktaiḥ**
- chosen: rāga-dveṣa-viyuktaiḥ qualifies indriyaiḥ: the senses are separated from the pair attraction and aversion; rāga-dveṣa is an internal dvandva, and the whole compound is a tṛtīyā-tatpuruṣa adjective. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `rāgaś ca dveṣaś ca rāga-dveṣau` — Rāga and dveṣa together are rāga-dveṣa.
- gita [1]: `tābhyāṃ viyuktaiḥ śrotrādibhir indriyair` — with the senses beginning with hearing, separated from those two

**J2. Sense and object of caran with viṣayān**
- chosen: viṣayān caran means encountering or experiencing unavoidable sense-objects, not roaming in or indulging sense-objects generally. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `viṣayān avarjanīyān caran upalabhamānaḥ` — moving among, i.e. experiencing, unavoidable objects

**J3. Meaning of ātma-vaśya and its agreement with indriyaiḥ**
- chosen: ātma-vaśyaiḥ means that the senses are under one’s own control. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `ātma-vaśyair ātmano vaśyāni vaśībhūtāni indriyāṇi tair ātma-vaśyair` — ātma-vaśya: the senses are subject to the self, brought under control; with those ātma-vaśya senses

**J4. Analysis of vidheyātmā**
- chosen: vidheyātmā is a bahuvrīhi: one whose inner organ is obedient to his will; ātman here is antaḥ-karaṇa, not simply “soul.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `vidheyātmā icchātaḥ vidheya ātmā antaḥ-karaṇaṃ yasya so 'yaṃ` — vidheyātmā: he whose ātmā, the inner organ, is subject at will

**J5. Meaning of prasāda**
- chosen: prasāda is serenity or clear inner soundness, not divine favor or social grace. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `prasādaḥ prasannatā svāsthyam` — prasāda is serenity/clarity, health or soundness

**J6. Force of tu**
- chosen: tu is rendered contrastively as “but.” (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> But one whose inner organ (antaḥ-karaṇa) is obedient to his will,
while encountering unavoidable sense-objects
with senses free from attraction and aversion (rāga-dveṣa) and under his own control,
attains serenity (prasāda).
