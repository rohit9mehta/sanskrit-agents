## Justifications

**J1. The genitive dehinaḥ is not translated merely as ‘of the embodied person’ or ‘of a living creature’; Śaṅkara identifies the dehin here as the body-possessing ātman, the self enveloped in a body.**
- chosen: dehinaḥ = ‘of/for the embodied self’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `deho 'sya astīti dehī, tasya dehino dehavataḥ ātmano` — ‘One who has a body is dehin; of that dehin, of the body-possessing self ...’
- gita [4]: `avikriyasyaiva ātmana ity arthaḥ` — ‘The meaning is: of the self alone, which is changeless.’

**J2. The three nouns are rendered as ‘childhood, youth, and old age’, not as generic juvenility/youthfulness/decay, because Śaṅkara defines them as successive avasthās: childhood, middle/youthful condition, and decrepit old age.**
- chosen: kaumāram, yauvanam, jarā = three distinct bodily life-states (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `kaumāraṃ kumāra-bhāvo bālyāvasthā, yauvanaṃ yūno bhāvo madhyamāvasthā, jarā vayo-hāniḥ jīrṇāvasthā, ity etāḥ tisro 'vasthāḥ anyonya-vilakṣaṇāḥ` — ‘Kaumāra is the condition of a child, the state of childhood; yauvana is the condition of a youth, the middle state; jarā is loss of age/vitality, the worn-out state. These three states are mutually distinct.’

**J3. The yathā/tathā structure is taken to mean: just as the self is not destroyed or produced when bodily states perish or arise, so too its attainment of another body does not imply change in the self. The translation therefore includes ‘by/for that same unchanging self’.**
- chosen: The analogy is about attainment of states and then another body by the changeless self (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `tāsāṃ prathamāvasthā-nāśe na nāśaḥ, dvitīyāvasthopajane na upajana ātmanaḥ` — ‘When the first of those states is destroyed, there is no destruction of the self; when the second state arises, there is no arising of the self.’
- gita [3]: `avikriyasyaiva dvitīya-tṛtīyāvasthā-prāptiḥ ātmano dṛṣṭā` — ‘Rather, the attainment of the second and third states is seen to belong to the self alone, which is changeless.’
- gita [4]: `tathā tadvad eva ... dehāntara-prāptiḥ avikriyasyaiva ātmana ity arthaḥ` — ‘So, just in that same way ... the attainment of another body belongs to the self alone, which is changeless—that is the meaning.’

**J4. The compound is not taken as ‘attainment between bodies’ or as a vague ‘bodily transformation’; Śaṅkara gives a two-step analysis: dehāntara is a body other than the present body, and dehāntara-prāpti is the attainment of that.**
- chosen: dehāntaraprāptiḥ = ‘attainment of another body’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `dehād anyo deho dehāntaram, tasya prāptiḥ dehāntara-prāptiḥ` — ‘A body different from the body is dehāntara, another body; the attainment of it is dehāntara-prāpti.’

**J5. Dhīraḥ is rendered ‘wise one’, following the gloss dhīmān, rather than primarily ‘firm’ or ‘brave’. Tatra is taken situationally (‘in this matter, when this is so’), not spatially. Muhyati is active intransitive: ‘falls into delusion / is bewildered’.**
- chosen: dhīraḥ tatra na muhyati = ‘the wise one is not deluded about this / when this is so’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [5]: `dhīro dhīmān tatraivaṃ sati na muhyati na moham āpadyate` — ‘The dhīra is the intelligent/wise one; when this is so, he is not deluded, does not fall into delusion.’

## Translation

> As, for the embodied self, in this body there are childhood, youth, and old age,
so too there is the attainment of another body by that same unchanging self.
The wise one is not deluded about this.

## Analyzer disagreements

- ByT5 marks asmin and dehe as locative singular neuter (SLNe). I take both as masculine locative singular, because dehe is construed as masculine ‘body’; Śaṅkara’s line 4 has masculine deha-forms, dehād anyo deho. Commentary line 1 appears to print dehā in the phrase asmin vartamāne dehā yathā, evidently a plaintext error for dehe, so I do not rely on that reading.
- ByT5 segments deha_antara_prāptiḥ as compound pieces. I analyze the printed dehāntara-prāptiḥ as one inflected feminine nominative singular compound stem dehāntaraprāpti; the hyphen is treated as editorial. Śaṅkara’s explicit vigraha is tasya prāptiḥ dehāntara-prāptiḥ in line 4.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render dehin simply as ‘the embodied person’, missing Śaṅkara’s insistence that the referent is the body-possessing ātman, and specifically the changeless self.
- It might describe childhood, youth, old age, and rebirth as changes of the self itself; Śaṅkara explicitly denies destruction, production, or modification of the self across these states.
- It might take dehāntara-prāptiḥ vaguely as ‘body-change’ or even ‘attainment between bodies’; the bhāṣya fixes it as ‘attainment of a body other than this body’.
- It might translate dhīraḥ as merely ‘steadfast’ and tatra as locative ‘there’; Śaṅkara glosses them as dhīmān and evaṃ sati: the wise one is not deluded in this matter.

## Open questions

- Commentary line 1 appears to read asmin vartamāne dehā yathā where the verse and context require dehe yathā. I treat dehā as a plaintext error and have not cited it as an independent reading.
