## Justifications

**J1. The locative is taken as the circumstance in which the result arises; Śaṅkara frames the verse as praise of prasannatā, serenity, and glosses the related prasanna-cetasaḥ by a well-composed inner organ.**
- chosen: prasāde = in/when there is mental serenity (prasāda), not divine favor or mere stylistic clarity. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `seyaṃ prasannatā stūyate --` — This serenity is being praised.
- gita [2]: `prasanna-cetasaḥ svasthāntaḥ-karaṇasya` — Of one whose mind is serene, whose inner instrument is well-composed.

**J2. The compound is read as “all sorrows,” not “sorrows of all [people/things].” Śaṅkara glosses the genitive plural with ādhyātmikādīnām.**
- chosen: sarva-duḥkhānām = of all sorrows, specifically including the ādhyātmika and other classes. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `sarva-duḥkhānām ādhyātmikādīnāṃ` — Of all sorrows, beginning with the internal/psychophysical ones.

**J3. Although hāni can mean decrease, Śaṅkara explicitly glosses it by vināśa, destruction.**
- chosen: hāniḥ = destruction/cessation, not mere loss or diminution. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `hānir vināśo` — hāniḥ means destruction.

**J4. The pronoun is not left as a vague “of this”; Śaṅkara supplies yateḥ as its referent.**
- chosen: asya = of this yati, this disciplined ascetic/practitioner. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `vināśo 'sya yater upajāyate` — Destruction arises for this yati.

**J5. The form could be read superficially as “of a serene mind,” but Śaṅkara treats it as qualifying the person: svasthāntaḥ-karaṇasya, and then speaks of the prasanna-cetas as having an established buddhi.**
- chosen: prasanna-cetasaḥ = of the serene-minded person, one whose inner organ is composed. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `prasanna-cetasaḥ svasthāntaḥ-karaṇasya` — Of one whose mind is serene, whose inner instrument is well-composed.
- gita [3]: `evaṃ prasanna-cetaso 'vasthita-buddhiḥ` — Thus, for the serene-minded one, the intellect is established.

**J6. Śaṅkara glosses hi by yasmāt and āśu by śīghram, so the second hemistich explains the first rather than merely adding emphasis.**
- chosen: hi is causal, “for/because,” and āśu is adverbial, “quickly.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `hi yasmāt āśu śīghraṃ` — For/because; quickly, swiftly.

**J7. Śaṅkara expands the verb as complete, all-around abiding and then states the intended sense as niścalībhavati, becoming motionless, with ātma-svarūpa. The translation therefore uses “becomes firmly established,” not merely “stands” or “pervades.”**
- chosen: buddhiḥ paryavatiṣṭhate = the intellect quickly becomes firmly established, immovable as Self-nature itself. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `buddhiḥ paryavatiṣṭhate ākāśam iva pari samantāt avatiṣṭhate, ātma-svarūpeṇaiva niścalībhavatīty arthaḥ` — The intellect becomes established; it abides all around, as it were like space; the meaning is that it becomes motionless as Self-nature itself.

## Translation

> When serenity (prasāda) is present, the destruction of all sorrows arises for this yati.
For, in the serene-minded one, the intellect (buddhi) quickly becomes firmly established, immovable as Self-nature itself.
