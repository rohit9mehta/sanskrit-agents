## Justifications

**J1. The nominative hṛṣīkeśaḥ is taken as the speaker, not merely as an etymological common noun. Śaṅkara repeatedly identifies the one about to speak as bhagavān vāsudevaḥ and introduces the following teaching with āha.**
- chosen: Hṛṣīkeśa is Bhagavān Vāsudeva, the subject of uvāca. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [10]: `tad-upadidikṣuḥ sarva-lokānugrahārtham arjunaṃ nimittīkṛtya āha bhagavān vāsudevaḥ--- aśocyān ityādi` — Desiring to teach that, for the benefit of all people, Bhagavān Vāsudeva, making Arjuna the occasion, spoke—beginning with “aśocyān …”.
- gita [57]: `bhagavān vāsudevaḥ tataḥ kṛpayā arjunam uddidhārayiṣuḥ ātma-jñānāyāvatārayann āha` — Bhagavān Vāsudeva, then, out of compassion, wishing to lift Arjuna up and bringing him down to the teaching of self-knowledge, spoke.

**J2. The accusative tam is the person addressed by uvāca, and viṣīdantam qualifies that same masculine singular object. Śaṅkara reads the whole prior scene as Arjuna’s śoka and moha, culminating in his need to be raised up by Vāsudeva.**
- chosen: tam viṣīdantam refers to Arjuna as the desponding recipient of the speech. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `arjunena rājya-guru-putra-mitra-suhṛt-svajana-saṃbandhi-bāndhaveṣu aham eteṣām mamaite ity evaṃ bhrānti-pratyaya-nimitta-sneha-vicchedādi-nimittau ātmanaḥ śoka-mohau pradarśitau` — By Arjuna, sorrow and delusion of the self were displayed, caused by affection and its rupture, themselves due to the mistaken notion ‘I am theirs, these are mine’ regarding kingdom, teachers, sons, friends, well-wishers, kinsmen, relatives, and connections.
- gita [4]: `śoka-mohābhyāṃ hy abhibhūta-viveka-vijñānaḥ svata eva kṣatra-dharme yuddhe pravṛtto 'pi tasmād yuddhād upararāma` — For, with his discriminative knowledge overpowered by sorrow and delusion, though already engaged in battle, the kṣatriya duty, he ceased from that battle.
- gita [57]: `dharma-saṃmūḍha-cetaso mithyā-jñānavato mahati śoka-sāgare nimagnasya arjunasya anyatrātma-jñānād uddharaṇam apaśyan` — Seeing no rescue for Arjuna—whose mind was confused about dharma, who had false knowledge, and who was sunk in the great ocean of sorrow—apart from self-knowledge.

**J3. uvāca takes both the person addressed (tam) and the utterance (idaṃ vacaḥ). Śaṅkara explicitly connects the speech now introduced with the words beginning aśocyān in 2.11.**
- chosen: idaṃ vacaḥ is the forward-pointing object: the teaching beginning aśocyān. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [10]: `āha bhagavān vāsudevaḥ--- aśocyān ityādi` — Bhagavān Vāsudeva spoke—beginning with “aśocyān …”.
- gita [58, 59, 60]: `śrī-bhagavān uvāca
aśocyān anvaśocas tvaṃ prajñā-vādāṃś ca bhāṣase |
gatāsūn agatāsūṃś ca nānuśocanti paṇḍitāḥ` — The Blessed Lord said: ‘You have grieved for those who should not be grieved for, and you speak words of wisdom; the learned grieve neither for those whose life-breaths are gone nor for those whose life-breaths are not gone.’

**J4. Grammatically, prahasann is a nominative singular present participle agreeing with Hṛṣīkeśa, and iva softens the action. Although Śaṅkara does not gloss prahasann directly, he describes Vāsudeva’s motive as kṛpayā, “out of compassion,” which excludes making mockery the governing sense.**
- chosen: prahasann iva is rendered “as though smiling,” not as derisive mockery. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [57]: `bhagavān vāsudevaḥ tataḥ kṛpayā arjunam uddidhārayiṣuḥ` — Bhagavān Vāsudeva, then, out of compassion, wishing to lift Arjuna up.

**J5. The genitive duals senayoḥ and ubhayoḥ depend on the locative madhye, a regular construction meaning “in the middle of / between.” The commentary is silent on this case construal, so the decision rests on grammar and dictionary usage for the sense of senā, “army,” and madhya, “middle”; ubhayoḥ is analyzed as the dual adjective “both.”**
- chosen: senayor ubhayor madhye means “in the midst of the two armies.” (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> To him, desponding there in the midst of the two armies,
Hṛṣīkeśa—Bhagavān Vāsudeva—as though smiling,
spoke these words, O Bhārata.
