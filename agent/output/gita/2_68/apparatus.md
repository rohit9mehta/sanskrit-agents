## Justifications

**J1. tasmāt is an inferential connective grounded in the fault already shown in the activity of the senses.**
- chosen: “Therefore,” not merely “from that.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `indriyāṇāṃ pravṛttau doṣa upapādito yasmāt tasmāt` — Because the defect in the activity of the senses has been demonstrated, therefore...

**J2. The genitive relative-correlative construction is to be understood as “of the ascetic whose ... of that one.”**
- chosen: yasya/tasya refer to a yati, “ascetic/disciplined person.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `yasya yateḥ he mahābāho, nigṛhītāni ... tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā` — of whose—the ascetic’s—O mighty-armed one, [the senses] are restrained ... that one’s wisdom is established.

**J3. indriyārthebhyaḥ is ablative plural with nigṛhītāni; it means sense-objects, not “purposes” in the abstract.**
- chosen: “restrained from the objects of the senses,” i.e. from sound and the rest. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `nigṛhītāni ... indriyāṇi indriyārthebhyaḥ śabdādibhyas` — the senses restrained from the sense-objects, from sound and the rest.

**J4. sarvaśaḥ modifies the restraint by specifying all modes or manners, not simply “all the senses.”**
- chosen: “in every way,” with modes including the mental. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `sarvaśaḥ sarva-prakāraiḥ mānasādi-bhedaiḥ` — sarvaśaḥ: by all modes, with distinctions beginning with the mental.

**J5. The word is not syntactically part of the yasya phrase; it directly addresses Arjuna.**
- chosen: mahābāho is a vocative address, “O mighty-armed one.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `he mahābāho` — O mighty-armed one.

**J6. The verse’s formula tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā is read in the sthita-prajña context, where Śaṅkara characterizes the person as one in whom discriminative knowledge has arisen.**
- chosen: prajñā is “wisdom/discriminative knowledge,” and pratiṣṭhitā means “established, firmly settled.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `tasya prajñā pratiṣṭhitā` — that one’s wisdom is established.
- gita [2]: `utpanna-viveka-jñānasya sthita-prajñasya` — of the person of established wisdom, whose discriminative knowledge has arisen.

## Translation

> Therefore, O mighty-armed one, the wisdom (prajñā) of that ascetic whose senses are restrained in every way from the objects of the senses is firmly established.
