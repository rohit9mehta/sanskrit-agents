## Justifications

**J1. The commentary defines nāsti by absence/non-existence and explicitly supplies asti in the second and third negative clauses, so the repeated construction is existential rather than a mere predicative ‘is not’.**
- chosen: Render the negative clauses as existential: “there is no …”. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `nāsti na vidyate na bhavatīty arthaḥ` — ‘nāsti’ means ‘is not found, does not exist / does not occur.’
- gita [2]: `na ca asti ayuktasya bhāvanā ātma-jñānābhiniveśaḥ |` — ‘Nor is there, for the ayukta, bhāvanā—absorption in Self-knowledge.’
- gita [3]: `tathā -- na cāsty abhāvayata ātma-jñānābhiniveśam akurvataḥ śāntir upaśamaḥ |` — ‘Likewise, there is no śānti—quieting—for one not doing absorption in Self-knowledge.’

**J2. Buddhi is not translated as generic intelligence or practical cleverness; Śaṅkara specifies its object as ātma-svarūpa.**
- chosen: buddhi = understanding directed to the nature of the Self. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `buddhiḥ ātma-svarūpa-viṣayā` — ‘Buddhi’ is ‘having the nature of the Self as its object.’

**J3. The genitive ayuktasya is construed substantively of a person, and its sense is fixed by the gloss asamāhitāntaḥ-karaṇasya, not merely ‘unyoked’ in a physical sense.**
- chosen: ayuktasya = ‘for/of the uncollected one’, i.e. one whose inner organ is not composed. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `ayuktasya asamāhitāntaḥ-karaṇasya` — ‘Of the ayukta’ means ‘of one whose inner organ is not collected/composed.’

**J4. The word is not taken as imagination, production, or contemplation in general; Śaṅkara glosses it as ātma-jñānābhiniveśa.**
- chosen: bhāvanā = absorption or devoted application in Self-knowledge. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `bhāvanā ātma-jñānābhiniveśaḥ` — ‘Bhāvanā’ is ‘absorption/intent application in Self-knowledge.’

**J5. Although the dictionary head can suggest ‘unconscious’ or ‘unwise’, the bhāṣya reads the form as a genitive participial substantive and supplies its object: ātma-jñānābhiniveśam akurvataḥ.**
- chosen: abhāvayataḥ = ‘of one who does not cultivate/do that absorption’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `abhāvayata ātma-jñānābhiniveśam akurvataḥ` — ‘Of one not doing/carrying out absorption in Self-knowledge.’

**J6. The translation uses ‘quietude’ rather than a broad social ‘peace’, because the commentary defines śānti as upaśama.**
- chosen: śānti = quietude/quieting (upaśama), and aśānta = one lacking that quietude. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `śāntir upaśamaḥ` — ‘Śānti’ is ‘quieting, subsidence.’

**J7. The last pāda is not promising ordinary pleasure from objects. Śaṅkara explains that happiness is the senses’ withdrawal from thirst for objects; object-directed craving is suffering, and with craving present not even the slightest trace of happiness is possible.**
- chosen: sukha is not sense-object craving; it is possible only with withdrawal from craving, so kutaḥ sukham is rhetorical: ‘whence happiness?’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `aśāntasya kutaḥ sukham ? indriyāṇāṃ hi viṣaya-sevā-tṛṣṇātaḥ nivṛttir yā tat-sukham, na viṣaya-viṣayā tṛṣṇā |` — ‘For the unquieted, whence happiness? For the withdrawal of the senses from the thirst to frequent objects—that is happiness, not craving whose object is sense-objects.’
- gita [5]: `duḥkham eva hi sā |` — ‘For that [craving] is only suffering.’
- gita [6]: `na tṛṣṇāyāṃ satyāṃ sukhasya gandha-mātram apy upapadyate ity arthaḥ ||bhgs_2.66||` — ‘When craving exists, not even the merest scent of happiness is possible—this is the meaning.’

## Translation

> There is no understanding (buddhi) of the Self’s nature for the uncollected (ayukta);
nor, for the uncollected, is there absorption in Self-knowledge (bhāvanā).
And for one who does not cultivate that, there is no quietude (śānti);
for the unquieted, whence happiness (sukha)?

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render buddhi as ordinary ‘intellect’; the bhāṣya restricts it to understanding whose object is the Self’s nature.
- It might take bhāvanā as imagination or generic meditation; Śaṅkara defines it as ātma-jñānābhiniveśa, absorption in Self-knowledge.
- It might follow the dictionary sense of abhāvayat as ‘unconscious/unwise’; the commentary instead construes abhāvayataḥ as ‘of one not cultivating/doing that absorption’.
- It might understand sukha as sense pleasure; Śaṅkara explicitly denies that object-directed craving is happiness and says happiness is withdrawal from such craving.
