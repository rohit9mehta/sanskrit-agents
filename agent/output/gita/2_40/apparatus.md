## Justifications

**J1. The locative/adverbial scope of iha is not ‘in this world’ in general but the presently taught karma-yoga as a mokṣa-path.**
- chosen: iha = ‘here’ in the path to liberation, specifically in karma-yoga (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `neha mokṣa-mārge karma-yoge` — ‘Here’ means ‘on the path to liberation, in karma-yoga.’

**J2. The compound is taken as a ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa: ‘destruction/loss of the abhikrama,’ where abhikrama is glossed as prārambha, a beginning or undertaking. The point is that in yoga the initiated effort is not fruitlessly lost as in ordinary ventures such as agriculture.**
- chosen: abhikrama-nāśaḥ = loss/destruction of the undertaking or beginning (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `abhikrama-nāśo 'bhikramaṇam abhikramaḥ prārambhas tasya nāśo nāsti yathā kṛṣyādeḥ` — Abhikrama is the act of beginning, a commencement; there is no destruction of that, unlike agriculture and the like.
- gita [2]: `yoga-viṣaye prārambhasya na anaikāntika-phalatvam ity arthaḥ` — The meaning is: with respect to yoga, the beginning does not have uncertain fruit.

**J3. The term is not merely ‘diminution’ or ‘annoyance’; Śaṅkara’s comparison with medical treatment points to a harmful adverse result, which is denied in karma-yoga.**
- chosen: pratyavāyaḥ = adverse consequence or harmful reverse result (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `kiṃ ca nāpi cikitsāvat pratyavāyo vidyate bhavati` — Moreover, nor is there an adverse consequence, as in medical treatment; it does not exist/occur.

**J4. Śaṅkara identifies dharmasya as yoga-dharmasya and supplies anuṣṭhitam, so the subject of trāyate is not an abstract ‘smallness’ but even a slight practiced portion of this discipline.**
- chosen: svalpam api asya dharmasya = even a little of this yoga-dharma, when practiced (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `kiṃ tu svalpam api asya dharmasya yoga-dharmasya anuṣṭhitaṃ trāyate` — But even a little of this dharma, the dharma of yoga, when practiced, protects.

**J5. The verb trāyate is glossed by rakṣati, ‘protects,’ and mahad bhayam is specified as saṃsāra-bhaya characterized by birth, death, and so on.**
- chosen: trāyate mahato bhayāt = protects from the great fear of saṃsāra (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `trāyate rakṣati mahataḥ bhayāt saṃsāra-bhayāt janma-maraṇādi-lakṣaṇāt` — Trāyate means ‘protects’; from the great fear, from the fear of saṃsāra, characterized by birth, death, and the like.

## Translation

> Here, on the path to liberation (mokṣa-mārga), in karma-yoga,
there is no loss of an undertaking (abhikrama);
no adverse consequence (pratyavāya) is found.
Even a little of this discipline (dharma), when practiced,
protects one from the great fear of saṃsāra.

## One-shot delta

- It might leave ‘the great fear’ unspecified, whereas Śaṅkara identifies it as the fear of saṃsāra marked by birth and death.
