## Justifications

**J1. The verse is introduced as giving the reason for the preceding point, not merely as an emphatic “indeed.”**
- chosen: hi is causal: “for/because.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `indriyāṇāṃ hi yasmāt` — “indriyāṇām hi — because...”

**J2. The genitive plural participle caratām modifies indriyāṇām, and Śaṅkara specifies the field of their movement as sva-sva-viṣayeṣu.**
- chosen: indriyāṇāṃ caratām means “of the senses as they range/proceed in their own respective objects.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `caratāṃ sva-sva-viṣayeṣu pravartamānānām` — “moving/proceeding in their own respective objects.”

**J3. Although the form is passive, the commentary glosses its contextual sense with anupravartate, so the translation should not be a bare “is regulated” or “is assigned.”**
- chosen: anuvidhīyate is translated by sense as “follows after / is led to conform to.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `yat mano 'nuvidhīyate anupravartate` — “the mind which is anuvidhīyate — follows along after.”

**J4. Śaṅkara explicitly expands tat as the mind set in motion by sense-object construction, and later says that the mind, having carried away self-directed wisdom, makes it sense-object-directed.**
- chosen: tat refers to the mind, not to a sense or sense-object; that mind is the subject of harati. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `tat indriya-viṣaya-vikalpanena pravṛttaṃ mano 'sya yateḥ harati prajñām` — “that mind, set in motion by the construction of sense-objects, carries away this striver’s wisdom.”
- gita [2]: `evam ātma-viṣayāṃ prajñāṃ hṛtvā mano viṣaya-viṣayāṃ karoti` — “so the mind, having carried away the wisdom whose object is the Self, makes it have sense-objects as its object.”

**J5. The pronoun is not left as a vague “his”; Śaṅkara supplies yateḥ as the possessor whose wisdom is carried away.**
- chosen: asya means “of this striver/ascetic.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `asya yateḥ` — “of this yati/striver.”

**J6. Śaṅkara glosses prajñā not as general intelligence but as ātmānātma-vivekajā and ātma-viṣayā.**
- chosen: prajñā is discriminative wisdom concerning the Self, born from self/not-self discrimination. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `prajñām ātmānātma-vivekajāṃ` — “wisdom born of discrimination between Self and not-Self.”
- gita [2]: `ātma-viṣayāṃ prajñāṃ` — “wisdom whose object is the Self.”

**J7. The main verb preserves the boat simile’s “carry away” sense, but Śaṅkara adds nāśayati and explains the carrying away as a redirection of wisdom toward sense-objects.**
- chosen: harati is “carries away,” with the destructive effect of ruining/diverting wisdom. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `harati prajñām ātmānātma-vivekajāṃ nāśayati` — “it carries away the wisdom born of self/not-self discrimination — destroys it.”
- gita [2]: `prajñāṃ hṛtvā mano viṣaya-viṣayāṃ karoti` — “the mind, having carried away the wisdom, makes it directed to sense-objects.”

**J8. Śaṅkara glosses ambhasi as udake and expands the simile: wind takes a boat from the desired path and propels it onto a wrong path.**
- chosen: The simile is a wind driving a boat on water off its intended course. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `vāyuḥ nāvam iva ambhasi udake jigamiṣatāṃ mārgāduddhṛtya unmārge yathā vāyuḥ nāvaṃ pravartayati` — “as the wind, in water, taking a boat away from the route of those wishing to go, propels the boat onto a wrong course.”

## Translation

> For the mind (manas) that follows after the senses (indriyāṇi),
as they range in their own objects,
that mind carries away this striver’s discriminative wisdom (prajñā),
as the wind carries a boat on the waters.

## Analyzer disagreements

- caratām: ByT5 gives the verbal lemma car; since this is a subanta participle, I record the nominal stem carat- while retaining its genitive plural neuter analysis.
- yat: ByT5 left the morphosyntactic features unspecified; I analyze it as yad, neuter nominative singular, agreeing with manaḥ and correlating with tat, as supported by Śaṅkara’s tat ... mano.
- anuvidhīyate: ByT5 gives the lemma anuvidhā; following the required format for verbs, I decompose it as bare root dhā with prefixes anu and vi. The passive Lat third-singular analysis is retained.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might take tat as “that sense” or “that sense-object”; Śaṅkara identifies it as the mind set in motion by sense-object ideation.
- It might render prajñā as generic intelligence; Śaṅkara defines it as wisdom born of self/not-self discrimination and directed toward the Self.
- It might miss the causal force of hi and the specification that the senses range in their own respective objects.
- It might flatten the boat simile, whereas Śaṅkara reads it as the wind diverting a boat from its intended route onto a wrong course, parallel to the mind diverting wisdom toward sense-objects.
