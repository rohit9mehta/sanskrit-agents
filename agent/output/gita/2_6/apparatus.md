## Justifications

**J1. katarat and garīyaḥ are neuter singular forms forming the embedded question governed by vidmaḥ; the first naḥ/no is dative plural ‘for us’, not the negative particle no and not a genitive ‘of us’ in translation.**
- chosen: katarat no garīyaḥ = ‘which of the two is the weightier/better course for us’ (depends on commentary: no)

**J2. The paired vā-particles and the parallel optatives jayema/jayeyuḥ make the first yad function conjunctively with yadi, not as an ordinary relative pronoun needing its own nominal antecedent.**
- chosen: yad vā ... yadi vā introduces alternatives: ‘whether ... or whether’ (depends on commentary: no)

**J3. The verb ji ‘conquer’ can take an object; here naḥ supplies that object for jayeyuḥ. Taking no as indeclinable ‘not’ would leave the clause semantically and syntactically distorted after the already present yadi vā.**
- chosen: no before jayeyuḥ = naḥ, accusative plural ‘us’ (depends on commentary: no)

**J4. Both forms express the uncertain alternatives being weighed: ‘we should conquer’ and ‘they should conquer’. This optative force is required by the indirect deliberative question, not a simple present ‘we conquer / they conquer’.**
- chosen: jayema and jayeyuḥ are optatives of √ji (depends on commentary: no)

**J5. yān is masculine accusative plural governed by hatvā, while te dhārtarāṣṭrāḥ is masculine nominative plural subject of avasthitāḥ. The construction identifies the very men whom Arjuna would not wish to live after slaying as the Dhārtarāṣṭras standing before him.**
- chosen: yān eva ... te is a relative-correlative construction (depends on commentary: no)

**J6. The reduplicated stem jijīviṣa- is desiderative from √jīv. Thus na jijīviṣāmaḥ is not merely ‘we do not live’ but ‘we do not desire to live’.**
- chosen: jijīviṣāmaḥ means ‘we wish to live’ (depends on commentary: no)

**J7. The nominative plural masculine agrees with te and avasthitāḥ; in this epic context it is best taken substantively as Dhṛtarāṣṭra’s sons, not merely as the adjective ‘belonging to Dhṛtarāṣṭra’.**
- chosen: dhārtarāṣṭrāḥ as substantive patronymic, ‘sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra’ (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> Nor do we know this: which of the two is the weightier course for us—
whether we should conquer [them], or whether they should conquer us.
Those very sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra (dhārtarāṣṭrāḥ), after slaying whom we would not wish to live,
stand arrayed at the front.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind or sandhi-blind translation could take the second no as ‘not’, producing the wrong sense ‘or if they should not conquer’ instead of ‘or whether they should conquer us’.
- It could miss that katarat no garīyaḥ asks which alternative is preferable for us, not which person is greater or heavier.
- It could flatten the optatives jayema and jayeyuḥ into indicatives, losing Arjuna’s deliberative uncertainty.
- It could translate jijīviṣāmaḥ as simple ‘we live’ rather than the desiderative ‘we wish to live’.
- It could fail to link yān with te and so miss that the very people whose death would make life undesirable are the Dhārtarāṣṭras standing in front.
