## Justifications

**J1. The verse is not merely saying that ‘action’ in general is low. Śaṅkara glosses dūreṇa as an extreme separation, avaram as low/inferior, and specifies karma as action performed by one who seeks fruit.**
- chosen: dūreṇa hy avaraṃ karma = ‘action done for fruit is indeed far inferior’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `dūreṇa ativiprakarṣeṇa atyantam eva hy avaram adhamaṃ nikṛṣṭaṃ karma phalārthinā kriyamāṇaṃ` — ‘dūreṇa’ means ‘by an extreme remoteness, utterly’; ‘avaram’ means ‘lowest, inferior’; the action is one performed by a person seeking its fruit.

**J2. The ablative is comparative with avaram. Śaṅkara does not take buddhi-yoga here as merely abstract intellectual concentration; he glosses it as karma endowed with samatva-buddhi, the insight of equanimity.**
- chosen: buddhi-yogāt = ‘than action joined with the insight of equanimity’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `buddhi-yogāt samatva-buddhi-yuktāt karmaṇaḥ` — ‘from buddhi-yoga’ means ‘from action endowed with the buddhi of equanimity.’

**J3. The locative buddhau is the locus in which refuge is to be sought; śaraṇam is not a physical refuge but āśraya, the cause of attaining fearlessness. Śaṅkara further identifies the relevant buddhi as either yoga-oriented buddhi or the Sāṃkhya-buddhi born from its maturation, culminating in refuge in ultimate knowledge.**
- chosen: buddhau śaraṇam anviccha = ‘seek refuge in insight’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `yoga-viṣayāyāṃ buddhau tat-paripāka-jāyāṃ vā sāṃkhya-buddhau śaraṇam āśrayam abhaya-prāpti-kāraṇam anviccha prārthayasva, paramārtha-jñāna-śaraṇo bhavety arthaḥ` — In the buddhi whose object is yoga, or in the Sāṃkhya-buddhi born from its maturation, seek refuge—an āśraya, the cause for obtaining fearlessness; the meaning is: become one whose refuge is knowledge of ultimate reality.

**J4. The compound phala-hetavaḥ is taken substantively of people impelled by desire for fruit, not as ‘causes that produce fruits.’ Śaṅkara glosses kṛpaṇāḥ as dīnāḥ and phala-hetavaḥ as those prompted by thirst for results.**
- chosen: kṛpaṇāḥ phala-hetavaḥ = ‘wretched are those whose motive is fruit’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `kṛpaṇāḥ dīnāḥ phala-hetavaḥ phala-tṛṣṇā-prayuktāḥ santaḥ` — ‘kṛpaṇāḥ’ means ‘wretched’; ‘phala-hetavaḥ’ means ‘being impelled by thirst for fruit.’
- gita [3]: `yo vā etad akṣaraṃ gārgy aviditvāsmāl lokāt praiti sa kṛpaṇaḥ [bau 3.8.10] iti śruteḥ` — Śruti says: ‘Whoever, O Gārgī, departs from this world without knowing this imperishable is kṛpaṇa.’

**J5. The vocative force is confirmed explicitly by Śaṅkara’s he dhanaṃjaya.**
- chosen: dhanaṃjaya is vocative address to Arjuna (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `he dhanaṃjaya` — O Dhanaṃjaya.

## Translation

> Indeed, action done by one who seeks its fruit is far inferior

to action joined with the insight of equanimity (buddhi-yoga), O Dhanaṃjaya.

Seek refuge in insight (buddhi);

wretched are those whose motive is fruit.

## Analyzer disagreements

- anviccha: ByT5 gives lemma anviṣ; the analysis here separates the preverb and root as required: root iṣ with prefix anu.
- phala-hetavaḥ: ByT5 only segments phala + hetu; Śaṅkara’s gloss requires a bahuvrīhi/substantive sense, ‘those whose motive is fruit / who are impelled by thirst for fruit,’ not a simple tatpuruṣa ‘causes of fruit’.
- buddhiyogāt and phalahetavaḥ: revised the pausal surfaces to remove editorial hyphens, matching the derivable compound forms while keeping the printed hyphenated forms in surface_in_sandhi.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render buddhi-yogāt as simply ‘than knowledge’ or ‘than intellectual discipline’; Śaṅkara makes it action endowed with the insight of equanimity.
- It might treat karma as all action indiscriminately; Śaṅkara narrows the condemned action to that performed by a fruit-seeker.
- It might translate phala-hetavaḥ literally as ‘causes of fruit’; Śaṅkara interprets it as persons impelled by thirst for fruit.
- It might miss that buddhau śaraṇam means taking refuge in insight/ultimate knowledge as an abhaya-producing āśraya, not merely ‘use your intellect to seek shelter.’
