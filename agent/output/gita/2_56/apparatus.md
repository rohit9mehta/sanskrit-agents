## Justifications

**J1. The locative duḥkheṣu is not merely an abstract setting; Śaṅkara supplies prāpteṣu and duḥkha-prāptau, so the sense is 'when sufferings are encountered.' The compound anudvigna-manāḥ is a bahuvrīhi: the mind of this person is not udvigna, glossed as not prakṣubhita, 'not disturbed/shaken.'**
- chosen: duḥkheṣu ... anudvigna-manāḥ = 'in sufferings, when they arise, one whose mind is unagitated.' (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `duḥkheṣv ādhyātmikādiṣu prāpteṣu na udvignaṃ na prakṣubhitaṃ duḥkha-prāptau mano yasya so 'yam anudvigna-manāḥ |` — In sufferings such as the bodily/inner and the like, when they have arrived: the mind of whom is not agitated, not disturbed at the arrival of sorrow—this one is anudvigna-manāḥ.

**J2. Śaṅkara again supplies prāpteṣu for the locative and explicitly glosses spṛhā as tṛṣṇā, 'craving/thirst.' The fire simile rules out taking the phrase as mere indifference of mood: the point is that pleasures do not cause craving to grow.**
- chosen: sukheṣu vigata-spṛhaḥ = 'in pleasures, when they arise, one whose craving has gone.' (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `tathā sukheṣu prāpteṣu vigatā spṛhā tṛṣṇā yasya, na agnir iva indhanādy-ādhāne sukhāny anu vivardhate sa vigata-spṛhaḥ |` — Likewise, when pleasures have arrived, he whose spṛhā—craving—has gone; he does not grow along with pleasures like fire when fuel and the like are put on: he is vigata-spṛhaḥ.

**J3. The compound is construed as a bahuvrīhi with three separate factors—rāga, bhaya, and krodha—gone from the person, not as a chain such as 'anger caused by fear and attachment.' The cited plaintext appears unproofread: rāga/krodha lack expected visarga and vītā/vigatā likely stand for plural vītāḥ/vigatāḥ, but the yasmāt gloss makes the relation clear.**
- chosen: vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhaḥ = 'from whom attachment, fear, and anger have gone.' (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhaḥ rāga ca bhayaṃ ca krodha ca vītā vigatā yasmāt sa vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhaḥ |` — Vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhaḥ: attachment and fear and anger have gone, have departed, from whom—he is vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhaḥ.

**J4. Śaṅkara identifies sthita-dhīḥ with sthita-prajñaḥ and glosses muniḥ as saṃnyāsī. Therefore muni is not translated merely as 'silent one' or generic inspired seer; in this bhāṣya it has the renunciate sense, and sthita-dhī is the same technical condition as stable wisdom/understanding.**
- chosen: sthita-dhīḥ muniḥ = 'a sage/renunciate of steady wisdom.' (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `sthita-dhīḥ sthita-prajño muniḥ saṃnyāsī tadā ucyate ||bhgs_2.56||` — Sthita-dhīḥ means sthita-prajñaḥ; muniḥ means saṃnyāsī; then he is so called.

**J5. The form ucyate is present passive third singular from √vac; the nominatives describe the designation applied to the person meeting the stated conditions.**
- chosen: ucyate = 'is called/is said to be.' (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> When sufferings arise, his mind unagitated;
when pleasures arise, his craving gone;
with attachment, fear, and anger gone from him—
he is then called a sage-renunciate (muni), one of steady wisdom (sthita-dhī).

## One-shot delta

- Without the commentary one might render muni as a generic 'silent sage'; Śaṅkara specifically glosses it as saṃnyāsī, so the renunciate sense should be heard.
- A commentary-blind reading might take spṛhā as ordinary desire or pleasure; Śaṅkara glosses it as tṛṣṇā, craving, and stresses that pleasures do not make it increase.
- The compound vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhaḥ could be flattened ambiguously; Śaṅkara's yasmāt gloss shows a bahuvrīhi: attachment, fear, and anger have departed from him.
- The locatives duḥkheṣu and sukheṣu could be translated statically as 'in sorrow/in happiness'; Śaṅkara's repeated prāpteṣu makes the sense conditional: when sufferings or pleasures arise.
