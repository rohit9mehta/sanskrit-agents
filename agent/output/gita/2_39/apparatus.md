## Justifications

**J1. te is dative “to you,” abhihitā means “declared/said,” and buddhi is not merely a psychological faculty but jñāna, knowledge/understanding in the domain of discriminating ultimate reality.**
- chosen: eṣā te ’bhihitā sāṃkhye buddhiḥ = “this understanding has been declared to you in Sāṅkhya.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `eṣā te tubhyam abhihitā uktā sāṃkhye paramārtha-vastu-viveka-viṣaye buddhir jñānaṃ sākṣāt śoka-mohādi-saṃsāra-hetu-doṣa-nivṛtti-kāraṇam` — “This has been declared, said, to you; in Sāṅkhya, in the sphere of discriminating the ultimate real thing, buddhi is knowledge, the direct cause of the removal of the defect that is the cause of saṃsāra, beginning with grief and delusion.”

**J2. The feminine imām supplies buddhiṃ as object of śṛṇu. Yoga is construed as the means to the attainment of the preceding knowledge, specifically including non-attached performance of action as karma-yoga and also samādhi-yoga.**
- chosen: yoge tv imāṃ śṛṇu = “but in Yoga hear this [understanding now to be taught].” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `yoge tu tat-prāpty-upāye niḥsaṅgatayā dvandva-prahāṇa-pūrvakam īśvarārādhanārthe karma-yoge karmānuṣṭhāne samādhi-yoge cemām anantaram evocyamānāṃ buddhiṃ śṛṇu` — “But in Yoga, the means to the attainment of that, in karma-yoga—performance of action for the worship of the Lord, with non-attachment and preceded by abandoning the pairs of opposites—and also in samādhi-yoga, listen to this buddhi, about to be stated immediately hereafter.”

**J3. The instrumental buddhyā/yayā is construed with yuktaḥ, and the relevant buddhi is the yoga-directed one praised for encouragement.**
- chosen: buddhyā yayā yuktaḥ = “endowed with which understanding.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `tāṃ ca buddhiṃ stauti prarocanārtham -- buddhyā yayā yoga-viṣayayā yuktaḥ` — “And he praises that buddhi in order to make it attractive: endowed with the buddhi by which, which has Yoga as its subject.”

**J4. The compound is taken as a karmadhāraya/appositional compound: karma itself, called dharma and adharma, is the bandha.**
- chosen: karmabandham = “the bondage that is karma,” not merely “the bond produced by karma.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `karma-bandhaṃ karmaiva dharmādharmākhyo bandhaḥ karma-bandhas taṃ` — “karma-bandha: karma itself, called dharma and adharma, is bondage; that karma-bandha…”

**J5. The future verb is understood as the abandonment of that karma-bandha, ultimately through the obtaining of knowledge occasioned by the Lord’s grace.**

## Translation

> This understanding (buddhi) has been declared to you in Sāṅkhya (sāṃkhya);
but in Yoga (yoga), listen to this [understanding now to be taught].
Endowed with which understanding, O Pārtha,
you shall cast off the bondage that is karma (karmabandha).

## Analyzer disagreements

- te: ByT5 marked tvad_SG, plausibly genitive singular; Śaṅkara glosses te by tubhyam, so it is taken as Caturthi Eka, “to you”; Pum is supplied from the male addressee Pārtha, while the second-person form itself is not gender-distinct.
- abhihitā: ByT5 gave lemma abhidhā; the form is treated here as the feminine nominative singular past passive participle abhihita, confirmed by Śaṅkara’s uktā.
- śṛṇu: ByT5’s imperative tag is normalized to the requested lakāra label Lot.
- yuktaḥ: ByT5 lemmatized to yuj; for subanta analysis the nominal participial stem yukta is used, with a note that it is from √yuj.
- karmabandham: ByT5 segmented karma + bandham; Śaṅkara explicitly treats karma-bandhaḥ as a compound, so it is analyzed as one karmadhāraya compound.
- prahāsyasi: ByT5 gave prahā as the lemma; following the instruction to give the bare root separately from prefixes, it is analyzed as root hā with prefix pra.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might take te as possessive “your”; Śaṅkara’s tubhyam makes it dative, “to you.”
- It might render sāṃkhya as simple enumeration or unqualified later Sāṅkhya doctrine; Śaṅkara takes it as the sphere of discrimination of ultimate reality.
- It might make yoga generic “union” or meditation only; Śaṅkara defines it as the means to that attainment, including detached karma-yoga and samādhi-yoga.
- It might translate karmabandham as a genitive “bond of action” or “bond caused by action”; Śaṅkara says karma itself, under the names dharma and adharma, is the bond.
- It might miss that imām supplies buddhiṃ as the object of śṛṇu, since Śaṅkara explicitly supplies emām … buddhiṃ śṛṇu.
