## Justifications

**J1. The verb is translated with force because Śaṅkara glosses pra- as prakarṣeṇa and then gives parityajati, ‘abandons/renounces.’**
- chosen: prajahāti = “utterly abandons / gives up,” not merely “leaves” in a weak sense (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `prajahāti prakarṣeṇa jahāti parityajati` — ‘Prajahāti’ means ‘abandons emphatically, leaves, gives up.’

**J2. The object is not translated as ‘pleasures’ or external objects, because Śaṅkara defines kāmān as icchā-bhedān, varieties of desire.**
- chosen: kāmān = all desires, specifically differentiated forms of wishing (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `sarvān samastān kāmān icchā-bhedān` — ‘All’ means ‘entire’; ‘desires’ means ‘varieties of wishing.’

**J3. The compound is construed as manasi gatān, ‘gone into the mind,’ and Śaṅkara adds hṛdi praviṣṭān; hence ‘mind-borne’ alone would be too vague, and ‘mentally conceived’ would understate the sense of lodged internal desire.**
- chosen: manogatān = entered into / lodged in the mind, i.e. in the heart (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `mano-gatān manasi praviṣṭāan hṛdi praviṣṭān` — ‘Mano-gatān’ means ‘entered into the mind, entered into the heart.’

**J4. The locative ātmani is read not as the body or empirical personality but as the pratyag-ātman, the inward Self, because Śaṅkara glosses it as pratyag-ātma-svarūpa.**
- chosen: ātmani eva = in the Self alone, specifically the inner Self’s own nature (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `ātmany eva pratyag-ātma-svarūpe eva` — ‘In the Self alone’ means ‘in the very nature of the inward Self alone.’

**J5. The instrumental is construed as svenaiva, ‘by his own [self] alone,’ and the following phrase makes the contentment independent of any external gain.**
- chosen: ātmanā = by/with his own self alone; not by external acquisition (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `ātmanā svenaiva bāhya-lābha-nirapekṣas tuṣṭāḥ` — ‘By the self’ means ‘by his own alone, content without dependence on external acquisition.’

**J6. Śaṅkara answers the implied objection that, after abandoning all desires, there would be no cause of satisfaction; he explains a positive ground of satisfaction in realization, not mere dull passivity.**
- chosen: tuṣṭaḥ = content through the nectar-like savor of realizing the highest truth (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `sarva-kāma-parityāge tuṣṭi-kāraṇābhāvāt ... ity ata ucyate --- ātmany eva ... bāhya-lābha-nirapekṣas tuṣṭāḥ paramārtha-darśanāmṛta-rasa-lābhena` — ‘Since, when all desires are abandoned, there would seem to be no cause for satisfaction ... therefore it is said: content in the Self alone ... independent of external gain, through attaining the nectar-like savor of the vision of ultimate reality.’

**J7. The compound is a bahuvrīhi, not a simple karmadhāraya meaning ‘steady wisdom’ as an abstract quality. Śaṅkara defines it as a person possessing established wisdom born from discrimination of Self and non-Self, and identifies him as vidvān.**
- chosen: sthitaprajñaḥ = the wise person whose discriminative wisdom is established (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `sthita-prajñaḥ sthitā pratiṣṭhitā ātmānātma-vivekajā prajñā yasya saḥ sthita-prajñaḥ vidvān tadā ucyate` — ‘Sthita-prajña’ is he whose wisdom, born of discrimination between Self and non-Self, is steady, established; he is then called a wise person.’

**J8. The preceding Śaṅkara prose states that from this verse to the end of the chapter both the marks of the sthita-prajña and the means are being taught, since in spiritual scripture the marks of the fulfilled person are also taught as disciplines because they are attainable by effort.**
- chosen: The verse describes both the accomplished mark and the discipline to be cultivated (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2, 3]: `tyakta-putra-vitta-lokaiṣaṇaḥ saṃnyāsī ātmārāma ātma-krīḍāḥ sthita-prajña ity arthaḥ` — ‘The meaning is: the renunciant who has abandoned desire for sons, wealth, and worlds, delighting in the Self, sporting in the Self, is the sthita-prajña.’

## Translation

> The Blessed Lord said:

When, O Pārtha, one utterly abandons all desires
lodged in the mind,
and is content in the Self alone, by the Self itself—
then he is called one of steady wisdom (sthita-prajña).

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render kāmān as ‘objects of desire’ or ‘pleasures’; Śaṅkara instead glosses them as icchā-bhedān, varieties of desire themselves.
- It might take ātmany evātmanā as merely ‘satisfied with himself’; Śaṅkara makes it specifically the inward Self (pratyag-ātman) and independence from external gain.
- It might treat sthita-prajña as an adjective meaning simply ‘steady-minded’; Śaṅkara analyzes it as a bahuvrīhi: a knower whose wisdom born of Self/non-Self discrimination is established.
- It might miss Śaṅkara’s answer to the worry that abandonment of all desire would make embodied life impossible or mad; for him the contentment arises from the realization of ultimate reality.
