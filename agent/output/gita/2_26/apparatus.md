## Justifications

**J1. Force of atha ca at the opening.**
- chosen: ‘Now/even if, granting the point’ rather than a merely sequential ‘now/and’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `atha ca iti abhyupagamārthaḥ |` — ‘atha ca’ has the sense of an admission or concession.

**J2. Referent of enam.**
- chosen: enam refers to the already introduced self (ātman), not simply to the body or to Arjuna as an empirical person. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `enaṃ prakṛtam ātmānaṃ` — ‘this’, the self under discussion.

**J3. Sense of nitya-jātam and its compound construal.**
- chosen: nityajātam means ‘born again and again/continually born’ in connection with repeated bodily origination, according to ordinary worldly belief. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `enaṃ prakṛtam ātmānaṃ nitya-jātaṃ loka-prasiddhayā praty aneka-śarīrotpatti jāto jāta iti manyase |` — You think this self under discussion to be nitya-jāta: according to worldly understanding, with the origination of many bodies, ‘born, born’.

**J4. Construe nityam with mṛtam.**
- chosen: nityaṃ ... mṛtam is taken adverbially: ‘dead again and again/continually dead’ at each destruction, not as a single static eternal death. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [3]: `tathā prati-tat-tad-vināśaṃ nityaṃ vā manyase mṛtaṃ mṛto mṛta iti |` — Likewise, with respect to each such destruction, you think [it] constantly dead—‘dead, dead’.

**J5. Scope of tathāpi and force of na evaṃ śocitum arhasi.**
- chosen: Even on the conceded supposition that such birth and death belong to the self, grief is still not warranted; śocitum arhasi is therefore ‘you should/ought to grieve’, negated by na. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `tathāpi tathābhāve 'py ātmani tvaṃ mahābāho, na evaṃ śocitum arhasi, janmavato nāśo nāśavato janma cety etāv avaśyaṃbhāvināv iti ||bhgs_2.26||` — Even so, even if such were the case in the self, you, O mighty-armed, should not grieve thus; for destruction for what is born and birth for what has been destroyed are both inevitable.

**J6. Compound relation in mahābāho.**
- chosen: mahābāho is a bahuvrīhi vocative: ‘O one whose arms are great’, idiomatically ‘O mighty-armed one’. (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> Now, even if—granting the point—
you think this self (ātman) born again and again,
or dead again and again,
still, O mighty-armed one,
you should not grieve in this way.

## Analyzer disagreements

- ByT5 gives enam as enad_SAM; I revise the lemma to idam, masculine accusative singular, because the verifier rejects enad as deriving enam, while Śaṅkara’s construal of enam as an accusative referring to the prakṛta ātmā is retained.
- ByT5 splits nitya-jātam into nitya_Cp + jātam_jan_SAPaM; I analyze the printed/commented nitya-jātaṃ as one inflected compound stem nityajāta-, masculine accusative singular, because Śaṅkara quotes and glosses nitya-jātam as a unit in line 2.
- ByT5 gives mṛtam with the verbal root mṛ; I record the nominal participial stem mṛta- as the lemma, retaining masculine accusative singular, because the form is a predicate accusative past participle and Śaṅkara glosses mṛtam/mṛtaḥ in line 3.
- ByT5 splits mahā-bāho into mahā_Cp + bāho; I analyze the word as the whole compound stem mahābāhu-, vocative singular masculine, with bahuvrīhi vigraha.
- ByT5 gives tvam under tvad; I use the traditional personal-pronoun stem yuṣmad and supply masculine nominative singular by context, since Arjuna is addressed, while noting that the form itself is gender-common.

## One-shot delta

- Without Śaṅkara, atha ca might be rendered as a simple ‘and now’; the commentary makes it an admitted hypothesis, so the translation says ‘even if, granting the point’.
- A commentary-blind reading might let enam refer vaguely to the body or person; Śaṅkara fixes it as the prakṛta ātmā, the self under discussion.
- A bare translation ‘ever born or ever dead’ could sound like static eternal birth/death; Śaṅkara explains both as repeated with the origination and destruction of bodies.
- The final ‘do not grieve’ is not merely a repetition of the earlier eternal-self argument; Śaṅkara says it holds even if the birth-and-death premise is granted, because birth and destruction would then be inevitable.
