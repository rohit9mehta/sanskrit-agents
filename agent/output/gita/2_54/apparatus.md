## Justifications

**J1. sthita-prajña is not translated as an abstract “steady wisdom,” but as a person whose prajñā is firmly established, with Śaṅkara specifying that prajñā as the cognition “I am the supreme Brahman.”**
- chosen: bahuvrīhi: “one whose wisdom is established.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `sthitā pratiṣṭhitā aham asmi paraṃ brahma iti prajñā yasya saḥ sthita-prajñas tasya sthita-prajñasya` — “He whose wisdom—‘I am the supreme Brahman’—is steady, established: he is a sthita-prajña; of that sthita-prajña …”

**J2. bhāṣā is taken as the verbal description or defining characteristic by which such a person is spoken of, not merely as “language.”**
- chosen: “defining description/expression” for bhāṣā. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `kā bhāṣā kiṃ bhāṣaṇaṃ vacanaṃ katham asau parair bhāṣyate` — “What is his bhāṣā? What speaking, what utterance? How is he spoken of by others?”
- gita [4]: `sthita-prajñasya lakṣaṇam` — “the characteristic of the sthita-prajña.”

**J3. samādhi-sthasya is construed as samādhau sthitasya, with samādhi as the locus in which the person is established.**
- chosen: locative tatpuruṣa: “one established in samādhi.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `samādhi-sthasya samādhau sthitasya he keśava` — “O Keśava, of one established in samādhi—standing in samādhi.”

**J4. The second half continues to ask about the same realized person; sthita-dhīḥ is not a separate abstract “fixed thought.”**
- chosen: sthita-dhīḥ is the same person as the sthita-prajñaḥ, a masculine nominative bahuvrīhi. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `sthita-dhīḥ sthita-prajñaḥ svayaṃ vā kiṃ prabhāṣeta` — “sthita-dhīḥ means sthita-prajñaḥ; or, what would he himself say?”

**J5. The prabhāṣeta question is taken as concerning the sthita-prajña’s own utterance, contrasted with how he is spoken of by others in the bhāṣā question.**
- chosen: “What would he himself say?” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `katham asau parair bhāṣyate` — “how is he spoken of by others?”
- gita [2]: `svayaṃ vā kiṃ prabhāṣeta` — “or what would he himself say?”

**J6. For āsīta and vrajeta, kim is not taken as an object but as asking the manner or character of the person’s sitting and walking.**

## Translation

> Arjuna said:
O Keśava, what is the defining description (bhāṣā) of one whose wisdom is established (sthita-prajña),
of one abiding in samādhi (samādhi-stha)?
What would the steady-minded one (sthita-dhī) himself say?
How would he sit? How would he walk?

## Analyzer disagreements

- ByT5 splits sthita-prajñasya into sthita + prajñasya; Śaṅkara’s line 1 requires the compound stem sthita-prajña-, a masculine genitive singular bahuvrīhi.
- ByT5 splits samādhi-sthasya into samādhi + sthasya; Śaṅkara’s line 1 gloss samādhau sthitasya supports a locative tatpuruṣa compound samādhi-stha-, masculine genitive singular.
- ByT5 splits sthita-dhīḥ into sthita + dhīḥ; Śaṅkara’s line 2 equates it with sthita-prajñaḥ, so it is analysed as a masculine nominative singular bahuvrīhi compound.
- For the first kim, ByT5’s analysis as a neuter accusative singular interrogative is retained in function, but the lemma/stem is revised from ka to kim after the verifier showed that ka gives kam, not kim.
- ByT5 tags all three instances of kim as neuter subantas; the two associated with āsīta and vrajeta are taken as adverbial avyayas, because Śaṅkara glosses the question about sitting and walking with katham at line 3.
- For prabhāṣeta, ByT5 gives the prefixed lemma prabhāṣ; here the bare root is recorded as bhāṣ with prefix pra, per the required format.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render bhāṣā simply as “language” or “speech,” missing Śaṅkara’s sense: a defining description and how the person is spoken of by others.
- It might treat sthita-prajña and sthita-dhī as separate phrases or abstractions, whereas Śaṅkara reads both as personal bahuvrīhis and explicitly equates sthita-dhīḥ with sthita-prajñaḥ.
- It might translate all three kim forms uniformly as “what” or uniformly as “how”; Śaṅkara distinguishes the person’s own utterance (“what would he say?”) from the manner/nature of sitting and walking (“how?”).
- It might miss that Arjuna is asking for the lakṣaṇa, the recognizable characteristic, of the sthita-prajña.
