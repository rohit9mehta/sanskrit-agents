## Justifications

**J1. The bhāṣya explains the first member by instrumental plurals śrutibhiḥ śravaṇaiḥ and glosses vipratipannā as vikṣiptā satī. The line’s headword śruti-vipratinnā and nānā-pratinnā appear to be plaintext corruptions; the decision does not adopt those spellings.**
- chosen: śruti-vipratipannā is an instrumental tatpuruṣa: ‘distracted/confounded by śrutis/hearings,’ not ‘opposed to scripture.’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `śruti-vipratinnā aneka-sādhya-sādhana-saṃbandha-prakāśana-śrutibhiḥ śravaṇaiḥ pravṛtti-nivṛtti-lakṣaṇaiḥ vipratipannā nānā-pratinnā vikṣiptā satī te tava buddhir` — ‘śruti-vipratipannā’: by śrutis/hearings that reveal many relations of ends and means and are characterized by engagement and withdrawal; being vipratipannā, scattered; your buddhi.

**J2. Śaṅkara explicitly glosses te by tava and identifies buddhi as the item under discussion; therefore the phrase is ‘your buddhi/understanding.’ The gender value for te is not overt in the form and is supplied only to complete the nominal feature set.**
- chosen: te is genitive singular, ‘your,’ with Pum recorded for the male addressee; buddhiḥ is the subject. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `te tava buddhir` — te means tava, ‘your’; buddhi is the subject.
- gita [3]: `buddhir antaḥkaraṇam |` — buddhi is the inner organ.

**J3. The bhāṣya glosses sthāsyati as sthirībhūtā bhaviṣyati and niścalā as vikṣepa-calana-varjitā, so the steadiness is specifically freedom from scattering/agitation.**
- chosen: sthāsyati niścalā means ‘will stand, having become steady, free from the motion of distraction.’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `yadā yasmin kāle sthāsyati sthirībhūtā bhaviṣyati niścalā vikṣepa-calana-varjitā satī` — When, at which time, it will stand—will have become steady; motionless, being free from the motion of distraction.

**J4. Śaṅkara derives samādhi as that in which the mind is placed and then states that this samādhi is the Self; the locative therefore means ‘in that Self.’**
- chosen: samādhau is construed as ‘in samādhi, i.e. in the Self (ātman).’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `samādhau, samādhīyate cittam asminn iti samādhir ātmā, tasmin ātmani ity etat |` — In samādhi: ‘that in which the mind is placed’ is samādhi, the Self; that means ‘in that Self.’

**J5. The bhāṣya distinguishes acalā by tatrāpi vikalpa-varjitā, ‘even there devoid of alternatives/conceptual wavering.’**
- chosen: acalā adds ‘there too free from vikalpa,’ not merely a second word for motionless. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [2]: `acalā tatrāpi vikalpa-varjitā ity etat |` — acalā means ‘there too devoid of vikalpa.’

**J6. Śaṅkara explains yogam by viveka-prajñāṃ samādhiṃ and avāpsyasi by prāpsyasi, making the object not generic yoking but the attained state/knowledge.**
- chosen: yogam avāpsyasi means ‘you will attain yoga,’ glossed as discriminative insight and samādhi. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [4]: `tadā tasmin kāle yogam avāpsyasi viveka-prajñāṃ samādhiṃ prāpsyasi` — Then, at that time, you will attain yoga; you will attain discriminative insight, samādhi.

**J7. The unit’s final avāpsyasyi is treated as a typographical error: it is not the expected 2nd singular future form, while the bhāṣya has avāpsyasi and glosses it with prāpsyasi.**

- gita [4]: `yogam avāpsyasi viveka-prajñāṃ samādhiṃ prāpsyasi` — You will attain yoga; you will attain discriminative insight, samādhi.

## Translation

> When your understanding (buddhi), distracted by scriptural hearings (śruti),
shall stand motionless—free from scattering—in samādhi, that is, in the Self,
there too unmoving, without alternative thought,
then you will attain yoga: discriminative insight (viveka-prajñā), samādhi.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might take śruti-vipratipannā as ‘opposed to the Veda’; Śaṅkara instead makes the intellect scattered by many śrutis/hearings about means and ends.
- It might render samādhau only as ‘in trance/concentration’; Śaṅkara explicitly construes it here as ‘in the Self.’
- It might flatten niścalā and acalā into two identical adjectives; the bhāṣya distinguishes freedom from distracting motion and freedom from vikalpa.
- It might translate yoga generically as ‘union’ or ‘practice’; Śaṅkara glosses it as viveka-prajñā and samādhi.
- It might reproduce the final typo avāpsyasyi; the grammatical and commentarial reading is avāpsyasi.
