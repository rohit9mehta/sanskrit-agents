## Justifications

**J1. The compound buddhi-yuktaḥ is not a generic epithet. Śaṅkara construes it as one joined with a buddhi whose specific viṣaya is karma-samatva, equality/equanimity regarding action; line 4 restates the verse’s instruction as becoming samatva-buddhi-yukta.**
- chosen: “Joined with the understanding whose object is equanimity,” not merely “intelligent” or “wise.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `buddhi-yuktaḥ karma-samatva-viṣayayā buddhyā yukto buddhi-yuktaḥ` — “buddhi-yuktaḥ: joined with buddhi whose object is equality with respect to action—that is buddhi-yuktaḥ.”
- gita [4]: `tasmāt samatva-buddhi-yukto bhava tvam` — “Therefore become one joined with the understanding of equanimity.”

**J2. Śaṅkara glosses jahāti by parityajati, iha by asmin loke, and sukṛta-duṣkṛte by puṇya-pāpe. The abandonment is therefore not simply “stopping good and bad deeds,” but relinquishing both karmic opposites, through purification and attainment of knowledge.**
- chosen: The object abandoned is both merit and demerit/sin, here in this world. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `saḥ jahāti parityajati iha asmin loke ubhe sukṛta-duṣkṛte puṇya-pāpe sattva-śuddhi-jñāna-prāpti-dvāreṇa` — “He abandons, gives up, here in this world, both sukṛta and duṣkṛta—merit and sin—through the means of purification of sattva and attainment of knowledge.”

**J3. The dative yogāya is not left as an undefined “to yoga.” Śaṅkara expands it to samatva-buddhi-yogāya and glosses yujyasva as ghaṭāsva, so the imperative is rendered idiomatically as “apply yourself/strive,” not as a merely physical “be yoked.”**
- chosen: “Apply yourself to yoga,” with yoga specified as samatva-buddhi-yoga. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- gita [1]: `tasmāt samatva-buddhi-yogāya yujyasva ghaṭāsva` — “Therefore apply yourself, strive, for the yoga of the understanding of equanimity.”

**J4. Śaṅkara prevents reading kauśalam as ordinary clever efficiency. He defines it as kuśala-bhāva consisting in samatva-buddhi toward siddhi and asiddhi, in the context of sva-dharma actions and īśvarārpita-cetastā.**

## Translation

> Joined with the understanding (buddhi) whose field is equanimity, one abandons here in this world both merit and demerit. Therefore apply yourself to yoga—the discipline of equal understanding (samatva-buddhi-yoga); yoga is skillfulness (kauśala) in actions, the actions of one’s own duty.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render buddhi-yuktaḥ simply as “wise” or “intelligent,” missing Śaṅkara’s specific “understanding whose object is equality in action.”
- It might take sukṛta-duṣkṛte as merely “good and bad deeds”; Śaṅkara glosses the pair as puṇya-pāpa, merit and sin/demerit, abandoned through purification and knowledge.
- It might read yujyasva literally as “be yoked” without the idiomatic force “apply yourself/strive,” supplied by ghaṭāsva.
