- ys [1]: `aśuddhyāvaraṇamalāpetasya prakāśātmano buddhisattvasya rajastamobhyām anabhibhūtaḥ svacchaḥ sthitipravāho vaiśāradyam.` — Vaiśāradya is the transparent steady-flow of the luminous buddhisattva, free from the impurity-covering stain and not overpowered by rajas and tamas.

**J2. The compound is construed as a genitive tatpuruṣa, with nirvicāra qualifying samādhi; the sūtra’s locative is temporal/conditional, not simply a static location.**
- chosen: nirvicāravaiśāradye = ‘when lucidity in/of the nirvicāra samādhi has arisen’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [2]: `yadā nirvicārasya samādher vaiśāradyam idaṃ jāyate tadā` — When this vaiśāradya of the nirvicāra samādhi arises, then ...

**J3. The second compound is not taken as ‘grace of the Supreme Spirit’ or external favor. Vyāsa says it comes to the yogin and qualifies it by its real-object scope and clarity.**
- chosen: adhyātmaprasādaḥ as the yogin’s inner clarity, a clear cognition concerning things as they are (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [2]: `tadā yogino bhavaty adhyātmaprasādo bhūtārthaviṣayaḥ kramānanurodhī sphuṭaḥ` — Then the yogin has adhyātmaprasāda, whose object is the real thing, not dependent on sequence, distinct/clear.

**J4. The sūtra has no finite verb; the commentary supplies jāyate for the condition and bhavati for the result, so the translation uses ‘has arisen ... comes to the yogin.’**
- chosen: supply the finite sense as ‘comes to be/arises’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [2]: `jāyate tadā yogino bhavaty adhyātmaprasādaḥ` — [It] arises; then adhyātmaprasāda comes to be for the yogin.

## Translation

> When lucidity (vaiśāradya) has arisen in the nirvicāra samādhi, inner clarity (adhyātma-prasāda) comes to the yogin.
