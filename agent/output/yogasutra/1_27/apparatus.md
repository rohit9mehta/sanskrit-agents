## Justifications

**J1. The genitive pronoun is not left as an unspecified ‘of that’; Vyāsa explicitly states that Īśvara is the vācya, the object denoted by praṇava. Hence the translation supplies ‘[Īśvara]’ after ‘him.’**
- chosen: tasya = ‘of him,’ i.e. of Īśvara (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `vācya īśvaraḥ praṇavasya.` — Īśvara is the denoted object of praṇava.

**J2. Although vācaka can mean ‘speaker’ in other contexts, here the bhāṣya repeatedly frames the term in the technical pair vācya/vācaka, ‘denoted object/designator.’ Therefore ‘designator’ is required.**
- chosen: vācakaḥ = ‘designator/signifier’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [2, 3]: `kim asya saṃketakṛtaṃ vācyavācakatvam atha pradīpaprakāśavad avasthitam iti. sthito 'sya vācyasya vācakena saha saṃbandhaḥ.` — Is this relation of denoted object and designator made by convention, or is it established like lamp and light? The relation of this denoted object with its designator is established.
- ys [6]: `sargāntareṣv api vācyavācakaśaktyapekṣas tathaiva saṃketaḥ kriyate.` — Even in other creations, convention is made in the same way with regard to the powers of denoted object and designator.

**J3. Line 2 raises the alternative that the vācya-vācaka relation might be saṃketakṛta, ‘made by convention’; line 3 answers that the relation is sthita, established. The father-son analogy and the appeal to nityatva reinforce that convention manifests or recognizes an already established relation rather than inventing it.**
- chosen: The praṇava is not merely an arbitrary conventional name for Īśvara; its word-object relation is treated as established. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [2, 3]: `kim asya saṃketakṛtaṃ vācyavācakatvam atha pradīpaprakāśavad avasthitam iti. sthito 'sya vācyasya vācakena saha saṃbandhaḥ.` — Is this relation of denoted object and designator made by convention, or is it established like lamp and light? The relation of this denoted object with its designator is established.
- ys [5]: `yathāvasthitaḥ pitṛputrayoḥ saṃbandhaḥ saṃketenāvadyotyate, ayam asya pitā, ayam asya putra iti.` — Just as the relation of father and son, already existing, is illuminated by convention: ‘this is his father, this is his son.’
- ys [7]: `saṃpratipattinityatayā nityaḥ śabdārthasaṃbandha ity āgaminaḥ pratijānate.` — The āgamins maintain that the relation of word and meaning is eternal because common recognition is eternal.

**J4. The identification of praṇava as the sacred syllable Oṃ is lexical/traditional, supported by MW s.v. praṇava, 660,3 (‘the mystical or sacred syllable om’). The commentary does not define the sound-form here, so this choice does not depend on the cited bhāṣya lines.**
- chosen: praṇavaḥ = ‘the praṇava (Oṃ)’ (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> Of him [Īśvara], the designator is the praṇava (Oṃ).

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind rendering might translate vācakaḥ as ‘speaker’ or ‘one who utters’; the bhāṣya’s vācya/vācaka discussion shows it means ‘designator/signifier.’
- It might leave tasya vague as ‘of that’; the bhāṣya fixes the referent as Īśvara.
- It might treat Oṃ as a merely conventional label; Vyāsa explicitly chooses an established word-object relation over a merely convention-made one.
