**J8. Avirati is not merely lack of restraint in the abstract; Vyāsa identifies it as craving whose nature is the mind’s conjunction with objects.**

**J9. The compound bhrāntidarśana is translated by its technical gloss ‘erroneous cognition,’ not as physical wandering of sight.**

**J10. The last two terms are taken specifically with respect to the stages/grounds of samādhi: first not attaining such a ground, then not remaining established in one that has been attained.**
- chosen: alabdhabhūmikatva and anavasthitatva as samādhi-stage non-attainment and instability (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [11]: `alabdhabhūmikatvaṃ samādhibhūmer alābhaḥ.` — Non-attainment of a stage is failure to obtain the ground of samādhi.
- ys [12]: `anavasthitatvaṃ yal labdhāyāṃ bhūmau cittasyāpratiṣṭhā.` — Instability is that non-establishment of the mind in a ground that has been attained.
- ys [13]: `samādhipratilambhe hi sati tadavasthitaṃ syād iti.` — For when samādhi has been obtained, the mind would be established in it.

## Translation

> Disease (vyādhi), mental stagnation (styāna), doubt (saṃśaya), heedlessness (pramāda), sloth (ālasya), non-abstention or craving (avirati), false seeing (bhrāntidarśana), non-attainment of a stage (alabdhabhūmikatva), and instability (anavasthitatva): these are distractions of the mind (cittavikṣepāḥ); they are the obstacles (antarāyāḥ).
