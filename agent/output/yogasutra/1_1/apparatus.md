## Justifications

**J1. Force of atha in the sūtra**
- chosen: atha is a topic-opening/undertaking marker: “Now:” in the sense of opening the adhikāra. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `athety ayam adhikārārthaḥ.` — “The word atha has the sense of adhikāra.”

**J2. Scope of yogānuśāsanam**
- chosen: yogānuśāsanam denotes the śāstra of authoritative instruction on yoga now being undertaken. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [2]: `yogānuśāsanaṃ śāstram adhikṛtaṃ veditavyam.` — “The yogānuśāsana is to be understood as the śāstra that has been taken up as the topic.”

**J3. Technical sense of yoga**
- chosen: Yoga (yoga) is rendered technically as samādhi, not as generic yoking or union. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [3]: `yogaḥ samādhiḥ.` — “Yoga is samādhi.”
- ys [4]: `sa ca sārvabhaumaś cittasya dharmaḥ.` — “And that is a property of the mind in all its grounds.”

**J4. Technical scope of samādhi/yoga after the opening sūtra**
- chosen: The yoga to be expounded is not any distracted concentration; it is the samādhi that illumines a real object, attenuates kleśas, loosens karmic bonds, turns toward nirodha, and also the asaṃprajñāta samādhi of total cessation of vṛttis. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [6]: `tatra vikṣipte cetasi vikṣepopasarjanībhūtaḥ samādhir na yogapakṣe vartate.` — “There, in a distracted mind, a samādhi subordinated to distraction does not fall within the class of yoga.”
- ys [7]: `yas tv ekāgre cetasi sadbhūtam arthaṃ pradyotayati kṣiṇoti ca kleśān karmabandhanāni ślathayati nirodham abhimukhaṃ karoti sa saṃprajñāto yoga ity ākhyāyate.` — “But that which, in a one-pointed mind, makes a real object shine forth, destroys the kleśas, loosens the bonds of karma, and turns toward cessation is called saṃprajñāta yoga.”
- ys [9]: `sarvavṛttinirodhe tv asaṃprajñātaḥ samādhiḥ.` — “But when all vṛttis have ceased, it is asaṃprajñāta samādhi.”

**J5. Prayer syntax of the maṅgala verse**
- chosen: saḥ … vaḥ avyāt is the benedictory frame: “may that god protect you,” with avyāt from √av, not √vī. (depends on commentary: no)

**J6. Antecedent of yasya in the verse**
- chosen: yasya prītaye is taken with jagataḥ: the deity manifests for the world’s manifold benefit and for its delight. (depends on commentary: no)

**J7. Compound relation in prakṣīṇakleśarāśiḥ**
- chosen: prakṣīṇakleśarāśiḥ is a bahuvrīhi epithet: “one whose mass of kleśas is exhausted.” (depends on commentary: no)

**J8. Gender and function of sarvajñānaprasūtiḥ**

## Translation

> At the beginning he manifests for the world’s benefit in many ways—his mass of afflictions worn away, bearing dread poison, many-mouthed, fair-coiled; source of all knowledge, with serpents for his retinue, always for its delight. May that god, the Lord of Serpents, white and stainless in body, giver of yoga, joined to yoga, protect you.

Now: the authoritative instruction on yoga (yoga, i.e. samādhi).
