## Justifications

**J1. The sūtra compound is not merely ‘a passionless object’ nor ‘an object belonging to a passionless person’; Vyāsa takes the yogin’s citta to be colored by the ālambana consisting of vītarāga-citta. Thus viṣaya is read as meditative object/support, and the whole compound is an adjective of cittam.**
- chosen: vītarāgaviṣayaṃ = ‘whose object/support is the mind of one free from rāga’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `vītarāgacittālambanoparaktaṃ vā yoginaś cittaṃ sthitipadaṃ labhata iti.` — Or, the yogin’s mind, tinged by the support consisting of the mind of one free from rāga, attains the state of steadiness. The printed labhata is taken as the expected labhate.

**J2. Although cittam is formally also accusative, the bhāṣya explicitly construes yoginaś cittaṃ as what attains sthitipada. Therefore the sūtra is elliptical: ‘the mind [becomes steady/attains steadiness]’, not an instruction with cittam as an object such as ‘meditate on the mind’.**
- chosen: cittam is nominative singular, the subject of the implied result (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `yoginaś cittaṃ sthitipadaṃ labhata iti.` — The yogin’s mind attains the state of steadiness; labhata is printed, probably for labhate.

**J3. The bhāṣya repeats vā in its paraphrase, so the particle continues the series of optional supports for stabilizing the mind.**
- chosen: vā = ‘or’, introducing another alternative means (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `vītarāgacittālambanoparaktaṃ vā` — Or, tinged by the support of the mind of one free from rāga.

**J4. In vītarāga, rāga is taken in its affective yogic sense, so vītarāga means ‘free from attachment/passion’, not simply ‘devoid of color/redness’. The commentary repeats the technical compound but does not define rāga here, so this decision rests on grammar and standard yogic usage rather than a fresh gloss in this line.**
- chosen: rāga = attachment/passion (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> Or [steadiness comes for] the mind whose object is the mind of one free from attachment (vītarāga).
