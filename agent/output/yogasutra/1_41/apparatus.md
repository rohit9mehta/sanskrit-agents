## Justifications

**J1. The form is taken morphologically as a feminine genitive singular karmadhāraya, while Vyāsa’s artha-gloss and later mention of cetas determine that the relevant bearer is the mind.**
- chosen: Grammatically, kṣīṇavṛtteḥ is ‘of the subsided vṛtti’; in the sūtra’s commentarial sense, it describes the mind whose vṛtti/pratyaya has subsided. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `kṣīṇavṛtter iti pratyastamitapratyayasyety arthaḥ.` — ‘kṣīṇavṛtti’ means ‘whose pratyaya has set/subsided’ or ‘of the subsided pratyaya.’
- ys [11]: `abhijātamaṇikalpasya cetaso` — Vyāsa explicitly supplies the referent as ‘of the mind comparable to a fine jewel.’

**J2. The phrase is not an additional doctrinal subject; Vyāsa marks it as a dṛṣṭānta and explains its point through the crystal’s taking on the color/form of its support.**
- chosen: abhijātasya maṇer iva is an illustrative simile of a clear crystal-jewel. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [2]: `abhijātasyeva maṇer iti dṛṣṭāntopādānam.` — ‘Like a fine jewel’ is the introduction of an example.
- ys [3]: `yathā sphaṭika upāśrayabhedāt tattadrūpoparakta upāśrayarūpākāreṇa nirbhāsate tathā grāhyālambanoparaktaṃ cittaṃ grāhyasamāpannaṃ grāhyasvarūpākāreṇa nirbhāsate.` — As a crystal, depending on different supports, is colored by this or that form and appears in the form of the support, so the mind colored by a graspable support appears in the graspable’s own form.

**J3. The compound is taken as a locative plural dvandva naming the possible supports in which the mind’s samāpatti occurs.**
- chosen: grahītṛ-grahaṇa-grāhya denotes the triad: grasper/puruṣa, grasping-instruments/indriyas, and graspable objects/bhūtas. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [11]: `grahītṛgrahaṇagrāhyeṣu puruṣendriyabhūteṣu` — Vyāsa glosses ‘in the grasper, grasping, and graspable’ as ‘in puruṣa, senses, and elements.’
- ys [7]: `tathā grahaṇeṣv apīndriyeṣv api draṣṭavyam.` — In the case of grahaṇas, it is to be understood with respect to the senses too.
- ys [9]: `tathā grahītṛpuruṣālambanoparaktaṃ grahītṛpuruṣasamāpannaṃ grahītṛpuruṣasvarūpākāreṇa nirbhāsate.` — When colored by the support that is the grahītṛ-puruṣa, it has entered into the grahītṛ-puruṣa and appears in that form.

**J4. tat-stha is read as ‘situated in them’ and tad-añjanatā as taking on their form/color, not literal smearing or collyrium. The compound relation is represented as tatsthasya tadañjanatā, following Vyāsa’s teṣu sthitasya tadākārāpattiḥ.**
- chosen: tatsthatadañjanatā is ‘the assumption of their form by what is situated in them.’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [11]: `yā tatsthatadañjanatā teṣu sthitasya tadākārāpattiḥ sā samāpattir ity ucyate.` — That tat-stha-tad-añjanatā is the assumption of that form by what is situated in them; that is called samāpatti.
- ys [8]: `grahaṇālambanoparaktaṃ grahaṇasamāpannaṃ grahaṇasvarūpākāreṇa nirbhāsate.` — Colored by a grahaṇa-support and coalesced with grahaṇa, it appears in the form of grahaṇa.

**J5. Vyāsa repeatedly explains samāpatti with samāpanna and svarūpākāreṇa nirbhāsate: the mind, colored by a support, appears in that support’s own form. The sūtra’s predicate defines this as samāpatti.**
- chosen: samāpatti is rendered as cognitive ‘coalescence’ or engrossed conformity, not as accident or mere meeting. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [3]: `grāhyālambanoparaktaṃ cittaṃ grāhyasamāpannaṃ grāhyasvarūpākāreṇa nirbhāsate.` — The mind, colored by a graspable support and coalesced with the graspable, appears in the graspable’s own form.
- ys [11]: `sā samāpattir ity ucyate.` — That is called samāpatti.

## Translation

> For the mind whose fluctuation (vṛtti) has subsided, as with a clear crystal-jewel, coalescence (samāpatti) is its taking on their form while it is situated among the grasper (grahītṛ), the grasping-instruments (grahaṇa), and the graspables (grāhya).
