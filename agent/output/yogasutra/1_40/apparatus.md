## Justifications

**J1. The compound is read as a bahuvrīhi adjective of vaśīkāraḥ: ‘whose two ends are the atom and supreme magnitude.’ The bhāṣya explicitly splits the range into subtle and gross, with paramāṇu as one end and paramamahattva as the other, then calls them ‘both limits’ (ubhayī koṭi).**
- chosen: paramāṇu and parama-mahattva are the two limiting extremes of the mastery, not a single object called ‘atom-supreme-greatness’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `sūkṣme niviśamānasya paramāṇvantaṃ sthitipadaṃ labhata iti.` — When it enters into the subtle, it obtains a station of steadiness ending in the atom.
- ys [2]: `sthūle niviśamānasya paramamahattvāntaṃ sthitipadaṃ cittasya.` — When it enters into the gross, there is for the mind a station of steadiness ending in supreme magnitude.
- ys [3]: `evaṃ tām ubhayīṃ koṭim anudhāvato yo 'syāpratīghātaḥ sa paro vaśīkāraḥ.` — Thus, as it runs along both those limits, its non-obstruction is supreme mastery.

## Translation

> For this mind, mastery (vaśīkāra) has as its limits the atom and supreme magnitude.

## Analyzer disagreements

- ByT5 segmented mahā_tva as if tva were an independent compound-member; here the relevant form is mahattva, the abstract noun ‘magnitude/greatness,’ in paramamahattva, supported by the bhāṣya’s paramamahattvāntam (line 2).
- ByT5 gave asya as idam_SGM without fixing gender; I specify neuter genitive singular because the bhāṣya identifies the referent as citta/cetas: cittasya (line 2), yoginaś cittaṃ (line 4).

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might take asya as referring mainly to the yogin—‘his mastery’—whereas Vyāsa makes the immediate referent the citta/cetas.
- It might flatten paramāṇuparamamahattvāntaḥ into a vague ‘from small to large’; the bhāṣya specifies two extremes: the subtle limit paramāṇu and the gross limit paramamahattva.
- It might render vaśīkāraḥ as mere coercive control; the bhāṣya defines it more precisely as apratighāta, non-obstruction, of the mind as it ranges over both limits.
