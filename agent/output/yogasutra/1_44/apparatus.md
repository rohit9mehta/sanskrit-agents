## Justifications

**J1. The instrumental pronoun is not left as a vague ‘by this’; Vyāsa explicitly supplies nirvitarkayā, so the sūtra points back to the preceding nirvitarkā explanation/samāpatti as the model by which the present two are explained.**
- chosen: etayā eva = ‘by this very [nirvitarkā]’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [7]: `evam ubhayor etayaiva nirvitarkayā vikalpahānir vyākhyāteti.` — Thus, for both, by this very nirvitarkā, the removal of vikalpa has been explained.

**J2. The term is a single compound adjective qualifying an understood samāpattiḥ. Vyāsa defines it not as ordinary reflection in general, but as samāpatti concerning subtle elements that have manifest dharmas delimited by place, time, cause, and experience, with an arisen-dharma-qualified subtle element standing as the object of samādhi-prajñā.**
- chosen: savicārā as the technical ‘with-vicāra’ samāpatti (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1, 2]: `tatra bhūtasūkṣmakeṣv abhivyaktadharmakeṣu deśakālanimittānubhavāvacchinneṣu yā samāpattiḥ sā savicārety ucyate. tatrāpy ekabuddhinirgrāhyam evoditadharmaviśiṣṭaṃ bhūtasūkṣmam ālambanībhūtaṃ samādhiprajñāyām upatiṣṭhate.` — There, the samāpatti in subtle elements whose dharmas are manifest and delimited by place, time, cause, and experience is called savicārā. Even there, the subtle element qualified by an arisen dharma, to be grasped by a single cognition, stands present in samādhi-prajñā as the object.

**J3. The negative compound is read privatively and technically. Vyāsa defines nirvicārā through a subtler object-presentation not delimited by the mentioned dharmas, and finally through prajñā becoming as if empty of its own form and only the object.**
- chosen: nirvicārā as the technical ‘without-vicāra’ samāpatti (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [3, 4, 5]: `yā punaḥ sarvathā sarvataḥ śāntoditāvyapadeśyadharmānavacchinneṣu sarvadharmānupātiṣu sarvadharmātmakeṣu samāpattiḥ sā nirvicārety ucyate. evaṃsvarūpaṃ hi tadbhūtasūkṣmam etenaiva svarūpeṇālambanībhūtam eva samādhiprajñāsvarūpam uparañjayati. prajñā ca svarūpaśūnyevārthamātrā yadā bhavati tadā nirvicārety ucyate.` — But the samāpatti in those [subtle elements] which are in every way and on every side not delimited by quiescent, arisen, and indeterminable dharmas, which follow all dharmas and consist in all dharmas, is called nirvicārā. For that subtle element of this nature, becoming the object in just that form, colors the very form of samādhi-prajñā. And when prajñā becomes as if empty of its own form and only the object, then it is called nirvicārā.

**J4. The compound is construed externally as a bahuvrīhi qualifying the understood samāpattiḥ: its object-field is subtle. Vyāsa explicitly contrasts the earlier savitarkā/nirvitarkā as mahadvastuviṣayā with the present savicārā/nirvicārā as sūkṣmavastuviṣayā.**
- chosen: sūkṣmaviṣayā = ‘having subtle objects’ and applying to both savicārā and nirvicārā (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [6]: `tatra mahadvastuviṣayā savitarkā nirvitarkā ca, sūkṣmavastuviṣayā savicārā nirvicārā ca.` — There, the savitarkā and nirvitarkā have gross/large things as their object; the savicārā and nirvicārā have subtle things as their object.

**J5. Although the sūtra’s predicate is feminine singular, the scope is not just one item: Vyāsa says ubhayor, ‘for both’. He also clarifies that what is transferred from the nirvitarkā account is especially the removal of vikalpa.**
- chosen: vyākhyātā carries the explanation over to both subtle-object forms, specifically regarding vikalpa-hāni (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [7]: `evam ubhayor etayaiva nirvitarkayā vikalpahānir vyākhyāteti.` — Thus, for both, by this very nirvitarkā, the removal of vikalpa has been explained.

## Translation

> By this very [nirvitarkā], the with-vicāra (savicārā) and the without-vicāra (nirvicārā) [samāpatti]—whose objects are subtle—have been explained.
