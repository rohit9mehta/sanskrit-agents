## Justifications

**J1. Vyāsa first gives smell-cognition at the nose-tip and explicitly calls it gandha-pravṛtti; he then lists rasa-, rūpa-, sparśa-, and śabda-saṃvit and sums them up as vṛttayaḥ. Hence the English ‘cognitive operation’ is chosen for pravṛtti.**
- chosen: pravṛttiḥ = a cognitive/mental operation, exemplified by extraordinary sensory cognitions, not merely external activity or undertaking. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `nāsikāgre dhārayato 'sya yā divyagandhasaṃvit sā gandhapravṛttiḥ.` — For one holding [concentration] at the tip of the nose, the cognition of divine smell is smell-pravṛtti.
- ys [2, 3, 4, 5]: `jihvāgre rasasaṃvit. tāluni rūpasaṃvit. jihvāmadhye sparśasaṃvit. jihvāmūle śabdasaṃvid ity etā vṛttaya utpannāś cittaṃ sthitau nibadhnanti` — At the tip of the tongue there is taste-cognition; at the palate form-cognition; in the middle of the tongue touch-cognition; at the root of the tongue sound-cognition—these vṛttis, when arisen, bind the mind-stuff in steadiness.

**J2. The adjective is not taken pejoratively as ‘attached to sense-objects’. Vyāsa says that other such pravṛttis too are to be understood as viṣayavatī, and his examples specify definite contents—smell, taste, form, touch, sound, and even objects connected with the moon, sun, planets, jewels, lamps, and rays.**
- chosen: viṣayavatī = object-bearing, furnished with a determinate object-content. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]: `divyagandhasaṃvit ... rasasaṃvit ... rūpasaṃvit ... sparśasaṃvit ... śabdasaṃvid` — Cognition of divine smell, taste-cognition, form-cognition, touch-cognition, and sound-cognition are the named contents.
- ys [6]: `etena candrādityagrahamaṇipradīparaśmyādiṣu pravṛttir utpannā viṣayavaty eva veditavyā` — By this, an arisen pravṛtti concerning the moon, sun, planets, jewels, lamps, rays, and so on is to be understood precisely as viṣayavatī.

**J3. Vyāsa repeats the same construction with vṛttayaḥ utpannāḥ and with pravṛttiḥ utpannā, showing that the word qualifies the operation after its arising rather than functioning as a separate finite predicate.**
- chosen: utpannā is a past participial qualifier of pravṛttiḥ: ‘once arisen’ / ‘having arisen’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [5]: `ity etā vṛttaya utpannāś cittaṃ sthitau nibadhnanti` — These vṛttis, having arisen, bind the mind-stuff in steadiness.
- ys [6]: `pravṛttir utpannā viṣayavaty eva veditavyā` — An arisen pravṛtti is to be understood as object-bearing.

**J4. The compound is read as ‘binding in sthiti’, not as ‘a bond that is steadiness’ or ‘a steady bond’. Vyāsa’s direct paraphrase has cittaṃ as the object and sthitau as the locus: cittaṃ sthitau nibadhnanti. This also establishes manasaḥ as corresponding to cittaṃ, the mind to be fixed.**
- chosen: manasaḥ sthitinibandhanī = ‘a fixer/binder of the mind in steadiness’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [5]: `etā vṛttaya utpannāś cittaṃ sthitau nibadhnanti` — These arisen vṛttis bind the mind-stuff in steadiness.

**J5. Because nibandhanī can sound like ‘binding’ in a negative sense, the translation adds ‘fixer’ alongside ‘binder’. Vyāsa says these vṛttis dispel doubt and become doorways to samādhi-prajñā; they are prescribed as cittaparikarma to strengthen direct conviction.**
- chosen: The effect is positive meditative stabilization, not a fettering distraction. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [5]: `saṃśayaṃ vidhamanti, samādhiprajñāyāṃ ca dvārībhavantīti.` — They dispel doubt and become doorways to insight born of samādhi.
- ys [8, 10, 12]: `tasmāc chāstrānumānācāryopadeśopodbalanārtham evāvaśyaṃ kaścid arthaviśeṣaḥ pratyakṣīkartavyaḥ. etadartham evedaṃ cittaparikarma nirdiśyate. tathā ca sati śraddhāvīryasmṛtisamādhayo 'syāpratibandhena bhaviṣyantīti.` — Therefore some particular object must necessarily be made direct for strengthening scripture, inference, and the teacher’s instruction. For just this purpose this purification/training of the mind is taught. When this is so, faith, energy, memory, and samādhi will arise for him without obstruction.

**J6. The commentary gives no special gloss for vā here; the choice rests on ordinary Sanskrit grammar and the sūtra’s sequence of alternative practices.**
- chosen: vā = ‘or’, marking this as an alternative stabilizing means. (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> Or, an arisen object-bearing cognitive operation (viṣayavatī pravṛtti) is a binder/fixer of the mind (manas) in steadiness (sthiti).

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render pravṛtti as ordinary ‘activity’ or ‘engagement’; Vyāsa instead treats it as a specific cognition/mental operation such as divine smell-cognition.
- It might take viṣayavatī as ‘sensuous’ or as attachment to sense-objects; the bhāṣya makes it a determinate, even yogically direct, object-bearing cognition.
- It might read sthitinibandhanī as a generic ‘cause of continuance’ or as negative ‘bondage’; Vyāsa glosses it as fixing citta in steadiness and as dispelling doubt.
- It might miss that manasaḥ corresponds to the citta being bound/fixed, because Vyāsa paraphrases the construction with cittaṃ sthitau nibadhnanti.
