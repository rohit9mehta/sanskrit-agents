## Justifications

**J1. abhyāsavairāgyābhyām is an instrumental dual dvandva, not a loose list or an alternative pair; the cessation is dependent on both.**
- chosen: “by practice and dispassion,” construed as two jointly operative means (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [4, 5]: `tatra vairāgyeṇa viṣayasrotaḥ khilīkriyate. vivekadarśanābhyāsena vivekasrota udghāṭyata ity ubhayādhīnaś cittavṛttinirodhaḥ.` — There, by dispassion the stream toward objects is made barren; by the practice of discriminative seeing the stream of discrimination is opened; thus the cessation of mental fluctuations is dependent on both. The printed udghāṭyata is taken as the intended passive udghāṭyate.

**J2. tan-nirodhaḥ refers to citta-vṛtti-nirodha, the cessation of the mental fluctuations, not to the cessation of practice/dispassion or of the two streams themselves.**
- chosen: “the cessation of those [mental fluctuations of consciousness]” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [5]: `ity ubhayādhīnaś cittavṛttinirodhaḥ.` — Thus the cessation of mental fluctuations is dependent on both.

**J3. The terms are translated neutrally as “practice” and “dispassion,” but their operative senses here are fixed by Vyāsa’s river metaphor: dispassion checks the viṣaya-current, while practice opens the viveka-current.**
- chosen: abhyāsa as practice oriented to discriminative seeing; vairāgya as blocking the object-current (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1, 2, 3]: `cittanadī nāmobhayatovāhinī vahati kalyāṇāya vahati pāpāya ca. yā tu kaivalyaprāgbhārā vivekaviṣayanimnā sā kalyāṇavahā. saṃsāraprāgbhārāvivekaviṣayaniṃnā pāpavahā.` — The river called consciousness flows in both directions: it flows toward good and toward evil. That which slopes toward kaivalya and has discrimination as its field is good-bearing. That which slopes toward saṃsāra and has non-discrimination as its field is evil-bearing.
- ys [4, 5]: `tatra vairāgyeṇa viṣayasrotaḥ khilīkriyate. vivekadarśanābhyāsena vivekasrota udghāṭyata` — There, by dispassion the stream toward objects is made barren; by the practice of discriminative seeing the stream of discrimination is opened.

## Translation

> Through practice (abhyāsa) and dispassion (vairāgya), there is the cessation (nirodha) of those [mental fluctuations of consciousness].

## Analyzer disagreements

- ByT5 gives only the lexical split abhyāsa + vairāgya, but the printed unit has one dual ending; I analyze abhyāsavairāgyābhyām as a single itaretara-dvandva subanta, Trtīyā Dvivacana, supported by Vyāsa’s vairāgyeṇa ... abhyāsena and ubhayādhīnaḥ at lines 4–5.
- ByT5 segments tad + nirodha; I record the printed surface as the compound tannirodhaḥ, a Pum Prathamā Ekavacana ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa. An independent tat would not agree with masculine nirodhaḥ, and Vyāsa glosses the expression as cittavṛttinirodhaḥ at line 5.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might leave tan-nirodhaḥ as simply “that cessation”; Vyāsa identifies it specifically as cittavṛttinirodhaḥ, the cessation of mental fluctuations.
- It might treat practice and dispassion as alternatives; Vyāsa says the nirodha is ubhayādhīnaḥ, dependent on both.
- It might take abhyāsa as generic repetition only; in this context Vyāsa construes it as vivekadarśanābhyāsa, practice of discriminative seeing.
