## Justifications

**J1. The genitive singular tasya formally points back to a masculine singular saṃskāra, but Vyāsa explains the relevant target in plural terms as prajñākṛta and samādhija saṃskāras. Thus “even that” is not puruṣa or citta in general, but the refined saṃskāra produced by samādhi-prajñā, which too is blocked by a nirodha-born saṃskāra.**
- chosen: tasya = the preceding samādhi-/prajñā-born saṃskāra(s); api marks that even these are inhibited. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `sa na kevalaṃ samādhiprajñāvirodhī prajñākṛtānām api saṃskārāṇāṃ pratibandhī bhavati.` — It is not only opposed to samādhi-prajñā; it also becomes an inhibitor of the saṃskāras produced by prajñā.
- ys [2]: `kasmāt, nirodhajaḥ saṃskāraḥ samādhijān saṃskārān bādhata iti.` — Why? Because the saṃskāra born of nirodha sublates the samādhi-born saṃskāras. The input’s bādhata is quoted verbatim, though it appears to stand for bādhate/bādhati.

**J2. Vyāsa does not use nirodha here in the dictionary sense of physical confinement. He describes a nirodha-born saṃskāra and a sustained nirodha-state in the mind; hence the locative nirodhe is read as the condition “when/upon that inhibition.”**
- chosen: nirodha = inhibitory cessation/restraint; nirodhe = “upon the inhibition.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [2]: `nirodhajaḥ saṃskāraḥ samādhijān saṃskārān bādhata iti.` — The nirodha-born saṃskāra sublates the samādhi-born saṃskāras.
- ys [3]: `nirodhasthitikālakramānubhavena nirodhacittakṛtasaṃskārāstitvam anumeyam.` — From the experience of the sequence of time during which nirodha stands, the existence of a saṃskāra made by the nirodha-mind is to be inferred.

**J3. The commentary expands “all” into the whole range of saṃskāras connected with emergence, nirodha, samādhi, and kaivalya. The ablative is causal/result-grounding: because the mind with its completed function turns back, all seed-causes have ceased.**
- chosen: sarvanirodhāt = causal ablative, “because of the inhibition of all [saṃskāras],” with sarvanirodha as a ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa: sarvasya nirodhaḥ. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [4]: `vyutthānanirodhasamādhiprabhavaiḥ saha kaivalyabhāgīyaiḥ saṃskāraiś cittaṃ svasyāṃ prakṛtāv avasthitāyāṃ pravilīyate.` — With the saṃskāras sprung from emergence, nirodha, and samādhi, together with those tending toward kaivalya, the mind dissolves into its own prakṛti, as that stands in itself.
- ys [5]: `tasmāt te saṃskārāś cittasyādhikāravirodhino na sthitihetavo bhavantīti.` — Therefore those saṃskāras, being opposed to the mind’s function, do not become causes of its continued standing.
- ys [6]: `yasmād avasitādhikāraṃ saha kaivalyabhāgīyaiḥ saṃskāraiś cittaṃ nivartate, tasmin nivṛtte puruṣaḥ svarūpamātrapratiṣṭho 'taḥ śuddhaḥ kevalo mukta ity ucyata iti.` — Because the mind, its function completed, turns back together with the saṃskāras tending toward kaivalya; when it has turned back, puruṣa, established solely in its own nature, is therefore called pure, alone, liberated.

**J4. The word nirbīja lexically means “seedless,” but Vyāsa supplies the doctrinal force of “seed”: saṃskāras are no longer sthitihetavaḥ, causes of the mind’s continued operation; the mind turns back and puruṣa is established in its own nature.**
- chosen: nirbījaḥ samādhiḥ = seedless samādhi, seedless because no saṃskāra remains as a cause for citta’s continued standing. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [5]: `tasmāt te saṃskārāś cittasyādhikāravirodhino na sthitihetavo bhavantīti.` — Therefore those saṃskāras, being opposed to the mind’s function, do not become causes of its continued standing.
- ys [6]: `tasmin nivṛtte puruṣaḥ svarūpamātrapratiṣṭho 'taḥ śuddhaḥ kevalo mukta ity ucyata iti.` — When it has turned back, puruṣa, established solely in its own nature, is therefore called pure, alone, liberated.

## Translation

> Upon the inhibition (nirodha) even of that [saṃskāra born of samādhi-prajñā], because of the inhibition of all [saṃskāras], [there is] seedless samādhi (nirbīja samādhi).

## Analyzer disagreements

- ByT5 gives sarva_sarva_Cp + nirodhāt_nirodha_SBM; I analyze the printed sarvanirodhān (< sarvanirodhāt nir-) as one compound stem sarvanirodha, masculine pañcamī ekavacana, because the sūtra has the compound sarva-nirodha and Vyāsa construes it as cessation of all relevant saṃskāras (lines 4–6).
- ByT5 treats nirbījaḥ simply as nirbīja_SNM; I retain nominative masculine singular but add its privative compound analysis nir- + bīja, semantically bahuvrīhi “whose seed is gone/seedless.”

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might take tasya as an unspecified “that” or as puruṣa/citta; Vyāsa points instead to samādhi-/prajñā-born saṃskāras as the refined residue that is also inhibited (lines 1–2).
- It might render sarvanirodhāt vaguely as “from every suppression”; Vyāsa reads the scope as all classes of saṃskāras and the mind’s turning back into prakṛti (lines 4–6).
- It might gloss nirbīja as simply “objectless”; Vyāsa makes seedlessness depend on the absence of saṃskāras as causes for the continued standing of citta (lines 5–6).
