## Justifications

**J1. The sūtra is read as a nominal statement: ‘the prajñā there is ṛtaṃbharā.’ Vyāsa explicitly says that the prajñā which arises has the designation ṛtaṃbharā.**
- chosen: ṛtaṃbharā is predicated of the prajñā, not a separate substantive. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `tasmin samāhitacittasya yā prajñā jāyate tasyā ṛtaṃbhareti` — The wisdom that arises in that, for one whose mind is concentrated—of it [the designation] is ‘ṛtaṃbharā.’

**J2. The adverb is locative in force. The Bhāṣya glosses tatra with tasmin and links it with the concentrated mind.**
- chosen: tatra means ‘in that [state/locus]’, not ‘thither’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `tasmin samāhitacittasya yā prajñā jāyate` — The wisdom that arises in that, for one whose mind is concentrated...

**J3. Although the sūtra is verbless, Vyāsa supplies jāyate and qualifies the possessor/context as samāhitacittasya.**
- chosen: prajñā is a cognition/insight that arises for the concentrated mind. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `samāhitacittasya yā prajñā jāyate` — the wisdom that arises for one whose mind is concentrated

**J4. The Bhāṣya here identifies the epithet but does not unpack its semantics. The English ‘truth-bearing’ follows the compound ṛtaṃ-bhara and MW s.v. ṛtaṃbhara, ‘bearing the truth in one’s self’.**
- chosen: ṛtaṃbharā is rendered ‘truth-bearing’. (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> There, the insight (prajñā) is truth-bearing (ṛtaṃbharā). In Vyāsa’s terms: it is the prajñā that arises in that state for one whose mind is concentrated.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might miss that ṛtaṃbharā is specifically the designation of the prajñā, not an independent entity.
- It might leave tatra as a vague ‘there’; Vyāsa’s tasmin makes it a locative referring back to the preceding yogic state/context.
- It might translate the sūtra as a bare static equation and omit Vyāsa’s point that this prajñā ‘arises’ for the samāhita-citta, the concentrated mind.
