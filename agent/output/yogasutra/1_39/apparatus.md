## Translation

> Or, through meditation (dhyāna) on just whatever is agreeable/chosen (abhimata) to one, [the mind becomes steadied].

## Analyzer disagreements

- ByT5 segments yathā / abhimata / dhyānāt as separate analytical items. I treat the printed pada yathābhimatadhyānād as one inflected compound, pausal yathābhimatadhyānāt, because -āt + vā gives the printed sandhi -ād vā and because Vyāsa glosses the whole by a single predication, yad evābhimataṃ tad eva dhyāyet.

## One-shot delta

- Without Vyāsa’s gloss, one might translate yathābhimatadhyānāt as “by meditating however one likes,” making yathābhimata describe the manner of meditation; the commentary instead makes the agreeable/chosen thing the object: yad evābhimataṃ tad eva dhyāyet.
- A commentary-blind rendering might miss the practical point of the aphorism: the chosen-object meditation is a way to gain stability there and then obtain steadiness elsewhere too, as line 2 states.
