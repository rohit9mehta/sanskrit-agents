## Justifications

**J1. The sūtra’s āsannaḥ, “near / close at hand,” is not left as a bare “it.” Vyāsa supplies what is close at hand: samādhilābhaḥ and samādhiphalam. The masculine singular āsannaḥ most directly agrees with samādhilābhaḥ, with the bhāṣya adding samādhi’s fruit as included in what comes about.**
- chosen: The implied subject/scope is “the attainment of samādhi and the fruit of samādhi.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `samādhilābhaḥ samādhiphalaṃ ca bhavatīti.` — “The attainment of samādhi and the fruit of samādhi come about.”

**J2. The genitive plural is construed as referring to practitioners, not to plural abstract “intense excitements.” This is represented morphologically as a substantivized bahuvrīhi: tīvraḥ saṃvegaḥ yeṣām. The bhāṣya’s supplied result—attainment of samādhi—fits persons possessing such ardor.**
- chosen: tīvrasaṃvegānām means “for/of those whose saṃvega is intense.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `samādhilābhaḥ samādhiphalaṃ ca bhavatīti.` — “The attainment of samādhi and the fruit of samādhi come about.”

**J3. MW allows both “violent agitation/excitement” and “vehemence/intensity” or “desire of emancipation” for saṃvega. Here Vyāsa connects it directly with samādhi-attainment and samādhi’s fruit, so “ardor” or “urgent impetus” is preferable to a merely psychological “agitation.”**
- chosen: saṃvega is rendered “ardor,” i.e. urgent soteriological impetus, not mere agitation. (depends on commentary: **yes**)

## Translation

> For those whose ardor (saṃvega) is intense, the attainment of meditative concentration (samādhi), together with samādhi’s fruit, is close at hand.

## Analyzer disagreements

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might leave āsannaḥ as “it is near” without saying what “it” is; Vyāsa identifies the referent as samādhilābha and samādhiphala.
- It might translate saṃvega as mere “agitation” or “excitement,” whereas the bhāṣya’s connection with samādhi makes soteriological ardor the better choice.
- It might construe tīvrasaṃvegānām as “of intense urges” rather than “of those whose ardor is intense.”
