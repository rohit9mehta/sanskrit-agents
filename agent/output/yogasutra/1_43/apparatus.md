## Justifications

**J1. smṛtipariśuddhau is not an attributive ‘purified memory’ but a locative setting for the arising of this samāpatti: when that purification is present, the prajñā is called nirvitarkā.**
- chosen: ‘Upon/when memory is purified’ as a locative condition. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `yā śabdasaṃketaśrutānumānajñānavikalpasmṛtipariśuddhau grāhyasvarūpa.uparaktā prajñā svam iva prajñāsvarūpaṃ grahaṇātmakaṃ tyaktvā padārthamātrasvarūpā grāhyasvarūpāpanneva bhavati sā tadā nirvitarkā samāpattiḥ.` — That cognition which, when memory involving verbal convention, testimony, inference, and conceptualized knowledge has been purified, is colored by the form of the graspable object, as it were abandoning its own cognitive form whose nature is grasping, and becomes of the mere object’s form, as though having taken on the graspable object’s form—that then is nirvitarkā samāpatti.

**J2. The sūtra’s subject is supplied from the prior sūtra and made explicit by Vyāsa: the prajñā in such concentration is ‘then nirvitarkā samāpatti’. Hence svarūpaśūnyā, arthamātranirbhāsā, and nirvitarkā are nominative feminine singular predicates of that understood samāpatti/prajñā.**
- chosen: The feminine nominatives describe an understood samāpatti, mediated by prajñā. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `yā śabdasaṃketaśrutānumānajñānavikalpasmṛtipariśuddhau grāhyasvarūpa.uparaktā prajñā svam iva prajñāsvarūpaṃ grahaṇātmakaṃ tyaktvā padārthamātrasvarūpā grāhyasvarūpāpanneva bhavati sā tadā nirvitarkā samāpattiḥ.` — That cognition ... becomes of the mere object’s form ... that then is nirvitarkā samāpatti.

**J3. Vyāsa’s gloss makes sva-rūpa the prajñā-svarūpa, specifically grahaṇātmaka, ‘whose nature is apprehension/grasping’; and he preserves the force of iva by saying svam iva ... tyaktvā and grāhyasvarūpāpannā iva. The translation therefore says ‘as if devoid of its own form,’ not simply ‘empty of essence’.**
- chosen: ‘As if devoid of its own form’ means as if lacking the cognition’s own grasping-form, not ontological non-existence. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `svam iva prajñāsvarūpaṃ grahaṇātmakaṃ tyaktvā padārthamātrasvarūpā grāhyasvarūpāpanneva bhavati` — as it were abandoning its own form, the form of cognition, whose nature is grasping, it becomes of the mere object’s form, as though having taken on the graspable object’s form

**J4. The bhāṣya glosses the state as padārthamātrasvarūpā and grāhyasvarūpāpannā, and later says the avayavī becomes the viṣayin of nirvitarkā samāpatti. Thus ‘object alone’ is the safest translation.**
- chosen: artha in arthamātranirbhāsā is the graspable object/padārtha, not ‘purpose’ or merely verbal ‘meaning’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `padārthamātrasvarūpā grāhyasvarūpāpanneva bhavati` — it becomes of the mere object’s form, as though having taken on the form of the graspable object
- ys [11]: `tasmād asty avayavī yo mahattvādivyavahārāpannaḥ samāpatter nirvitarkāyā viṣayī bhavati.` — Therefore there is a whole, an avayavī, which is the basis of practical dealings such as largeness and becomes the object of nirvitarkā samāpatti.

**J5. Rather than using MW’s general ‘unreflecting/inconsiderate’, the bhāṣya explicitly identifies the described state as nirvitarkā samāpatti. The translation therefore preserves the technical term and gives only the literal orientation ‘without vitarka’.**
- chosen: nirvitarkā is a technical name of the samāpatti, rendered ‘without vitarka’ and retained in Sanskrit. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `sā tadā nirvitarkā samāpattiḥ.` — that then is nirvitarkā samāpatti.

**J6. The external sandhi is grammatical: final ā of śūnyā with initial i of iva gives e, and final a of iva with initial a of artha gives ā, producing śūnyevārtha-.**
- chosen: The printed sequence resolves as svarūpaśūnyā iva arthamātranirbhāsā. (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> Upon the purification of memory, [the samāpatti], as if devoid of its own form, shining forth as the object alone, is nirvitarkā (without vitarka).

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might make svarūpaśūnyā sound like an absolute denial of essence; Vyāsa makes it ‘as if’ the cognition has abandoned its own grasping-form.
- It might translate artha as ‘meaning’ because of the preceding discussion of word and object; Vyāsa’s padārtha/grāhya gloss and the avayavī discussion point to ‘object’.
- It might miss the supplied subject: the sūtra predicates these feminine adjectives of samāpatti, explicitly supplied by Vyāsa as nirvitarkā samāpattiḥ.
- It might flatten smṛtipariśuddhi to ordinary memory improvement; the bhāṣya links it to purification from verbal, conventional, testimonial, inferential, and conceptual-memory admixture.
