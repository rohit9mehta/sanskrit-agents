## Justifications

**J1. The defining compound is translated as ‘a cognition/presentation of absence.’ Vyāsa argues that sleep is a pratyayaviśeṣa, a specific pratyaya, because on waking there is retrospective awareness; without an experienced pratyaya such recollection would not occur. Thus the sūtra does not define sleep as blank non-cognition.**
- chosen: pratyaya = a cognition/presentation, not a mere absence of cognition (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `sā ca saṃprabodhe pratyavamarśāt pratyayaviśeṣaḥ.` — And it, because of retrospective awareness on full awakening, is a particular cognition.
- ys [10]: `sa khalv ayaṃ prabuddhasya pratyavamarśo na syād asati pratyayānubhave` — This retrospective awareness of one who has awakened would not exist if there had been no experience of a cognition.
- ys [11]: `tasmāt pratyayaviśeṣo nidrā.` — Therefore sleep is a particular cognition.

**J2. The compound is taken as a bahuvrīhi adjective: ‘that vṛtti whose ālambana is an abhāva-pratyaya.’ The commentary does not give an explicit vigraha for the compound, so the compound relation is decided grammatically; Vyāsa’s pratyayaviśeṣa discussion controls only that the relevant pratyaya is positive enough to ground later recollection.**
- chosen: abhāvapratyayālambanā qualifies vṛttiḥ as ‘having a cognition of absence as its support/object’ (depends on commentary: no)

**J3. The nominatives are construed definitionally: ‘Sleep is a vṛtti…’. Vyāsa confirms that sleep belongs among pratyayas/vṛttis and is subject to nirodha in samādhi, so it is not excluded from the class of mental modifications.**
- chosen: nidrā is being defined as a vṛtti/pratyayaviśeṣa to be restrained like other pratyayas (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [11]: `tasmāt pratyayaviśeṣo nidrā.` — Therefore sleep is a particular cognition.
- ys [12]: `sā ca samādhāv itarapratyayavan niroddhavyeti.` — And in samādhi it is to be restrained like other cognitions.

**J4. The commentary here does not separately gloss abhāva. Its basic lexical sense is used, while avoiding the commentary-rejected idea that sleep is simply the absence of pratyaya.**
- chosen: abhāva = absence/non-presence (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> Sleep (nidrā) is a mental modification (vṛtti) whose support is a cognition/presentation (pratyaya) of absence (abhāva).

## Analyzer disagreements

- ByT5 exposes abhāva, pratyaya, and ālambanā as compound elements; the apparatus treats the printed abhāvapratyayālambanā as one inflected compound qualifying vṛttiḥ. This changes segmentation, not the feminine nominative singular analysis of the final element.
- ByT5 gives the final member’s lemma as ālambana; per the requested rule that the stem of an inflected compound is the whole compound stem, the lemma is recorded as abhāvapratyayālambanā, while ālambana is retained among the compound members.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind rendering might translate the compound as ‘sleep is a modification based on absence of cognition,’ implying no cognition occurs in sleep. Vyāsa explicitly argues the opposite: sleep is a pratyayaviśeṣa, evidenced by recollection on waking.
- It might miss that ālambanā is a feminine compound adjective agreeing with vṛttiḥ, not a separate neuter ‘support’.
- It might treat nidrā as outside the sequence of restrainable vṛttis; Vyāsa says it is to be restrained in samādhi like other pratyayas.
