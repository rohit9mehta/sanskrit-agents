## Justifications

**J1. The adverb is not translated as a vague spatial ‘there’ only; Vyāsa’s yatra … sa construction makes it a locative of the possessor/locus, and line 4 identifies that possessor as the puruṣa-viśeṣa.**
- chosen: tatra = ‘there/in him,’ the locus being the special puruṣa in whom the unsurpassed knowledge-seed is found. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `yad idam atītānāgatapratyutpannapratyekasamuccayātīndriyagrahaṇam alpaṃ bahv iti sarvajñabījam etad vivardhamānaṃ yatra niratiśayaṃ sa sarvajñaḥ.` — This apprehension of past, future, and present, individually and collectively, and of supersensible things, whether small or great, is the seed of omniscience; where this, increasing, is unsurpassed, he is omniscient.
- ys [4]: `sa ca puruṣaviśeṣa iti.` — And he is the special puruṣa.

**J2. The compound is not a karmadhāraya ‘omniscient seed’ nor a literal seed belonging to an omniscient person. Vyāsa defines it as a kind of grahaṇa and then equates its culmination with jñāna reaching its limit.**
- chosen: sarvajñabījam = ‘the seed/germ of omniscience,’ a ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa in sense. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `yad idam atītānāgatapratyutpannapratyekasamuccayātīndriyagrahaṇam alpaṃ bahv iti sarvajñabījam` — This apprehension of past, future, and present, individually and collectively, and of supersensible things, whether small or great, is the seed of omniscience.
- ys [3]: `yatra kāṣṭhāprāptir jñānasya sa sarvajñaḥ.` — Where knowledge has reached its limit, he is omniscient.

**J3. The commentary construes niratiśaya as the culmination of an increasing, gradated knowledge-seed, expressed by kāṣṭhāprāpti, not merely as an adverb ‘exceedingly.’**
- chosen: niratiśayam = ‘unsurpassed, at the highest limit,’ qualifying sarvajñabījam. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `etad vivardhamānaṃ yatra niratiśayaṃ sa sarvajñaḥ.` — Where this, increasing, is unsurpassed, he is omniscient.
- ys [2]: `asti kāṣṭhāprāptiḥ sarvajñabījasya sātiśayatvāt parimāṇavad iti.` — There is an attainment of the limit for the seed of omniscience, because it has gradation, like a quantity.
- ys [3]: `yatra kāṣṭhāprāptir jñānasya sa sarvajñaḥ.` — Where knowledge has reached its limit, he is omniscient.

**J4. Because the neuter nominative and accusative singular forms coincide, grammar must decide the construal. With no transitive verb present, the natural reading is subject plus predicate adjective with an understood ‘is.’**
- chosen: niratiśayam and sarvajñabījam are nominative neuter singular in an elliptical nominal sentence: ‘the seed … is unsurpassed.’ (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> There, in him, the seed of omniscience (sarvajñabīja) is unsurpassed (niratiśaya).
