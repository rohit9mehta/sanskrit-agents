## Justifications

**J1. The compound dṛṣṭānuśravikaviṣaya is not taken as merely ‘visible and audible sense-objects’. Vyāsa distinguishes worldly seen objects from scripturally heard attainments.**
- chosen: dṛṣṭa-viṣaya means ordinary experienced objects, and ānuśravika-viṣaya means objects or attainments known from scripture/tradition. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `striyo 'nnapānam aiśvaryam iti dṛṣṭaviṣaye vitṛṣṇasya svargavaidehyaprakṛtilayatvaprāptāv ānuśravikaviṣaye vitṛṣṇasya` — ‘Women, food-and-drink, sovereignty’—in regard to a seen object, one without thirst; and, in regard to an ānuśravika object, namely the attainment of heaven, bodilessness, and absorption into prakṛti, one without thirst.

**J2. The sūtra’s genitive is translated as belonging to a mind without thirst, not primarily as ‘of a person’ or ‘of a yogin’.**
- chosen: The genitive dṛṣṭānuśravikaviṣayavitṛṣṇasya is construed with an implied cittasya, ‘of the mind’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `divyādivyaviṣayasaṃprayoge 'pi cittasya viṣayadoṣadarśinaḥ` — of the mind, even in contact with divine and non-divine objects, seeing the defects in objects

**J3. ‘Mastery’ is not active enjoyment or domination of objects; Vyāsa defines this grade of dispassion as arising from seeing defects in objects and as lacking both appropriation and rejection.**
- chosen: vaśīkārasaṃjñā is the mastery designation/state marked by non-engagement toward objects. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `viṣayadoṣadarśinaḥ prasaṃkhyānabalād anābhogātmikā heyopādeyaśūnyā vaśīkārasaṃjñā vairāgyam` — for [the mind] seeing the defects in objects, through the power of discriminative discernment, the mastery-designation—of the nature of non-engagement, empty of what is to be rejected or taken up—is dispassion.

**J4. Although it stands beside neuter vairāgyam, the word is not analyzed as a neuter adjective; the bhāṣya’s feminine qualifiers show that saṃjñā is the syntactic head.**
- chosen: vaśīkārasaṃjñā is a feminine head-noun compound: ‘the designation called mastery’. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `anābhogātmikā heyopādeyaśūnyā vaśīkārasaṃjñā vairāgyam` — the mastery-designation, of the nature of non-engagement and devoid of anything to be rejected or taken up, is dispassion

**J5. The neuter form could be accusative by form alone, but the construction is a nominal definition with no transitive verb; therefore Prathama Eka is chosen.**
- chosen: vairāgyam is nominative singular, the predicate/subject term in an equational sūtra. (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> Dispassion (vairāgya), in a mind without thirst for objects seen (dṛṣṭa) and scripturally heard (ānuśravika), is the state bearing the designation “mastery” (vaśīkāra-saṃjñā).
