## Justifications

**J1. vṛttayaḥ is not translated by a general sense such as ‘conducts’ or ‘ways of living’; it is kept as the Yoga technical term vṛtti and rendered ‘modifications’. The bhāṣya immediately treats vṛttis as items in a vṛtti–saṃskāra cycle belonging to citta.**
- chosen: “modifications (vṛttis)” as a technical mental process-term (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [6, 7, 8, 9]: `tathājātīyakāḥ saṃskārā vṛttibhir eva kriyante. saṃskāraiś ca vṛttaya iti. evaṃ vṛttisaṃskāracakram aniśam āvartate. tad evaṃbhūtaṃ cittam avasitādhikāram ātmakalpena vyavatiṣṭhate pralayaṃ vā gacchatīti.` — Impressions of the same kind are produced precisely by vṛttis, and vṛttis by impressions. Thus the wheel of vṛtti and saṃskāra turns without cease. Such a citta, when its function has ended, abides in the form of self, or else goes to dissolution.

**J2. The adjective pañcatayyaḥ is construed predicatively with vṛttayaḥ: the vṛttis are fivefold. Vyāsa glosses it with pañcadhā and the next sūtra lists the five: pramāṇa, viparyaya, vikalpa, nidrā, smṛti.**
- chosen: pañcatayyaḥ = ‘fivefold’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [10, 12]: `tāḥ kliṣṭāś cākliṣṭāś ca pañcadhā vṛttayaḥ. pramāṇaviparyayavikalpanidrāsmṛtayaḥ` — Those vṛttis, afflicted and unafflicted, are fivefold: pramāṇa, viparyaya, vikalpa, sleep, and memory.

**J3. The compound is taken as an itaretara-dvandva adjective, not as a karmadhāraya or as meaning that each vṛtti is simultaneously both. Vyāsa expands it with ca … ca: kliṣṭāś ca akliṣṭāś ca.**
- chosen: kliṣṭākliṣṭāḥ as coordinated ‘afflicted and unafflicted’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [10]: `tāḥ kliṣṭāś cākliṣṭāś ca pañcadhā vṛttayaḥ.` — Those vṛttis, afflicted and unafflicted, are fivefold.

**J4. ‘Afflicted’ is chosen over a merely physical ‘pained’ or ‘tormented’ sense. Vyāsa defines kliṣṭa vṛttis as having kleśas as their cause and as serving as a field for the accumulation of karmic deposits.**
- chosen: kliṣṭa = ‘afflicted’, specifically kleśa-based and karmically fertile (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `kleśahetukāḥ karmāśayapracaye kṣetrībhūtāḥ kliṣṭāḥ.` — Afflicted ones are those caused by kleśas and become a field for the accumulation of karmic deposits.

**J5. ‘Unafflicted’ is not just ‘untroubled’ in an ordinary sense. Vyāsa characterizes akliṣṭa vṛttis as having khyāti as their object and as opposing the authority of the guṇas; he also notes that unafflicted and afflicted currents may occur in the gaps of one another.**
- chosen: akliṣṭa = ‘unafflicted’, oriented to discriminative knowledge and opposing guṇa-dominion (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [2, 3, 4, 5]: `khyātiviṣayā guṇādhikāravirodhinyo 'kliṣṭāḥ. kliṣṭapravāhapatitā apy akliṣṭāḥ. kliṣṭacchidreṣv apy akliṣṭā bhavanti. akliṣṭacchideṣu kliṣṭā iti.` — Unafflicted ones have discriminative knowledge as their object and oppose the dominion of the guṇas. Even when fallen into an afflicted stream they are unafflicted; even in the breaks in the afflicted there are unafflicted ones; and in the breaks in the unafflicted there are afflicted ones.

## Translation

> The modifications (vṛttis) are fivefold: afflicted (kliṣṭa) and unafflicted (akliṣṭa).

## Analyzer disagreements

- ByT5 gives pañcatayyaḥ with lemma pañcataya. I record the inflectional feminine stem pañcatayī, since the printed form pañcatayyaḥ is the feminine nominative plural of pañcatayī, the feminine form of lexical pañcataya ‘fivefold’.
- ByT5 segments kliṣṭākliṣṭāḥ as kliṣṭa + akliṣṭāḥ and tags kliṣṭa as PaCp. I analyze the printed word as an inflected itaretara-dvandva compound stem kliṣṭākliṣṭa; the bhāṣya expands it as kliṣṭāś cākliṣṭāś ca in line 10. The participial value of kliṣṭa is retained as a compound member, not as a finite verb.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render kliṣṭa merely as ‘painful’ and akliṣṭa merely as ‘not painful’; Vyāsa instead defines them by relation to kleśa, karmāśaya, khyāti, and the guṇas.
- It might miss that pañcatayyaḥ is glossed by pañcadhā and points forward to the five named vṛttis in 1.6.
- It might treat kliṣṭākliṣṭāḥ as if every vṛtti were simultaneously both afflicted and unafflicted; the bhāṣya’s kliṣṭāś cākliṣṭāś ca supports two coordinated classes.
