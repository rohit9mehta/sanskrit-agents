## Justifications

**J1. The genitive is not taken as ‘of other things’ in isolation. Vyāsa glosses this sūtra as applying to yogins, later classifies them as yogins, and explicitly states the result as asaṃprajñātaḥ samādhiḥ. Hence I supply ‘it’ as the asaṃprajñāta samādhi and translate ‘the others’ as the other yogins.**
- chosen: itareṣām means ‘of/for the other yogins,’ and the understood subject is asaṃprajñāta-samādhi. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `upāyapratyayo yogināṃ bhavati.` — For yogins it comes about with means as its condition.
- ys [9]: `tadabhyāsāt tattadviṣayāc ca vairāgyād asaṃprajñātaḥ samādhir bhavati.` — From practice of that and from dispassion toward each such object, asaṃprajñāta samādhi comes about.
- ys [11]: `te khalu nava yogino mṛdumadhyādhimātropāyā bhavanti.` — Those yogins are indeed nine, having mild, medium, and intense means.

**J2. The compound is not merely ‘beginning with faith’ or loosely ‘accompanied by’ the five. Vyāsa presents a chain: from faith arises energy; with energy, recollection attends; with recollection, the mind is concentrated; from concentration, discriminating insight arises; through practice and dispassion asaṃprajñāta samādhi comes about. Therefore pūrvakaḥ is rendered ‘preceded by’ or ‘having as antecedent means.’**
- chosen: pūrvakaḥ expresses an ordered antecedent/means-sequence: faith, energy, recollection, concentration, insight. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9]: `tasya hi śraddadhānasya vivekārthino vīryam upajāyate. samupajātavīryasya smṛtir upatiṣṭhate. smṛtyupasthāne ca cittam anākulaṃ samādhīyate. samāhitacittasya prajñāviveka upāvartate. yena yathārthaṃ vastu jānāti. tadabhyāsāt tattadviṣayāc ca vairāgyād asaṃprajñātaḥ samādhir bhavati.` — For one who has faith and seeks discrimination, energy arises. For one in whom energy has arisen, recollection attends. When recollection is present, the mind, unconfused, becomes concentrated. For one whose mind is concentrated, discriminating insight comes into play, by which he knows an object as it really is. From practice of that and from dispassion toward each such object, asaṃprajñāta samādhi comes about.

**J3. Rather than bare credulity or merely ritual faith, Vyāsa defines śraddhā directly as cetasaḥ saṃprasādaḥ, a lucid settling/confidence of the mind, and gives the protective mother-simile. The translation therefore keeps ‘faith’ but signals its technical sense by adding the Sanskrit.**
- chosen: śraddhā is ‘faith’ as confident clarity or composure of mind. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [2, 3]: `śraddhā cetasaḥ saṃprasādaḥ. sā hi jananīva kalyāṇī yoginaṃ pāti.` — Faith is the clear composure of the mind. For she, beneficent like a mother, protects the yogin.

**J4. Vyāsa does not give a formal definition here, but he places smṛti in the inward causal chain: after energy it ‘attends,’ and when it is present the mind becomes unconfused and concentrated. Hence I render it as recollection/mindfulness rather than as scriptural tradition or mere external memory.**
- chosen: smṛti is ‘recollection’ with a mindfulness nuance, not the textual genre ‘Smṛti.’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [5, 6]: `samupajātavīryasya smṛtir upatiṣṭhate. smṛtyupasthāne ca cittam anākulaṃ samādhīyate.` — For one in whom energy has arisen, recollection attends. And when recollection is present, the mind, unconfused, becomes concentrated.

**J5. Because the sūtra’s compound itself contains samādhi while the result is also called asaṃprajñāta samādhi, the two levels could be conflated. Vyāsa distinguishes them by using the verbal gloss cittam ... samādhīyate for the intermediate settling of mind, and only afterward states that asaṃprajñātaḥ samādhiḥ comes about.**
- chosen: samādhi within the compound is the meditative concentration of the mind that precedes the final asaṃprajñāta samādhi. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [6]: `smṛtyupasthāne ca cittam anākulaṃ samādhīyate.` — When recollection is present, the mind, unconfused, becomes concentrated.
- ys [9]: `tadabhyāsāt tattadviṣayāc ca vairāgyād asaṃprajñātaḥ samādhir bhavati.` — From practice of that and from dispassion toward each such object, asaṃprajñāta samādhi comes about.

**J6. The bhāṣya links prajñā with viveka and with knowing vastu yathārtham. The printed line 7 reads prajñāviveka; I cite it as printed, but take it as pointing to prajñā in its discriminative function. Therefore I do not translate merely ‘wisdom’ in a broad sense.**
- chosen: prajñā is ‘discriminating insight’ that knows the object as it really is. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [7, 8]: `samāhitacittasya prajñāviveka upāvartate. yena yathārthaṃ vastu jānāti.` — For one whose mind is concentrated, prajñā-viveka comes into play, by which he knows an object as it really is.

## Translation

> For the others, [asaṃprajñāta samādhi] is preceded by faith (śraddhā), energy (vīrya), recollection (smṛti), meditative concentration (samādhi), and discriminating insight (prajñā).
