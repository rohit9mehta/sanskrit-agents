## Justifications

**J1. The pronoun tat refers back to vairāgya, and param means the latter/higher member of a twofold vairāgya, not an adverbial ‘therefore’ or ‘beyond’ attached to puruṣakhyāti.**
- chosen: tat param is ‘that higher [dispassion]’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [2, 3]: `tad dvayaṃ vairāgyam. tatra yad uttaraṃ taj jñānaprasādamātram.` — That pair is dispassion. Of these, that which is the latter/higher is nothing but the lucidity of knowledge.

**J2. The compound puruṣakhyāti is a tatpuruṣa, ‘knowledge/perception of Puruṣa.’ Its case is read as ablative because Vyāsa explains it through puruṣadarśanābhyāsāt, ‘from the practice of seeing Puruṣa.’**
- chosen: puruṣakhyāteḥ is causal ablative: ‘from / owing to the knowledge-seeing of Puruṣa’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `puruṣadarśanābhyāsāt tacchuddhipravivekāpyāyitabuddhir` — whose intellect is nourished by the purity and distinct discernment of that, through the practice of seeing Puruṣa

**J3. The compound is not ‘the quality of desirelessness’ or a moral ‘virtue.’ Vyāsa glosses it with guṇebhyo ... viraktaḥ, showing both that guṇa is the technical object from which detachment occurs and that vaitṛṣṇya is virāga, freedom from craving.**
- chosen: guṇavaitṛṣṇyam means ‘thirstlessness/dispassion toward the guṇas’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `guṇebhyo vyaktāvyaktadharmakebhyo virakta iti.` — is dispassionate from the guṇas, which have manifest and unmanifest characteristics.

**J4. Vyāsa distinguishes the lower dispassion based on seeing defects in seen and scripturally-heard objects from the higher one associated with the arising of knowledge.**
- chosen: The sūtra defines the higher form of vairāgya as a lucidity born of knowledge, not as ordinary disgust alone (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1, 3, 4]: `dṛṣṭānuśravikaviṣayadoṣadarśī viraktaḥ ... tatra yad uttaraṃ taj jñānaprasādamātram. yasyodaye sati yogī pratyuditakhyātir evaṃ manyate` — The dispassionate one sees the defects in seen and scripturally-heard objects ... Of these, the latter/higher is nothing but the lucidity of knowledge. When it arises, the yogin has awakened knowledge and thinks thus.

## Translation

> That higher [dispassion] is thirstlessness toward the guṇas, owing to the knowledge-seeing of Puruṣa.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might take guṇa as ordinary ‘qualities’ or ‘virtues’; Vyāsa’s guṇebhyo vyaktāvyaktadharmakebhyo shows the technical Sāṃkhya-Yoga guṇas are meant.
- It might render puruṣakhyāteḥ as a simple genitive, ‘of the knowledge of the person’; Vyāsa’s puruṣadarśanābhyāsāt supports a causal ablative, ‘because of seeing/knowing Puruṣa.’
- It might miss that tat param is locating this as the higher member of two forms of vairāgya; Vyāsa explicitly says tad dvayaṃ vairāgyam and yad uttaraṃ.
