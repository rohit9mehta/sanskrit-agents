## Justifications

**J1. The sūtra is nominal in form, but the bhāṣya explicitly supplies bhavati with dṛḍhabhūmiḥ. Hence the English should not leave dṛḍhabhūmiḥ as a bare appositional noun such as “a firm ground,” but make it a predicate of the prior practice.**
- chosen: saḥ ... dṛḍhabhūmiḥ is to be translated with an understood bhavati: “that [practice] becomes/is firmly grounded.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [2]: `tapasā brahmacaryeṇa vidyayā śraddhayā ca saṃpāditaḥ satkāravān dṛḍhabhūmir bhavati.` — Brought about by austerity, celibacy, knowledge, and faith, and furnished with reverence, it becomes firmly grounded.

**J2. The bhāṣya unpacks the long compound distributively, repeating āsevitaḥ with each condition. Therefore nairantarya should be rendered adverbially as “without interruption/continuously,” and the compound should not be read as a single opaque noun phrase.**
- chosen: dīrghakāla, nairantarya, and satkāra are three separate qualifications of āsevitaḥ: cultivated for a long time, continuously, and with reverence. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [1]: `dīrghakālāsevito nirantarāsevitaḥ satkārāsevitaḥ.` — Cultivated for a long time, cultivated continuously, cultivated with reverence.

**J3. The bhāṣya connects satkāra with tapas, brahmacarya, vidyā, and śraddhā, so in this yogic context it is an inward respectful seriousness in practice. “Reverent care” best captures that force.**
- chosen: satkāra is “reverent care/esteem,” not hospitality or merely social honor. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [2]: `tapasā brahmacaryeṇa vidyayā śraddhayā ca saṃpāditaḥ satkāravān dṛḍhabhūmir bhavati.` — Brought about by austerity, celibacy, knowledge, and faith, and furnished with reverence, it becomes firmly grounded.

**J4. The bhāṣya’s explanation of dṛḍhabhūmiḥ is functional: the practice’s field is not immediately overwhelmed by the saṃskāra of mental emergence/outgoing activity. This confirms the bahuvrīhi predicate “having firm ground,” rather than a literal “firm earth.”**
- chosen: dṛḍhabhūmiḥ means “firmly grounded,” specifically not quickly overpowered by vyutthāna-saṃskāra. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [3]: `vyutthānasaṃskāreṇa drāg ity evānabhibhūtaviṣaya ity arthaḥ.` — The meaning is: its domain is not at once overpowered by the saṃskāra of emergence.

**J5. The particle marks a turn from the preceding definition of practice to the condition under which it is stable. The commentary does not gloss tu explicitly, so this is decided on grammar and normal usage.**
- chosen: tu is rendered “but.” (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> But that [practice (abhyāsa)], when cultivated for a long time, without interruption, and with reverent care (satkāra), becomes firmly grounded (dṛḍhabhūmi).

## One-shot delta

- Without the bhāṣya, one might miss that āsevitaḥ is to be distributed over three conditions: long duration, continuity, and satkāra.
- One might translate satkāra as “hospitality” or external honor; the bhāṣya makes it reverent seriousness supported by tapas, brahmacarya, vidyā, and śraddhā.
- One might take dṛḍhabhūmiḥ literally as “firm ground”; the bhāṣya explains it as a stable practice not quickly overcome by vyutthāna-saṃskāra.
