## Translation

> For the prevention of that [distraction, vikṣepa], there is cultivation (abhyāsa) of the mind resting on a single principle/object (eka-tattva).

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might take tat as an unspecified plural ‘those obstacles’; Vyāsa glosses the scope as vikṣepa, distraction.
- It might render eka-tattva as ‘the One Reality,’ whereas the bhāṣya explains it as the mind’s support in one tattva and as concentration on one artha, a single object.
- It might leave abhyāsaḥ as a bare noun ‘practice’; Vyāsa recasts it as the optative prescription cittam abhyaset, ‘one should cultivate the mind.’
- It might miss the polemic against momentariness: Vyāsa insists that practice of one-pointedness belongs to a single enduring citta, not to isolated momentary cognitions that would already be ‘one-pointed.’
