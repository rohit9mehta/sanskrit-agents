**J3. The bhāṣya explains the relevant cognition by saying that buddhi-sattva is shining and that the pravṛtti takes the forms of solar, lunar, planetary, and jewel-radiance. This excludes unrelated dictionary senses such as a night, brick, or metre.**
- chosen: jyotiṣmatī means ‘luminous, light-bearing.’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- ys [2]: `buddhisattvaṃ hi bhāsvaram ākāśakalpaṃ, tatra sthitivaiśāradyāt pravṛttiḥ sūryendugrahamaṇiprabhārūpākāreṇa vikalpate.` — ‘For the sattva of buddhi is shining, like space; there, through mastery of steadiness, the activity takes shape in the form of the radiance of sun, moon, planet, and jewel.’

**J4. The bhāṣya locates the practice in concentration on the heart-lotus and identifies the relevant occurrence as buddhisaṃvit, ‘awareness/cognition of buddhi.’ Therefore ‘cognitive activity’ is preferable to a merely physical ‘light.’**

- chosen: viśokā is rendered ‘free from sorrow’ or ‘sorrowless.’ (depends on commentary: no)

## Translation

> Or, the sorrowless, luminous cognitive activity—once arisen—is a basis for the mind’s steadiness.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render the sūtra as ‘sorrowless or luminous,’ making viśokā and jyotiṣmatī two alternatives; Vyāsa instead continues the singular pravṛttiḥ and treats the expression as one alternative method.
- It might miss the anuvṛtti from 1.35 and therefore omit ‘an arisen cognitive activity, a basis for the mind’s steadiness.’
- It might take jyotiṣmatī in an unrelated lexical sense such as ‘night,’ ‘sacrificial brick,’ or ‘metre’; the bhāṣya’s bhāsvaram and sūryendu-graha-maṇi-prabhā language fixes the sense as luminous.
