## Justifications

**J1. The object karmāṇi should not be translated as any and all activity in a secular sense. Śaṅkara identifies it as Agnihotra and other prescribed rites, and repeats that one who wishes to live should do precisely those śāstra-enjoined works.**
- chosen: karmāṇi = śāstra-prescribed ritual works, especially Agnihotra and the like; kurvan eva = only while doing them. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [1]: `karmāṇyagnihotrādīni` — “works, Agnihotra and the like.”
- isup [5]: `ataḥ śāstravihitāni karmāṇyagnihotrādīni kurvanneva jijīviṣet` — “Therefore one should wish to live only while doing the works enjoined by scripture, Agnihotra and the like.”

**J2. The optative jijīviṣet is glossed as ‘should wish to live,’ and samāḥ as ‘years.’ Śaṅkara adds that the wish to live a hundred years is a prāptānuvāda, a restatement of a natural fact; what is enjoined is ‘doing works alone.’ The translation therefore avoids making desire for longevity an independent Upaniṣadic duty.**
- chosen: jijīviṣet śataṃ samāḥ = one should seek/wish to live a hundred years, but the real injunction is to do works while living. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [1]: `jijīviṣejjīvitumicchecchataṃ śatasaṅkhyākāḥ samāḥ saṃvatsarān` — “jijīviṣet means ‘should wish to live’; śatam means ‘numbered by a hundred’; samāḥ means ‘years.’”},{

**J3. The words evam, anyathā, and itaḥ are construed around prakāra: ‘in this manner,’ ‘a different manner,’ and ‘from this present manner.’ This rules out a merely spatial reading such as ‘not otherwise from here.’**
- chosen: evam … nānyathā itaḥ asti = in this manner there is no other way than this present mode of doing the prescribed works. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [4]: `evamevamprakāreṇa ... etasmādagnihotrādīni karmāṇi kurvato vartamānātprakārādanyathā prakārāntaraṃ nāsti` — “evam means ‘in this very manner’ ... apart from this present manner of doing Agnihotra and other works, there is no other different manner.”

**J4. Śaṅkara construes tvayi with nare and supplies jijīviṣati and naramātrābhimānini. The translation therefore treats the second half as addressed to the person on the action-path, not to the renunciant knower described in the previous mantra.**
- chosen: tvayi and nare are locatives in apposition: ‘in/for you, as a man,’ specifically one wishing to live and identifying merely as a man. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [4]: `tvayi jijīviṣati nare naramātrābhimāninīta` — “in you, in a man wishing to live, [read from the unsegmented text as naramātrābhimānini itaḥ:] identifying merely as a man; from this ...”

**J5. Although karma is morphologically the neuter nominative/accusative singular form in the mantra, Śaṅkara explains the intended sense as non-taint by karma and even specifies aśubha karma. Thus ‘does not cling/taint’ is preferable to a flat passive ‘karma is not smeared in the man.’**
- chosen: na karma lipyate nare = action does not cling to/taint the man; idiomatically, he is not tainted by karma. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [4]: `yena prakāreṇāśubhaṃ karma na lipyate karmaṇā na lipyata ityarthaḥ` — “by which manner evil karma does not cling; the meaning is, ‘he is not tainted by karma’ [lipyata is taken as the unproofread lipyate].”

**J6. Śaṅkara explicitly frames mantra 1 as sannyāsin-jñānaniṣṭhā and mantra 2 as karmaniṣṭhā for the one incapable of that, insisting on the firm opposition between knowledge and action and on two distinct paths.**
- chosen: The mantra teaches karmaniṣṭhā for one unable to take the renunciant knowledge-path; it is not a doctrine of combining knowledge and ritual action. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [6]: `pūrveṇa saṃnyāsino jñānaniṣṭhoktā dvitīyena tadaśaktasya karmaniṣṭheti` — “By the previous [mantra] the knowledge-discipline of the renunciant was taught; by the second, the action-discipline of one incapable of that.”
- isup [7]: `jñānakarmaṇorvirodhaṃ parvatavadakampyaṃ` — “the opposition of knowledge and action, unshakable like a mountain.”
- isup [11]: `imau dvāveva panthānāvanuniṣkrāntatarau bhavataḥ kriyāpathaścaiva purastātsaṃnyāsaścottareṇa` — “These two paths alone stand out: the path of ritual action first, and renunciation thereafter.”

## Translation

> Only doing here the ritual works (karmāṇi),
one should seek to live a hundred years.
Thus, for you—one who would live, taking yourself as just a man (nara)—
there is no other way than this:
action (karma) does not cling to the man.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render karmāṇi as generic ‘actions’; Śaṅkara takes them as śāstra-prescribed rites such as Agnihotra.
- It might make the desire to live a hundred years itself the injunction; Śaṅkara says this is prāptānuvāda and that ‘doing works alone’ is what is enjoined.
- It might read itaḥ spatially as ‘from here’; the commentary glosses it modally as ‘from this manner/course.’
- It might miss that tvayi nare is aimed at the person identifying as merely a man and wishing to live, not at the renunciant knowledge-seeker of mantra 1.
- It might use this mantra to support a combination of knowledge and action; Śaṅkara explicitly rejects that by asserting their opposition and distinguishing the two paths.
- It might translate na karma lipyate mechanically as ‘karma is not smeared in the man’; Śaṅkara gives the idiom ‘he is not tainted by karma.’
