**J1. Translate as “O Pūṣan (nourisher), O ekarṣi (sole traverser), O Yama (restrainer), O Sūrya, O Prājāpatya (offspring of Prajāpati),” not as unrelated deities or as a separate “one sage.”**
- chosen: The opening five forms are vocatives to the one solar deity, each rendered by Śaṅkara’s etymological sense. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [1]: `he pūṣan! jagataḥ poṣaṇātpūṣā ravistathaika eva ṛṣati gacchati ityekarṣiḥ-- he ekarṣe! tathā sarvasya saṃyamanādyamaḥ-- he yama! tathā raśmīnāṃ prāṇānāṃ rasānāñca svīkaraṇāt sūryaḥ-- he sūrya! prajāpaterapatyaṃ prājāpatyaḥ-- he prājāpatya!` — “O Pūṣan: because he nourishes the world he is Pūṣan, the Sun. Likewise, because he alone moves/goes, he is ekarṣi—O ekarṣi. Likewise, because of restraining all, he is Yama—O Yama. Likewise, because of taking in rays, vital breaths, and essences, he is Sūrya—O Sūrya. The offspring of Prajāpati is Prājāpatya—O Prājāpatya.”

**J2. Take raśmīn as the accusative object of the imperative vyūha, with vyūha in the sense of vigamaya, “make depart/remove,” rather than “arrange” or “marshal.”**

## Translation

> O Pūṣan (nourisher), O ekarṣi (sole traverser), O Yama (restrainer), O Sūrya, O Prājāpatya (offspring of Prajāpati):
disperse your rays; draw together your scorching brilliance.
That form of yours which is most auspiciously beautiful—through your own favor—I behold.
That person (puruṣa) who is yonder, yonder in the solar disk: that am I.

## Analyzer disagreements

- ByT5 split ekarṣe into eka + ṛṣe; the printed mūla and the commentary’s “ityekarṣiḥ-- he ekarṣe” show a single vocative singular stem ekarṣi.
- ByT5 gives prefixed verbal lemmas vyūh and samūh; following the required format, these are recorded as bare root ūh with prefixes vi and sam.
- For tejaḥ I specify accusative singular, not merely the ambiguous neuter singular form, because the commentary glosses it as the object of samūha/upasaṃhara in line 2.
- For both occurrences of te I specify genitive singular, following Śaṅkara’s gloss tava/tavātmanaḥ in line 3.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind rendering would likely translate ekarṣi as “one seer/sage”; Śaṅkara instead derives it from eka eva ṛṣati gacchati, “he alone goes.”
- It might take vyūha as “arrange” the rays; the bhāṣya gloss vigamaya requires “disperse/remove.”
- It might miss that tejaḥ is the accusative object of samūha and that the phrase means withdrawing the scorching light, not praising splendor in general.
- It might take the final puruṣa as an ordinary “man”; the commentary locates him in the solar disk and reads the conclusion as identity, not mere servile petition.
