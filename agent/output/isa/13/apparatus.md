## Justifications

**J1. The neuter anyat is not left as an unspecified ‘something else’, nor is saṃbhavāt taken as the object of a simple comparative ‘other than saṃbhava’. Śaṅkara supplies phalam and glosses anyad eva with pṛthag eva, so the verse contrasts distinct results.**
- chosen: anyat eva = ‘quite/separately different is the fruit’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [1]: `anyadeva pṛthagevāhuḥ phalaṃ sambhavātsambhūteḥ kāryabrahmopāsanādaṇimādyaiśvaryalakṣaṇaṃ vyākhyātavanta ityarthaḥ` — ‘Different indeed’ means ‘separate indeed’; they explained the fruit from saṃbhava/sambhūti, from worship of the effect-Brahman, characterized by lordly powers beginning with aṇimā.
- isup [3]: `ye nastadvicacakṣire vyākṛtāvyākṛtopāsanaphalaṃ vyākhyātavanta ityarthaḥ` — Those who explained that to us explained the fruit of worship of the manifest and the unmanifest.

**J2. Śaṅkara identifies saṃbhava with sambhūti and with kārya-brahmopāsanā. Thus the ablative denotes the source/cause, namely that upāsanā, not mere ‘birth’, ‘coming into being’, or an abstract metaphysical ‘existence’.**
- chosen: saṃbhavāt = ‘from saṃbhava/sambhūti, worship of the effect-Brahman’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [1]: `sambhavātsambhūteḥ kāryabrahmopāsanādaṇimādyaiśvaryalakṣaṇaṃ` — From saṃbhava, from sambhūti, from worship of the effect-Brahman, whose mark/result is lordly power beginning with aṇimā.
- isup [3]: `vyākṛtāvyākṛtopāsanaphalaṃ` — The fruit of worship of the manifest and the unmanifest.

## Translation

> Quite different, they say, is the fruit from saṃbhava (sambhūti: worship of the effect-Brahman, kārya-brahman);
different, they say, is the fruit from asaṃbhava (asambhūti: worship of the unmanifest, avyākṛta).
Thus have we heard from the wise,
who explained that to us.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render saṃbhava/asaṃbhava as abstract ‘being/non-being’ or ‘birth/non-birth’; Śaṅkara instead reads them as two upāsanās: of the effect-Brahman/manifest and of the unmanifest.
- It might miss the supplied phalam and translate ‘different from saṃbhava’ rather than ‘a different fruit from the worship called saṃbhava’.
- It might take vicacakṣire in its visual sense ‘saw/perceived’; the bhāṣya explicitly glosses it as ‘explained’ (vyākhyātavanta).
- It might take naḥ as genitive ‘our’; in context and in the commentary it is dative, ‘to us’.
