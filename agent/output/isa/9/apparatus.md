## Justifications

**J1. The phrase is not taken as a separate noun ‘the blind’ plus ‘darkness’, nor specifically as a named hell, but as a neuter accusative object: darkness whose nature is non-seeing/obscuration.**
- chosen: andhaṃ tamaḥ as ‘blind/sight-obscuring darkness’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [15]: `tatra andhaṃ tamaḥ adarśanātmakaṃ tamaḥ praviśanti` — Here, ‘blind darkness’ is darkness whose nature is non-seeing; they enter it.

**J2. In this verse Śaṅkara does not allow avidyā to mean ordinary ignorance alone; he explicitly defines it as karma, because it is other than vidyā and opposed to it.**
- chosen: avidyā = karma, especially Agnihotra and similar rites (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [16]: `ye 'vidyāṃ vidyāyā anyā avidyā tāṃ karma ityarthaḥ` — Those who [pursue] avidyā: avidyā is what is other than vidyā; by that, karma is meant.
- isup [17]: `karmaṇo vidyāvirodhitvāt ; tāmavidyāmagnihotrādilakṣaṇāmeva kevalāmupāsate` — Because karma is opposed to vidyā; that avidyā, characterized by Agnihotra and the like, alone they upāsate.

**J3. Because its object is karma, Śaṅkara explains upāsate by tatparāḥ santaḥ anutiṣṭhanti: being intent upon it, they perform it. The translation therefore avoids the misleading ‘worship ignorance’.**
- chosen: upāsate as ‘devote themselves to / practise’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [17]: `tāmavidyāmagnihotrādilakṣaṇāmeva kevalāmupāsate tatparāḥ santo 'nutiṣṭhantītyabhiprāyaḥ` — They upāsate that avidyā alone, characterized by Agnihotra and the like; the intention is: being intent upon it, they perform it.

**J4. The vidyā condemned here when isolated is not supreme Self-knowledge. Śaṅkara identifies it as deity-directed knowledge connected with karma and expressly excludes paramātma-jñāna.**
- chosen: vidyā = devatā-jñāna, knowledge concerning deities (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [5]: `yaddaivaṃ vittaṃ devatāviṣayaṃ jñānaṃ karmasambandhitvenopanyastaṃ na paramātmajñānam` — That divine wealth is knowledge whose object is deities, presented as connected with karma, not knowledge of the supreme Self.
- isup [18]: `ye u ye tu vidyāyāmeva devatājñāna eva ratāḥ abhiratāḥ` — ye u: those, however, who are delighted—attached—in vidyā alone, that is, in deity-knowledge alone.

**J5. The second relative clause is contrastive: ‘those, however’. Ratāḥ is taken as ‘delighted/attached’, reinforced by Śaṅkara’s abhiratāḥ.**
- chosen: u as contrastive tu, and ratāḥ as abhiratāḥ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [18]: `ye u ye tu vidyāyāmeva devatājñāna eva ratāḥ abhiratāḥ` — ye u means ‘those, however’; ratāḥ means ‘attached/delighted’ in deity-knowledge alone.

**J6. The ablatival tatas refers back to the blind darkness, while bhūya iva is glossed as bahutaram eva; the second clause also carries the verb praviśanti supplied in the commentary.**
- chosen: tato bhūya iva ... tamaḥ as ‘a darkness still greater than that, indeed’ (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [18]: `tatastasmādandhātmakāttamaso bhūya iva bahutarameva te tamaḥ praviśanti` — From/than that blind darkness, into a still more abundant darkness indeed, they enter.

**J7. The translation’s ‘alone’ is justified by Śaṅkara’s explanation that the verse censures the separate performance of each member in order to motivate a combination of karma and deity-knowledge; it is not an absolute rejection of scripture-enjoined rites.**
- chosen: the condemnation targets isolated practice, not scripturally enjoined karma or vidyā as such (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [4]: `iha tu samuccicīṣayā avidvadādinindā kriyate` — Here, however, censure of the ignorant and so on is made with the desire for combination.
- isup [9]: `tayorjñānakarmaṇoriha ekaikānuṣṭhānanindā samuccicīṣayā na nindāparaiva ekaikasya pṛthakphalaśravaṇāt` — Here there is censure of the separate performance of each of the two, knowledge and karma, for the sake of combining them; it is not mere censure, since a separate result is heard for each.
- isup [14]: `na hi śāstravihitaṃ kiñcidakartavyatāmiyāt` — For nothing enjoined by scripture can become something not to be done.
- isup [19]: `tatrāvāntaraphalabhedaṃ vidyākarmaṇoḥ samuccayakāraṇamāha` — There he states the distinction of subsidiary results of vidyā and karma as the reason for their combination.

## Translation

> Into blind darkness enter
those who devote themselves to avidyā—ritual action alone.
Into a darkness still greater than that, indeed, enter those
who, however, delight in vidyā—deity-knowledge alone.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation would plausibly render avidyā simply as ‘ignorance’; Śaṅkara instead says it means karma, specifically Agnihotra and the like.
- It would likely render vidyā simply as ‘knowledge’ or even saving Self-knowledge; Śaṅkara explicitly restricts it here to deity-knowledge and excludes paramātma-jñāna.
- It might take the verse as a blanket denunciation of Vedic rites or knowledge; Śaṅkara says the target is isolated performance, in order to motivate combination.
- It might translate iva weakly as ‘as it were’; Śaṅkara’s bahutaram eva makes the force ‘still greater indeed’.
- It might miss u as contrastive ‘however’ and miss the commentary’s kevalam/eva, which motivates translating both pursuits as being undertaken ‘alone’.
