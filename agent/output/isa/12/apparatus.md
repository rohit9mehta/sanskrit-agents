## Justifications

**J1. The object of upāsate is interpreted through Śaṅkara’s explicit identification of asambhūti as prakṛti, kāraṇa/avidyā, called avyākṛta, and as the seed of desire and action, of the nature of non-seeing.**
- chosen: asambhūti = the unmanifest causal prakṛti, the cause/avidyā, not simply “non-existence” or “destruction.” (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [1]: `tasyā anyā asambhūtiḥ prakṛtiḥ kāraṇamavidyā avyākṛtākhyā tāmasambhūtimavyākṛtākhyāṃ prakṛtiṃ kāraṇamavidyāṃ kāmakarmabījabhūtāmadarśanātmikāmupāsate ye` — Different from that is asambhūti: prakṛti, the cause, ignorance, called the unmanifest; those who worship that asambhūti—called unmanifest, prakṛti, the cause, ignorance, become the seed of desire and action, of the nature of non-seeing.

**J2. The locative sambhūtyām is not left as generic “birth” or “production”; Śaṅkara specifies the referent as kāryabrahman, Hiraṇyagarbha.**
- chosen: sambhūti = the effected/produced Brahman, called Hiraṇyagarbha. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [1]: `sambhavanaṃ sambhūtiḥ sā yasya kāryasya sā sambhūtiḥ` — Coming-into-being is sambhūti; that product which has it is sambhūti.
- isup [2]: `ya u sambhūtyāṃ kāryabrahmaṇi hiraṇyagarbhākhye ratāḥ` — Those who are devoted to sambhūti, the effected Brahman called Hiraṇyagarbha.

**J3. Śaṅkara makes the darkness not merely physical gloom but adarśanātmaka, of the nature of non-seeing, and says it corresponds to the worshipped asambhūti.**
- chosen: andhaṃ tamaḥ is “blind/sightless darkness,” a state of non-seeing corresponding to the object worshipped. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [1]: `te tadanurūpamevāndhaṃ tamo 'darśanātmakaṃ praviśanti` — They enter just a blind darkness corresponding to that, of the nature of non-seeing.

**J4. The second pāda is comparative, not merely temporal “then”; Śaṅkara glosses tataḥ as tasmād api and bhūyaḥ as bahutaram iva, and explicitly repeats tamaḥ praviśanti.**
- chosen: tataḥ bhūya iva means “greater than that, as it were,” and the verb praviśanti is supplied in the second half. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [2]: `tatastasmādapi bhūyo bahutaramiva tamaḥ praviśanti` — tataḥ means even than that; they enter darkness more, much greater as it were.

**J5. Śaṅkara’s paraphrase preserves the accusative tām asambhūtim with upāsate and the locative sambhūtyām with ratāḥ, fixing the syntactic construal.**
- chosen: asambhūtim is the accusative object of upāsate, while sambhūtyām is a locative with ratāḥ. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [1]: `tāmasambhūtimavyākṛtākhyāṃ prakṛtiṃ kāraṇamavidyāṃ kāmakarmabījabhūtāmadarśanātmikāmupāsate ye` — Those who worship that asambhūti, called unmanifest, prakṛti, the cause, ignorance, of the nature of non-seeing.
- isup [2]: `ya u sambhūtyāṃ kāryabrahmaṇi hiraṇyagarbhākhye ratāḥ` — Those who are devoted in/to sambhūti, the effected Brahman called Hiraṇyagarbha.

## Translation

> Into blind darkness enter
those who worship the Unmanifest (asambhūti), causal prakṛti;
into darkness greater than that, as it were, enter
those indeed who are devoted to Manifestation (sambhūti),
the produced Brahman called Hiraṇyagarbha.

## Analyzer disagreements

- ByT5 lemmas praviś and upās include prefixes; the required format gives bare roots viś and ās with prefixes pra and upa.
- ByT5 leaves bhūyas without a full inflectional tag; I analyze pausal bhūyaḥ as Napumsaka Dvitiya Eka agreeing with tamaḥ, supported by Śaṅkara’s gloss bhūyo bahutaramiva tamaḥ.
- ByT5 gives ratāḥ derivationally under ram; for subanta analysis I give the inflected participial stem rata, while noting derivation from √ram.
- ByT5 normalizes asaṃbhūtim; I keep the source/commentary spelling asambhūtim/asambhūti. This is orthographic, not a different grammatical analysis.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation might render asambhūti from the lexicon as mere “non-existence” or “destruction”; Śaṅkara instead makes it the unmanifest causal prakṛti/avidyā.
- A commentary-blind translation might take sambhūti as generic “birth” or “production”; Śaṅkara specifies the produced Brahman, Hiraṇyagarbha.
- One might read tataḥ as simply temporal “then”; the bhāṣya makes it comparative—“even than that”—and supplies the repeated verb “enter.”
- One might miss that “darkness” is adarśanātmaka, “of the nature of non-seeing,” corresponding to the worshipped principle.
