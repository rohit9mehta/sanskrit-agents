## Justifications

**J1. Translate the term as ‘asambhūti, the unmanifest’, while recording the printed morphology as sambhūti. This is not a generic ‘birth/production’ here.**
- chosen: The two printed forms saṃbhūtim/saṃbhūtyā are understood, in Śaṅkara’s interpretation, as asambhūtim/asambhūtyā: the unmanifest, the avyākṛta, and its worship. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [2]: `sambhūtiṃ ca vināśaṃ cetyatrāvarṇalopena nirdeśo draṣṭavyaḥ prakṛtilayaphalaśrutyanurodhāt` — In the expression ‘sambhūtiṃ ca vināśaṃ ca’, the statement is to be seen as made with elision of the vowel a, because that agrees with the scriptural statement of a fruit consisting in dissolution into prakṛti.
- isup [1]: `asambhūtyā avyākṛtopāsanayā amṛtaṃ prakṛtilayalakṣaṇamaśnute` — By asambhūti, that is, by worship of the unmanifest, he attains the deathless, whose mark is dissolution into prakṛti.

**J2. Do not translate vināśena as ‘by destruction’ in the abstract. In Śaṅkara’s construal, the thing called vināśa is the kārya whose property is destruction; the instrumental is upāsanā-instrumental.**
- chosen: vināśa denotes the perishable effect/dharmin, and vināśena means ‘by worship of that’, identified in the commentary with Hiraṇyagarbha-upāsana. (depends on commentary: **yes**)
- isup [1]: `vināśo dharmo yasya kāryasya sa tena dharmiṇā abhedena ucyate vināśa iti, tena tadupāsanena` — The effect whose property is destruction is, by non-difference from the possessor of that property, called ‘vināśa’; by that, by worship of it...

## Translation

> He who knows that pair together—
asambhūti, the unmanifest (the text’s saṃbhūti read with a-elision), and vināśa, the perishable effect—
having crossed death (mṛtyu) by vināśa, that is, by its worship,
attains the deathless (amṛta), the state marked by dissolution into prakṛti, by asambhūti.

## Analyzer disagreements

- tīrtvā: ByT5 gives root tṛ; I analyze the absolutive as from √tṝ ‘cross/pass beyond’, confirmed by Śaṅkara’s atītya in line 1.

## One-shot delta

- A commentary-blind translation would likely render saṃbhūti as ‘birth/production/manifestation’; Śaṅkara instead reads it as asambhūti, the unmanifest, through a-varṇa-lopa.
- It might take vināśena as ‘by destruction’; Śaṅkara takes vināśa as the perishable effect and the instrumental as referring to its worship, specifically Hiraṇyagarbha-upāsana.
- It might translate amṛtam as unrestricted immortality or final liberation; Śaṅkara glosses it as prakṛtilaya, dissolution into prakṛti.
- It might take mṛtyu as ordinary death alone; Śaṅkara identifies it with anaiśvarya and defect-born limitation such as adharma and kāma.
